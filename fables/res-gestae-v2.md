# Res Gestae

The heat under the salt-house kang had settled into the bricks for the night, steady and dry through the quilts.

Along the middle of the platform, Amy had turned sideways in her sleep again, one arm flung across Chisa’s shoulder and one wool-socked ankle hooked squarely over the edge of Iuno’s coverlet. Further down by the central post, Cartethyia sat propped upright with her chin on her chest and the window shutter left off its hook above her head, her fingers looped loosely into the end of Ciaccona’s braid. Near the far wall Suisui and Yangyang lay back to back under one heavy quilt.

Iuno lay near the inner wall with a brick from Wen’s bread-oven wrapped in grey flannel and wedged snug under her bare left shoulder, keeping the night damp out of the frost-crack in the crystal.

Inside her folded coat beside the bolster, her Terminal gave a low buzz against the dented side of her tea flask.

It buzzed a second time. Iuno reached across her ribs with her right hand, slid the glass rectangle out of the pocket, and angled it under the tent of the blanket so the glare wouldn’t hit Amy’s eyelids.

Augusta’s name stood white in the middle of the dark screen.

Iuno clicked the side key to quiet the ring, slipped two fingers under Amy’s ankle, and lifted it onto Chisa’s side of the seam. Amy mumbled into her jumper sleeve and burrowed deeper without waking. Getting the rest of herself off the kang one-handed was clumsier work: Iuno braced her knuckles on the warm brick, tucked the flannel-wrapped oven stone and her coat together into the crook of her elbow, and swung both bare feet toward the wooden kerb by the stove.

Lupa was asleep along the outer rim in a heap of rust-red sheepskin, one heavy tail sprawled flat across the step-down like a draught-bolster.

Iuno balanced on her right heel, hitched the slipping coat higher with her chin, and stepped wide over the fur. As she tilted forward, the jagged crystal at her left socket brushed her collar-button and gave a thin, clear chime. Lupa’s left ear twitched; the tail thumped once against the masonry and drew itself back under Lupa’s own shin.

Behind Iuno, the inner quilt rustled. Mei pushed herself up onto one elbow, hair standing sideways, her eyes catching the tiny red glow from the charging crate by the door.

"<font color=moccasin>Brick gone cold?</font>" Mei whispered, thick with sleep.

"<font color=royalblue>Still warm,</font>" Iuno whispered back, working her feet into her unlaced boots on the flagstones. "<font color=royalblue>Go back to sleep, sweetheart.</font>"

"<font color=moccasin>Mm.</font>" Mei dragged Iuno’s empty side of the quilt tight against her stomach to hold the heat in it, propped her cheek on her fist, and kept one golden eye open on the threshold.

Iuno lifted the wooden latch, eased the plank door clear of the sill, and drew it shut behind her until the iron tongue settled home.

Frost lay white across the cobbles of the yard and along the coping of the well. Beside the well-head stood the cabbage-seller’s trestle, the red-ruled ledger closed beneath a square of oiled cloth with a flat grey stone sitting on top to hold the corners down. Pale mist was rising out of the gorge below the terraces, smelling of wet limestone and banked coal.

Iuno sat on the narrow cedar bench backed against the warm flue-wall under the salt-house eaves, pulled her coat across her shoulders, wedged the flannel brick beneath her left socket, and opened the line.

Yellow lamplight filled the glass.

Augusta sat in her linen shirtsleeves at the oak window-table of her house above the old forum, cuffs turned back from her scarred forearms. Through the dark casement behind her shoulder, the roofs of the lower porticoes lay black and quiet; down on the flagstones, a night-watchman’s lantern swung past at an unhurried walk and vanished behind the steps of the Senate porch. On the braided rug behind Augusta’s boot-heels lay Cub, a dark, heavy bulk rising and falling in slow sleep.

A pair of narrow iron-rimmed spectacles sat low on the crooked bridge of Augusta’s nose. The moment Iuno’s face came clear on the screen, Augusta reached up, plucked the spectacles off as though someone else had left them there, and set them down beside the oil-lamp.

Behind her, the long oak table and two high-backed chairs pulled against it were buried under three pasteboard Temple boxes bound in faded tape, a fat buckram ledger, and a thick rolled bundle of heavy paper jutting over the chair-arm. Near the brass foot of the lamp lay a pair of garden shears and a short offcut of copper wire.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>You’re sitting outdoors,</font>" Augusta said, squinting slightly at the gorge mist curling past the eaves.

"<font color=royalblue>The house is asleep.</font>"

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Put your arm through the sleeve, Mouse. Don’t wear a coat like an invalid at the baths. Button the neck.</font>"

Iuno rested the Terminal upright against her thigh, worked her right arm down the wool sleeve, pulled the left lapel round over the crystal and the warm brick, and fastened the horn button under her chin with thumb and forefinger. "<font color=royalblue>Better?</font>"

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Barely.</font>" Augusta shifted her weight in the wooden chair, pressing the heel of her palm once against her bad left hip. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Squall came in off the headland before dusk. Ripped the winter canes loose along the south rose-trellis. Spent an hour in the wet tying them back to the laths before supper.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>Did the old yellow climber by the cistern pipe hold?</font>"

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Held better than the mortar. Which is more than I can say for the harbour committee.</font>" Augusta’s mouth went flat. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Kept me sitting in the curia until the lamps came in, arguing over whether the new fish-market awnings ought to be striped red for the magistracy or left plain tarred sailcloth.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>Plain sailcloth,</font>" Iuno said. "<font color=royalblue>Red madder rots in salt spray before the second winter. Tell the clerks to look up the dockyard bills.</font>"

Augusta snorted once through her nose. Her fingers strayed toward the iron-rimmed spectacles beside the lamp, stopped, and picked them up anyway, hooking the wires back behind her ears with an irritable set to her jaw.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose finished,</font>" she said.

Iuno’s hand stilled on her coat button.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Brought the lot up from the hill a few evenings back,</font>" Augusta said, resting one broad palm on the fat buckram ledger. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Wheeled it up the cobbles herself in the bakehouse handcart after the dusk bell. Two of the gate-orderlies went down the steps to lift the boxes off the barrow and she told them to keep their greasy thumbs off her tape. Carried all three up the stair on her own hip, set this ledger on top, and wiped her hands on her apron.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>What did she say?</font>"

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Said, 'Everything that had a space her size round it is in the tape, Ephor, and keep the bottom box clear of the hearth-fender, it came up out of the salt.' Then she turned the cart round and walked back down to the Temple.</font>"

The oil in the glass bowl beside Augusta’s elbow was burned well past the middle mark. Dark smudges sat under her lower lids in the yellow light.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>I’ve been through it since,</font>" Augusta said. "<font color=#c8c8e4>All of it. More than once, on most of the wrappers.</font>"

She opened the heavy buckram cover and tilted the book toward the lens so the wick lit Rose Marigold’s square, unhurried caretaker’s hand.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Bottom box is what she’d already gathered out of the eastern tower,</font>" Augusta said, turning past the first leaves. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Your diagram from the wall. The disposition table at the foot of the sheet, and the two words in the margin beside Mei’s name. Rose left those in their old wrappers and tied them at the bottom.</font>"

She turned another leaf toward the archive section and held the book steady against the edge of the oak. Leaning closer to the small glass in the frost, Iuno read the entry at the top of the page for herself:

> Eastern Archive, fourth press. Official tariff and harvest codices, vellum, bound in calfskin. Outer margins scraped bare throughout with a pumice-knife, saving one broken downstroke at the foot of the oldest tide-folio. I took these volumes down to dust every winter and read what was written in the gutters before shelving them. The skin is thin where the pumice went, and I cannot call a word of it to mind.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>That was how she worked the rest of the city,</font>" Augusta said, laying the ledger flat again and untying the faded tape on the uppermost box. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Looked for the holes. Warrants with the Great Signet whole and nobody’s hand on the signature line. Columns where the clerk’s ink jumps across an empty berth. Anything with a byname the water didn’t know to take.</font>"

She lifted out a dark pine board chalked over under tallow-varnish and a packet of folded municipal dockets, glancing down at the open book to read Rose’s note aloud:

> Grain Office cellar, bundle behind the coal-bunker. Pine price-boards from the third-district public ovens, chalk sealed with tallow, from the winter after the Silva forfeiture; together with the Senate notary’s minute thanking the Ephor for opening the harbour-dredging chest to cheapen the common loaf. Wood sound; worm in the lower batten.

Augusta looked over the iron rims of her spectacles. "<font color=#c8c8e4>I spent that whole winter up at the north fords in freezing mud. Never signed a warrant on the dredging chest.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>You couldn’t have,</font>" Iuno said at once. "<font color=royalblue>The dredging chest was empty before the autumn equinoctial. The Senate notary guessed because he didn’t care to walk down the hill to the customs shed. I drew the wheat against the confiscated Silva wharf-rents at the Salt-Gate and made the millers in Fuller’s Lane grind at cost against their sluice-arrears.</font>"

Augusta’s mouth twitched once at the left corner as she checked the top of Rose’s page. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose put the bundle under *Vestry Accounts, Irregular*.</font>"

*<font color=thistle>Irregular. I kept the whole lower ward in bolted wheat-bread through a black frost without touching the war-chest, and Rose shelves me behind the tallow-candles.</font>*

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Staying with that winter,</font>" Augusta said, setting the pine board back and drawing a single sheet of cream Temple stock from the top of the folder. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Sister Livia walked into Rose’s parlour last week on her own feet.</font>"

She angled the buckram ledger toward the glass again. Iuno read the short entry in the lamplight:

> Residence Wing, Caretaker’s parlour. Statement given in person by Sister Livia, formerly of House Silva, novice from her eighth winter, now keeper of the third basin. One leaf, fair copy, signed in the witness’s own hand. Dry.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Told Rose she remembers two sanctuary sisters taking her by the wrists in the Colosseum passage and pulling a grown woman’s blue wool robe over her ears, pinned up at the sleeves with brass bodkins,</font>" Augusta said, looking down at the cream sheet in her fingers. "<font color=#c8c8e4>And she remembers coughing through that first winter at the end of the novices’ bench while someone sat down beside her every evening before vespers and cut up her supper into her lentils so she wouldn’t have to manage the heavy knife. Says whenever she tries to picture the face above the blue cuff, her eyes water and there’s only the whitewash on the refectory wall.</font>"

Augusta turned the leaf slightly toward the flame. "<font color=#c8c8e4>She put a question at the bottom. Asked Rose to pass it along if anyone ever answered for the boxes.</font>"

Inside her coat, Iuno’s fingers tightened on the rough flannel round the brick. "<font color=royalblue>Go on.</font>"

Augusta read from the foot of the page:

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Did you keep me off the ship because of the cough, or to keep my mother quiet?</font>"

A drift of cold mist slid along the salt-house eaves and caught the silver-blue hair loose at Iuno’s temple. Her breath went out white across the dark wool at her chin and thinned into the yard while Augusta laid Livia’s sheet carefully back inside the tape.

From the chair beside her elbow Augusta lifted the heavy rolled bundle of paper, clearing a space before the lamp with her forearm.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Brasca’s tallow-leather flight books from the Upper Mews are in the middle box,</font>" Augusta said as she slipped the twine knot off the roll. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Every autumn and spring flight since I took the chair. Rider, bird, came home. The column for who oiled the bridles is empty down every page—no kitchen-sister’s name in it either, saving that one autumn the corporals got into sour wine and asked for Lucilla.</font>"

She spread the top strip of paper flat across the oak, weighted its curling corner with her iron ink-pot, and read the slip pinned to the margin:

> Vaulted salt-cistern beneath the eastern stair. Paper rubbings in cobbler’s wax taken from the suspended brass tide-rings above the central well. Names cut into the inner rims with a smith’s graver in one cutter’s hand across sundry seasons; no maker’s stamp. Brass spotted green at the pins; lettering clear throughout.

Augusta ran two scarred fingers slowly down the grey wax rubbing where pale letters stood through the dark grain, stopping halfway along the curve.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Rufus from the Rope-Walk is on the second ring,</font>" Augusta said. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Boy never could get his helmet-strap flat when his hands were cold. I leaned out of my saddle at the timber gate that morning and made him buckle it over.</font>"

She lifted her eyes from the rubbing and looked straight through the glass. "<font color=#c8c8e4>How long before the gates opened did you know?</font>"

Beneath the hem of Iuno’s coat, her right hand gave a small, fine tremor against her knee. She pressed her palm flat down against her shinbone until the tendons held still, looking back into the yellow circle of Augusta’s lamp.

Augusta waited the space of two breaths, lifted the iron ink-pot off the corner, and let the stiff paper roll itself quietly back into a tube on the chair.

She drew the middle box forward. Even over the relay, the dark tide-stains crusted into its lower pasteboard corners showed grey in the light.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Harbour-works,</font>" Augusta said, untying the tape. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Night-log from the Fourth Sea-Lock, the winter the surge broke the harbour boom. Entry says the outer iron portcullis was knocked off its oak wedges and spiked into the sill before the sand-glass turned, with the curing-sheds and the fish-quay still full on the seaward side. Entered *By order*, and clean paper after it.</font>"

She lifted out a folded clerk’s transcript and tilted Rose’s ledger toward the screen. Iuno read off the glass:

> Harbour Works chest, lower drawer. Two strips of sheet-lead brought up in one dredger’s bucket from the silt outside the Fourth Sea-Lock after the winter surge, with the clerk’s fair copy made before lock-up. First strip whole, wrapped round a copper piece, thanking the lady in blue on the hill for a child kept dry inside the gate. Second strip folded flat and driven through with an iron spike.

Augusta began to lay the clerk’s copy down beside the lock-book. "<font color=#c8c8e4>First one’s an herb-wife named Clodia. Says her girl was fetching leeks on the town side when the iron dropped.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>Read me the second one,</font>" Iuno said.

Augusta’s hand stayed on the folded sheet. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose walked down to the curing-sheds and the rope-walk herself. Found two old net-menders in the loft who remembered a Fulvia at the pilchard-troughs on the outer slip. They told her Fulvia had a younger sister who spun hemp on the walk and went inland with a hand-bundle before the spring rains. Nobody down by the water knows which road she took, or whether she’s still above ground.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>Read it, Augusta.</font>"

Augusta brought the clerk’s sheet back into the light beside the wick and read it flat, from first word to last:

"<font color=#c8c8e4>To the gods beneath the harbour mud I give the woman who ordered the fourth gate dropped while the fish-quay was still full. My sister Fulvia drowned against the outer bars when the black water came over the sill. As the gate stood shut to Fulvia, let every door stand shut to her. Take the sight from her eyes, the steadiness from her hands, the rest from her bed, the warmth from her fire, and let no stone in this city keep her dry.</font>"

Under Iuno’s wool lapel, the cracked shoulder crystal clicked once against the cooling brick as she drew a long breath through her nose. Her face stayed still in the pale wash of the screen until Augusta folded the paper along its crease and set it back inside the box.

Augusta reached toward the third box—the salt-whitened one pushed furthest from the hearth—and stopped with her fingers on the knotted tape. Outside her casement, the watchman’s lantern came back along the dark forum flags from the other direction, throwing a thin gold bar across her ceiling beams before passing out of sight.

She took the iron-rimmed spectacles off for good, set them beside the garden shears, and rubbed her thumb once across the old break in her nose. Untying the third box, she drew out a parchment warrant with a lead seal hanging from its cord and a thin stitched booklet of foreign rag-paper.

She read Rose’s wrapper-note aloud without putting the lenses back on:

> Temple Muniment Press, bottom drawer, and the bricked chamber beneath the third arch of the aqueduct. Surveyor’s warrant and lease to a company of players, Great Signet unbroken, officiant’s line blank, from the winter the Champion was lost at the mole; together with a fair-copied masque on foreign paper, found in an oiled tin on the inner stone shelf when the grate was forced.

Augusta laid her broad palm flat over the stitched booklet. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose took a crowbar to the grate under the third arch,</font>" she said, low. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Brought these two up inside her shawl instead of the cart.</font>"

Her pale eyes came up to the screen. "<font color=#c8c8e4>There’s a speech in here about the space at the harbour boom before your white lamp lit in the tower. I never knew anyone off the point had a glass on me when I put my boot on the coping-stone. And I never knew what was paid to keep what I hear in the water out of the barber-shops.</font>" Her fingers pressed once into the foreign rag-paper. "<font color=#c8c8e4>The dry vault under the third arch. And Mya’s body off the cedar wood.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>Julia Silva’s boatmen brought her up the sewer-culvert under a tarpaulin,</font>" Iuno said quietly. "<font color=royalblue>I wrote the warrant on the rim of the cistern well.</font>"

Augusta nodded once, slow, and slid both pieces back beneath the salt-stained lid.

Only one small sheet of tissue rubbing-paper remained outside the boxes, lying separate by the brass foot of the oil-lamp where Augusta had kept it out on the oak.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>One more,</font>" Augusta said. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose sent a mason’s boy round the lower arcade at the Colosseum before she shut the book. Down by the Water-Gate steps, where they used to chalk the dusk pairs.</font>"

She lifted the tissue toward the lens first so the lamp shone behind the pinned slip in Rose’s hand:

> Colosseum, lower arcade, travertine pier beside the Water-Gate stair, directly beneath the old win-cuts for Augusta of the Locks. Tissue rubbing from red plaster, incised with a knife-point, worn smooth along the bottom edge by shoulders brushing past.

Augusta turned the thin sheet back toward herself and read the cut letters aloud in her level arena voice:

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Augusta of the Locks, standing. Never lay copper against her when the Mouse is on the end bench in the big blue robe. She has to stand on her own hem to see over the coping, and she hasn’t missed a bout since the frost.</font>"

Augusta lowered the tissue to the oak beside the lamp-base.

Right beside her cuff, the finger-length offcut of copper trellis-wire stirred by itself on the dark wood. Slowly, without a click, both cut ends lifted a hair’s breadth and curled inward toward each other, winding tight until they locked into a small copper ring against the brass foot of the lamp. Augusta’s gaze rested on the black windowpane beside her shoulder; through the glass, Iuno watched the wire settle in the yellow light, and raised her eyes back to Augusta’s face.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Nobody else has seen a line of this,</font>" Augusta said. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Not the Senate. Not the Council. Not that lottery-drawn auditor you built to catch people steering the city from behind the curtains. Not Angel. Rose locked her rough slips in the cistern press and gave me the iron key.</font>" She folded both hands on top of the buckram ledger. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Tell me what you want done with it.</font>"

"<font color=royalblue>You’re the Ephor, Augusta,</font>" Iuno said.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>It isn’t my name missing from the paper,</font>" Augusta said, at the exact same pitch.

Under the frosty eaves, Iuno sat still against the warm chimney-bricks with the Terminal balanced on her knee and listened to the mist drip off the gutter-spout.

*<font color=thistle>Give the auditor the grain-boards and drop the middle box in the grate, darling. Every magistrate since the hill was walled has trimmed her own monument, and half the Senate owes you its roof-tiles. Or better yet, tell her to heat the bath-copper with the whole cartload—we walked out of that city on our own feet once already, and nobody left inside it can spell our name to summon us.</font>*

Iuno heard her all the way to the end, let her breath out slow through her nose, and looked into the lens.

"<font color=royalblue>Keep the originals taped in your room until I get back,</font>" Iuno said. "<font color=royalblue>Have Rose make a fair copy of the whole lot—her ledger, the rubbings, both lead strips, the tower sheets, all of it—or give it to one clerk she’d trust with her own keys, and send the copy across to me here.</font>"

Augusta gave a single short nod, listening.

"<font color=royalblue>I want my own people here to read it first,</font>" Iuno went on, clear and steady now that her feet were on it. "<font color=royalblue>Mei, and Amy, and Lupa, and anyone else under this roof who wants the lid off. Then, when we come back across the water, I’m going up to the third basin to see Sister Livia before I see anyone else, and answer her to her face. Her statement is hers; she chooses whether it stays in the tape.</font>"

Her right hand lay quiet on the dark wool over her knee. "<font color=royalblue>After Livia, the others, on foot, one door at a time. Starting with Fulvia’s sister, if she’s still living. I don’t know which town she went to, and I don’t know if she’s above ground. I’ll go and find out. Once I’ve knocked on those doors, the whole cartload goes onto the lottery auditor’s desk without a word scraped out—the bread, the tower papers, the fourth lock, and the spiked lead together. Where the warrants and the flight-books have an empty berth, I’ll sit down with a reed and write my name in by hand.</font>" She met Augusta’s eyes through the glass. "<font color=royalblue>I don’t know whether the ink will hold on the page. I don’t know how things turn out beforehand any more.</font>"

Before Augusta could speak, Iuno added, "<font color=royalblue>Except the aqueduct warrant and the play. That one is about you. You say whether it goes to the auditor.</font>"

"<font color=#c8c8e4>It goes in,</font>" Augusta said at once.

She leaned an inch closer to the lamp, her broad shoulders squaring back into working order. "<font color=#c8c8e4>Rose copies the ledger herself. She won’t trust a vestry clerk not to blot the gutters anyway. I’ll have the lot soldered into an oiled tin dispatch-case and sent overland from the coast with the grain-guild courier, key in a separate pouch. Now get out of that mist before you split that stone on your shoulder worse, eat something hot in the morning, and get back into bed.</font>"

Behind Augusta’s chair, the inner bedroom door creaked open a hand’s width. Angel stood barefoot on the threshold with a dark wool cloak pulled round her sleep-shirt and her hair loose over one collarbone, looking once at the taped Temple boxes and then at Augusta’s bare forearms.

"<font color=#fcf9d9>The grate’s stone cold in here, Gus,</font>" Angel murmured, resting two fingers briefly against the top rail of Augusta’s chair before turning back into the dark bedroom.

Augusta glanced over her shoulder after her, then looked back into the glass at Iuno buttoned to the chin under the frosty eaves.

"<font color=#c8c8e4>Good to see your face, Mouse,</font>" Augusta said, and reached out a broad thumb to tap the red key before Iuno could answer.

The screen went black, holding only the dim reflection of whitewash and drifting gorge mist. Under Iuno’s left arm, the flannel-wrapped brick had gone lukewarm all the way through. Across the quiet yard, the flat grey stone sat dry on the oiled cloth over the well-head ledger.

Iuno stood up from the cedar bench, lifted the iron latch, and stepped back into the dark salt-house, drawing the plank door gently home against the jamb.

From the outer edge of the kang, Lupa grunted into her sheepskin collar without lifting her head. "<font color=indianred>Draught, princess.</font>"

Iuno toed her damp boots off beside the door-crate and set the lukewarm brick down by the stove-flue for morning. Standing in the tiny red-and-green glow of the charging harness on the packing-box, she worked her small ruled pad and brass-capped pencil out of her coat pocket, thumbed past the gridded week to the dateless page near the back where *Spring. Egla. Bell tower, north side* and *Bring a ladder* stood in neat strokes, braced the pad against her knee, and wrote underneath:

*Septimont. Third basin — Sister Livia.*
*Inland from the fish-quay — Fulvia’s sister.*

She slid the pencil into the spine, dropped the pad back into her pocket beside the unopened velvet box, and stepped carefully over Lupa’s tucked-in tail onto the warm bricks.

By the inner wall, Mei lay awake on her side, golden eyes clear in the dark, holding Iuno’s corner of the quilt lifted to keep the warmth underneath. Iuno lay down with her chilled crystal shoulder turned in against Mei’s chest; Mei drew the quilt tight behind her back, settled a warm palm over the cold edge below the stone, and shut her eyes.
