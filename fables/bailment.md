### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

## I.

The list came out of Iuno's coat on the Sunday and went under the pot, which was where the house kept its business.

Three names, one under the other, in her small hand.

Mei read it standing, with her jacket half on and one arm still hunting the sleeve.

<font color=moccasin>"Ah."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You've made a to-do list out of people."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I've written down three people."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"With a *pot* on them."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The pot was already there."</font> Iuno moved it two inches left, which resolved nothing and had the shape of resolving something. <font color=royalblue>"You have two days. You have been finding jobs since Wednesday. On Wednesday you regrouted the shower."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"The grout was going."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The grout was eleven months old."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It was going *early.*"</font>

Lupa came through for the kettle, read the list over Mei's shoulder at a dead stop, made a sound low in her chest, and left with the kettle.

<font color=indianred>"Coward,"</font> she said, from the corridor.

<font color=moccasin>"I heard that."</font>

<font color=indianred>"You were meant to."</font>

Mei sat down. She turned the paper round with one finger, a thing she had picked up from Iuno and had never noticed picking up, and which Iuno noticed every single time and kept a private file on.

Three names. And under the third, a fourth line.

The fourth line had been added in a hand nobody in that house wrote — small, upright, architecturally neat, the letters spaced as though they had been set rather than written — and then struck through once, cleanly, with a rule.

Mei held it up to the lamp.

The strike was thorough. Underneath it, legible if you were patient, were characters, and the characters had no business being on a shopping-list-sized piece of paper in an underground city four seas from anywhere that wrote them.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I've seen it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"When."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Friday. It was three names on Thursday."</font> Iuno set out cups. <font color=royalblue>"I photographed it, put it back exactly where it was, and said nothing for two days. I'm telling you now because I said I'd tell you things now, and it has become a habit, which is the cheapest form of integrity on the market."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Is it the same hand as—"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The rule through it is somebody's ordinary pen. The characters under it are the hand from the letter and the hand out of your own fingers. I have three examples of it in this building and I can read one stroke of none of them."</font>

Mei put the list down.

<font color=moccasin>"Somebody wrote a fourth person onto my goodbyes."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"And then crossed them off."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's worse."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It is considerably worse,"</font> Iuno agreed, and poured.

---

Amy came down at eight with wet hair and her boots in her hand and put both boots on the table, which was a declaration.

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm coming."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You've got physio."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Moved it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"When?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Cancelled it Friday."</font> She sat. <font color=mistyrose>"I've been watching you find things to do instead for four days. On Wednesday you regrouted a shower."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"The grout—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"The grout was practically new, Ma, Lynae took a picture of it and captioned it *evidence.*"</font>

Iuno turned a page of the manifest, and her silence carried across the room.

<font color=moccasin>"You don't have to do this one."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I know."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It's a long walk to the science quarter and it's uphill at the end—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"*Ma.*"</font> Amy pulled a boot on. <font color=mistyrose>"She fed me for thirteen years. You get to come with *me.*"</font>

The kitchen took that.

Mei looked at the third name on the list, then at the struck-out fourth, then at her daughter, who was doing her laces with the terrible efficiency of somebody who had decided the conversation was over.

<font color=moccasin>"Okay,"</font> she said.

<font color=mistyrose>"You can't just say okay."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Why does everybody keep telling me that."</font>

## II.

Professor Mornye's office had a window onto the Solistree avenue, a kettle she operated personally as a point of doctrine, and the same square metres of floor it had held on the day she was assigned it at nineteen.

She was at the kettle when they came in. She had heard the chair before the door, because there was a chair now, and it hummed at rest, and she had learned the entire acoustics of the building from a seated height.

*"Sit anywhere,"* she said. *"The left one lists."*

Amy sat in the left one immediately.

Buddy came off the top of the hovering Starstack in a slow drift, considered Amy at length from about a foot away, and settled on the chair arm, pulsing warm and amber, the way a small relative arrives and simply stays.

<font color=mistyrose>"Hi,"</font> Amy told it.

The kettle went on.

Mei watched it go on and felt something reorganise behind her sternum, because there was a woman in her kitchen who did that — who had once set a kettle so that the length of the boil would decide the length of a confession — and here was a second woman doing it, and the two of them had never in their lives met over one.

*"Four minutes,"* Mornye said, without turning round. *"That's the visit. You'll want the rest of the day."*

<font color=moccasin>"We've got time."</font>

*"You have thirty-one hours and you've allocated me four of them, and I'd rather have four honest minutes than nine polite ones."* She turned the chair. *"I've prepared."*

The box was on the desk. It was a small box. It had a manifest taped to the lid, in two columns, and the manifest had been typed.

Amy leaned over and read it upside down, which she could do, and which nobody had taught her.

<font color=mistyrose>"You *typed* it."</font>

*"I hand-write badly and the recipient is going somewhere with weather."*

<font color=mistyrose>"You typed a *present.*"</font>

*"I typed a manifest. The present is inside the manifest."* Mornye took the lid off. *"Item one. Salt tablets, ninety. Jinzhou sits at four hundred metres in a river bowl with a dam above it and a summer that runs humid. Six of you sweat like Royans and two of you sweat like nothing on record."*

<font color=moccasin>"Mornye—"</font>

*"Item two, hydration salts. Different chemistry, don't mix them, the instructions are on the sachet and also on the lid and also here."* She lifted out a flat card the size of a hand, printed both sides. *"Item three. Frequency reference. Local Discord classes from the Ministry's public bulletins, nine seasons, sorted by what they do to a resonator's inner ear rather than by taxonomy, because taxonomy is for people with a laboratory. Chisa will find the ordering congenial. It's laminated."*

<font color=mistyrose>"She *laminated* it."</font>

*"Item four. Socks."*

<font color=moccasin>"For a *war.*"</font>

*"Nine pairs, a specific weave. I've had them since the autumn."*

<font color=moccasin>"That's eight months."</font>

*"They were correct in the autumn and they have remained correct."* Mornye set them down in a squared stack. *"Item five, a chart of seasonal river level against the dam's stated discharge for eleven years, obtained through the Collective's open-data desk, which is a thing that exists and which nobody uses, and which took one afternoon and a formal request."*

She stopped.

Then she took out a bag of marshmallows and put it beside the socks.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Amy picked up the manifest.

<font color=mistyrose>"'Item six. Morale, calorific.'"</font>

*"That's correct."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Professor."</font>

*"It is calorific, it does affect morale, and I hold data on both."*

<font color=mistyrose>"You have *data* on marshmallow morale."</font>

*"I have thirteen years of data on you,"* Mornye said, and reached past her for the kettle, and the sentence went and stood in the middle of the room where everybody could see it.

Buddy pulsed twice.

---

The tea was strong and slightly wrong and nobody mentioned it.

Mei got up and walked the office, which she did in rooms, and Mornye let her, which was its own hospitality.

There was a lot to walk. The whiteboard carried four working problems in four hands, the professor's among them and the smallest, wedged into a corner with an arrow pointing at somebody else's line. A shelf of student theses bound the cheap way. A photograph of eleven people on a relay floor, taken from a low angle by somebody lying down, most of the faces blurred because most of the people were moving.

<font color=moccasin>"You've kept the room."</font>

*"Thirteen years, and one refurbishment I declined in writing."*

<font color=moccasin>"Bad light."</font>

*"It's the light I've got."*

<font color=moccasin>"Move the board off the north wall,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"You're working in your own shadow half the morning. Put it where the window's behind you and you'll stop leaning."</font>

The room went quiet.

*"Noted,"* Mornye said.

And her hand went out for the stylus on the desk and picked it up and wrote it into the margin of a real page of real work, and she got three words down before her arm understood what it was doing and stopped.

Mei was looking at the window.

Amy was looking at Mornye's hand.

The kettle ticked as it cooled.

*"That happens,"* Mornye said, in exactly the tone she used for reactor tolerances. *"Four times since spring. I've logged them."*

<font color=moccasin>"Of course you have."</font>

*"All in this room. Twice at the board, once in the corridor outside, once here."* She set the stylus down and squared it with the edge of the page, which she did when she wanted her hands somewhere specific. *"Whatever it is sits below memory. There's an object in the room and my arm holds a policy about it."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Sorry."</font>

*"Don't."* A beat. *"It's the only thing I own outright. Everything else about her arrived secondhand. I'd like to keep the one item undamaged by apology."*

Mei laughed, once, at the window, with her head down.

---

Amy waited until the second cup, because she had spent ten years somewhere with no clocks and had come out of it with a horrible sense of timing.

<font color=mistyrose>"Come with us."</font>

Mei's cup stopped halfway.

Mornye put hers down.

*"Three pages,"* she said. *"Drawer on your left, second folder. I did the logistics in March."*

Amy opened the drawer. Three pages, second folder, dated, thorough, with a cost column.

<font color=mistyrose>"That's before the kite."</font>

*"The kite came in in April. I did the pages in March, on the general principle that this household leaves."*

<font color=moccasin>"That's a rough sentence."</font>

*"It's an accurate one and I've had a long time to sand it."* Mornye folded her hands. *"Page one is transport. The chair goes by mech transport, I ride the second cargo bay, it is undignified and it is solved. Page two is my supervision load — four postgraduates, two at write-up, all four routable by courier with annotated replies inside the fortnight. I've done it across a continent. It works. Page three is Helios."*

<font color=moccasin>"Page three is where it stops."</font>

*"Mm."*

She said it level. She had made every word the same size as every other word, which was how she did it, and everybody in the room heard the effort inside the flatness the way you hear a hand laid over a bell.

*"The Drive runs its long maintenance in the ninth month. Helios crosses in front of it for eleven days. That crossing has been modelled by four people and observed by one, and the one is me, and my observation notes are in my own hand and would be insufficient for a stranger."* A pause. *"I could make them sufficient. It would cost a season. I ran that arithmetic too, and the arithmetic says I'd spend the eleven days teaching a substitute the eleven days."*

<font color=mistyrose>"That's not the real reason."</font>

The office went very quiet.

Mornye looked at Amy for some time.

*"No,"* she said.

<font color=mistyrose>"Say the real one."</font>

*"Forty-one."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Forty-one what."</font>

*"Applications to the Synchronist track, logged since the President finished with the investor board. The intake before the sealing was eleven. The intake in your year was six."* She turned her cup a quarter-turn, which she had done for years, which was hers, and which she had never once in her life connected to anybody. *"By autumn there will be children in Rabelle who grew up hearing that a girl held a door for ten years and came home to a party. I write the load curves that decide which of them sits in a chair."*

<font color=moccasin>"So stay,"</font> Mei said.

*"I intend to stay."*

<font color=moccasin>"Then—"</font>

*"I'd like it recorded that I want to go,"* Mornye said.

Buddy stopped pulsing.

*"I have spent my working life in a seat doing sums while other people walked into things. I did it at the relay. I did it at the gate. I did it in a control room for ten minutes and then eleven and then twelve, with my palm flat on a console and a countdown on the wall, while six people I like went somewhere I could not follow, and I was *useful.* Useful in a chair with a headset on is a particular flavour and I have eaten enough of it to have an opinion."*

Nobody moved.

*"I said a thing in a field once,"* she went on. *"In flowers, which was undignified, to a woman who had not heard the version it was correcting. I said: this time, we'll walk together. I've had eleven months of being pleased with that sentence."*

She picked up the manifest and put it down again.

*"It appears I get to revise it a second time. I'll want a while."*

<font color=moccasin>"Mornye."</font>

*"You are under no obligation,"* Mornye said.

Mei's mouth closed.

*"That's the whole of it. I said it once before to somebody in this room and it did nothing then either."* She reached over and squared the socks, which were already square. *"Go. Come back. Send me river levels — the Ministry's published figures run eight per cent optimistic and I want to know by how much."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Same to you,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"On the obligation."</font>

*"I know."*

<font color=moccasin>"Do you."</font>

*"I know it the way you know a thing you have proved and don't believe,"* said Mornye, *"which is the commonest way of knowing anything, and I'd appreciate not being examined on it before lunch."*

## III.

*"There's a cabinet,"* Mornye said, at the door.

Mei had her coat over her arm and had already taken the two steps toward leaving that her body took ahead of her mind agreeing to anything.

*"Aemeath. There's a cabinet."*

Amy stopped.

*"You asked me a question when you were fourteen,"* Mornye said. *"You asked it in the mess hall with a jelly in your hand. You asked it twice, and then you stopped asking, and I noticed the stopping and logged that too, because logging is what I do in place of acting, which is a documented feature."* She rolled back from the door. *"You asked who the report was for."*

Amy said nothing.

*"I'd like to show you the working."*

<font color=mistyrose>"...Okay."</font>

Mei put her coat down on the left chair.

<font color=moccasin>"I'll get water,"</font> she said.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Ten minutes."</font>

Amy looked at her.

<font color=mistyrose>"Fourteen,"</font> she said.

Mei's face did something very brief and entirely private, because that was the rule — the number coming back had to differ from the number going in, so that the thing returning could not counterfeit the thing that went — and her daughter had just used it, in an office, in daylight, to buy four extra minutes with a professor.

She went to find water in a building with a tap every thirty metres.

---

The cabinet stood in the inner room. Steel, four drawers, a lock that took a physical key.

*"It's off-system,"* Mornye said. *"It has always been off-system. On the strictest reading of my terms of employment it is a disciplinary matter, and I have kept it in a room with my name on the door for thirteen years, and nobody has ever asked."*

She unlocked the second drawer.

It was full. Working-full, with card dividers gone soft at the top edge from being pushed at with a thumb ten thousand times.

<font color=mistyrose>"That's — "</font> Amy stopped. <font color=mistyrose>"How often?"</font>

*"Weekly, the first four years. Fortnightly after that, because you acquired friends and the data got noisy. Weekly again from your fifth year."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Why weekly again."</font>

*"Because you got good at hiding things and I raised the sampling rate,"* Mornye said, *"which is the most arrogant sentence I have ever produced, and I stand behind the underlying method."*

Amy laughed. The second half of it came out wrong.

She pulled a card at random. Postcard-sized. A date, a weight, a sleep figure, four lines of prose.

*Ate two dinners. Cried in stairwell C, duration unknown, ended before I arrived. Says the campus cocoa is wrong. It is wrong; I checked against the commissary specification and the specification is wrong; I have submitted a note.*

<font color=mistyrose>"You submitted a *note*—"</font>

*"They rejected it. Twice. I resubmitted with a costing."*

Amy pulled another. Then four more, out of order, the way you eat something standing at a counter.

*Grew. Complains about knees. Pamphlet delivered; reception better than modelled.*

*Barred from Lab 2. Argued her corner with Warren for forty minutes. He is a coward and I enjoyed it.*

*Sang on the roof at 0300 into a stolen transmitter. Did not report it. Stood in the stairwell eleven minutes.*

She stopped at that one and read it three times.

<font color=mistyrose>"Eleven minutes."</font>

*"Approximately."*

<font color=mistyrose>"You stood in a stairwell at three in the morning for eleven minutes listening to a fifteen-year-old do pirate radio."</font>

*"The acoustics in stairwell C are outstanding,"* Mornye said. *"It's the only good thing about that stairwell and I have written to the buildings committee about the rest of it."*

---

Amy went back to the front of the drawer, because she had already seen the thing and had been working toward it sideways.

Every card in the first two years carried a mark at the top. A single character, brushed rather than written, sitting in the corner of the ruled space the way an address sits on an envelope.

She had seen that shape twice in her life. Once on a form on a kitchen table. Once on a piece of paper her mother had held up to a lamp.

<font color=mistyrose>"What's this."</font>

*"That's the heading."*

<font color=mistyrose>"It's the same as—"</font>

*"Yes."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Where did you get it?"</font>

*"She wrote it on a scrap for me,"* Mornye said, *"the night she asked me to delete her. On the corner of a requisition slip. She pushed it across the bench and said* that's where these go, *and then she left the building, and the record, and the city, in that order, inside forty minutes."*

Amy held the card very carefully, by two corners.

<font color=mistyrose>"So you've been writing to her."</font>

*"I have been writing to a character I cannot read, at no address, about a child who was not mine, inside a building where I had personally erased the only person who might have wanted them."* Mornye's hands were folded. *"You may use the word. Several people have. I have a preferred one."*

<font color=mistyrose>"What's your preferred one?"</font>

*"Filing."*

Amy looked at her.

Then she went through the drawer fast, thumb on the top edge, the way Mornye did it, and found where the mark stopped.

It stopped clean. One card had it. The next card did without. Between them lay nine weeks, and the nine weeks were full, because Mornye filed everything, and what filled them was two sheets of Institute letterhead.

The first was a request for release from an informal supervisory arrangement. Two sentences. The reasons box left blank.

The second was a withdrawal of that request, submitted eleven days later, with a note in Mornye's own hand in the margin: *Withdrawn by requester. No reason to be recorded.*

<font color=mistyrose>"You tried to quit me."</font>

*"I tried to quit an assignment."*

<font color=mistyrose>"You put in a *form.*"</font>

*"I put in a form, sat on it eleven days, and put in the other form. I have kept both in the same folder for eleven years, in order, because a result you dislike is still a result."*

<font color=mistyrose>"And then you stopped writing her name on them."</font>

*"I stopped putting the heading on them."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Why."</font>

Mornye looked at the drawer.

*"The addressee was doing nothing with them,"* she said.

Amy stood in an inner room with a card in each hand and kept to herself the thing she knew: that she owned a soft-covered diary with the Academy crest worn off the front, that it opened *Dear Ma* and ran that way for a long time, and that at some point the salutation came off and the entries went on for years afterward addressed to nobody at all, in a smaller hand, about work.

She had never told anyone the salutation came off. She had never told anyone she remembered the day.

<font color=mistyrose>"Yeah,"</font> she said. <font color=mistyrose>"Okay."</font>

*"That's a very small response."*

<font color=mistyrose>"It's the one I've got."</font>

*"Mm,"* said Mornye, and left it there, which cost her something visible, because leaving things was the discipline she was worst at and had been practising longest.

---

The third drawer had a divider in it about a third of the way back, and past the divider the cards stopped.

Amy already knew the date. She had known it before she opened the drawer. She had known it before she came up the hill.

There was one entry on the last card. Two words and a figure, in a hand that had gone slightly wrong halfway through the second word and then continued anyway.

*99.6. Held.*

<font color=mistyrose>"Held,"</font> Amy said.

*"The override."*

<font color=mistyrose>"I know what it means."</font>

*"I want the sentence to have a subject and a verb,"* said Mornye, *"because I have spent ten years not writing it down properly and I am tired of the shorthand."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Then say it."</font>

*"I had the abort in my hand, at ninety-nine point six, with my thumb on it, for four and a half seconds. You asked me to leave it. I left it."*

The room hummed, low, the way rooms in that building hummed.

<font color=mistyrose>"I was nineteen."</font>

*"You were nineteen."*

<font color=mistyrose>"I was nineteen and showing off and I asked you and you did what I asked."</font> Amy's jaw did the thing she had inherited without ever being taught. <font color=mistyrose>"Do you know how many people have told me that was your fault?"</font>

*"Nine."* A pause. *"I heard four of them personally."*

<font color=mistyrose>"They're wrong."</font>

Mornye's hands stayed folded.

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm saying it in a room with the paperwork open,"</font> Amy said, <font color=mistyrose>"because you're a person who needs it in the file. They're wrong. Somebody in that room did what I asked when literally everyone else in my entire life has done what they thought was best for me, and it was the worst outcome available, and it was *mine,* and I'd have hated you forever if you'd taken it."</font>

The building hummed.

<font color=mistyrose>"That came out sideways,"</font> Amy said.

*"It came out at the correct magnitude and in the wrong units,"* Mornye said. *"I can convert."*

Amy put both cards back where they had been, in order, squared, the way you put a thing back in somebody else's system.

Then she closed the drawer, and stood with her hand flat on the front of it.

<font color=mistyrose>"There's a fourth drawer."</font>

*"There is."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Is it started?"</font>

*"Three cards,"* said Mornye.

<font color=mistyrose>"Since when?"</font>

*"Since the gate."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Three cards in a *year*—"</font>

*"You may read them."*

Amy opened the fourth drawer.

Three cards. No heading on any of them.

*Returned. Ambulatory. Ate a full meal in my presence. No measurements taken.*

*Attended a festival at the new shrine. Laughed at something I did not hear.*

*Asked me for the cocoa recipe. I gave her the wrong one, deliberately, to see. She corrected me inside four seconds, including the salt.*

Amy read the third one twice.

<font color=mistyrose>"You *tested* me."</font>

*"I ran one measurement in one year and I picked the cheapest one available."*

<font color=mistyrose>"I *knew.*"</font> Amy turned round. <font color=mistyrose>"I knew you'd got it wrong on purpose, you said 'a pinch of salt' and you've never said 'a pinch' about anything in your life, you'd have said *point four grams,* I clocked it in the shop and I let it go because you looked like you needed it—"</font>

*"Yes,"* said Mornye.

<font color=mistyrose>"—and you've been walking around for a year thinking you got away with it."</font>

*"I have."*

<font color=mistyrose>"How's that feel."</font>

Mornye considered the question the way she considered load-bearing questions, which is to say completely, and for slightly too long.

*"Familiar,"* she said.

Fourteen minutes had been fourteen minutes. Somewhere down the corridor, at a tap, a woman who had walked past four of them was filling a cup very slowly.

<font color=mistyrose>"Professor."</font>

*"Mm."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Thanks."</font>

Mornye reached out and squared the divider in the third drawer, which had been square.

*"Noted,"* she said.

---

Mei came back with a paper cup that had been full for most of fourteen minutes and was three-quarters full now, because she had been standing in a corridor holding it at an angle.

She looked at the two of them, and at the closed drawer, and then did the thing she did, which was to arrive in the middle of a room and occupy whatever space it had going spare.

<font color=moccasin>"Right,"</font> she said. <font color=moccasin>"Marshmallows."</font>

*"They are an item on a manifest."*

<font color=moccasin>"They're an item on a manifest that's *open,*"</font> Mei said, and had one, and sat down on the arm of the listing chair with her hip against her daughter's shoulder, and Amy leaned into it while giving every appearance of holding still.

Buddy came off the Starstack and settled on the box.

Mornye watched all of it for a while, the way she watched a run of clean data.

Then she took out the stylus and wrote something into the corner of a real page of real work, and this time her arm had nothing to do with it, and she read it over twice afterward, and left it where it was, and offered it to nobody.

## IV.

The President's office kept low lamps and high paperwork, and the funding mirror lived face-down on the blotter, which from Lucilla was the sound of a battlefield being swept.

On the desk beside it stood a badly carved heron with a hairpin, and, more recently, a penguin listing hard to port with one wing on and a second, smaller penguin standing behind it in the relationship of a tugboat.

Iuno looked at the arrangement for slightly longer than politeness required.

*"She insists it's nautical,"* Lucilla said.

<font color=royalblue>"It is nautical."</font>

*"It's foundering."*

<font color=royalblue>"That's a naval condition."</font>

*"Sit down, Priestess."*

Iuno sat.

On the corner of the desk, squared to the edge, lay a buff form she recognised at forty paces, because she had filled it in herself at four in the morning with a pen that had blotted.

*"Field nine,"* said Lucilla.

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

*"Name: Lilith. Relation: correspondent. Address: Penrose-512, last recorded heading, the Etheric Sea."* She turned the form round with one finger. *"Your registry accepted it."*

<font color=royalblue>"My registry is me."</font>

*"I'm aware. I countersigned it on Monday."*

Iuno's hand stopped on the arm of the chair.

*"There's a clerk in Records who has appealed it twice,"* Lucilla went on, *"on grounds that are technically flawless, and he will appeal it a third time in about a fortnight with a citation he is currently very pleased about. I've countersigned it three times. The file now has four signatures on one line and reads, to any auditor, like an institution that has thought about it."*

<font color=royalblue>"I didn't ask you to do that."</font>

*"You would have taken four months to."* Lucilla opened a drawer, took out a tin, opened the tin, and put a sour candy in her mouth with the unhurried finality of a woman closing a topic. *"I dislike an open line in a file belonging to somebody who is leaving. Now. The other business."*

<font color=royalblue>"You won."</font>

*"I did."*

<font color=royalblue>"Say the sentence."</font>

Lucilla looked at her for a moment with an expression Iuno was coming to recognise and had stopped enjoying, which was the expression of a woman being handed exactly the question she had left the door open for.

*"'Therefore the Executive Committee of the Spacetrek Collective has determined that we will, once again, challenge the sky.'"* She said it the way you read out a weather bulletin. *"Fourteen words. Eleven weeks. Two of my faculty have had it framed."*

<font color=royalblue>"And the rest of it? The half they put in front of that."</font>

*"Extraordinarily costly. High-risk. Fraught with inestimable difficulties. Incalculable losses for the Collective, or for humanity itself."* Lucilla moved the candy tin two inches. *"They are correct on every count and I said so in the room, which is why I won."*

<font color=royalblue>"How many."</font>

*"Forty-one applications. Nine will withdraw before the aptitude screening. Eleven will fail it and be relieved. Of the remainder, on the historical curve, four reach the chair and one holds past ninety."*

<font color=royalblue>"You've costed it out to one child."</font>

*"I've costed it out to one child since February,"* said Lucilla. *"I do it most evenings, in this chair, and it comes out at one every time, and on two occasions I have done it a third time hoping for a different figure."*

The lamp hummed.

<font color=royalblue>"Then say the rest of that too."</font>

*"You want me to say that I stood in front of eleven investors and used a girl who spent a decade in the dark as an exhibit."* Lucilla folded her hands. *"I did. I used her twice. Once for the funding and once for the amnesty, and the second use is the reason your household has legal enrolments and your daughter has a birth record, and I would like that noted in the same breath, because I have found people will hold either half of it against me and I'd rather be held to the whole."*

<font color=royalblue>"That's a well-built sentence."</font>

*"I've had eleven weeks to build it."*

<font color=royalblue>"It's still a well-built sentence."</font>

*"Yes,"* said Lucilla, and ate the candy.

---

*She likes you,* the High Priestess observed. *This is what it looks like. It's very disconcerting; I've been watching for an hour.*

*Be quiet.*

*She has been steering you toward something for eleven minutes and you have let her, twice, on purpose, because you want to see the shape of it. Darling, I know that face. It's mine.*

---

*"I want a rule,"* Lucilla said.

<font color=royalblue>"From me."</font>

*"From you."*

<font color=royalblue>"Name it."</font>

*"An abort protocol for the Synchronist chair. Who may stop a sync, on what evidence, at what threshold, and against what objection — including the objection of the candidate."* She turned the mirror over and back, once, which for Lucilla was a fidget. *"The current instrument is one line long, dates from before the sealing, and reads: *supervising faculty shall exercise judgment.* On the ninth of the month, a woman with a doctorate and thirteen years of standing exercised her judgment for four and a half seconds and then did what a nineteen-year-old asked her to, and I have read every word of that transcript, and I cannot find the place where the instrument failed, because there is no instrument. There is a verb."*

<font color=royalblue>"No."</font>

*"That was quick."*

<font color=royalblue>"It was thirty years quick."</font> Iuno set her palm flat on the arm of the chair. <font color=royalblue>"I wrote instruments for a city-state for three decades. Every one of them was correct, load-bearing, defensible, and taught to novices. And in a basin, at twenty-two, with clear sky in every direction, I watched a wind lean east on the water for one hour, and I opened the instrument, and the instrument had a column, and the column had a word in it, and the word was *weather.* And I wrote it. Correctly. Per the instrument."</font>

Lucilla waited.

<font color=royalblue>"She spent thirty years crossing to a shore I had already used. One line, in a ledger, in a young priestess's hand, and it is why a Threnodian is walking on the youngest gate of Huanglong and my household is leaving on Thursday."</font>

The mirror lay face-down. Lucilla left it there.

<font color=royalblue>"You are asking me to write a category and hang it over the heads of forty-one children. In twenty years somebody will die inside the category and it will be my handwriting."</font>

*"Yes,"* said Lucilla.

<font color=royalblue>"You agreed with that far too fast."</font>

*"Because it's true and I have no counter for it and I'd rather spend the time on the part where I ask you anyway."* She leaned forward. *"Somebody is going to write that rule this year. The Collective has minuted it. If you decline, the pen goes to a very careful man in the compliance office who has never signed a form that ended in a funeral, and his rule will be excellent, and it will be built out of thresholds, and the first time a threshold and a person disagree, the threshold will win, and he will be commended."*

<font color=royalblue>"That is a genuinely disgusting argument."</font>

*"It's the strongest one I have."*

<font color=royalblue>"It's a good one, and it's disgusting, and those aren't in tension."</font> Iuno turned her cup a quarter-turn. <font color=royalblue>"...Conditions."</font>

*"Go on."*

<font color=royalblue>"Every limit in it is stated as somebody's tolerance and carries that person's name. Not 'the threshold is ninety-two.' *Mornye's threshold is ninety-two, and Mornye has signed this page, and Mornye is who you argue with.* Named, in the text, in the copy the candidate reads before they sit down."</font>

The office went very quiet.

*"The Collective will refuse it."*

<font color=royalblue>"The Collective will refuse it because an unnamed limit is a limit nobody can be sued over, and a named one is a limit somebody has to keep. That is the entire proposal and it is the only part worth having."</font>

*"They'll hate it."*

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

*"Faculty will hate it more."*

<font color=royalblue>"Considerably more. You will spend a year of your life on it and you will lose two good people over it."</font>

Lucilla sat back.

Then she reached into a drawer and took out a single sheet of paper and laid it in front of Iuno, and it was headed **DRAFT PROVISION — ABORT AUTHORITY (RABELLE)**, and the first clause read: *Every limit stated in this instrument shall be attributed by name to the individual whose tolerance it describes, and shall be signed by that individual.*

It was dated four days earlier.

Iuno looked at it for a long moment.

<font color=royalblue>"You had it."</font>

*"I had a draft."*

<font color=royalblue>"You had *that* draft. You had my sentence, four days ago, in your drawer, and you sat there and let me arrive at it out loud like a woman discovering fire."</font>

*"I had a clause,"* said Lucilla, *"which I wrote at two in the morning after reading a five-day protocol built at a kitchen table by nine people, in which eight rules were owned by name and six of the eight were the owner's own limit, and which I obtained through channels I decline to characterise. I had the clause. I lacked an author with standing who could survive the committee. Those are separate problems and I only solved one of them by writing."*

<font color=royalblue>"You steered me."</font>

*"I did."*

<font color=royalblue>"Say the reason."</font>

*"Because a rule you were argued into is a rule you'll defend for a season, and a rule you arrived at is a rule you'll defend from a war,"* said Lucilla. *"And because I need you to defend it from a war. Possibly literally."*

*Oh, I like her,* said the High Priestess, with feeling. *I like her enormously. Darling, marry the other one and hire this one.*

<font color=royalblue>"I'll want it in writing that you steered me."</font>

*"You'll want *what?*"*

<font color=royalblue>"One line. Your hand. Filed with the draft."</font> Iuno picked the sheet up. <font color=royalblue>"Because in twenty years somebody will die inside this and there will be an inquiry, and there will be a file, and I would like the file to say what actually happened in this room instead of the tidy thing. I have spent my whole life on the other side of that arrangement and I'm told the view is better from here."</font>

Lucilla looked at her.

Then she uncapped a pen and wrote four lines at the bottom of the draft in her own hand, and signed and dated them, and pushed it across, and did not turn it round so Iuno could read it, and Iuno read it upside down, and put it in her coat.

---

*"There's the other thing,"* Lucilla said.

<font color=royalblue>"There always is."</font>

*"You photographed a field-nine box a fortnight ago and you have been carrying the photograph against your ribs, on the left, with a folded sheet of syllables."*

<font color=royalblue>"...How."</font>

*"You gave a copy to my paleography faculty on the Tuesday under a request number, which is the most law-abiding act of espionage I have ever witnessed and I have had it framed in spirit."* Lucilla opened the second drawer. *"I had it read. Twice. By two readers who don't know each other and were paid separately, because one reading of three characters is an opinion."*

She laid two envelopes on the desk. Iuno looked at them the way you look at a door that has opened on its own.

*"First reader. Academy staff, third floor, you may go and shout at her tomorrow morning. Her finding: three characters, Huanglong hand, provincial schooling, and the schooling is roughly thirty years out of date in its stroke order, which she was very excited about. She reads it as a personal name. Family character, then given name in two. She's confident."*

<font color=royalblue>"And the second."</font>

*"The second reader is a public letter-writer at a gate in Jinzhou who has been writing other people's correspondence for forty years and charges by the character. She agrees about the first one. She declines to call the other two a name."*

<font color=royalblue>"On what grounds."</font>

*"Her position is that those two characters are a formula. They're cut on lintels and painted on gates, all over the province, in that exact pairing, and they're on grave-tablets as well, and she has written them into perhaps four thousand letters in her life."* Lucilla turned the second envelope a quarter-turn on the blotter. *"So a family with that surname could put those two characters over a door, or under a name on a stone, or on a daughter. And from three characters on a form, she says, no one alive can tell you which was meant."*

<font color=royalblue>"What do they mean."</font>

*"Together? 'There is someone.'"*

The lamp buzzed once and settled.

*"Over a door it means the house is occupied. Don't pass by. Somebody's in."* Lucilla ate another candy. *"On a stone it means what you'd expect. And as a given name, my reader says, it happens after a bad year — a family that has lost everybody names the next child that, and puts it in the register, and it looks ridiculous on paper and nobody who was there thinks so."*

Iuno sat entirely still.

<font color=royalblue>"And the first character."</font>

*"They agree."*

<font color=royalblue>"Both of them."</font>

*"Both of them, independently, on a surname, and it is a real surname held by real households in a specific prefecture, and I am going to write it down rather than say it, because you're going to have to be the one who says it to her, and I'd like you to do that in your own house with your own mouth and not with mine in the room."*

She wrote on a slip, folded it once, and pushed it across.

Iuno unfolded it, and read it, and her face did something small and complete and went back.

She folded the slip and put it in her coat with the draft, against her ribs, on the left.

<font color=royalblue>"Address for the letter-writer."</font>

*"In the envelope. East gate, a stall with a cloth awning, she works mornings and she is impossible after eleven."* Lucilla capped the pen. *"Take coin. She charges by the character and she will count yours."*

---

At the door, Iuno stopped, which is a thing she had learned to do on purpose, because she had spent thirty years leaving cleanly.

<font color=royalblue>"There was an offer."</font>

Lucilla did not look up from the blotter.

*"There was."*

<font color=royalblue>"In a doorway, with an entire board of directors listening, which I have concluded was deliberate."</font>

*"Everything in that doorway was deliberate."*

<font color=royalblue>"It requires you to be alive in three months and in office and in a jurisdiction that recognises the instrument, and it requires the same of me, and of her, and the honest arithmetic on all five conditions is—"</font>

*"Say it properly."* Lucilla looked up. *"You do it to everyone else."*

<font color=royalblue>"...I would like it to still be on the table when I get back."</font>

*"It's a standing offer."*

<font color=royalblue>"Standing offers lapse."</font>

*"Mine don't,"* said Lucilla. *"I have one out at thirty years and renewed annually and it was answered this spring, and the answer took an afternoon and a pot of tea and I have not been the same since. I am not in the habit of withdrawing things."* She turned the mirror face-up, which meant the meeting was over. *"Bring your household home, Priestess. I'll want a fortnight's notice and a guest list, and I will be insufferable about the seating."*

<font color=royalblue>"Understood."</font>

*"And Iuno."*

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

*"The candy's in the drawer,"* Lucilla said, to the mirror. *"It's always in the drawer. That's the whole of that arrangement and I've been running it for eleven years and precisely two people have ever taken one without being told twice."*

Iuno took one.

It was sour all the way to the back of the jaw, and she carried the taste of it down four flights and out onto an avenue full of amber light, and found, somewhere around the second landing, that she was walking faster than she needed to.

---

## V.

It was a Tuesday, so the dish was full.

Luuk Herssen was at his desk with the departure files squared in front of him, seven of them, and a pen, and the white gloves, and the particular stillness of a man who had been sitting for some time in a room he intended to keep sitting in.

*"Worst first,"* he said, without looking up. *"You."*

<font color=moccasin>"That's a hell of an opener."</font>

*"You've been sixth on my list for four days and you have walked past that door on three of them."* He turned a page. *"Sit down. This takes eleven minutes and then we can have the other conversation, which takes longer and which I have been rehearsing since April."*

Mei sat down.

The examination was quick and thorough and mostly silent. He listened to things, and pressed on things, and wrote in a small clean hand, and at the end of it he ran the panel three times, which she had watched him do before, and which had come back the same way every time since she had first sat in this chair.

Noise. Clean, structureless, cheerful noise, where a person's numbers should be.

*"I'm going to write* unremarkable,*"* he said.

<font color=moccasin>"You always do."</font>

*"I always do."*

<font color=moccasin>"Is it true yet?"</font>

*"It has never once been true and I intend to keep writing it until I retire."* He wrote it. *"There's a box. It has to have a word in it. Every honest word I could put there puts a Collective inspector in this building inside a fortnight, and I have been in rooms with those inspectors, and I know exactly which of my patients they'd ask about first, and her name is on the same page as yours."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Right."</font>

*"So: unremarkable. Nine years running for one of you and one year running for the other."* He capped the pen. *"Departure kit's on the counter. Read the list. You'll ignore items four and seven and I've doubled them accordingly."*

<font color=moccasin>"Which are four and seven?"</font>

*"Sleep and water."*

<font color=moccasin>"I sleep."</font>

*"You sleep at the pace of someone with an appointment,"* Luuk said. *"I've been watching you do it for a year through the medium of your friends' blood pressure."*

---

He put the files to one side, squared them, and looked at her properly for the first time.

*"Ask it,"* he said.

<font color=moccasin>"Ask what."</font>

*"You've been in that chair eleven times in a year and you've got to the door with it in your mouth on nine of them, and you're leaving on Thursday, so ask it."*

Mei sat back.

<font color=moccasin>"What was I planning?"</font>

*"I don't know."*

<font color=moccasin>"Luuk."</font>

*"I'm giving you the true answer first because the true answer is the smallest one and I want you to have it before I bury it in the rest."* He took the gloves off, one and then the other, and set them on the desk, palm-down, and left his hands where she could see them. The left one had a seam running corner to corner, and it took the lamplight and held onto it half a beat longer than skin has any business doing. *"I don't know. She never told me. In four years she told me a great many things at two in the morning and none of them were that, and I have gone over it professionally and I've concluded she was doing it on purpose."*

<font color=moccasin>"So what do you have?"</font>

*"Three things. Do you want them in order of usefulness or in the order that will hurt?"*

<font color=moccasin>"Order they happened."</font>

*"Good answer."* He turned his left hand over. *"One. She kept a list. I saw the cover of it perhaps twenty times and the inside of it never. It was city names. I know that because she asked me things about cities — climate, water, what the local physicians did with pressure sickness — and the questions came in a rhythm, one city at a time, for about a year, and then they stopped and started again from the top."*

<font color=moccasin>"How many cities?"</font>

*"I never counted. If you put a knife in me, more than five."*

<font color=moccasin>"Was Jinzhou on it?"</font>

*"She asked me about a river bowl with a dam above it, in a country that writes downward and works right across the page,"* Luuk said. *"That was twenty-four years ago and she asked it while stealing from a dish."*

---

*"Two,"* he said. *"She asked me a question once and I have thought about it approximately every week since."*

<font color=moccasin>"Go on."</font>

*"We were on the roof of the old infirmary at some indefensible hour and she said: if a door is holding something shut, and you take the door away — where does the thing go?"* He set his hands flat. *"I said: through. She said: through to where. I said that's a question for someone else's department. And she said—"*

He stopped, which he did rarely, and Mei watched a careful man decide to say the whole thing.

*"She said, 'Then I'll need somewhere for it to go.'"*

The room's ventilation cycled.

<font color=moccasin>"That's a horrible sentence."</font>

*"It is a horrible sentence and I have had the better part of three decades to find a benign reading. I've produced four. I believe none of them."* He picked up the pen and put it down. *"She was cheerful when she said it. That's the part I'd have left out if I were being kind."*

<font color=moccasin>"Don't be kind."</font>

*"I'd worked that out."*

---

*"Three,"* said Luuk.

He got up, went to the cabinet behind the desk — the one with the physical lock, because every serious person in that building kept one thing behind a key — and came back with an envelope.

It was old. It had been handled a great deal at the corners and then not at all for a long time.

*"She left this with me eleven days before she went. And an instruction."*

Mei looked at it and did not take it.

<font color=moccasin>"What's the instruction."</font>

*"You'll want to read it, because you'll want to see that it's her hand and not mine."* He opened the envelope and took out two sheets and laid the second one down first. *"That's the instruction. That's in the common script and it's plain and I have kept to it, and I want it said out loud that I have hated keeping to it for a year."*

Mei read it.

The hand was quick and slanted and had the small trailing hook of somebody who had learned to write in a hurry from somebody else in a hurry.

> *Luuk —*
>
> *If I come back and I don't know you: leave it. Let me be new. You'll want to fix it. Don't.*
>
> *If I go again: give me this. Don't ask me what it says. I won't know either.*

Mei read it three times.

<font color=moccasin>"'I won't know either.'"</font>

*"Mm."*

<font color=moccasin>"She knew."</font>

*"She knew she was going to arrive not knowing, and she knew that when she got here her own hand would be shut to her, and she wrote a contingency for both, in advance, on a Tuesday, in my office, while eating my candy."* Luuk's voice stayed exactly level. *"I have been a physician for thirty-one years and I have never had a patient hand me a note about their own future amnesia and then ask what was for lunch."*

<font color=moccasin>"What was for lunch?"</font>

*"Fish."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Was it good?"</font>

*"It was institutional,"* said Luuk. *"She had two."*

---

He turned over the first sheet.

It was a full page, close-written, in the hand that had come out of Mei's fingers on a form at a kitchen table, and out of a wind's typewriter on a red kite, and once, in a single character, onto the corner of a requisition slip pushed across a bench toward a nineteen-year-old with glass legs.

Mei looked at it for a long time.

<font color=moccasin>"You've read it."</font>

*"I opened it in the second year and I have never once pretended otherwise, and I'd have opened it in the first if I'd been braver."* He folded his hands. *"I couldn't read a stroke. That's the whole of my crime and it cost me nothing, which is the humiliating part. I did the wrong thing and the universe declined to charge me for it."*

<font color=moccasin>"Did you try to get it read?"</font>

*"Twice."*

<font color=moccasin>"And?"</font>

*"The first man I asked was a colleague and I lost my nerve at the door and asked him about a rota instead. The second time I got as far as writing to a philology department under a false name."* He almost smiled. *"And then I sat with the reply slip for four days and burned it, because I worked out that if it said something I could act on, I would act on it, and there was a woman in this city walking around new, with a daughter she didn't know she had, sleeping badly, and my instruction said* let me be new."*

<font color=moccasin>"That was eleven months of my life."</font>

*"Yes."*

<font color=moccasin>"You could have—"</font>

*"I could have,"* said Luuk. *"I'm aware of the whole list. I've had it inside my coat for eleven months and I've read it more often than I've read the page."*

Mei put her hand flat on the desk.

<font color=moccasin>"Why now?"</font>

*"Because the condition's met."* He pushed both sheets across. *"You're going again."*

---

She took it. She held it the way you hold something you can neither read nor set down.

<font color=moccasin>"There are two other pieces of this in my house."</font>

*"I'd assumed there was one."*

<font color=moccasin>"There's three now. This, a form with three characters on it, and a professor in the science quarter with thirteen years of cards headed in the same hand and one of them's the same character as the first one on the form."</font>

Luuk was quiet for a moment.

*"Mornye kept those."*

<font color=moccasin>"You knew?"</font>

*"She told me one night in the spring, at four in the morning, on a stairwell, in about nine sentences with all the verbs in the wrong place."* He picked up his gloves and turned them over and did not put them on. *"I told her the lone genius routine works right up until it doesn't. She said 'noted' and went back to work for another year, which is what she says instead of thank you, and which I have learned to bank."*

<font color=moccasin>"She kept a *drawer.*"</font>

*"You'd asked her to make you disappear,"* Luuk said. *"There are two ways to hold that. She chose the one that involved thirteen years of index cards. I've stopped being surprised by her and started just being on her side."*

---

<font color=moccasin>"Luuk."</font>

*"Mm."*

<font color=moccasin>"Why this city."</font>

He put the gloves down again.

*"Twenty-seven years."*

<font color=moccasin>"That wasn't the question."</font>

*"It was the beginning of the answer and I'm going to give you the whole of it, at the wrong speed, and you're going to let me."* He sat back. *"I came here for a man. He wasn't here. He has never been here. He was, as far as I can establish, never within four seas of this city."*

<font color=moccasin>"Then—"</font>

*"He said he'd come. He said it on a specific day, in a specific room, to a man he had made a specific number of promises to and kept most of them."* Luuk's left hand lay quiet on the desk with its seam catching the light. *"So I came, and I got a post, and I got a second post, and then I got a life. And there's a thing that happens after about the eighth year, which is that you stop being able to tell whether you're waiting or whether you simply live here."*

<font color=moccasin>"Do you write to him?"</font>

*"Every month."*

<font color=moccasin>"For twenty-seven years?"</font>

*"Three hundred and twenty-odd letters,"* said Luuk. *"They go into a system. They leave this building; I've verified that four times, twice at some expense. Whether they arrive anywhere is a separate question and I have declined to investigate it, on the grounds that I know what I'd find and I like the ritual."*

<font color=moccasin>"Any answers?"</font>

*"No."*

<font color=moccasin>"Twenty-seven years of that and you keep going."</font>

*"Mm."*

Mei sat with that.

<font color=moccasin>"Everybody in this building writes to somebody who doesn't answer."</font>

*"Yes,"* said Luuk. *"I've noticed. It's the local industry."*

---

<font color=moccasin>"I'll take one."</font>

*"You'll take one what?"*

<font color=moccasin>"A letter. To him. I'm going four seas and I'm going through three ports and I'll be standing at a lot of counters, and if you give me one, I'll carry it, and I'll put it somewhere with a stamp on it and a person behind the desk."</font>

Luuk looked at her for a long moment.

*"No,"* he said.

<font color=moccasin>"Luuk—"</font>

*"You will take it, and you will carry it against your ribs on the left with everything else you're carrying against your ribs on the left, and at some point in a bad month it will become one more thing you owe somebody, and I have seen exactly what you do with a thing you owe somebody."* He said it without heat. *"I've read the file on the gate. Ten minutes became fourteen because a woman with your medical history decided a promise outranked a barrier schedule. So: no. My correspondence stays in my building."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Okay."</font>

*"There's something else you can do and it costs you nothing."*

<font color=moccasin>"Name it."</font>

*"You'll meet physicians. In a garrison city you'll meet a great many, and they'll be good, because a garrison city trains them properly."* He picked up his gloves and, this time, began to put them on. *"When you're standing next to one, at a table, off duty — ask whether they've ever treated a patient who bled gold."*

Mei watched the left glove go on.

<font color=moccasin>"That's a strange question."</font>

*"It's a perfectly ordinary question and you'll ask it lightly, the way you'd ask about a local dish."* He worked the cuff straight. *"And then don't watch the face. Watch the hands."*

<font color=moccasin>"What am I looking for?"</font>

*"You'll know."*

<font color=moccasin>"That's not an answer."</font>

*"It's an answer with the working left out, and if I show you the working you'll see it in the first four people you ask, because human beings are suggestible and you are the most suggestible person I have ever put a stethoscope on."* He flexed the hand once. *"Ask. Watch. If it happens, write to me. Use my name and my post and put it on the ordinary post and don't be clever about it."*

<font color=moccasin>"And if it doesn't happen?"</font>

*"Then it doesn't and I'll have had a year of checking the pigeonhole,"* Luuk said, *"which I would be doing anyway."*

---

She stood up to go with the two sheets folded into her jacket, and got as far as the door, and the dish was on the shelf by the door where it had always been, brimming, because it was Tuesday and Tuesday was the little ones' clinic.

She stopped in front of it the way she had stopped in front of it eleven times.

*"Take one,"* Luuk said.

<font color=moccasin>"I always feel like I'm robbing you."</font>

*"You are. It's a rotten dish and the budget line is 'patient morale' and I have defended it at three separate finance meetings."* He was already writing again. *"Your daughter takes the toffees. Every Tuesday for thirteen years, two toffees and one sour, and she thought I was reading a chart."*

<font color=moccasin>"...I know. She told me. She was very proud of the technique."</font>

*"The technique was appalling. She reached across my sightline every single time."* The pen kept moving. *"You took the sour ones."*

The pen stopped.

Mei stood at the dish with her hand halfway to it.

<font color=moccasin>"What?"</font>

*"You took the sour ones. Every Tuesday for four years. Same dish, same shelf, thirteen years earlier, same absolute conviction that I hadn't noticed."* Luuk put the pen down. *"And I bought extra for you and then I stopped, and then thirteen years later a child came through that door with a fifty-credit haircut and no manners and I started buying extra again, and it took me until the second year to hear what I was doing."*

The ventilation cycled.

<font color=moccasin>"Did you ever tell her?"</font>

*"I told her on departure day, in a corridor, in one sentence, badly, because it was the last chance I was going to get."* He picked the pen back up. *"I'm telling you now for the same reason and I'd like it noted that I've improved."*

Mei looked into the dish.

She reached in without looking, the way you reach into a drawer in your own house, and her hand came out with a sour one, and the wrapper was the colour it had always been.

She turned it over twice.

<font color=moccasin>"Huh,"</font> she said, to her own hand.

*"Mm,"* said Luuk, writing. *"There's more of that coming. Eat properly and take the salt tablets — I'm told there are ninety of them and I'm told they're excellent."*

---

## VI.

The house was quiet at one in the morning in the way it was quiet only in the last week of anything: fully occupied, everybody breathing, nobody asleep.

Iuno had three things on the table when Mei came down.

The photograph of Field 9. The folded slip from Lucilla's blotter. And, laid flat between them, a page of close-written characters that Mei had brought home in her jacket and had not been able to stop touching.

<font color=royalblue>"Sit down."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'll stand."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Then stand where I can see you."</font>

Mei stood at the end of the counter.

Iuno told it in order, the way she told things now — the office, the mirror face-down, the countersignature, the draft with her own sentence in it four days early, and then the two readers, and the surname both of them agreed on, and the two characters neither of them would settle.

<font color=moccasin>"Say the last bit again."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"'There is someone.'"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"And that could be a name."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It could be a name. Given after a bad year, in a family that had lost everyone, entered in a register by somebody who meant it literally. Or it could be two characters off a lintel, which mean the house is occupied and a traveller shouldn't pass by. Or it could be what you cut on a stone."</font> Iuno turned the photograph a quarter-turn. <font color=royalblue>"Two readers. Two readings. Both paid, neither talking to the other, and I have looked at it for six hours and I cannot get it to be one thing."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"So I wrote either a person or a door."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You wrote it in a box that asks for next of kin,"</font> Iuno said, <font color=royalblue>"at the speed of a signature, with the strokes in the right order and a hook on the end like somebody who learned it in a hurry from somebody else in a hurry, and then you turned your hand over and looked at it as though it belonged to a stranger."</font>

Mei picked up the slip with the surname on it and read it and put it down.

<font color=moccasin>"Doesn't do anything."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I keep waiting for it to do something."</font> She turned it over. <font color=moccasin>"Chisa's mother heard my heel on a floor and knew me. I look at this and it's a shape."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Give it a country and a stall with an awning and a woman who charges by the character."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Optimist."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Registrar,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"Optimism is what registrars have instead of a religion."</font>

---

Mei pulled the blank pad across.

<font color=moccasin>"Do the lake one again."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You'll get one stroke."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Then I'll get one stroke."</font>

She picked up the pen and put the nib down and drew a horizontal line, clean, left to right.

Her hand sat there.

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

She set the pen down beside the mark. Then she picked it up again, and, without any ceremony at all, wrote the thing from the form.

It came out fast and whole and beautiful, exactly as it had on the buff paper, and her hand kept going.

Four characters.

Iuno was around the counter before the last stroke landed.

<font color=royalblue>"Again."</font>

Mei wrote it again. Four.

<font color=royalblue>"Again."</font>

Four. Four. The same four, at a signature's speed, six times, and on the seventh Mei's hand stopped after three and sat there like a limb gone to sleep, and then produced the fourth one anyway, slower, with a wobble in it that the other six had lacked.

They looked at the page.

<font color=moccasin>"There's one more than there was."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Is that good?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It is *information,*"</font> said Iuno, in a voice that was doing a great deal of work, and she photographed the page four times from three angles and then stood with both hands on the counter and her head down for a while.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I'm having a moment. Give me nine seconds."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Take eleven."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"...Thank you."</font>

---

Amy came down at two for water.

She stopped in the doorway the way she had stopped in that doorway before, with her hand on the frame and the corridor dark behind her, and looked at the two of them and the pages spread over the counter and the pen.

The rule that night had been that nobody spoke about it. That rule had expired eleven days ago.

<font color=mistyrose>"What is it?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Come and look."</font>

So she came and looked, and got her water, and drank it standing at the counter with the glass in both hands, and stayed.

<font color=mistyrose>"There's four."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"As of about an hour ago."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"There were three on the form."</font> Amy set the glass down. <font color=mistyrose>"Mornye's got one. Just the first one. Top corner, thirteen years, hundreds of them."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I know."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Does she know what it says?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"She's had four opportunities to have it read and declined all four."</font>

Amy thought about that.

<font color=mistyrose>"Because then she'd know."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That's what she told me. Word for word, in about that many syllables, with her hands folded."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"...Yeah."</font> Amy turned the glass on the counter. <font color=mistyrose>"Are you going to tell her?"</font>

Iuno looked at the slip with the surname on it, folded on the counter between a photograph and a page nobody in the room could read.

<font color=royalblue>"Not before Thursday,"</font> she said.

<font color=mistyrose>"That's not an answer."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It's a date,"</font> Iuno said, <font color=royalblue>"which is the only species of answer I've been able to produce all week."</font>

Amy nodded slowly, which she did when she was filing something, which she had learned in a room with a steel cabinet in it and had never once suspected.

Then she pulled the bag of marshmallows out of the box on the sideboard, tore it open with her teeth, and put the pan on.

<font color=mistyrose>"Cocoa,"</font> she announced. <font color=mistyrose>"Ma's making it. She's the only one here who does it right and I'd like a second data point before we all leave."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It's two in the morning."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"It's two in the morning on *Wednesday,*"</font> Amy said, and got down three cups, and put them on the counter with the handles all pointing the same way.
