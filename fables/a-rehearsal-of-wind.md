# A Rehearsal of Wind
### being the working papers of the Plum Rain Troupe, toward an opera never staged, kept against instructions
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

大音希聲。
*The greatest music is faint of sound.*
— Laozi

---

> **THE APPRENTICE'S NOTE.** *My master ordered these papers burned. I have weighed his order against the commissioner's, which came later, on better paper. The trunk sits in the property room, under the third-act armor, facing east, because when it faces any other way the pages rattle on still nights, and the night porter has a family.*
>
> *The folio is arranged as he left it. Where he cut, I have kept the cuts.*

## Paper the First — The Commission

*A letter, unsigned, delivered to the stage door of Master Ping's teahouse after the two-hundredth night of* The Maiden of Two Names. *Crisp new coin enclosed, a year's run of it.*

> To Master Qu of the Plum Rain —
>
> You staged the Maiden well. The god dies on schedule and the house cries on cue. Craft.
>
> Now write me the wind. You have a year. Spend what you like.
>
> — a friend of the house
>
> *P.S. The second act is where it will break. Break it beautifully.*

*Beneath, in the master's hand:* Every subject yields to structure. A city yielded. A saint yielded. A god yields nightly, at the interval, to music. Wind is air with a habit. I have broken habits for a living for thirty years.

*And beneath that, in the same hand, much later, the ink almost dry in the brush:* I was thirty years wrong about what I do for a living.

## Paper the Second — Testimony of the Attendant

*Taken at Master Ping's, between houses. The attendant is sixteen and has worked the door since she was nine. She kept wiping the same table throughout.*

— You saw her.

"Nine nights."

— Describe her.

"Back left, by the door hinge. Standing. There's a stool there — we keep it for the fire warden. It stayed empty." A pause in the wiping. "Eight nights it stayed empty."

— And the ninth?

"Warm."

*The master's marginal stroke here is heavy enough to have torn the page.*

— What did she watch?

"Act two. The tea act, we call it backstage. The cold-cup scene, the window scene. She'd lean in for those. Both shows, same lean. Like checking a stitch."

— And the war? The god?

"She left at the interval. Every night, out the door before the drums." The rag stopped. "Ninth night she stayed. We'd changed nothing. The priestess got her arrow, the house got its cry, and when the lanterns came up the door stood open and the stool was warm and there was a coin in the box that shouldn't be there."

— Shouldn't be?

"Jinzhou half-copper. Worn smooth, like it had crossed water in somebody's shoe. Master Ping nailed it over the door. Says it's for luck." The rag moved again. "He dusts it himself."

— Did she ever make a sound?

"Once." The attendant looked at the stage, empty at that hour, the boards still holding the afternoon's chalk marks. "Ninth night. The priestess says *I'm selfish* — third time it comes round, the house always sighs, it's the sigh line — and from the back there's this… breath. A laugh the size of a moth. Master Ping thought a bat had got in." She folded the rag into a square, corners true. "It wasn't the sigh line to her. It was the funny one."

*Margin, the master's hand:* She laughed where the widows sigh. File under: knows something the house does not. File under: act two.

## Paper the Third — From the Wind-List of the Almanac Bureau, Eastern Copy

*The Bureau keeps the register of named winds, as it keeps the register of named stars: against the day someone asks. The keeper's epigraph stands at the head of every copy.*

> *You will meet many winds. You will meet each of them once.*

**磨刀風 — the Knife-Whetting Wind.** Twelfth month, from the north, dry, with an edge. Grandmothers say it trims what the year still owes. Sharpen indoors.

**送嫁風 — the Bride-Sending Wind.** Fourth month, smells of rapeseed flower. Follows the wedding boats one li downriver and turns back at the first bend. Boatmen tip their hats at the bend.

**數樑風 — the Beam-Counting Wind.** Autumn. Enters by the eaves and goes along the rafters, beam by beam, and out. A house it skips is sold within the year. Disputed by the guild of housewrights; recorded anyway.

**客風 — the Guest Wind.** Unfiled.

*Marginalia, four hands, four inks, in order:*
*Filed under omens.* ~~struck~~
*Filed under ghosts.* ~~struck~~
*Filed under weather.* ~~struck, twice, hard~~
*Files itself.*

**On winds that linger** *(appended custom, eastern counties)*: speak. A lingering wind is listening. Tell it what the living must be spared. It carries and keeps. It has no one to tell.

*A later hand, small, beneath:* It has one.

## Paper the Fourth — The Pear-Tree Transcript

*The master's travel note:* Went west with the court's leave to the Xuanfang line, where the great dolls stand off the dark. Section seven, the post the garrison calls the parting-tree: a pear grows through the wall there, roots in the fighting side, crown in the home side, and pear and parting share a sound in the old tongue, so the soldiers hang nothing on it and sweep beneath it daily. The unit at that post is third-generation, forty-one years on the wall. The garrison sergeant walked me up at dusk and told me the rule: you may ask it anything; it answers with what it keeps.

*Transcript follows. The unit reproduces sound as it reproduces weather: exactly. The passages below are entered in her voice. The transcriptionist declines further description of the voice.*

The unit banked its core when I named my subject. Petals came down off the pear, out of season. Then it began.

> *"My teacher set a mark I never reached. I reached everything else by aiming at it."*

> *"There was a county called Qixi. The flood took the bridge, and I carried the children over, eleven trips, and when the water went down the village asked me to leave before the funerals. An outsider's luck shames the drowned. I understood it. I dug the graves that night from the far bank, and left before the ground was light enough to read."*

*(Bureau annotation, later: no county of that name stands in the gazetteer. The Yuezhou she names elsewhere stands in the gazetteer. The Bureau has stopped checking.)*

> *"At the siege of ———, a general broke the line a day after I broke it. They made an opera of him within the year. It was very good. I saw it twice. I sat where I could watch the audience."*

The sergeant, at my shoulder, quietly: "That one's an old friend. Wait. There's one it saves."

The pear moved. The wall lamps leaned and steadied. The unit's tone shifted — later, is how I would set it, a night-time register, a voice with its armor off —

> *"There is a woman I was made to find. In another —"*

*(a gap here, held to the length of a breath; the unit reproduces her pauses)*

> *"— she is loved the way harvests are loved. Some nights I hate her. Then the wind changes."*

The sergeant looked at the pear tree. "It plays that one at the third watch, some nights. To itself. First time it did, the whole post stood to, thinking we were bewitched." He straightened a lamp that stood straight. "Now the watch just… lets it finish."

I asked him what the wind was, to the post. He took me around the unit's shield-side and showed me a vent, a hand's width, high on the plate, kept open. Standing order says shields seal at dusk. The court's inspection entered it in the register as: *modification — vent, one; combat function — void; filed under — faith.* "She sheltered there through the bad winter," the sergeant said. "Talked all night, some nights. Old Seven holds the door for her since. You'd have to scrap it to close it."

Before I left, I asked the unit the question the whole country asks its walls: why the Drum sits over the youngest gate. Why war, of all countries, keeps house in this one.

The core cycled. The recitation, when it came, was in her voice, and even, and I have been three weeks trying to stage a line reading for it and I decline:

> *"It came for the same reason I did."*

I gathered my papers. At the gate the unit spoke once more, unprompted, to my back. The sergeant says it keeps this one too, and plays it rarely. I set it down entire, because it is the whole opera, and I will spend the rest of this folio failing to stage it:

> *"Every wind is met once. She will meet me once. Let it be a good wind."*

## Paper the Fifth — The Fight-Master

*From the master's notes, undated:*

Hired Fausto out of Septimont for the second act. Twenty years of arena floors; choreographed the Maiden's god-slaying; the man can make a duel out of two chairs and a grudge. He read the treatment twice, turned it face down, and looked at me the way surgeons look at the untrainable.

"Where's the bout?"

"There's a war god."

"And she draws on it when?"

"She endures it."

"For how long?"

"That's the play."

He quit by dinner. Kept the advance. Fair. At the door he turned around, and this is why I keep hiring Septimontians, they say the quiet part at full volume: "In my country we'd have built her a statue by now."

"In hers," I said, "they trimmed her a lamp."

He thought about that the whole way down the stairs. I could hear him thinking. It sounded like a man losing an argument with a staircase.

## Paper the Sixth — Testimony of the Child

*Taken on the shrine street, Jinzhou, with her grandmother's leave. The child is seven and answered every question twice, once for me and once for her doll.*

— You saw the fire.

"The lantern store. In festival season, so there was paper everywhere. Everyone was screaming and passing buckets."

— And the wind came.

"And the fire stood up." She raised both arms, doll and all, to show the standing. "Tall as the shrine gate. Grandmother says fire loves wind the way bad boys love an audience."

— What did the wind do?

"Went away." She put her arms down. "Fast. Like—" She looked at her grandmother, then leaned in, and gave me the line I have written four drafts around and will now stop trying to improve: "Like when Ma burns the rice and says nothing and cooks it again."

— And then?

"Three hours. Then rain." She held the doll up to the clear sky, demonstrating the miracle to it. "Out of nothing. All night. Only on our street — Auntie Two-Doors was furious, her street got smoke and ours got clean roofs." A shrug. "The rain-wind came back after and dried our shoes. All of them. In rows."

*Bureau annotation, appended at my request: single-street rain from a clear sky is entered in the almanac as* 賠禮雨, *the apology rain. Recorded five times in two hundred years. Each instance follows a fire, and a wind that fled it.*

*Margin, the master's hand:* A being whose element feeds the one disaster she cannot stand before. She has an enemy she strengthens by approaching. Stage that, Fausto. Then the labor: three valleys of sky hauled over one street, all night, for shoes. The apology outweighing the fault five to one. That is her arithmetic. That is the whole ledger of her.

## Paper the Seventh — House Notice

*Posted by the door at Master Ping's, in the house's own hand, some weeks after the ninth night. Copied here as found.*

> **THE HINGE CUP** — one copper.
> A cup of clear tea, poured hot and left by the door hinge, for the guest.
> Paid cups are marked in chalk on the frame. Strokes are wiped at dawn.
> The house makes nothing on this. Ask for: wind tea.

*The attendant, asked about it:* "Started with a bargeman who'd heard the coin story. Then his whole crew. Then everyone." She glanced at the door frame, chalked to the lintel. "After a Maiden night, some nights, we run out of frame."

— Does the cup ever empty?

"It cools." The rag, folded, corners true. "Cooling's drinking, for some guests. Master Ping says a house should know what it's pouring for."

*Margin, the master's hand:* A whole city buying tea for an absence. Somewhere across the water, I am told, a nation once did its believing in cathedrals. We do ours by the door hinge, one copper at a time, and wipe the ledger every dawn so it stays a gift.

## Paper the Eighth — The Ninth Drum

*Assembled from the war college wall, the almanac bureau, and the children of the eastern counties, in that order of reliability, reversed.*

Other nations keep war's portraits. War keeps its residence here.

At the youngest gate there are eight watchtowers, and when a sortie comes home the towers beat once each as the column passes beneath, tower by tower, eight beats for a full return. The count is the victory. The garrison hearths sing it plainly: 凱歌先數還鄉人 — *the triumph song counts the homecomers first.* Generals appear in the second verse, weather permitting, after the cooks.

The children of the wall counties, raised on eight drums, ask the question children ask: what beats the ninth?

Their elders give it names instead of answers. **The Patient Guest.** **The Debt-Collector.** **The Ninth Drum** — the beat past the count, owed and unpaid, waiting past the north face with the posture of a creditor who has seen your granary and is in no hurry at all.

The war college keeps two prefaces on the subject, bound into the same treatise, facing each other across the stitching, arguing for three hundred years:

> **The First Preface.** *War eats glory. Where they sat it at the head of the table and sang to it, it grew fat, and moved on when the songs ran out. Here it was seated by the door and served last, and so it stays: a beggar-god camped outside the one larder locked against it. It besieges us for the meal we withhold. Hold the recipe and it starves.*

> **The Second Preface.** *War eats duration. Ask the congee-eater which lasts longer, the banquet or the bowl. A thousand years of the held wall is a meal that never ends; we have been feeding it politely for ten centuries, one watch at a time, and calling the diet virtue. That is why it lives here. It lives above the longest kitchen in the world.*

*Margin, a third hand, older than both prefaces by the ink:* Things wait where the door is.

*Margin, the master's hand, recent:* The parting-tree post keeps a fourth opinion, in her voice, one sentence long, and the college has spent a season arguing the antecedent of "reason." I have stopped arguing. Whatever she came for, it came for. The gate should think hard about what it is keeping that is wanted twice.

## Paper the Ninth — Testimony of the Keeper

*Taken on the crest wall of the dam, Jinzhou, at the trimming of the lamps. Keeper Pei, forty-one years of watch. He worked while he talked and talked while he worked and I have kept the order of both.*

"You trim a lamp forty years, you talk to weather. That's the wall's oldest custom and its cheapest. You tell a lingering wind your dead." He thumbed the wick, took the black off, set the glass. "Keepers, soldiers, fishwives. It's where the wall puts what it can't put down."

— What did you tell it?

"My brother." The next lamp. "North face, the bad spring. I told it the whole man — the debts, the snoring, the time he cried at a puppet show and blamed the dust. Took me three watches." He looked out at the water. "It stayed for all three. You can tell when a wind is staying. The flame leans one way and forgets to wander."

— And the hospital story. The one the wall tells.

He worked a while first. "Breach year. Triage under the third tower. A boy from the terraces, gut-cut, going, asking for his mother's hand. Everyone's hands were full of other men." The glass went back on the lamp with great gentleness. "The wind was at his hair till light. Off the brow, back. Off the brow, back. The way mothers do it." He straightened. "It found the one handhold a wind has, and used it till dawn. He went easy. His mother sends the wall pears every autumn for a wind she's never met."

— You asked it something once.

"Everyone tells it. Nobody had asked it." He took the last lamp's measure with his thumb and let it be. "So one night, fourth watch, flame leaning, I said: and you. Who does the wind tell?"

— And?

"The flame stood up straight. Then it bent the other way — toward the lake. And the grass on the bank lay down in a line, beacon to water, like something walking out that weighed nothing." He shrugged, forty years of shrug. "It was out there till first light. Whatever it said, the lake kept."

He walked me down. At the third watch-post the Magistrate's runner passed us going up, and Pei answered the question before it was asked: "Wind all night, son. Log it." I asked what the log was for. The keeper considered me, the way walls consider strangers.

"Her Excellency dreams things. The runners check her dreams against our wind." He held the rail. "They match."

At the gate he trimmed the lamp that lights the stair, though the oil was fresh and the flame good.

"Habit," he said. "You trim lamps for guests."

## Paper the Tenth — Three Drafts

**The first draft** was titled *The Sword of the East Wind.* One fragment survives the master's brush:

> EAST WIND *(drawing)*: Ten thousand li I whetted this edge on the rim of the world —
> come DOWN, Drum, and be quartered! Come down and count with me!
> *(Chorus of gales. Thunder machine. The Drum descends on the fly-lines, credibly enormous.)*

*Margin:* Read it to the attendant. She listened all the way through, out of politeness, which should have warned me. Then: "She'd leave at the interval." Struck. Burn the thunder machine.

**The second draft** was titled *The Ghost of Ten Thousand Doors.* Willows. Unswept thresholds. A weeping part in every scene, and a moon. It went to the commissioner by courier and came back the same week, rewrapped, one line on the wrapper in that swift small hand:

> *She laughed once. Where is the laugh?*

*Margin:* How does she know that. I have checked. The testimony went into the trunk the day it was taken. I told nobody. I told the trunk.

**The third draft** has no title. It is one page, and I give it whole:

> ACT TWO.
> The stage stands empty. House lamps banked to coals. Doors unbarred.
> For the length of a meal, the audience hears wind.
> Whatever enters, enters.
> When a lamp relights of itself, act three may begin.
> If every lamp stays dark, the play is over: the house refunds the coppers at the door and apologizes for the weather, and the audience goes home having seen the true version, which is the one where you wait.

*Attached, the impresario's letter:*

> Master Qu. I have read your act two. It is a draft of my bankruptcy.
> The Maiden runs because a god dies in it, on stage, to music, at the same hour nightly. You are asking me to sell tickets to weather. My patrons can get weather outside, where it is free and there is food.
> Come back when you have a duel. Or a death. Or, gods forgive us both, a wedding.
> — Ping
> *P.S. The hinge cup stays on the menu.*

## Paper the Eleventh — The Master's Last Entry

Went east. Told Ping it was for the mountain air. The dam before dawn is a held breath of stone, and I stood on the public stair with the fishermen and watched the beacons go out one by one as the light came.

There is a girl who stands at the northeast shoulder at first light. Outrider's badge. A hat with a little bird stitched at the brim. She checks the horizon the way you check a platform — for someone particular, on no schedule she could name if you asked, and she would blush if you asked, and buy you breakfast to change the subject. I know this because I asked, and she did, and the fried dough was excellent.

I went back the next dawn and kept my distance and my trade's oldest skill, which is watching.

The wind came off the water at the grey hour. It crossed, climbed the cliff face, and stopped on her cheek.

Like a hand.

She closed her eyes and stood for it, the way you hold still for a shy animal, and it stayed a moment past its own momentum, and then she said one word to it — too low for the stair, meant for one listener in all the world — and the wind took the word and went up the sky with it like a spark going home to its fire.

That scene is hers. I leave it where I found it.

Here ends my year. The account: thirty years I have made things happen on schedule, and I took a rich girl's coin to do it to the weather. But her second act is already running — nightly, at the youngest gate, admission free — and the audience sleeps through it, and the fault is the house's, never the play's. I set out to stage a wind. The wind is staged. What it lacks is a house that knows it is watching.

Very well. I have spent my life building houses that watch. I will go home and open the doors.
