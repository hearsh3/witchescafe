# THE YOUNGEST GATE

### Books One through Eleven — A Continuous Account

---

## I. THE HOUSE THAT CAME EAST

Seven people shared one stove in Lahai-Roi, and that stove is what later made them a household in the eyes of a foreign clerk. Mei has golden eyes, a Tacet Mark branched across her right hand, and no memory of the twenty-odd years that produced her: twenty-three years ago she left a sealed page with a physician, told him to let her come back a stranger, and did exactly that. She has died more times than anyone has a clean count for, and has come back every time.

Iuno spent thirty years as High Priestess of Septimont, which is a way of saying she stood near documents belonging to other people and read them faster than anyone expected. She built a calculus in which one child's annihilation sounded like civic duty and signed it, and it cost her an arm, eleven years, and a hundred and seventy-three names she can still recite. Her failure mode is protection performed three moves ahead and disclosed afterward.

The rest: Lupa, Co-Champion of Septimont, combustion class, a disaster at comfort and therefore the best at it. Ciaccona Toccata, who plays badly on purpose so strangers will correct her and tell her the truth about their city. Cartethyia, who spent twenty years sealed in the dark naming dolls so the dark could not erase the fact that someone remained inside, and who becomes eight feet of Fleurdelys when a room can take it. Chisa, who cannot see faces including her own, reads rooms by echo and pulse, survived twenty years in a city that reset itself before every rainy season, and keeps a red string at her wrist out of the sun. And Amy, nineteen outside with twenty-nine years in her, ten of them under a city in the Nihility holding a door shut for two hundred thousand people and talking the entire time, because a load-bearing wall may as well be a funny one.

They came east because a wind asked. It had been a woman in a Jinzhou that burned — library, flood, a red patroller's cap out of black water — a city afterward described as having *held*, a word she went and looked up the price of. She unbound herself into wind and crossed the dark between printings, which strips everything except what is made of sound, arriving with one sentence: *the Gorges of Spirits. Let her wake there.* The errand was refused, because the woman was already awake and already loved, so the wind spent twenty years carrying Huanglong's grief and paying out of its own remaining vocabulary — which is why the compositors on Bookbinders' Row have been short of particular sorts for years.

Outrider Yangyang, who files what repeats, spent a year filing it, called it *shijie*, and finally stood in a room and told her Magistrate that the weather is a person; she had also written forty-one letters to a stranger at an open window because she needed somewhere to put things, and the stranger was Mei. The household voted under rules built from its own named weaknesses and passed Amy's clause eight to one: nobody is the last resort, and if the arithmetic wants a person as payment, the arithmetic can have the city. On the next-of-kin forms Mei's hand — which produces the first stroke of any Huanglong character and then stops — wrote five characters fluently: a surname, the lintel formula *there is someone in*, and a river-bend place with no road. Then the characters grew by one, and by another. At the Gorges of Spirits the wind moved the hair off Mei's face, spent one word — *Found* — and stopped where its own name always fails, and Mei's hand finished the character without her knowledge. The Almanac Bureau struck *weather* and entered *family*.

A census taker asked who the head of household was, and five voices said *Iuno* at once, which is worse than election, because it is affection; Amy wrote *niece* in the relation column, in front of a foreign clerk, where it can still exist in forty years. Household four-one-one, ward four, one stove, entered the twenty-first of the fifth month. Underneath the welcome, two things: Iuno had told the Magistrate, before the Magistrate met Mei, that Mei does not stay dead — because a ruler facing a war will eventually run one recoverable body against a hundred and ten thousand people, and Iuno wanted the cost named before the arithmetic became convenient — and Jinhsi declined to promise she would never use it. And somebody inside the Huanglong system had reassigned overnight, in a correct hand under a correct seal, a Mingting berth held all season for this household, steering them to a frontier gate instead of the capital. No forgery occurred, and nobody knew whose hand it was. Then Mei was summoned to City Hall, received by a bodyguard named Sanhua, and told the Magistrate had been called away three days, leaving four objects: a sugar pearl, a mangosteen, a miniature sundial, a green leaf.

## II. FOUR OBJECTS, AND A ROAD NORTH

*(Gate 1)*

Iuno recognized the method with horror, because it was hers: give people something to investigate so they feel autonomous while you control the frame. When Mei asked lightly whether Iuno had scared the Magistrate off, Iuno disclosed what she had told her; Mei asked whether it was the right call, accepted *I believe so*, and let it stand — and within the hour Yangyang politely asked Mei to see a physician, and the chain Iuno had built came back link by link.

At Huaxu Academy, Baizhi found waveforms of impossible stability with near-zero Overclock risk, as though Mei's body housed a mechanism, and under load in a Sonoro Sphere the simulation broke on an enormous moon, a scythe-bearing Discord, and a voice saying *Leave… Mother…* The tokens resolved: the mangosteen was fruit, the leaf carried Waveworn coordinates, the pearl was an expired vaccine from an epidemic the city had barely survived, and the sundial was a puzzle box whose indexing sent Mei to a redacted archive evaluation of the **First Resonator** — amnesiac, Tacet Mark on the right hand, golden glow under Forte, non-convergent waveforms, zero risk of destabilization — who defeated the Crownless at the Battle of Gorges of Spirits, stood beside the Sentinel Jué three days later, and whose victory founded Jinzhou that same year.

Mei brought home a smaller version of it, and Chisa heard the omission immediately; later Iuno declined to accept *it glitched*, got the rest, and annotated nothing. In the dark Mei tested one sentence against the ceiling — *It might not be me* — and Iuno covered the marked hand with her living one and let her have the night. At breakfast Mei described a dream of cracked ground and rock formations moving wrong, Yangyang named the Norfall Barrens, and Iuno reported that Sanhua had registered a northern Access Pass to Mei's Terminal the previous day: Jinhsi had left a legal road north before Mei solved anything.

At the checkpoint they collected Jianxin, who had spent a day asking successive guards the same question because one of them might have different information — an old man had sat three hours on the letter-writer's steps because his grandson Zhiyuan had stopped writing, and going seemed more efficient. At Beacon Seven three displaced civilians, six weeks on the road and three days without food, had stripped the equipment; Jianxin emptied her rations and her purse; they tried to rob her; Chisa caught a wrist and Lupa offered an old man with a gutting knife the courteous chance to reconsider. Mei asked all three to look at what they had chosen, and when the woman apologized, Jianxin held her hands out a second time, because they still had not eaten in three days.

The Midnight Rangers' camp had been built for three months and was entering its seventh: eleven resonance casualties, a resupply three days late, twelve names on the board carrying stars, the notation for people who went in and whose status is unresolved. Then a Resonator went into cascade forty metres from the medical station — Rong, twenty-two, fighting since the previous morning. Jianxin went in because a cascade is a problem of somewhere for the energy to go; Lupa went in because she has been combustion class for years and knows what it feels like when the flame has nowhere except back in; and Mei stood at the tent entrance and did not go in, and noticed that this was new, and stood there anyway. Afterward the household distributed itself without instruction — Iuno to Captain Xiulan, who had sat alone with twelve names for two days without relief; Amy to a quartermaster's ledger; Chisa to a Ranger whose injury had ruined her sense of distance; Cartethyia to write the twelve starred names into a notebook, because she makes dolls so names can stay somewhere.

Jianxin let Zhiyuan say six days of rehearsed argument, put him in the dirt in eight seconds without using her Forte, and told him he was not ready and that this was temporary. He took her to the memorial turret, where Mei was already sitting: forty-three names, of which he knew twenty-two — a cartographer who drew maps of the camp because she liked maps; a man with a daughter he never met who could cook five things badly; two brothers dead a week apart, filed as simultaneous because nobody knew how else to write it; a man of twenty-four who sat at the east wall off rotation because it was worse if nobody was watching the dark, and who died facing north. Mei listened without offering consolation and without locating the moment when he had said enough, asked only whether he would remember them at home, and told him they need somebody to. Yangyang, who had walked past that turret three times without feeling entitled to stop, wrote all twenty-two into her patrol log in the second ink — the private one, for things she intends to keep — and Mei gave her the callsign *Night Window*. Watching a Ranger's hands shake almost invisibly, Iuno worked out what the tokens had measured: whether these people could look at what the puzzle was protecting and decline to look away.

## III. THE SHAPE IN THE PLAIN

*(Gate 2)*

Iuno took temporary camp command under five lines of terms in Xiulan's own hand — two bells, no deployments, Yangyang countersigning, Cartethyia empowered to say when Iuno was becoming frightening — with the seal on the table where everyone could see it. Cartethyia cut twelve figures from spoiled requisitions, lettered each with a name, and stood them along the map, because the unresolved need somewhere to stand while people look for them. Ciaccona and Staff Sergeant Du then resolved the evening probes — measures of four, the third shifting east by one ridge, terminating once the camp answers with weapon fire and casualty transport — and Cartethyia said the conclusion out loud: they are learning how the camp moves. Iuno put that observation in front of everyone with its maker's name attached.

Chisa and an injured Ranger named Lian isolated the east patrol's distress cadence inside the tone in Lian's damaged ear. A flare would give them a bearing and hand the firing point to the north shelf, so Ciaccona proposed three notes on Aero resonance, Fleurdelys lifted the signal frame higher than the relay mast, Chisa adjusted Yangyang's foot half a boot-width, and Amy called the window. Ten went out that morning; fourteen came home. Iuno returned the seal when her term expired, without asking for an extension, and told Mei over the stew that twenty-five of her twenty-seven water buckets had arrived, because she counts everything she loves and occasionally the figures include buckets.

Captain Bao's map showed three evenings of Discord movement tracing old Tacet scars into a perimeter with the camp on its short side, and when Iuno rotated it a quarter-turn the shape settled. From the south shelf at sunset the plains opened toward Jinzhou, Tacet scars crossing them violet-black at the centre and silver along their raised edges, branching with the intimate geometry of a Resonator's mark, with life occupying every space the strokes allowed. Mei's right hand began to glow, and across the plains the western scar held the same turn — a branch completed that morning, when Beacon Nine went dark — running south past Shuanghe and the old scholar tree toward the highest terrace. **City Hall sat on the line.**

Mei said she wanted the archive's answer, and dinner, and night watch, and Rong's opinion of the stew, and that she could hold all four for one evening; and when she asked who holds the Outrider's post and heard Yangyang's list of the people who hold hers, she told her to leave a bowl there. A violet pulse travelled south beneath the road, Mei's mark answered once, and Yangyang asked which way the doors in her dream had swung. *South. The city stood beyond them.*

## IV. QICHI, AND THE BLACK LAMB

*(Gate 3)*

Jinhsi's cassia leaf had taken up a pulse overnight — human and Discord frequencies overlapping every forty-seven seconds — and resolved to Qichi Village: eighty-six households, no supply notice in three months, two patrols reporting a closed gate. A league out, Yangyang's Forte opened around the wrong current and the whole village arrived at once; Chisa gave her a narrower door and a count, Iuno put her six paces back from the forward edge, and Yangyang weighed it and accepted. They cleared the field as one instrument, with Mei taking its pulse through her marked palm and driving it into the sky — and one Discord remained afterward, pulsing *Brother… help…* at zero aggression, with one clear sustained call beneath its many dying voices. They let it walk away.

The village told the rest: ancestral plaques hung face to the wall with their names cut away and one character left at the bottom, *daughter*; a dragged body's trail; a broken bangle with a black lamb scratched inside it with a kitchen knife; SHE CALLED THE HUNGER and ONE LIFE FEEDS EVERY HOUSE along the route, with SHE LOVED YOU written later and alone at the well and hacked at until the plaster gave way. And the headman's ledger held the arithmetic — harvest tripled in the first year of the rite, medicine after it, every benefit matched to a household transfer in red ink, each row carrying age, health, family burden and projected contribution. A clerk had built a selection model, and Lupa told Iuno to close the book for a minute, and she did.

Under it lay cards showing a black lamb with a broken-spiral horn: an Overseer called **Scar**, who builds encounters around personal doubt and prefers an audience arriving with fresh grief. His pitch was assembled from true material — Jinzhou found an amnesiac with the power to drink gods and gave her an escort and a trail of violet leaves, while the top terrace keeps its hands clean — and he offered Mei kinship as black wool in a world that adores white innocence. Yangyang conceded that Jinhsi's authority turns consent into gravity and said the household accounts for that weight; Iuno noted that eight people had accepted the route under a known return plan; and Mei gave her ethic whole — people use her strength and she uses theirs, they name the price aloud, and they keep their own pens.

He told the fable walking: a poor flock, a Shepherd with fruit in one hand and a ledger in the other, and prosperity for the price of one lamb beneath the tree — first an old ewe already dying, then a thief, then a fever case, and then criteria, because good lambs love criteria and numbers keep wool clean. The prosperity was real, which gave the bargain teeth; and when the valley raised one black lamb who counted differently, because every feast showed her an empty chair, guilt proved expensive, so the flock called her cursed and dragged her to the tree with her own family holding the rope, and by morning had its grandest wish — freedom from consequence — and received corpses and a Tacet Field. Iuno told him she had once made a child's annihilation sound like civic duty, which gives her a precise ear for a shepherd hiding behind arithmetic; Mei said she has volunteered for the altar herself, and that the offer feels clean from inside your own ribs while everybody else learns to stand back and call the knife sacred.

Then Chisa said the thing he could not manage: he knew the person dragged along that path, because his foot turns toward the reservoir when the plea repeats and his breathing locks at the word family — and Yangyang added that the creature calls for her brother. For one heartbeat the smile left his eyes; then he told them to search the reservoir and fell backward into a red aperture as Iuno's arrow cut a strap from his shoulder.

Beneath the drained reservoir a dry shaft opened on a cavern where a tree with seven silver trunks bore Qichi's wishes as literal fruit — combs, coins, medicine bottles, roof tiles, toys, wedding pins — with human bones smooth as river stones between its roots, more than the ledger recorded, because the first pages had been removed. Amy named the mechanism: coherent human frequency converted into material probability. The weeping Discord drew out a lacquered box, and Mei sat down facing it with her sword on the grass and her hands open and told it that eight people had heard it this time.

The diary began large and careful: her brother brought sugar plums, said Elder Rui had chosen her because the tree listens when she sings, and called her his brave **A-Ling**. Then the storehouse voices — the coughing sickness had ended the previous year, and the argument had moved on to a field for one man and the city school for another woman's son, with Elder Rui saying seven offerings had bought twelve years and A-Ling's voice would buy twelve more — and, written twice hard enough to cut the paper, *I offered myself to save them.* The fourth page had her brother offering to walk her through the village because she trusts him, and then asking when his own wish would be granted, above a black lamb under a red slash matching Scar's card down to the broken curl of the horn: the same hand, Chisa said, years apart, the wrist stronger and the movement unchanged. The last lines: *I hear them fighting above… Brother always comes when I use the scared voice.* Then, through a wet stain, *Brother, help*, and under it, *I am still A-Ling.*

Iuno laid the page beside the ledger entry recording her as healthy, single, low future yield, and said consent given to a manufactured emergency belongs to the manufacturer. Mei said Scar had wanted witnesses and had left before her words reached the room, and that some part of him keeps bringing people to that tree while the rest builds exits — and, asked whether they would chase him, said they would carry her out first. The creature's borrowed voices then separated into the leaves until only the young woman remained; it looked at the empty stair, and then at the eight people around the box; and its arms loosened, and pearl light rose into the tree, and the black frequencies softened into pale moths that settled across A-Ling's written name. At the east gate Yangyang counted twenty-six through, and Mei said that every one of them crosses.

## V. THE SANCTUM

*(Gate 4)*

Chixia found Qichi's case closed long ago, offenders punished, villagers relocated — and no record whatsoever of a girl called A-Ling. Iuno reversed the household's reading: the Fractsidus had been preparing to lure Mei there, and Jinhsi had learned of it and sent them first, which made the leaf clue and warning at once. The sundial's last message was a map of the **Grand Library**, tied directly to the Sentinels and normally open to Magistrates alone; and Chisa asked whether City Hall is Jinzhou's centre, and declined to say why.

Mei said she was taking Amy, and Iuno named the cost aloud — she had just promised to be beside her and was now being asked to manage six people, a wolf, a wind and a city's politics — and agreed, with one term: if the floor drops, Mei comes out and finds her. The reason was specific: Amy had already looked at the worst version of her and called her Ma anyway. On the fourth level, behind a panel that looked like a wall with ambitions, a question was carved into a backing board — *Who held the youngest gate?* — with three blanks below it, and smaller text naming the requirement: a hand, a sound, and a return. Mei pressed her marked palm to the first blank, and the carving warmed, and recognized something, and asked no permission.

Scar followed them in, which Sanhua and Jinhsi had been waiting for, and then took the room away, opening an Elysium inside a sanctum whose wards are four hundred years old, keyed to the lake, and had never failed. Mei and Amy beat him from inside his own domain while the others broke it from outside; restrained, he went on working, insisting apparent truths may be lies and offering himself as the man who will reveal everything if Mei chooses him.

He claimed the Sentinel Jué was sealed by the Fractsidus, because an oracle that can correct the future is intolerable to people whose doctrine holds the future must be written by hand — and then congratulated the household on having taken a similar instrument off the board themselves, meaning Iuno. One further thing he said to Jinhsi privately, which did something under her restraint that everyone saw and nobody could read. He threw Mei another black-lamb card and told her that once she understands what she is, she will come to the Fractsidus of her own accord; and Jinhsi declined to repeat the whisper until she could verify it, because he builds distrust out of true materials.

## VI. HAND TALK

*(Gate 5 — the same afternoon, in two rooms four kilometres apart)*

**The sanctum.** Amy demanded everything Jinhsi knew before they walked in, and got seven items: apertures doubling in the prefecture between the third month and the fifth; an Overseer asking after Qichi through three intermediaries since winter; Qichi's file closed forty years ago by a dead man's hand; a cassia leaf that would put the household in the highlands inside four days with a Ranger camp between them and the village, so whatever they found would be found in company; an Overseer who has wanted witnesses for eleven years and leaves every site legible; and a fourth-level approach absent from every filed plan, surviving on a scriptorium copy she had **stopped correcting**. Seven was what she failed to know: the wards had never failed, and the survey in her own file is wrong. Asked what the plan had been if they had not come out, she said she would have written to their household, and agreed that a letter is a letter — and Amy thanked her anyway, because she could have stopped at six.

Then the dangerous thing. The First Resonator file has a **sealed annexe** of eleven redactions; three Magistrates have had the key and two left it in the drawer; Jinhsi has had it four years and never opened it, because the moment she knows she must write it down — and a rumour she may decline, while a fact she must forward, producing a Censorate memorandum, a Board of War request regarding the disposition of an asset, and by the ninth month a Grand Marshal with a policy about Mei. So she built a route outside her own office, with no line anywhere saying the Magistrate told her. Mei declined the key, saying she would open it somewhere with worse chairs, because opening it in that room turns it into evidence before she has had one afternoon to be sad about it alone. While Jinhsi spoke she turned her cup a quarter-turn by the rim and settled it; a moment later Mei, with no part of her mind attending, did the same, and both of them saw it, and Amy saw both of them see it, and let it pass.

Amy had also watched Jinhsi's left hand stay closed for eleven minutes, and said so — declining to ask what Scar had whispered, and stating only that she had clocked it, so Jinhsi could keep it on purpose instead of by acting. The hand opened on nothing, and **four nail-marks**. Then Amy gave the warning nobody had given her: the wishing keeps going after you start answering and gets worse, because now it works, and one day you run dry and they are all still standing there wishing, and by then the wish has a face and the face is yours. Her own escape was a radio and a sentence — she was a girl with a machine, and the machine had a maintenance schedule — which Jinhsi cannot say, because Jinhsi is the machine. So Mei sat on the floor beside the chair and offered a house rule: when it gets loud, say so out loud, and there is no second part. Jinhsi asked, flat, to whom.

Then Chisa's question got its answer. City Hall is the **geometric** centre: a stone under the floor took the survey pin, every wall and gate and tower was laid out from it, the compound came first and the wall came *to* it, and the Tacet scars point at it. Mei mentioned the middle of a weiqi board, and Jinhsi named it — 天元, worthless for two hundred moves, and then every fight on the board runs into it, and it is worth the game. And she corrected the metaphor: **four hands, and she can account for two.** The Fractsidus, who hold the sentence was already written by somebody who never asked and intend to take the pen, and portions of whose material are *correct*, which is the single most inconvenient fact in her prefecture. Her own office. **Ovathrax**, which does not play at all, being the weather over the board, and which in one printing has already won here, where the word used was *held* — with the tremors past the north face at six days, having been eight in the spring. And a stone off a different board: a wind on no roster, which arrived with a purse of about forty words and is spending them at a rate that will finish it, which is why she stopped trying to read it and doubled the kitchen budget instead.

Then the two she cannot account for. The lake dreams backward and has for four hundred years, and the court has twice ruled memory admissible and prophecy inadmissible — and on the night after the kite it dreamed **forward** once, showing her a sound and declining to show her the road. The sound was the **ninth tower**, silent since the dark because the column it is kept for never came back down that road; and on the thirtieth of the fifth month eight beats went along that wall at full morning for a column of one, on her order, given before she knew Mei's name. Since the morning at the shore the lake has shown her nothing: **nineteen nights.** So if the Sentinel was sealed in the spring, her office has run part of a year on prophecy from an unknown source; and if it is unsealed, it went quiet of its own accord on the morning a foreigner lay down at a waterline and let a dead woman finish a twenty-year errand. One may be a god; one may be a forgery; and she must run a city, a war and a sluice at the same speed either way.

Asked whether she is a player or is being played, she said there is no difference at this point. Mei said there is a third thing on a board: she has been the placed stone set on a shore to die on a schedule, and the hand with a plan eleven moves deep, and both left her exactly where she was — and what moved her was somebody who sat down next to her and refused to leave. Jinhsi said that is unavailable to her; Mei said it was sitting on her floor, and invited her to supper.

**The field.** Lupa smelled four hundred hectares of sorghum being cut green, and Iuno had already found why: advance harvest-loss remission for eleven villages, on one day, under one chop, at a fifth above value, which is what a state does when it already knows. In the field, **Changli**, Counselor to the Magistrate, with feather-shaped burn scars bare up her left forearm, was holding an umbrella over an old farmer who could not sign a paper saying the crop his family had grown for fifty-one years was lost — and found him a lawful alternative, so the prefecture bought it as **named seed-stock** on a receipt naming the strain and the grower, because it enters the record as a supply and somebody eats it next autumn and the ledger says whose it was.

Iuno put five facts down at once — the reassigned berth, the timing of the Magistrate's absence, the week the objects were given in, the uncorrected survey, and fifth, that she would have done all four in that order with better paperwork and would have been wrong every time — and Changli said yes to all five, because denial is a move that works once and she would rather spend it in the winter. Her reasons: tremors moving from nine days to eight, which is where the Bureau's model becomes a calendar; a red kite going out of Mengzhou against the prevailing wind, with the character-count of what the wind spent buying it public gossip on Bookbinders' Row; and a Marshal holding a Mingting berth and speaking fondly of it in front of people who write things down. In Mingting this household would have become whatever it became in a room with four ministries and a protocol officer assigning seats, so she moved them where they could be let alone — which she declined to pretend was a different act from moving them where she could see them.

The Magistrate's genuine absence she moved **eleven days forward**, because an Overseer who wants witnesses will enter a sanctum in the week the Magistrate is provably out of the prefecture and a foreigner with an interesting hand is visiting the fourth level. She has never in her life chosen another person's move; she chooses the hour at which they are asked. Iuno sat inside the size of that, because thirty years of arranging what people knew leaves fingerprints, and the fingerprints cost her an arm — and this woman moves calendars, and there is no audit for a calendar.

Then Changli asked what Jinhsi had declined to ask: the **interval**, because four hours is a doctrine, eleven days is a reserve, and a season is a rumour, and rumours stay out of the order of battle. Iuno refused, and Changli thanked her for refusing quickly and turned away to ask a clerk about the second column, which was worse than everything else she had done. So Lupa gave the numbers flat — blade quickest; fire slow, and Mei comes back wrong for a day; drowning worst, and Lupa sat with her nine hours on a beach once; fastest under an hour, slowest three days and a bit, and that was a god doing it on purpose — **nine deaths witnessed, and she has come up every time.** Holding it back, she told a horrified Iuno, was a good instinct and bad maths, because Changli was getting it regardless and would then have a number and no terms.

So Lupa charged for it: when this prefecture spends a member of that household, somebody says it to her face, in a room, out loud, before, while she still has a mouth to answer with. Changli said the clause exists in Jinhsi's own hand from four years ago — if she drowns those streets she will do it looking at them — and gave it formally, binding on her office and her person, and Clerk Wen entered it out of sequence in the prefectural day-book with her name and then Lupa's.

Walking them out, Changli produced her teacher's game record: one game recorded move by move for thirty-one years, left unfinished, the truth said to be in it, never once giving a name. Iuno, who does not play, read it as a document with columns and stopped four pages from the back at **Black playing twice** — moves two hundred and six and two hundred and seven, no White between them, the numbering unbroken — and said the four explanations Changli had been given over thirty years were all wrong, because the two moves sit nine lines from anything Black has touched in eighty moves: he was recording **two games on one board** under one numbering, because he only ever had the one book. At that page lay a card of Fractsidus stock, **blank on both sides**, arrived by post in the fourth month with a receipt and sitting there four months while she failed to decide whether it was an invitation or a joke — and Iuno named it a **pass**, because a player with nothing worth playing hands over a stone and says nothing, and it means *I am still here, and it is your turn.* Changli agreed that this was worse.

Then the disclosures. Jinhsi knows a Fractsidus channel exists inside her registry and does not know the name; Changli does — forty-four, a daughter at the Academy, recruited through a debt manufactured for the purpose, convinced he is protecting her, and passing Changli four true things for every one he passes them. And the dreams: the lake's answers began arriving backward **eleven** years ago, and Jinhsi has been told four, because she was fifteen with a Council hunting for a reason to un-choose her, and a Magistrate whose god has been talking nonsense since before her appointment can be retired on health grounds. So Changli dated the anomaly to the fourth year, in her own hand, has maintained it seven years, copies every runner's map fair before it goes up the stair, and asked for the act to be named correctly: she forged a record for a Magistrate, which is the worse thing.

Iuno knew the clean feeling of that decision in her hands and her teeth, and told her to tell her; Changli said she intended to, and produced silence when asked when, and Iuno said that is what she said. There is no method for escaping it, Iuno added — a wolf put a spear through her, and a woman with no arguments said *no* and stayed in the room — and it is a staffing problem: they have to already be there, and you have to have failed to drive them off. Changli already has one, and has spent seven years ensuring that person never gets to say no to her about anything that matters.

And the last number: eleven villages, four thousand one hundred and twelve people, places secured for three thousand eight hundred, and **three hundred and twelve** left without a floor — down from nine hundred in the second month, falling by fifty or eighty at a time, every one of them somebody's grandmother arguing about a well. Her hands were shaking. On the day the road closes, whatever the number is that morning is the number she will say out loud in a room, to a person, with her own mouth, because a wolf made her write it down that afternoon; and told not to decide the remainder alone, she said that is why they are here, and admitted she would have gone home and done it alone anyway.

That night the keeper of the ninth tower filed her fourth incident in six years: with the seal on the bell-rope intact and photographed, **the bell moved**, enough to touch the housing once on the north side — a tap rather than a beat, she wrote, like somebody checking the door is still there. She filed it under 天氣, weather, then struck that through and left the second column blank, and has declined twice, in writing, to fill it. The Magistrate wrote one word in the margin: *wait.*

## VII. SEVENTEEN PEOPLE, NINE CHAIRS

*(Gate 6 — Panhua's back room, that Tuesday)*

Eleven minutes' notice, a full house, and a table that seats nine. Panhua said the Magistrate could sit wherever there was a seat, and brought down from the top shelf a bottle Chixia, who eats there four times a week, has seen down four times in eleven years. Cartethyia took Jinhsi's coat and put a folded paper person in her hand on the grounds that everyone gets one; Lupa hooked out a stool; Amy told her she was late; Mei told her to have the fish. That was the whole reception, and seventeen people ate in relays with the overflow on the step, a rotation managed by nobody and working perfectly.

Chisa dismantled six years of Chixia's failures against the tax-office goose in three sentences — the right eye is clouded, the bird declines to turn its head because turning loses the door, so come at the right side, low, at walking pace — having watched it once. Lupa meanwhile established a rule without announcing it: anyone beginning something emotionally significant received food, delivered from a metre away, underhand, without a miss — and when Iuno set her cup down to raise that afternoon, Lupa slid a bowl in front of her and reminded her she had asked for a night and had not had one. Jinhsi watched a one-armed foreigner be stopped by a bowl, permit it, and lose nothing by it. Then Cartethyia asked what her favourite food was, of anything, and Jinhsi thought long enough that the table went still before admitting nobody had ever asked.

Chisa took the back door and Sanhua the front, neither having discussed it, each noticing the other take the other one — and after ninety minutes Chisa asked what her own eyes do, because she cannot see faces, and Sanhua said they finish: you look once, then you stop, and you have it, and hers do the same, and it troubles people constantly. At nine Baizhi took the Magistrate's wrist with the flat courtesy of a physician in a public place, counted ninety-one, and established that Jinhsi's last meal at a table was a working dinner in the fourth month with food *near* it; told her clerk would read anything sent to her office, Jinhsi said to mark it to her Counselor, who reads everything and keeps things — and Iuno's head came up out of a ledger for about a second and a half.

The corner by the window has had **a seat kept at it every week for a year**, laid by Yangyang before anybody sits down. Jinhsi wrote the ruling that made that seat lawful and said nothing about it all evening — and then, mid-argument about whether the absent guest likes bitter melon, passed the dish down the table toward the empty place, because it had come to her and the empty place was next along, and Yangyang saw it and said nothing.

At ten Changli came in with road dust on her boots and was absorbed in under a minute onto a rice sack, and confirmed under interrogation that Jinhsi has had four crates of mangosteens put through the ward offices in the second week of the season for six years running, and that the audit query is unresolved and will remain unresolved. Iuno alone saw Changli stop eating for four seconds to look down the table at a girl of nineteen with sauce on her cuff being shouted at about a pickle. Panhua said one sentence to the Magistrate all evening — that she eats too fast — and invited her back at four in the afternoon, when nobody is in and the oil is fresh.

Yangyang's count on the step refused to resolve, as it never has at that house, so she wrote down that it does not come out, and then wrote three more words underneath in the second ink, boxed them, and shut the book before Mei could look. In the empty room the lamp by the door hinge leaned once toward the third seat, steadied, and burned about a minute longer than it ever has.

## VIII. NIGHT TALK

*(Gate 7 — the same Tuesday, in pairs)*

Lupa told Iuno she has known about the ring since the second port and said nothing for two hundred and eleven kilometres because it was not hers; Amy has known since the landing; Chisa knew before Iuno did. Mrs Ren, handing back her ledger at Panhua's door, had told a woman she has known nineteen days that whatever is in the coat does not get lighter. Lupa's counsel came in two parts — feed her first, and do not make it a Moment, because feeding somebody is ordinary and the whole thing should stay that size, and it should happen on a Tuesday. Then Iuno raised the interval Lupa had sold, and Lupa said that this morning it was a thing four governments knew and none could point at, and tonight it is in a day-book with a date and her name — and, asked whether it was worth it, said to ask in eleven years.

The Magistrate walked home, because the chair kept for that office goes out nine times a year and nobody had been told to send it. At the corner where the ward changes she stopped at Old Feng's shuttered window: funeral paper, and along the front of the display, at the height of a person's hand, **eleven paper people three inches high, folded, made to be burned** — while in her own coat pocket was a paper person three inches high. She looked for about eleven seconds while Changli looked at the side of her face, then took her hand out of her pocket empty and walked on, and fifty metres later moved Thursday's Bureau of Waterways, because four in the afternoon is taken; Changli composed the entry walking, in terms four clerks would read and two would copy: the Magistrate of Jinzhou will attend a noodle house in the harbour ward at the ninth hour on Thursday for the purpose of eating fried food.

At the terraces Changli asked about the hand held shut since the third hour, and Jinhsi stopped in a lamp and opened it on four marks in a curve at the base of the fingers. Changli looked for as long as it takes to look at four marks and declined to ask what he had said, because seven years has taught her the difference between a thing a person carries and a thing a person holds: you can take the first off somebody, and reaching for the second costs you the hand. They set tomorrow — her office, door shut, no clerk, no runner, no Sanhua — and Jinhsi asked whether she would be angry and was told yes, and asked whether she would still have a Counselor at the end of it and was told that would be her own ruling, which Changli conceded is the only honest answer she has after seven years of looking for a better.

Elsewhere the night sorted itself. Panhua asked Yangyang whether to keep the third seat's corner, because a shelf is going up in the sixth month, and was told to keep it — and afterward took that bowl out of the rack, dried it, and put it on the shelf beside the little stitched cloth bird, where it has been going for about three weeks, which she has mentioned to nobody, including herself. Baizhi refused to tell Chixia what else she had seen in the Magistrate, on the grounds that a physician who takes a wrist in public buys that with everything else staying private — then went home, wrote *91* and *near*, turned the notebook over, and went in at the back, where there are **four pages in a different hand** she has shown to nobody in her life, and wrote one line on the fourth of them. On the landing, meanwhile, Amy discovered that Cartethyia's goose barley had been soaked hours before the plan existed, asked her to make a paper person for the corner seat at Panhua's, and was refused: Cartethyia asked Yangyang two weeks ago on a tower, and Yangyang told her, and she will not repeat it, because it was not hers. Amy let it go, and was handed a fifth paper person whose identity Cartethyia declined to give until it is true.

On the roof, Ciaccona said that sixteen people in a low room with wet plaster is a chord — a working chord, meant to be ugly and hold — and that striking one string sounds every string tuned to it, so who is tuned to that room? Chisa said the corner has **two strings on the third seat**, going different ways. One goes up, out at the seam over the door, past the eaves, past the weather; that one is easy, it has been in the house since the fifteenth, and it drinks the tea. The other goes **north along the crest wall and does not stop at the eighth tower**, which is the last one with a bell in it that anybody has heard; it is taut and old and held at both ends; it went tighter when the Magistrate put a dish down at the empty place; and it has not slackened. Ciaccona then named her own: the blank Fractsidus card is an **empty stave**, because somebody ruled five lines and sent it and is sitting somewhere with a pen, and whatever she writes, they will play. Told to write nothing, she said that is a rest, and rests are the loudest thing in the trade.

In the front room, Mei presented a bruise shaped like a fish, and Iuno read the **healing ledger** aloud — Ragunna ribs, four days; the ninth of the fifth, thirty-one hours; the eleventh, a declined stitching, nine hours; the second of this month, two days and a bit — and tonight's, gone before the fifth hour, which is about half of what it was, having gone to half **on the twenty-first** and stayed at half since, exactly. Mei knew what is filed on the twenty-first; Iuno knew that she knew; neither of them put it into a sentence. Mei asked her to keep the page and go on giving her the numbers flat, no run-up, because Iuno is the only person who tells her numbers about herself without doing a face first.

Then Mei got the rest out of her: Iuno had found the fault in a thirty-one-year game record in four minutes and had enjoyed it in her hands, and then a nineteen-year-old had described her Counselor as somebody who reads everything and keeps things, which is Iuno's old job description word for word. She wants no title from Jinhsi; she wants to be *useful to her*, which is worse, because it has no ceiling and no clerk to notice when she has overstepped — a title is a fence, and everything she has to answer for she did in the gap where nobody had written down what she was. Mei's answer took nine seconds: get a title, get written down, get a bit of paper saying what you are and what you are allowed and who can sack you.

Then Mei volunteered, unprompted, that Jinhsi had held out the annexe key and she had said no, because Jinhsi would have had to write down that she gave it to her, and Mei has spent about two lifetimes being the reason there is an entry about somebody. At one she reached for the coat on the chair back and was stopped faster than Iuno's sentences come out — and then Iuno told her there are two things in that coat, one carried since the frostlands and one since the twenty-fourth, that she is not showing her either tonight, and that she is *telling* her she is not showing her, because she would rather Mei had the shape of it than an evening where she moved a coat and pretended she had not. Mei said she was not asking, and did not ask, and fell asleep mid-clause. So Iuno took out the **ring** — one facet sawn off her own crystal shoulder at a price itemised to nobody — and then the other thing: a folded paper on good stock with **four characters** and an empty **seal space**, written at a gate by the woman with a tin of three hundred and six letters. She has known for a month what it says, and cannot work out whose chop it wants: four candidates, three of them disliked, and the fourth a nineteen-year-old who ruled that a woman with no origin is *returning* — which would require explaining the whole of it in a room, in advance, to her face, which is, as of that afternoon, a thing this household is entitled to.

## IX. THE MINE, THE LAB, AND THE WOMAN IN THE PROJECTION

*(Gate 8)*

City Hall sent Mei a location: ruins out of town, with the message that if she did not want to open the box yet, she could go there. Asked whether she should, Iuno asked whether it was running from the box or toward something, and Mei did not know, and was told to go and find out — because the file is inside a building and ruins are outside, and Mei has never been able to think clearly about herself in enclosed spaces — with the term that if it turned out to be running, they would walk back slowly and call that the information.

The road passed the Tiger's Maw Mine, and Amy stopped walking at the crest: sixty years of pit cut in concentric descending rings, ore carts on counterweighted rope lines, stamps arriving delayed from the sight, and most of the structural iron for the northern walls. Iuno mapped the flag-signal system in thirty seconds, later found six signals where she had counted four, wrote it down and said nothing more, because Mei would have found it unbearable and Iuno decided some time ago that she does not need credit for what she understands privately. Yangyang, who has filed six reports on that road under *infrastructure, operational, no anomalies*, said she found it strange once and then stopped finding it and then stopped finding it at all, and that it is good iron and the north wall holds — and over lunch said she did not know what she would have written instead. Mei, without looking up: *Operational. With lunch.*

The ruins sat in a shallow bowl with machinery still at its posts under forty years of even dust and no prints in it. Yangyang named the **Court of Savantae**; Chisa put her palm on an apparatus and said there is a resonance in the base, old, like something still counting, and nobody asked counting what. Then Amy dropped into the broadcasting register, because Fleet Snowfluff knew the Savantae — the bizarre experiments, the flying machines nobody could replicate, and one day every member vanishing, leaving the Chairman's last words: *You want my research? You can have it. I left everything I gathered in the Court of Savantae's labs. Now you'll just have to find it.* Yangyang then resolved the keystone diagram into a registry mark for **active** ownership, on a building everyone believes has stood empty forty years, on a road she has patrolled three times. Chisa found the lock by weight distribution, and standard Savantae security would be a frequency key attuned to the Chairman's own waveform, which they lacked — so Mei knelt and put her marked hand on the tile, and nothing happened, and then her fingers moved, placing rather than searching, clicked a bevel point, and turned a precise quarter-turn, the motion of somebody whose body remembered what her mind did not. Asked how, she said she did not know, without distress; Iuno wrote the time and did not ask.

The lab below was pristine, with a blackboard whose last line stopped mid-thought and the chalk still in the tray — and in the central chamber, **rain**: thousands of droplets hanging motionless in a column, each perfectly formed, declining to fall and declining to evaporate, above a placard reading **Artificial Retroactive Rain. Phase two.** Amy pointed out a single recent handprint in the dust, weeks rather than years, small, with the fingers spread. The logs ran in order: the Committee expelled Professor Heisen and eleven researchers, using *dangerous* to mean *uncontrollable*, and a patron funded them into that hillside, named only by a symbol Yangyang identified with a sharp breath as **Fractsidus**. Phase one was an ancient civilization's divine miracle, which Heisen meant to reproduce artificially and on demand; phase two was rain carrying encoded frequency backward through its own fall, capable in theory of reaching the conditions preceding the original event — and the column was the prototype, and it worked, and the miracle declined to trigger. Then the patron imposed a deadline and the contingency became an **Artificial Controllable Tacet Field**: designed rather than grown, controllable, deployable, aimable. The last logs concerned **Subject A038**, an artificial replica of a divine entity, whose readings the researchers labelled with visible reluctance as *hunger*, with items disappearing at night and sounds from containment that had *want* in them, and increased rations filed under *appeasement protocol*, which Iuno read aloud once, flatly, and then closed her eyes for three seconds.

Then Heisen's confession, made to a room he expected to be empty forever: time was his ultimate foe, his fear of death and thirst for truth had derailed the research, and they had already walked into a false *Gate*. And in the last minute, the clarity — *They are our 'past'… but they are also our 'future'… The future being hailing from our past… Please save us… once again… Please, keep pushing forward… until you reach… the real Gate that can change the world…* Then the projector activated once more, on a blurred figure at failing resolution, unannounced, standing where Heisen had been as though she had always been there and the recording had only now found power enough to show her, looking down at her hands, her hair moving in a hologram with no wind in it. Then she lifted her head. No features resolved, no eye colour, no mark on any hand — and she was looking **at Mei**, at the angle that tracked precisely to where Mei stood, as though the projection had measured the distance between the past and the present and found it navigable. Mei went still, the involuntary kind; her right hand rose two inches and stopped; Amy took one step and stopped herself deliberately; Iuno declined to catalogue or interpret. Then the charge failed and the image folded inward, edges first, then centre, then the face, last of all the face.

Behind a blast door marked **CLEARANCE: CHAIRMAN ONLY**, which read Mei's hand the way the floor had, A038 waited — having **built itself a body** out of the walls: sensor housings as joints, cables as tendons, screen casings fused into a torso shaped from the approximate memory of something that had once seen a person, with too many joints and a cluster of lenses where a face should be; and the containment rings had been bent outward from the inside, slowly, over years, with a patience worse than the damage. Chisa reported its output loud and confused rather than hostile, and looking for something. Mei had already drawn, with relief almost invisible on her face, and Amy, who has the same instinct and spent two years learning to identify it in herself, declined to stop her. It screamed and then ran for the door with the desperate momentum of something locked in a room for forty years that had just discovered the door was open; Chisa intercepted with her shoulders, redirecting rather than stopping; and Mei's fourth cut split the sensor housing, and it slumped into the doorframe like an exhausted child in a costume made of monitoring equipment and ambition. Amy said it had spent forty years building a body from the walls because there was nobody to talk to; Mei crouched, put her marked hand on its shoulder, and said that she knew — and the hum thinned under her palm and left, and Chisa said it was gone, and then, after a pause that was almost hesitation, that it had stopped looking.

On the road home, in the voice of somebody who had carried a thing exactly as long as she could carry it alone, Mei said she thinks she knows that woman — not from the archive, the file, or the mark. She once met a girl asleep a very long time with a journal of four thousand days of writing to no one, who wrote a story about a woman who gets to start over and wanted to be that woman so badly she fell into it; she did not say Lilith, a name she has said to two people, both walking beside her. What she said instead was that the woman in the hologram had been looking down at her hands like somebody who had been writing a long time and had just realized nobody was going to read it. Nobody interpreted it. Iuno walked with the coat pocket nearest Mei's side open, having stopped guarding it somewhere between the second ridge and the third, and was glad, simply: Mei had carried it about ninety-seven minutes and said it out loud on the road, before dinner, on the same day. The interval was getting shorter.

## X. TOLLHIDE

*(Gate 9)*

Chisa heard Ruan's treadle wheel stop from two terraces away, which meant her scissor-blade was ready; he had spent six mornings pretending the last adjustment needed another day, and she let him pretend. He had changed the lower bearing by half a grain, because the old one had learned her pull and started helping too early, and had left the old fracture visible as a silver seam — a blade with a history, whose future he had repaired — and asked that the first hard strike happen somewhere he could avoid watching.

Then a bell, and a bleeding driver: something the size of a sluice house had come through the pines above Lianshui, with Old Qiao drawing it uphill on a hand bell and six irrigation wardens. Baizhi read the torn boundary pylon it had carried — a Rocksteady Guardian with major deviations, its throat carrying bronze resonators that compress under charge and release a directional shockwave at intervals of seven, seven and five seconds, shortening under exertion. Cartethyia observed that four capable fighters were standing around the captain's emergency map, and Chixia produced the auxiliary field contract: a patrol sponsor, a registered route, a named objective, signatures from everyone, and the sponsor holding evacuation and civil command. Panhua intercepted them at the arch with two field baskets, bandages on top and food underneath, on the principle that people search harder when a warm meal survives.

Lianshui was evacuating with the practised shape of a city that had already begun imagining fire — and beside the road stood **eleven freight wagons, freshly painted, numbered 301 to 311.** Cartethyia read the numbers and slowed, because Iuno had seen a remainder of three hundred and twelve in Changli's ledger; nobody had told Lianshui that number at supper, and somebody had still put it onto wheels.

At the quarry, Tollhide's shockwave broke the crane ledge under Old Qiao's feet, and Fleurdelys crossed in one arc and held six wardens and several tons of stone on her back while they climbed. Lupa went off the rim with fire opening around her in a wolf's jaw; Chisa heard seven seconds become five, called the count, and sheared a bronze tusk at its root on the fifth step; Ciaccona drove the lesser Discords toward Lupa's fire on the quarry's own workers' tune. Fleurdelys split basalt from neck to spine and took the remaining horn through her side, and Ciaccona struck a dead chord and invoked **fourteen**, the household's word for a person turning annihilation into noble arithmetic — and Fleurdelys caught the horn and asked for a living plan within the next breath, and got one. Then the creature tore free into an extraction tunnel closed thirty years ago, after the survey bells started ringing underground.

In that tunnel, at the first branch, **a vast impact rolled through the hill from the north** and every bronze nail rang together, and Tollhide answered from ahead with hunger, pain, and a clean thread of terror. Chisa found the northern interval too broad for the creature ahead and concluded its pulse had accelerated and its first path through Lianshui had come south; Ciaccona held her blank card near a nail and a second grey mark wrote itself beside the first, the two together forming the opening interval of a march — a player beyond the ridge, with the countryside beginning to resonate toward the instrument. The final chamber held hundreds of the quarry's old survey bells, and Ciaccona read it in a glance: the ceiling will return the discharge into the core if the bells answer together, and she could hold them at one pitch for perhaps ten seconds. So they did, and the chamber became one bronze voice, and Tollhide released its shockwave and the bells returned it, and the core came toward Chisa, and she heard Sumika's red string move at her wrist and Ruan's new bearing carry the weight, and closed the blade.

Old Qiao counted his wardens by touching each shoulder, touched his own chest as the seventh, and bowed from the waist to Cartethyia, who bowed lower. Chixia sent the northern finding to the Ranger relay, then turned the evacuation into a drill — telling Lianshui the road was secure and asking them to finish the route anyway, through the document check, because the next bell might carry a different answer. Nobody cheered; they finished the count. Cartethyia laid her palm on wagon 311 and said that 311 has someone in, and Chixia said that today it held the Chen family's quilts and a lamp, and that this counts. And that evening, beyond the eighth tower and too far for the supper tables to hear, a pressure moved through the northern ground: the Tollhide Echo stopped chiming and turned its head toward the wall, Ciaccona's card cooled and its two grey notes darkened with a third space waiting after them, and Chisa crossed the room and rested two fingers on the lute's rim, and the strings trembled.

## XI. THE AIRING

*(Gate 10 — the sixth of the sixth month)*

Jinzhou airs everything it owns on the sixth, because in three weeks the air turns into a wet cloth for forty days and whatever goes into the plum rains damp comes out ruined; the Bureau of Rites posts six articles — Out from the fourth hour; You may look; Turn it at noon; Do not read; What falls is not yours; In, before the rain — under a heading that counts seven, because the seventh was struck in the year of the reform and the gap has been printed for four hundred years.

Mrs Ren does the coats herself, because a coat has to be turned out first and people are sentimental about pockets; she put a hand out and waited four seconds, and Iuno turned away from the line, went through it herself, moved **two things** into the shirt pocket underneath, and hung it up, and Mrs Ren straightened the crooked seam twice and said nothing at all. Mei's own coat went up at the fourth hour, and at half past five Iuno found her standing under that line doing nothing, and chose to leave it. Yangyang crossed them at six with the Bureau's forecast, fair to the ninth hour — Iuno said she would say the seventh — and came back four paces to say it was **a year today** since her report.

**The east gate.** Shen lays out **three hundred and six undelivered letters** face up on six boards once a year, because a woman at a gate worked out that the only way to keep an undeliverable letter is to take it out on the day everybody takes everything out, where it looks like housekeeping; nobody picked one up, and in forty years eleven have gone that way. Amy asked whether the plum of the plum rains is the same character as the first of the five on the truck's door, was told no, and was told she would be **the fourth to ask** — with the other three left unnamed. Then she laid out the deduction: the tin belongs to no ward, no post office and no heir, because a paid job belongs to the customer and the customer is the one who never came, so it is an estate item with no owner and no column, bound for the ward basket and, a year later, for a table where anybody may read anything on it. Shen has known since she was sixty-two, refused to hand it over, and sent her back on the sixth — any of them.

Iuno meanwhile held her folded paper against the low sun and saw what lamplight had refused to show her: inside the empty **seal space**, pressed into the fibre when the sheet was made, **a chop** — somebody had that paper made before they wrote on it, and left the space empty anyway. She turned round to find her daughter working a small square thing halfway out of a left pocket and putting it back; and Shen, without looking up, told Amy to bring it when she wants it read and not before, having clocked it **nine times.**

**The west terraces.** Lupa carried a chest and left the woman getting quieter in her own yard, and Chisa explained the debt: here it closes before dark, because everybody on that stair has to still be on it next year, and calling it nothing would only price it — so Lupa asked for **a chair** in the doorway for an hour, and the woman brightened up like a lamp and fetched the good one. Chisa then named the rest: Lupa has put nothing of her own in the sun, because the medal is on a wall in another country and everything else she owns is in a fire, and there is no article on that notice for people who keep their dead somewhere the weather cannot get at them.

Chisa's own pace changed by a fifth of a step at the dyer's turn, because quilts eat sound and every mark she navigates by had moved, and Lupa came up on her right at the same speed and walked her through two lanes and a bridge before she admitted to forty minutes lost — and then said it was also the most of Jinzhou she has ever had, with everything in every house out where she could hear it, and the best day since the shore. Higher up, an ironed summer school uniform hung on a line in a house whose youngest occupant is fifty-one, and Chisa stopped the way a person stops when the floor changes, because her own mother kept her room twenty years and told her the uniform was in a box and did not tell her it was ironed; Lupa said she is not on her own this year and told her to write tonight. Then the old woman looked at the red string Chisa has kept out of the light for four years and said the string wants the sun, that red goes first, and — asked what you do if it is already faded — that faded red is still red, like a fact about dye, which it was.

**Bookbinders' Row.** Ciaccona played badly on purpose for eleven minutes before an old man brought out a stool and told her she had done the bridge wrong eleven times in the same place, which means she can do it, so she should; and then came the thing she had gone for — **you can see the holes.** Every shop on one street should lose the same letters in the same order, because wear is wear and they all set the same language; instead there are **eleven shops and eleven different holes**, four of them in compartments that should be full, because those are rare sorts a shop buys once and keeps ninety years, gone from four separate presses, unreplaced, with every compositor giving the same two words. *It went.* One sentence between eleven of them, and it is the sentence you use about a cat. Cartethyia reached for a sort turned face down and Ciaccona caught her wrist four centimetres short: it has been face down since before the compositor's thirty-one years at that press, and it is out in the sun with the rest of them, and that is all it is owed.

Behind the fourth press, Master Qu, eighty-one, airs a run of **nine blank sheets** — the second act of a commission taken eleven years ago, finished nine years ago, never staged — nine of them because nine is how long the act runs, and he has never once put a thing on a stage without knowing how long it takes: nine minutes, doors open, nobody on the stage and nothing said, and the house does whatever the house does, which he has never found out. The commission was to write the wind, and an attendant who did not know she was telling him anything let him work out that the subject already attends, at the back door, through the domestic scenes, gone before the battle — and you do not write a play about somebody who is in the audience. He has never had the nerve, because a rest is the loudest bar in any piece of music, and if you put nine minutes of it in front of four hundred people and nobody comes, then you have found out. The fee sat out on a tray being aired for no reason except that it had been in the box: half-coppers, old minting, all of one year, never spent, and — Cartethyia asked exactly — **three hundred and six** of them. Ciaccona declined to chase it, on the grounds that if it is more than a coincidence, it will still be true on Thursday.

**Noon.** A bell goes at each ward office four seconds apart and a hundred and forty thousand people turn their paper over, a long dry hiss taking ninety seconds to reach the harbour — and Amy, on the wall with a receiver she never took out, said nobody is running it, and that she knows what that costs, because the thing that took her four years to build was two thousand people acting on a cue, and it broke every eleven weeks, and she had to be talking the entire time or it fell over. Then the real one, said to the sky, which is how Amy says the ones that count: **there is no job.** Everywhere else she arrives and inside a day knows what she is for; this place counted itself before she was born and will be counting when she is. Mei said it is the good version, and Amy agreed that this is what is annoying about it.

Iuno told Yangyang the seventh hour, disliking both sentences — a professional opinion at six, a fact about her arm at a quarter past twelve — and saying the second anyway, because a woman she lives with made a rule about it. Yangyang named her problem: she wants a **column**, because she can file *wind rising* off her own eyes and shorten the day inside forty minutes, and she cannot file *a foreigner's arm*, and writing it in remarks would get it struck and replaced with her own observation, and she would have got what she wanted and lied to get it. Iuno called it the most honest procedural objection she had heard in eleven years and told her to post nothing and watch her own weather with her own eyes — and four paces on, without turning round, Yangyang said the other thing: **she's holding it.** The air has sat still since the fourth hour, and eleven hundred lines have hung dead all day, on the one day of the year this country wants its weather to sit down and be quiet.

**The Ge house.** Cartethyia looked at a family scroll for four seconds and then said, out loud, from a stranger's gate, that somebody had written that name and crossed it out before the ink was dry — the seventh generation has four sons and three names, and the stroke through the fourth is the same ink and brush and hand and went on while the character was still wet. Ge Ruilin said they know, and that everyone in that house has known since they were nine, and that nobody says it at the gate: it comes out one day a year because paper rots, and for that day the whole street walks past and looks and does not read, which had worked for a hundred and fifty years until a foreigner did it in nine seconds. Cartethyia agreed with all of it and, asked why, answered honestly — a name was missing, and she saw where it went — while Ciaccona, who could have had the lane laughing inside a minute, kept her hands off the lute and let her friend be wrong in public.

Ge Suqin is seventy-four, and sent her nephew away to carry the table; he went, and did not look at Cartethyia again, and has not forgiven her, and there is no version of that day in which he does. Her grandmother told her in a kitchen when she was nine, once, and never again, having had it off her own grandmother the same way — four women in a row keeping an agreement made by people none of them met, which has held a hundred and fifty years and kept the yard quiet — and she has been waiting fifty years for somebody to say it out loud where she could hear it, and had given up, and wanted it understood that what she felt had no name on it and that grateful and angry would both have been simpler. Then she asked Cartethyia to say the struck character to her once, because she is the last one who is allowed; and Cartethyia said one thing into the side of her head, and Suqin closed her eyes, and afterward took one of the eight river stones off the eighth corner and put it into her hands, because it has held that corner fifty-one years and the paper needs six. At the top of the lane Ciaccona named it as the second name Cartethyia is carrying and will not say, the first being a seat at a table — one a name somebody is keeping *for* a person, the other a name somebody kept *from* one — and Cartethyia said she will hold both until they are true, because she did twenty years where the only thing she had was the names she gave things, and the way she stayed a person was that she did not spend them.

**The register yard.** Four hundred years of ward four lay flat on twelve trestles with the public admitted, and Iuno stood at the end of the first one and did not move: she could take the lot off in ninety minutes without breaking one of the six articles, because she would only have *looked*, which was her entire professional method for thirty years. Told to look, she said she intended to look at nothing at all, and asked Amy to stay and watch her do it — because Amy is the only other person in that courtyard who could, and because in about four years somebody is going to hand her something she should not have, and Iuno would like there to be a memory available of a one-armed woman being extremely boring in a yard. They stood twenty-two minutes and read nothing, which was by a considerable margin the hardest thing either of them did that day.

On the fifth article's table — where a page that leaves your line stops being yours, and the basket is sealed at the ninth hour and sits a year before anybody may read anything in it — lay a **shopping list** that has been out eleven times: six lines, five crossed through in a different hand each time, with five objects in a basket beside it, because the ward buys them one item a year, by nine people who have none of them ever discussed it. The sixth line is one character, argued three ways for eleven years — *give it back*; *still* or *yet*, half of an interrupted word; or a rare surname, on which the last item is a person. Chisa asked whether she might read it out, and anybody may, and she read it at lesson seven, badly, one line at a time — three catties of small coal, needles of the middle size, half a measure of lamp oil, a comb, black thread — and then the sixth, in exactly the same voice and at exactly the same pace, as an item on a list of things a person needs: *— still.* Chixia began to point out that you cannot get — and stopped, with her arms coming slowly uncrossed, having worked out in about three seconds why four thousand people have argued eleven years about a word every one of them can read. Clerk Wen wrote four characters in the remarks column and did not strike them: **Fourth reading offered. Foreign. Not ruled on.**

Amy read the whole table twice and stopped at the seventh item, the one nobody stops at: a page of foreign writing off a line in the harbour ward a year ago, unreadable to that office, with **a single brushed shape** in the top right corner. She stood in front of it with her thumb on the edge of something small and square that has been in her pocket, or in a rig case, since Lahai-Roi, and asked whether the mark was a character — and got three answers from three clerks about one corner: a fold-crease with ink in it, a paper-maker's mark, a compositor's sort-mark. Amy ran her thumb round the small square thing about four times, thanked him, and **wrote nothing down**; and Iuno, eleven feet away, watched her daughter fail to write something down, filed it, kept it, and said not one word about it all day. Mei, for her part, read her own household return — seven names, one stove, a one-armed head of household by acclamation, Amy's line saying *niece*, and Assessor Lü's four lines about name-giving to a person of unestablished origin — and said afterward that it means a man in an office spent four lines being careful about her on a wet Tuesday, when he could have written two words and gone home.

**In, before the rain.** At seven Iuno's shoulder went from four hours of ground glass to nothing inside two minutes, and she walked to the ninth tower, where Yangyang had her glass up with nothing at all in it: the north was clear, it had been building at five, and it had gone. Iuno gave her the rest — her arm has never once been wrong and never once gone quiet *early*, because it does not stop when the weather passes; it stops when the weather stops. **Fifteen hours**, and a whole country's laundry on top of it, and nobody down there knows: a hundred and forty thousand people will take their books in at nine on the posted schedule and think the Bureau got it right — and the Bureau *did* get it right, and the record will be correct, which Iuno called the worst sentence either of them would say that day. Then the bells went, the city took its paper in, Iuno put her dry coat back on with two things still in the shirt pocket underneath, Lupa went past with an armful of quilts and said one word — *Tuesday* — and four minutes after the last quilt was through the door, it began to rain.

They went up on the wall anyway, seven of them, at half past ten, with two lamps and a basket Panhua had handed over at nine with the words *I heard* and no further explanation ever. Yangyang tried her mother's flute for ten minutes and got three notes each time, and the fourth came apart, and the air over that wall could not take it up, could not finish it, could do nothing except be present, at about a degree above the rain. Mei asked how many of them were up there; Yangyang counted, because she is who she is, and said seven; Mei said eight. So they sang the donkey song instead — four hundred years old, of no distinction, and sung wrong at volume in the rain on a wall — with Ciaccona harmonising around the wrong notes, Cartethyia gloriously out of tune and loudest, and Chisa singing none of it and feeding Lupa the words four beats ahead, which is why Lupa came in on the beat and sounded for the first time in her life like a person who can sing. Yangyang's voice went halfway through the fourth verse, and by the ninth she was sitting on the wet stone with her mother's flute across her knees, being sung a stupid song about a donkey by eight foreigners on the anniversary of the only report she has ever been afraid to make. Around the seventh verse the rain over that one tower thinned by about a third, in a circle roughly eleven metres across, and stayed thin for four verses; nobody said anything about it; and Mei looked up into it once, with her face wet, in the tone of a woman noting a fact at a dinner table, and told it that was enough of that, and to come and sit down. And the rain came back over the tower. And the wind sat down.

Walking home, Mei observed that Iuno had been holding her coat shut all day, and Iuno confirmed it, and said there had been a moment that morning where she very nearly did something enormous because an old woman put her hand out for it — and that on a day like this she was not asking her anything at all. Asked why, she said she wants **the small one**: she has had the enormous ones, a cathedral and a chain and a prophecy and a city on its knees, and every one was a Tuesday somebody else chose, and what she would like is a bad evening in a rented room with the window that does not latch and nothing on at all.

**The basket.** Eleven pages went in; nine are ordinary. The tenth is a letter beginning *To you, whom I haven't met*, picked up at the foot of the wall below the ninth tower at about eleven at night by a patroller who folded it once without reading it — and there is no plausible route by which such a page reaches the ground at that place, and she declined to enter her own name in the finder's column, and Chixia can explain none of it and did it anyway. The eleventh came off a line in the east market at a quarter past five: one ruled foreign sheet, heavy stock worn soft along both folds and carried a long time before it was lost, with two printed column headings struck through in blue and relettered in a child's script, one heading left standing, thirty-four numbered entries and a further entry on the reverse; unreadable to that office; and the finder said the household was foreign and would ask, and no household has asked. Wen struck *weather* out of the remarks and replaced it with the observation that the paper is of no country trading through that port and the printed ink is thirty years older than the ink of the hand. It is a page a six-year-old once tore out of a trading-post weight-book at the far end of the world and filled with thirty-four experiments for making her mother stronger — lost, and searched for since, four times, at night, in the pockets of her mother's coat — and its owner spent that afternoon eleven feet away, reading a stranger's page and declining to write down what was in the corner of it. Wen sealed the basket and put it on the shelf at the level of his own eye, where it will sit for **three hundred and sixty-five days**, and then went out under a sky that had been fair to the ninth hour exactly as posted, put his hand out flat, and said *man zou* to whatever had just left.

## XII. THE PLUM RAINS

*(Gate 11)*

The first drop struck Deng Rui on the back of the hand while he was threading a needle, and then it **rose**, lifting to join others hanging beneath the receiver mast — and through one of them he could see the north watch platform, including the roof that burned down four years earlier. Sergeant Du stepped between three surviving posts onto a remembered stair over loose shale; Rui caught his belt; the northern drum sounded; a Discord unfolded from the ditch beside the mast, having followed the rising drops across the watch line; and Rui pulled the sergeant under him. General Jiyan's blade broke it apart, and its claw left Rui through the back of his jacket as it dissolved. Under the tarpaulin afterward Du sat holding a bowl in each hand, one of them a handle and a crescent of enamel, keeping his hands level, and said Rui had wanted the pepper out of his bowl, because his stomach always gave him trouble before a march.

Rain passed through the green command banner and briefly made another, black and torn along the lower edge, snapping above men who had been ordered to advance — and Jiyan's hand reached for a medical satchel he carried in those days and found the guard of his weapon. He kept looking until he could see green again, then had Du describe what he saw now. Bao reported recruits who can still read the route flags and men from the last campaign who keep seeing the bridge, which fell during that campaign and on which sunlight now lay along the rail; and Jiyan's standing order followed: **every order goes through an officer standing beside the people receiving it.** The drum came again at nine minutes and forty seconds, against almost nineteen the evening before. He sent the report south — Retroact Rain at the highland camp, spreading south along the Tacet seams, with the change in the interval — and directed that Rui's sister hear from an officer before the ward board goes up, and from somebody who knew him.

On the twenty-seventh in Jinzhou, Cartethyia's camellia cutting had put out a pale tip, and Ciaccona's ruled card showed **three** grey marks with the distance between the second and third shortening as she watched, while the Tollhide Echo put a low note through the table. Mei looked at the rain, and her hand remembered droplets hanging above a basin and the curve of a machine's shoulder under her palm, and she turned that hand over and asked how many people were still up there. Amy took the street receiver, because the ward bells cover the terraces and the dock speakers reach the people under canvas.

At City Hall, Iuno's river notes put water at the foot of the convoy's crossing by late afternoon, because the sluice office reads an upper gauge and a second flow enters below it from an old feeder behind the reeds — shown to her by the man who reads that gauge, who asked for a second measuring post last spring, and whose request Changli sent along with the works order. Opening the eastern spillway relieves the crossing and floods a drying ground three households use for seed-stock, so Jinhsi sent Wen at once with the forecast and the hour, and told him to say the gate opens at the necessary hour and that she wants their objections while there is still time to act on them. Baizhi then explained the rain: it carries condensed Remnant Energy from the Etheric Sea, past events survive in that energy, and contact allows a person's frequency to resonate with them. Mei asked what happens if the person remembers with their hands before they remember anything else, and was told to keep somebody beside her who can observe her hands; Iuno moved closer and left room beside her.

Then Jinhsi produced a plan with one chamber outlined beneath the oldest part of the building: **the foundation gauge has begun answering the northern pulses**, and the sealed annex may say why, and Mei may have access whenever she chooses. A warm line opened across Mei's palm; she asked for a reader who can explain the language and time to write down what she remembers, and said that first there are people trying to cross a place where the rain can put a bridge back. Iuno took the housing meetings under an appointment she specified herself: access to the allocations, a clerk who can enter agreements, a term ending tomorrow at noon, a spending limit in the margin, both Jinhsi and Changli able to end it earlier — and a clause she added, that the families may ask for another representative as well. At the cloth hall the caretaker took one look at the word *vacant* and went upstairs to six sleeping places, shoes under the beds, and a cup, a comb and half a peeled orange on a box, belonging to men who unload the night barges and have slept there since the second month; so Iuno folded the allocation so the total faced inward, added six people to the sheet, and, asked what happens when the rain ends, put a date on it — seven days, with the men at the table when the next seven are discussed.

At the causeway, Mei looked downstream at a timber footbridge with a roof over the middle and said it would keep people clear of the water; Yangyang said that bridge was taken down before she came to Jinzhou, and that today's crossing has six stone markers, the first beside her boot. Chisa, listening for the difference between real footsteps and the rain's, heard a second set of Lupa's steps come toward her from the water, and told her the rain had her walk now and asked for a hand against her shoulder while she set the rope. The drum sounded while they tightened the knot: **eight minutes and twelve seconds.**

Then, steadying an axle at the third marker, the rain opened around Mei: sun on the back of her neck; a child with a shaved head running between loads of timber with a plum in each fist while a workman called after him to mind the lime; the scholar tree at shoulder height. **She knew the workman** — his name pressed against the back of her teeth, and that his thumb had healed crooked from an injury he laughed about while his wife was in earshot. Her palm rested on something broad and smooth: a fitting, an opening, and she had stood there before with that part waiting in her hands, and the gate in front of her opened toward the houses people were framing beyond the timber stacks. Her right hand turned. **Gold entered the water around the marker.** Every suspended drop swung toward her at once; a mule reared; and far below her feet something enormous began taking up its slack. Chisa's grip closed on her left forearm and told her the wheel was slipping and she needed her there — and Mei lifted her right hand, which rose through the memory slowly, until cold water closed around her wrist and the house frame thinned and she saw the axle tilted toward the edge of the causeway, and caught it with both hands.

Yangyang reached her, and air gathered around her hat warm enough to lift the wet hair off Mei's forehead, and the rain slanted away. Then the wind faltered, and water flashed high above the road: **a city reflected in the air, windows open on black floodwater, a red cap turning slowly beneath a broken lamp.** Yangyang dropped to one knee as if somebody had struck her behind it, and her hand went up toward the air beside her cheek, and she told it to stay close to her shoulder — and then gave it work, because Bao has the drivers and Lupa has the animals, and she needed help finding the south marker and wanted it with her when she reached it. A small pressure settled into her sleeve. At the south bank Mei's fingers shook enough to tap a cup against her teeth, and she told Iuno by radio that she had known the people she saw and known where they were going to live, and that her hand had started opening something while she was looking at them; Iuno asked what her hand was doing now — holding the cup, in both hands, because it keeps shaking — and told her to keep doing that. And then the thing underneath: she had wanted to follow it, even after Chisa spoke, because she was so close to remembering his name.

At the eastern spillway, Changli took off her outer coat, handed it to Wen, and asked to be shown where to put her hands, carrying trays of silkworms out of a shed omitted from the survey; at the fourth trip the woman looked at her bare arm and the old feather-shaped scars and said she would want a cloth there because the edge catches, and her daughter brought one, and Changli let the girl fold it over her arm. The gate lifted with the charcoal stove left behind, and water rolled it onto its side and filled it, and the woman watched until the roof reflection broke apart; and when Wen said he could enter the stove and the feed under the same assessment, she told him to put the room down too, because her eldest was born in that room, and when he asks what it cost, the room goes down. At the school, meanwhile, Ciaccona heard the rain outside answer with a song her father used to play with the flat bridge, and put a different chord under the room instead, softly enough for Baizhi to go on asking her questions, while her eyes filled and she kept playing the one inside.

North of the city the rain had raised a second army between the watchfires, and the current army worked among them; at the western gun Jiyan found a young officer waiting for a captain who died in the previous campaign, and sat on the ammunition box beside her and had her take him through the placement of her own troops until her hand steadied on the map. Then something struck the earth far to the north, and the lamp flame flattened, and on the map a bead of water travelled across the Norfall plains and stopped against the weight holding Jinzhou down. **Seven minutes and thirty-six seconds.** Jiyan marked the time and wrote the name the dispatches had been approaching all day: **Ovathrax, Threnodian of War.** The deep impact reached the city through the foundation, and Jinhsi felt it in the soles of her feet, and her pen rolled across the table and struck the inkstone; she sent the ammunition reserve, opened the second medical store, put the armourers on rotation, and — told the city would ask how long the stores will last — said to give them the current figure and the rate of use and to read it at the ward meetings, so people can ask about their own streets. Then she asked for the larger register, which Changli had had brought that afternoon, and which lay on her chair under her folded cloak, heavy enough to bow the cushion; and she carried it to the table herself.

On Bookbinders' Row, a compositor setting the morning warning ran short of one sort, checked the drawer and the floor and the dish where his daughter puts pieces she finds while sweeping, then ruled a small space in the forme and reached for a brush. The woman at his counter asked whether the ward would accept a sheet finished by hand, and was told they will accept a warning people can read; and she set down her receipt, covering **several purchases of type over the previous month, all paid in advance.** He said he could fill part of it after the rain, and she told him to take the time he needs, because she will be in the city. His daughter poured for her father first and put the second cup on the counter beside the receipt, saying the handle stays cool if you take it here — and outside, rain had gathered in the lip of a broken paving stone, and for an instant the puddle held a mown field, with stubble against bare arms and a small finger hooked around another, warm and certain, and the light above it spreading until it filled the place where a face should have been. Her little finger moved against the porcelain. Asked whether she wanted sugar, **Nivora** asked for two, and for another cup for the girl, if her father could spare it.

At the school, Mei finished her bowl with her back against the warm side of the stove, leaned against Amy, and said she had made an appointment at the archive for that night and had asked for somebody to read the annex with her; Amy offered to sit outside if Mei would rather have Iuno in the room, and was told to come in with them and keep the questions Mei forgets to ask. Then Mei went down to the arch for Chisa's coat, and through the open gate the road ran north between lamps set low enough to show the mud, with a keeper lighting the next one and stopping to let a stretcher pass. Above him, the bell in the ninth tower **trembled on its beam**, and Mei felt the answering warmth across her palm and closed her hand around the coat and waited for it to pass. Beside the school door, Chixia had pinned the weather notice under oiled paper, with rain gathering along its lower edge and the two large characters at the top staying clear: 梅雨. Iuno took one sleeve, Mei kept the other, and together they lifted the coat clear of the wet step and carried it into the room.

## XIII. WHERE IT STANDS

At the start, seven foreigners were strangers on a ninety-day stamp holding four unexplained objects, and nobody in Jinzhou had a column for them. Mei knew a mark on her hand and a language she could speak and could not write; Iuno knew where every document in a building was and had learned, at the cost of an arm and eleven years and a hundred and seventy-three names, to stop reaching for them; Amy was frightened of being left behind again. The ninth tower had been silent since the dark, and the tremors past the north face were eight days apart and closing. A month later almost nothing has been settled by conquest, and everything has changed.

**Mei** has become an entry: household four-one-one, seven names, one stove, four lines of an assessor's careful remarks. She declined a key to her own sealed history because taking it would have made a Magistrate write down that she gave it — and has now booked the annex on her own schedule, with a reader, her daughter to hold the forgotten questions, and a woman whose job is to write the numbers flat. Her body keeps answering what her memory cannot reach: a puzzle floor and a blast door that recognised her hand, a hologram that looked at her across forty years, a gate fitting under her palm in rain that gives back the past. Each episode is shorter than the last, and each time somebody stands beside her watching her hands. Her healing halved on the twenty-first and has stayed halved, exactly, and neither she nor Iuno has said the obvious thing out loud.

**Iuno** did the thing she was most afraid of, which is want the work — and answered it by getting written down: a term ending at noon, a spending limit in the margin, two people who can end it early, and a clause she added herself letting the families ask for somebody else. She stood at the end of a trestle for twenty-two minutes reading nothing, with a witness recruited so the memory would exist. In her shirt pocket are a ring cut from her own shoulder and a folded paper whose seal space holds a chop pressed into the fibre at the mill, because somebody had that paper made before they wrote on it and left the space empty anyway. She wants the small version: a bad evening in a rented room with a window that will not latch. **Amy**, meanwhile, found a city that does not need her voice and said so, was told to sit with a thing for a year and accepted it, and stood in front of a stranger's page with a brushed mark in the corner and wrote nothing down.

**Lupa** sold the one number belonging to Mei's body and bought a clause in a foreign country's permanent record: nobody in that household gets spent without being told to her face, in a room, in advance, by a person present — and asked to be asked in eleven years whether it was worth it. **Ciaccona** carries a blank card with three grey marks and a closing interval. **Cartethyia** carries two names she will not repeat, a river stone that held a corner for fifty-one years, a camellia with a pale tip, and a gate that will stay shut. **Chisa** was lost for forty minutes in a city that moved its furniture into the street and called it the best day since the shore, was told faded red is still red, gave a ward a fourth reading of a character it has argued about for eleven years, and is going to write to a mother who has been ironing a summer uniform once a year for twenty years. **Yangyang** has had her anniversary noticed, has learned to refuse to lie in order to get a column, and watched the wind hold four hundred kilometres of weather off a city for fifteen hours and then fail at the fourth bar of a lullaby, and be sung to instead.

**Jinhsi** has eaten a meal sitting at a table, been asked what food she likes and discovered nobody had asked before, and moved a Bureau meeting so the official book can record the Magistrate attending a noodle house for the purpose of eating fried food. She carries a whisper she will not repeat until she can verify it with a Sentinel that has told her nothing for nineteen nights and counting, and her Counselor has promised her a conversation she will be angry about, which stands between them still. **Changli's** calendar has been read by somebody who does not play; her teacher's record holds two games under one numbering; her blank card is a pass, which means the player is still there and it is her turn. She holds the registry traitor's name, and a forged date maintained for seven years, and the three hundred and twelve — who have eleven painted wagons now, with thirty-four more being fitted, and a number still short of zero, which she has undertaken to say out loud in a room, to a person, on the day the road closes.

**Scar** sits in a cell, having left a card, an unrepeated whisper, and the promise that Mei will come to the Fractsidus of her own accord. Under a drained reservoir, **A-Ling** is in the archive in her own hand beside the ledger that priced her. Under a hillside on the north road, a laboratory stands with its rain still hanging, its blackboard unfinished, and a small handprint in the dust only weeks old; its patron's symbol was Fractsidus, and its **phase-two product is now falling on the highlands and spreading south**, raising a second army out of the ground and putting a fallen bridge back over a quarry road. The drum has closed from nineteen minutes to seven thirty-six in a single day, and a general has written the name under the time: **Ovathrax**, which does not play, which is the weather over the board, and which has already won here once in a printing where the word for it was *held*.

The **ninth tower's** bell was tapped once, on a night with cut grain in the wind; a keeper struck out *weather* and left the second column blank; a Magistrate wrote *wait* in the margin; and on the twenty-seventh it trembled on its beam while a lamp-keeper let a stretcher pass. On a shelf in the ward four office, at the level of a clerk's own eye, a sealed basket holds eleven pages — one a letter beginning *To you, whom I haven't met*, found at the foot of the wall below that tower by a patroller who declined to give her name; one a ruled foreign sheet relettered by a child, whose owner has been searching for it since she was six and walked past it by eleven feet without knowing. In three hundred and sixty-five days that basket goes on a table where anybody at all may read anything on it.

On Bookbinders' Row, eleven shops are missing eleven different sorts and every compositor gives the same two words — *It went* — and one sort has lain face down for thirty-one years, out in the sun with the rest of them, which is all it is owed. Behind the fourth press, nine blank sheets are aired every year: a second act, nine minutes long, doors open, never staged, paid for in three hundred and six half-coppers that have never been spent. At the east gate, three hundred and six letters go out on boards one day a year so the paper will last, and the woman who wrote them has told a girl to come back on the sixth — any of them. And on the twenty-seventh, at a counter on that same Row, a woman with a receipt for a month of type bought in advance drank tea from a cup with a chipped rim while a compositor filled a missing character in by hand.

The plum rains have forty days to run. The wind is in the house and has spent fifteen hours it did not have. Four hands remain on the board, and Jinhsi can still account for only two. The annex is unsealed and waiting, in a room being arranged for tonight. And every evening, one human hand at a time, the lamps of Jinzhou still come on.
