# Δέδυκε Μὲν Ἀ Σελάννα (The Moon Has Set)

*a fable of the Fractsidus*

---

*]the moon has set, and the Pleiades.[*
*]midnight. the hour goes by.[*
*]someone has to watch the dark do it.[*

---

## i.

Every Overseer hears the fable once. It is told on the night they receive their name, which is the night they surrender their old one, and it is told by the Grand Architect personally, over tea, in whatever face she is wearing that year. The tea is always excellent. The face is always borrowed. The fable is always the same, and it is different every telling, and both of these facts are true, and Overseers learn early which questions to keep.

It begins the way fables begin.

There was once a girl whose face would not hold.

Mirrors disagreed about her. Her mother reached for her in crowds and came up with strangers. By seven she had learned the trick of it: a face is a frequency, and frequencies can be sung. By ten she could be the baker's daughter, the ferryman's son, the magistrate at half scale. The village considered drowning her, considered sainting her, and settled — villages are practical — on employment.

This became her office. When someone died mid-sentence, the girl was sent for. She would sit with the family awhile, learning the dead by their leavings — the worn chair arm, the handwriting, the particular way a coat had been hung — and then she would go out the back door and come in the front wearing the departed, and the sentence would get finished. A father blessed a marriage from beyond his own funeral. A sister apologized. A wife said the thing wives save for last and often save too long.

One evening only. That was the law of the office. And a second law, older, unwritten: faces are kept. A face cannot be returned. Where would you leave it.

She was paid in bread and in faces. By seventeen she owned forty of the dead and one of herself, and the arithmetic of that sentence was already going the way arithmetic goes.

## ii.

(Cristoforo, when he tells it, gives the friend a name, a tragic flaw, and three lines of foreshadowing. The Architect tells it plainly. This is the difference between them, and very nearly the whole of it.)

There was one person the trick failed on.

The girl could stand in any doorway wearing anyone, and this one would look up from her work and say, *it's you*, in the flat voice of someone reporting weather. Asked how, she shrugged. You hold cups with both hands, she said. You laugh a beat before the joke lands, like you read ahead. Your feet turn in when you're happy. I could find you in a riot.

Summers, the two of them lay in the mown field past the mill, where the grass was cut too short and printed its stubble into their backs, and planned the repair of the world. The friend was the kind the world makes first — first up the wall, first into the cold water, first to volunteer for whatever the village feared that season. There was a shrine that wanted keeping and a sea that wanted watching and a long list of things broken in ways girls could see and elders could vote on. The friend meant to fix all of it, in order, out loud.

You'll help, the friend said. It had the grammar of settled fact.

I'll watch, said the girl. I'm good at watching.

Same thing, said the friend, and hooked her smallest finger through the girl's smallest finger, there in the stubble, under a sky with one star out early. You won't leave till we're done with this shit.

The girl looked at the joined hands. Warm, the friend's. Warm the way bread is, all the way through, from being made recently.

Deal, she said.

Of all the faces she would ever hold, the friend's she left alone. Everyone else fit her. That door she kept for knocking.

---

## iii.

Then the sky did the thing skies did, in those centuries.

The fable calls it a tide. Histories call it other things; histories are fables with budgets. The sea stood up. The frequency of the world tore along its oldest seam, and the village had one night to decide what it was worth, and the friend — being first, being always first — went up to the shrine gate to hold it shut, and held it, and the gate held, and the friend burned. This is called saving everyone. Everyone was saved. Count them: everyone.

Afterward a caller went door to door with the list. The girl stood in the queue of the living wearing a dead man's face — she had put it on to carry his mother through the wave-night, and had run out of right moments to take it off — and the caller read the name of the face, found it already crossed out, made the sound officials make, and moved past her. Death works from a list. Her line on it stood blank. It has stayed blank. This is called surviving, and she has been doing it ever since, the way a stone dropped down a well can be said to be still traveling.

She performed the office one last time. She learned the friend by her leavings — the plans in the margins, the finger-hooked grammar, the reading ahead — and she went out the back door of herself and came in through the mother's front gate wearing the girl who burned. The mother held her by both shoulders. The mother said the things mothers save. Go on now, the mother said at the end of the evening, at the end of the office, in the doorway, at dusk. Go on. You did enough. *You were always enough.*

The girl walked out of the village inside a borrowed goodbye, and kept walking, and the fable notes — every teller keeps this line; it is load-bearing — that both of the warmest sentences ever spoken to her were addressed to someone else.

She wore the face to remember her by. A path becomes real by being walked on; a face wears out the same way. She walked it smooth. Somewhere in a century she can no longer locate, the features let go of their owner the way water lets go of a shape, and now she dreams the field — the stubble, the early star, the warm hand, the deal — and above the hand, where the face goes, the dream keeps a bright patch of nothing, the exact size of a sun looked at too long.

## iv.

What does a survivor do with forever.

She collects the others. That is the founding, and the fable gets brisk here, because the founding was brisk: a woman with a thousand faces walking the ruins of the age, and in every ruin, one person standing. The sole survivor. The epilogue on legs. The one the story ended around and then left onstage with the lights up. She knew them by the eyes — eyes that had watched the same wave from the same distance — and to each of them she said the sentence she had built from her own case:

*Your story is over. You are still here. Someone should do something with that.*

The frightened called them a cult. Cults kneel; her people catalogue. The Order called them heretics, which from the Order was a professional courtesy. She called them the Fractsidus — the broken-off, the fraction that remains when the whole is subtracted — and she gave each of them a new name with her own mouth, and kept the old ones, the way she kept faces: in a room, in order, within reach.

The doctrine fits on a coin. Every story ends the same way. Fate is a landlord; the gods are his agents; the rent is a girl, collected at the gate, annually, in fire. Prophets lease out their cities. Sentinels sleep through the collection. Therefore: abolish the office of the author. Put the pen in the hands of the species. If a Lament is the sentence, then write the next one ourselves, on purpose, in our own grammar — and see whether a fire chosen reads differently from a fire suffered.

Recruits ask — one always asks; she waits for it — why the fire is required at all.

Because a pen is handed over by a hand that is burning, she says. Nobody surrenders authorship in fair weather. Ask a god. Better — ask the last fourteen girls who asked a god.

Then she pours the tea, and the recruit signs, and the recruit is called something new forever.

She named herself last of anyone. The word she chose is the word for a star's grave — for what a light collapses into when it is done being looked at, which takes everything that falls in and files it, and keeps it, and can itself only be inferred, from the way things orbit. Ask her why and she smiles with whatever mouth she has on.

It's where light goes to be kept, she says.

Kept for whom, a recruit asked once.

Kept, she said.

## v.

Two case files, because a doctrine is tested at its edges.

The first is the playwright. She found him after a performance in a canal city, drinking alone at the age when talent begins to audit itself. His tragedy had run four nights; the audience had wept in the correct places; he was inconsolable, and she recognized the disease on sight, because obedient weeping is applause, and applause is a solvent.

Your ending was wrong, she said, sitting down uninvited.

The ending, he said, is the truest part. It's the rest one is obliged to invent.

The ending is where the author's nerve runs out. You wrote the fall beautifully. You wrote it beautifully because you believe in it. A man who believes in the fall spends his whole genius on the falling and calls the ground an act of god.

And what would madam have the ground do? Open like a book?

I'd have you write one, she said, where something gets there first.

He laughed at her. He is still laughing at her; it has been decades; the laugh has become a collaboration. He writes her the same ending over and over, each time better, each time certain, and she commissions the next one the way you press a bruise. He believes proximity to a protagonist is power over the tale. She lets him believe it. An Overseer who thinks he holds the pen holds still.

Once, deep in a working night, over the wreck of a draft, he asked what she was actually looking for — in the reels, the vessels, the staged girls, all of it.

An ending I can use, she said.

Use for what?

She looked at him long enough that he checked his own face for fit.

Reference, she said.

The second file is the conductor. A village named for star-wind; a meteor with red in it; one survivor; four hundred years of concerts in empty halls, waiting on a promise. Standard case. Beautiful eyes. The recruitment required one evening only, and the Architect performed it herself, in a face she had taken the trouble to learn from a paradise's worth of leavings — the firewood stacked where the dead had stacked it, the stew at the dead girl's hour, the apron knotted the way the dead girl's hands had knotted it. She knocked on grief's door wearing grief's answer.

The conductor embraced her at the threshold and wept into her borrowed neck and said the things four centuries save up. The Architect stood inside the welcome the way she had once stood inside the goodbye — perfectly still, a thief in a house where everything she wanted was bolted to someone else's name — and when the evening ended (one evening only; the old office survives in her like an accent), the conductor signed.

Centuries of good service followed. Then a rover with gold eyes walked into the woman's dead paradise and apologized, and the conductor resigned by dying well, and these days she gardens in an afterlife that learned to water itself, where a Triss of its own making cooks the stew — the position filled, the face retired, the door of that particular house closed now to every skin the Architect owns.

She has filed this under *anomaly.* The file stays open on her desk. Some nights she reads it the way you press a bruise.

## vi.

Her library is the place where stories go to die. She fishes them out of the dark beneath the world — the Nihility keeps everything, chewed — and she splices what she catches and screens it in a room with one chair.

Fourteen reels. Fourteen worlds; one girl. The eyes come up gold every time. The name changes — traveler, doctor, hunter, commander, witness, light — the way her own faces change, which she has noticed, and noted, and declined to think about with the lamps lit. In every reel the girl gives herself away by the pound. In every reel a gate, a cliff, a hull, a hymn. In every reel the girl leaps.

And there the footage ends.

Her instrument is her Forte; her Forte is mimicry; a splice is a face put on a moment; and there is one frequency she has never once managed to wear. In her notes she has a word for it — *contact* — written the way sailors write of a sea they know from other sailors. Whatever it sounds like, her recordings shear off a beat before it. Fourteen stories, fourteen leaps, fourteen clean cuts to black at the identical instant of arc. Her archive agrees with itself perfectly. She has taken the agreement for proof.

*Every story ends the same way.* She has the tapes.

Out of the fourteenth reel she recovered a scrap of audio the splice should have destroyed: a cabin, snow-light, two voices — one grown, one small — singing over each other, laughing at the wrong bars, a song with its ending already inside it and the singers singing anyway. Joy, in the tense of something that knows. She has listened to it more times than she has years. She hums it in courtyards. It is the one artifact in her collection she can neither source nor wear nor file — light arriving from a star her whole cosmology says collapsed.

So she builds. This is the design the world calls a conspiracy and she calls a laboratory: grow the girl, stage the gate, run the story under controlled conditions, vary one variable, observe the leap. A gladiator, once. A saint, once. Lately, a quiet student with strings for a spine, seated at lunch between two best friends, one of whom is the experimenter. When a vessel fails, make another. When that one fails, another. Grief, industrialized, looks exactly like patience.

Somewhere in the procedure lives a hypothesis she has never written down, because writing it down would name the experiment, and she guards her names. It goes: *somewhere in the space of all possible girls is one who leaps and is caught. Find her. Record it. Learn, at last, what the frequency weighs.*

The hypothesis ends there. Hypotheses are permitted to.

## vii.

Last month, in the underground city, the fifteenth story ended.

She was in the crowd for it, wearing a volunteer's vest and a face with good sightlines. Live, this time. Present tense. Air for an instrument. The god came through the gate on his knees like a tired father, and his chest opened, and a mother she has studied across fourteen lifetimes came down the ramp with both hands full of a daughter the entire arithmetic of the deep had written off as spent — and the crowd roared, and the medics abandoned procedure, and the Grand Architect of the Fractsidus stood in the ordinary crush of shoulders and watched contact happen, at speed, in weather, with only strangers' breath between her and it.

Her instrument did what her instrument does. It failed to take.

The moment declined to be worn. It went past her like heat off a door, and into her like heat off a door, and stayed — unfileable, humming. Three notes, rising. The cabin song's opening interval, arriving in her chest from the inside, a direction new to her chest's zoning.

Where the ruin of a grand design should have been sitting, she took attendance of the feeling and came up with a word from very deep stacks: *interested.* Also a second word, older, from the field past the mill, which she confiscated at the border and kept out of the record.

She went home the long way. She slept, which she does the way other people visit relatives. She dreamed the field: stubble printing her back, one star out early, a warm hand hooked to her hand by the smallest finger, a voice laughing a beat ahead of the joke — and above the collar, the bright patch of nothing, same as always, sun-sized, patient.

You won't leave till we're done, said the voice.

I know, she told it. I'm still here. You'll notice the shit remains undone.

The dream, being hers, held its counsel. She woke with her hand closed.

The fable ends here, whoever tells it, because here is where it has gotten to. Overseers, hearing it, wait for the moral; fables owe one; she is aware of the debt. For several centuries the moral ran one sentence long, and she could deliver it in any mouth she owned: *every story ends the same way.*

The current draft carries a second line beneath the first. The ink is recent. She keeps it from the Overseers. It says:

*Show me what happens next.*
