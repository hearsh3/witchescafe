# Simulacrum of the Heart 1–5

## 1. The hand comes down

The valley did not agree on a century. A pagoda stood bright with new lacquer beside roofs half buried in river silt, and only the ground under Suoming’s oil-paper umbrella kept still. Twelve had fallen through Hsin’s Sanctum when Suoming struck the Prism of Fate with Nine Shadows. The Core turned again. Balanophora had lost its borrowed Ministry clerk, but the smell of rotten pears had gone down ahead of them. Yangyang had felt a small, warm hand catch her empty right cuff and pull her clear of a closing beam. Somebody had let out a long breath beside her ear.

Mei’s stitches were out, but the scar pulled on the climb. Yangyang put an arm around her despite her own sore ribs. At a culvert a vine-ridden Autopuppet opened Suoming’s right forearm from elbow to wrist before wind and sword took it apart. Yangyang made the Elder sit for a bandage, and Mei offered her a hand back up. Suoming knew the rot and said it would lead to the root. Far in the west a fox taller than the mountains held one hand up against the white sky. Suoming called the place Hsin’s Simulacrum Nexus of Mengzhou. She’d read about it in sealed Ministry records. This was her first time inside.

Elsewhere the others had heard enough of one another to know they’d lived. In the orchard terraces Buling told Ciaccona that Mengxuan Vale was where Hsin kept her Celestials. A whole village of them, she said, and they’d never once had a festival cake. In the Vale itself Qingxiao and Ruoyun found Mingxiao and the other Celestials tending the machinery. Qingxiao asked about Hsin. Mingxiao said they couldn’t say.

On the northern road Amy and Iuno fought stray Autopuppets, leaving fresh frost-cracks in Iuno’s crystal shoulder and a dent in her moonwheel. At a gate without a handle, Iuno tried to push a stone moon straight to its basin. Amy read the icy paving as a sliding puzzle and called the turns. They pushed the moons into place together. Inside they found hot red-bean buns in an empty steamer hall and a little toothed contrivance of nested brass rings. Amy pocketed the brass. Iuno pocketed the buns.

Lupa, Chisa and Cartethyia followed the eastern roads. Cartethyia called for Ciaccona over a gorge, and Ciaccona heard her from the orchards. Chisa’s shears could get the three out of a bad footing, but going straight through this valley could bring them back to the same broken brass hoop on the same shelf of rock. Lupa crossed the heights with a fist in Chisa’s coat. Cartethyia held Lupa’s belt when the joke stopped being funny.

In a lotus basin strung with green-lit rings, Cartethyia drank the pond water, ate a flower and climbed to a warm bronze key sunk in the mountain. She struck it with the heel of her hand. The key turned and came out. A bell rang without a clapper, the Vale’s astrolabe slowed, and the giant took her hand down. The sky stayed up. She folded her arms and gave a small, close *Hmph* beside Cartethyia’s ear. Under the East Arcade awnings, Yangyang held a palm to the warm Prism and heard the breath again, slower now. Mei took four pictures with her cracked Terminal. The edge of Nine Shadows stayed in the last one, shading both their faces.

Iuno and Amy found the other three by a dry basin in a little water-gate town. Cartethyia was soaked through and Lupa was wet to the knees from hauling her out. The brass in Amy’s pocket buzzed hard enough to feel in her teeth. Chisa recognized the missing piece of the cliff hoop just before the rings unfolded into a flat rosette. The paving turned under it. Lupa grabbed Chisa, Chisa caught Amy’s wrist, Amy caught Iuno’s empty sleeve, and Cartethyia got both arms around Lupa. The square folded over all five.

They landed on moss at the bottom of a lacquered tower. Chisa’s shears found no way out. A warm green orb coaxed vines up the bare trellis as long as it stayed near their tips, so Amy and Iuno carried it slowly up the galleries. The growth bridged the gaps and eventually gave them a tree to climb out on. Lupa climbed it, one grip at a time. The others gave her the filigree lotus they found at the top. A round window opened in the sky and a vast hand reached down to ruffle Cartethyia’s hair. *Hmph.* The finger was wider than she was and careful enough to do little more than tip her laurel. Cartethyia thought the fox sounded like someone who would scold you for eating the icing and still save you the corner piece.

The lotus opened, its rose-gold needle pointed south-west toward the green awnings, and the living bough grew out that way. Iuno meant to follow it to Mei. Lupa got down on hands and knees and asked someone to go in front so she’d have somewhere to look.

In Huizhen Plaza, Suoming asked how Yangyang had come by the Prism. Suisui had refused a guaranteed homecoming that would spend Yangyang’s shijie to buy it. Hsin gave her the stone after she said no. Suoming remembered it keeping papers down on Hsin’s desk. She wanted to hear how the Watcher would tell it. Then she told Yangyang to move it to her left, where it would leave her sword hand free, and to stop leaning on it when she got tired. Yangyang hadn’t known she did.

## 2. The Third Ferry Rest

The Terminal still read 11:42. The camera worked. The relay didn’t. Mei yawned until her jaw cracked, Yangyang caught a boot on a flat flagstone, and Suoming’s ferrule took longer and longer to come down. Mei called a halt. Yangyang backed her, and Suoming selected the Third Ferry Rest with the air of someone who’d been looking for it all afternoon. The inn was empty, the beds made, the stove laid. Mei got as far as the rice crock before putting it down and directing supper from a stool. Yangyang cooked congee, garlic mustard greens and eggs. Mei dressed Suoming’s arm again. Suoming finished her bowl, and Mei slid another egg onto her saucer.

In the Vale, Ruoyun reclaimed her cot from Chen’s nephew and got hot millet into Qingxiao. A mortal body could get cold and fall asleep now. Under the eaves Qingxiao found the same weiqi position she’d left at Xuanfang Hold, including her unanswered white stone. She set down another white stone. Nothing answered. Ruoyun took a stool at the threshold and stayed there while she slept.

At a mill-house along the living bough, Iuno wanted to keep walking until Lupa saw her hand shake and Amy sat in the doorway. Lupa lit the stove at last. They ate mushroom noodles, steamed the pocket buns and opened the salted plums. Amy and Iuno sat close by the stove and let themselves say they were frightened for Mei. Cartethyia kept looking north toward Ciaccona and fell asleep in wet boots. Iuno worked the knots open with one hand. Lupa covered everybody and laid the lotus on the south-west sill where she could see it from her mat.

Buling’s shortcut took her and Ciaccona back to the same loquat wall, whether they climbed or went down. The Vale stayed two terraces away. They slept in the twilight half of an unfinished pavilion. Ciaccona opened her lute case and shared the squashed festival rice cake she’d been saving. Then she played a boat-air on the low strings, careful of her splinted left forefinger, and hoped Cartethyia had taken off her boots.

At the inn Suoming shut the noon out of one upstairs room and took the window seat. Mei and Yangyang shared the bed. Mei said she would tell Iuno. Yangyang said good. Later, with Mei asleep, Yangyang told Suoming that Hsin had spoken through the Prism in the Skyworks while everything stood still. There had been a word missing after *Little*, a hollow in the sound like the missing character in her mother’s shell. Hsin had heard her plan to use the two Prisms to separate the living from the Snare’s echoes. Alongside the three vows, she’d asked Yangyang to count herself among the people coming out and ask for a hand when she needed one.

Yangyang asked for second watch. Suoming gave her third. Then Yangyang told her about the small hand, and the breath she’d heard all day, still steady somewhere west of the street. Suoming tightened her grip on Nine Shadows. The promise she’d made Hsin when the plum tree split had yet to be called in. She asked about Mei, too. Yangyang said Mei and Iuno had a daughter, that Iuno knew everything, and that she and Mei would walk by Jinzhou’s bird-bridge when they got back. There was no title for what they had. The love had been said aloud. For now, Mei and Iuno kept their kisses for each other. By the time Yangyang lay down, the giant’s chin had sunk toward her folded arms. In her sleep Mei found Yangyang’s wrist.

## 3. Morning by the body’s clock

Suoming woke Yangyang at the third and curled up on the window seat. She looked twelve when she slept. Mei came to sit against Yangyang on the baggage chest in her stockings. Downstairs there was white tea, breakfast, and news from the Hold. Suoming had heard something about a goose. Mei told her how it stole Cartethyia’s laurel and how Chisa bought it back with a radish, or a turnip, depending on who was telling it. Suoming filled Mei’s empty cup when she stopped for breath.

Beneath the mill, Amy and Chisa found a turning assembly made of the same metal as the brass rosette and the broken hoop. Its teeth skipped when the light dipped in the west. On the cellar step Amy said the dark under the folding square had felt like falling out of the world again, somewhere a shout couldn’t carry. Chisa said she’d had her wrist and would take it again. Upstairs, Cartethyia found her boots dry and helped Iuno pin the hair she couldn’t get up one-handed. Lupa made Iuno a buckwheat pancake the size of a shield and put too much honey on it.

Outside Qingxiao’s room Mingxiao and the others showed Ruoyun their registers. Loads were backing up across four basins, faster since the Sanctum floor dropped. Roads curled back at their margins, seasons leaked through terrace walls, and the north dormitories kept steaming buns for empty beds. Somebody had forced the fourth relief-key at the lotus basin and sent the backed-up pressure across the western hills. With the Core turning again, the Celestials had to tend the registers or the seams would tear from river to ridge. Nobody had seen Hsin walking in her own skin since before Gu came on the rolls. Asked what was in the middle of the Sanctum, Mingxiao bowed and left. Ruoyun pulled the door almost shut and kept her seat.

Buling and Ciaccona found Longjue in the orchard with mud on her boots and a pruning hook on her shoulder. Buling had been on the right stair after all. The third terrace laid its steps down twice when the western light dimmed. Step over the iron drain-cover on the left, Longjue said. Then she led them up to the Vale and told Buling that Ruoyun was back.

## 4. Over the gorge, down to the rain

Nobody in the Vale made much of Buling’s return after a century. A man with a bundle of laths said good morning and kept walking. Ruoyun, on the other hand, told her exactly what she thought of someone who walked off a post without leaving a note on the peg. Qingxiao came out barefoot and hungry, and Ruoyun sent Buling for tallow. Later Ruoyun pinned up Qingxiao’s hair with a bone clasp. Under the pavilion eaves Qingxiao showed Ciaccona weiqi, taking her stones with quiet efficiency, and Ciaccona showed her how to hold and sound the lute. Buling and Ruoyun got the astrolabe running smoothly with tallow, a lever and a talisman. When the western light went grey, the freshly greased ring stopped dead all the same.

The lotus took the five along a bare span of living wood over a gorge. Cartethyia picked a fight about who’d won their brawl in the Ragunna fountain and kept Lupa arguing until her boots hit the far bank. On the way she said low roofs and painted windows still frightened her. If Ciaccona latched the shutters, she could wake without knowing where she was. Lupa told her to leave the hook off next time. She’d build the fire bigger. Behind them Iuno asked Chisa how Amy was managing. Chisa told her to sit down beside Amy and ask. Iuno spoke of seeing the rock fall days before it loosened. Chisa remembered knowing every puddle and every loose roof tile on the same Tuesday, and standing alone waiting for four o’clock. She liked this way better. Iuno brushed Chisa’s sleeve and agreed. On solid rock Amy fed Lupa a piece of leftover pancake and thanked her for stopping at the mill. She kept the lotus. She was the navigator.

Down by the deserted presses, Suoming told Mei about the three-legged bathhouse cat called the Magistrate. When Suoming’s breath caught, Mei sat on a paper bale and claimed her scar pulled, so Suoming would sit too. Suoming told her about the house she’d like to have someday: two rooms, a low south window, grey tiles to hear the rain on, and hotpot brought over from the tavern. Mei offered to bring beans. Yangyang came back from scouting a lock-house. The scale went through the door, and the breath was louder past the sluice. Suoming told the Watcher to lead. At the bridge the white noon ended on a line. On the far side was Mengzhou drizzle. Suoming left her umbrella furled and stood in it with her eyes open. The other two stepped in after her.

## 5. The upper road

Longjue had wedged the terrace latch. Buling found her barefoot in a strip of violet dusk, in her teal robe with a smoke and her red gourd. They’d been sparring partners and short a fourth at mahjong since Buling left. Longjue said she’d looked straight at Buling on the stair and thought only of a hole in a weeding basket. The same tiles had come up in the same order twice, chip and all, and she’d found smokes burning down to her fingers without remembering the flint. Buling’s compass drifted west and slipped back. The scribes scrolled the same sluice figures. Only Ruoyun, who’d been topside, had acted like Buling had ever left.

They set out to see the upper boundary stones on the road to the cistern. Longjue couldn’t remember when the orchard crew had last gone that far. Buling vouched for Ciaccona, fetched the eggs, cracked one on the frost stair, and left the basket and a note on Ruoyun’s peg saying where she’d gone and with whom. Qingxiao let the game keep and told her former disciple to stop dropping her right elbow when she drew a talisman.

The bough led the five through an empty temple fair at autumn evening. Iuno paid for chestnuts and peeled them one-handed for Lupa, who objected until Chisa split the last one with her. Amy timed the shooting-gallery gears by the dips in the western light. Cartethyia hit things hard. Amy told her about needing to see stove-light after the square folded. Cartethyia used to name the corners of her seminary room for streets around Cosimo’s bakery. Next time she’d hold Amy’s sleeve in the dark and tell her which way the bakery was. Amy would break the lock if there was one. They left with a clay goose for Mei, a boxwood tuning pipe for Ciaccona, and a hideous badger for the doorstep. The lotus stayed in Amy’s hand as they went through the rear moon-gate.

The lock-keeper’s ledger was blank. Below the sluice the scale ran just under the waterline, and the three took a paper barge to follow it. Suoming’s breath caught and her bandaged arm couldn’t keep the sweep moving. Yangyang asked her to teach her the figure-eight. Mei slept on the bales while Yangyang steered and Suoming listened to news from the Wardens’ mess. The breath ahead was deeper and even now. Under it, every fourth breath, something hard tapped below the water. The rot only scraped. When Mei woke, Yangyang was still at the sweep and Suoming sat beside her. They’d come out of the rain into pale gold morning mist.

At the Vale cookhouse Qingxiao fried the eggs after Ruoyun burned the millet. Ruoyun said Celestials didn’t need to eat and ate anyway. When Qingxiao asked about the voices at her door, Ruoyun said the Vale was coming loose faster and nobody had seen the Fox in her own skin for longer than Gu had been on the books. Mingxiao still wouldn’t say what was in the Sanctum. Qingxiao gave her another egg and asked for the salt.

West of the Vale the hills stood doubled and the boundary stones were blank. Longjue walked on Buling’s far side until she passed the gourd to Ciaccona. Baijiu, a sparring story at Buling’s expense and a story from Septimont carried them the rest of the way into a drinking song. Longjue called the bard Pipa, sang off-key, and never once put a bare foot wrong. Then the west dimmed. Their echo came back late, half a tone low, from two directions. Ciaccona muted the strings. Buling caught her bell. Longjue held one foot above the slate for a breath, put it down, and called for the next verse. The three went on up toward the cistern house.
