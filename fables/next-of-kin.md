# Next of Kin
### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

臨行密密縫，意恐遲遲歸。
*At the going, stitch laid close on stitch — she fears the return will be slow.*
— Meng Jiao, "Song of the Traveling Son"

---

> **STARTORCH INSTITUTE — PERSONNEL RECORD, EXTENDED DEPLOYMENT (NON-DOMESTIC).**
> **FIELD 9. NEXT OF KIN.**
>
> *One name. The relation. An address at which the named person can be reached inside ninety days. The nominee shall remain outside the deploying party for its full duration. Blanks are disallowed.*
>
> *This field exists so that a letter has somewhere to go.*
>
> *The Institute regrets the necessity of this field.*

---

## I.

The kettle went on first.

Iuno did it in full view, the way she did everything now, so that it would cost her the whole four minutes: water, leaves, the ring, the wait. Mei watched her set it and understood that the length of the telling had already been decided by the length of the boil.

<font color=moccasin>"That long, huh."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Sit down."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'll stand."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Then stand where I can see you."</font>

Mei moved to the end of the counter, set the shell on it, set the letter beside the shell, and folded her arms.

Iuno told it. The east stairwell balcony past midnight. The steam that leaned against a vent drawing the other way. The second cup, poured hot, handle turned outward, guest-side. The voice that came through a door open a finger's width and paid for every word out of a purse going to zero.

She told the part about the audit, too, because that was the whole reason the telling had a table under it. A reading in a basin thirty years ago. Clear sky in every direction. Wind on the water for one hour, leaning east. One line in a ledger. *Weather.*

<font color=royalblue>"I saw her coming and I gave her a category,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"She spent her life crossing to a shore that had already been used, and I am the one who used it."</font>

She finished. The kettle had been off for some time.

Mei looked at the two cups on the counter: the one Iuno had poured for her, and the one Iuno had poured out of habit and set at the far edge with the handle out, before she noticed her own hand doing it.

<font color=moccasin>"Did she drink it?"</font>

Iuno's chin came up.

<font color=moccasin>"You put a cup out. Did she take it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The steam went round at the end. The way it does over a cup somebody's using."</font>

Mei nodded once. Her shoulders came down half an inch. Iuno filed the half-inch and would be thinking about it for years.

<font color=moccasin>"Say the name again."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Ovathrax."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"And the city."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Jinzhou. The youngest gate of Huanglong. It sits in a bowl of water and mountain with a dam above it, a garrison in it, and a Magistrate who is barely older than your daughter and has been dreaming her city burning for a season."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You looked it up."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The archive shuts at two. I was generally there by one."</font>

Mei picked up the shell and put it down again.

Then she went to the door for her jacket, and stopped.

The boots stood against the frame. The good pair, with the reinforced counter, the ones Iuno had resoled twice rather than replace. Cleaned. Laced to the second eyelet from the top, which was how she laced boots she meant to put on fast.

Every other surface in that room had three weeks of dust on it.

<font color=moccasin>"When."</font>

Iuno considered a version of the answer. Mei watched her consider it. The considering took a second and a half and was the most expensive second and a half of Iuno's month.

<font color=royalblue>"The night she came."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Before the kite cleared the passage."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

Mei stood at the door with her jacket over her arm.

<font color=moccasin>"So you decided you were coming before you told me I was going."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Say it properly. I'd like it in the room."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You built me a room and then invited me into it and called that a choice."</font>

The vent cycled. Somewhere three floors down, a door closed on the tram side of the building and someone laughed at something.

Iuno set her palm flat on the counter, which she did when she wanted to be somewhere specific.

<font color=royalblue>"She asked me for you,"</font> she said. <font color=royalblue>"I told her three weeks. I told her you had started sleeping through the night on Tuesday. She dropped fifteen degrees out of my balcony and skinned the tea with frost, and then she put it away, and spent nearly the last of what she had rewriting her own sentence."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"To what."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"*Her to choose.*"</font> A pause. <font color=royalblue>"A dead woman with less than a sentence left in the world audited her own wanting in front of me on a stairwell, at full price, at two in the morning. I came inside and looked at my boots for a while and then I put them by the door, and I have been standing next to that hypocrisy every day since, brushing my teeth."</font>

Mei laughed. One breath, at the doorframe, with her head down.

<font color=moccasin>"We just got everybody fixed."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Lupa's shoulder is eleven days out of a two-hour surgery. Cia's got ninety minutes a day and she's stealing forty of them in the practice room and she thinks nobody's counting. Carte's got a stick with a finch on it and a physio appointment. Amy sleeps in the middle of a bed that's too small for three and complains about it every single morning, and I have started *looking forward* to the complaining."</font> Her hand went to the shell and turned it. <font color=moccasin>"We carved this out of a hole in the ground with our hands."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"We built a house out of people who cannot be left behind,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"That was the specification. You signed off on it in a corridor with a spare pillow."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...Yeah."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"They'll follow you. All of them, inside a minute, and Lupa inside four seconds, and then she'll be angry about how fast it was."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I know."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That's the part to look at. The god is the cheap half of this."</font>

*Ask her,* the High Priestess said. *You have been at that counter eleven minutes hoping she'll save you the sentence.*

*I'm getting to it.*

*You are getting to it at the pace of a woman who has never once been late for anything in her life.*

Iuno picked up the letter and held it out flat on her palm, the way you present a thing for a decision.

<font color=royalblue>"Here or somewhere with sky?"</font>

Mei looked at it for a while.

<font color=moccasin>"Sky."</font>

---

She read it twice on the rooftop with her daughter watching and her hands perfectly steady, which was how you knew.

---

## II.

The forms came down from the Institute on the Monday, seven of them, in a buff envelope with a compliments slip somebody had typed personally.

Iuno read Field 9 once for content and once for the small type underneath, and then sat at the kitchen table for eleven minutes with her hand over her mouth.

Then she made tea, and set the forms out, and got everyone up.

<font color=skyblue>"What's box nine?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Next of kin."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Oh, easy."</font> Cartethyia wrote. She wrote fast, in the enormous round hand of somebody who had learned letters late and loved them. <font color=skyblue>"Done. What's ten?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Read the line under nine."</font>

Cartethyia read the line under nine.

<font color=teal>"Out loud, sweetheart."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"'The nominee shall remain outside the deploying party for its full duration.'"</font> She looked up. <font color=skyblue>"That's a stupid rule."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It's the only line on that page written by someone with sense."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It means I can't put Cia."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It means the letter has to arrive somewhere."</font>

The kitchen went quiet in the specific way it went quiet when Iuno stopped being warm and started being accurate.

Lynae was at the counter with her arms crossed and her weight on one foot. She stayed there through the next thirty seconds while every person at that table did the arithmetic in their own private order and arrived at the same door.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Right,"</font> she said. <font color=palegoldenrod>"So you're all writing me down."</font>

<font color=indianred>"You could sound less pleased about it."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm *first*. That's a separate thing from pleased and it's most of my personality."</font> She pulled a chair around backward and sat on it. <font color=palegoldenrod>"I want it said in the room, though. With the lights on. Because what that box means is that some morning in nine weeks a woman with a formal face is going to come up my stairs, and I'd like to have agreed to it here, at a table, over a pot of tea, instead of finding out on the landing."</font>

<font color=teal>"Lynae."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm pricing. Let me price."</font> She looked at the forms. <font color=palegoldenrod>"Seven of you. That's seven mornings I've now signed for, and the invoice clears when all seven of you come back through that door and take them off me."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Put me down too."</font>

Rebecca had been at the counter eating somebody's leftover pastry, and had stopped.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You don't know what you're—"</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"I've had it twice."</font> She said it flat and quick, chewing. <font color=greenyellow>"I know exactly what the door sounds like. It's two knocks and then a pause that's too long, because they're deciding whether to do the third one."</font>

The kitchen absorbed that. Cartethyia's pen had stopped.

<font color=greenyellow>"So split it,"</font> Rebecca said. <font color=greenyellow>"Four and three. She takes the ones she can stand and I take the rest, and neither of us has to do seven."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"...Okay."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"And I want the shift where somebody's actually coming back, so put the odds on my side."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"That's not how—"</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"I *know* it's not how it works, I want it written down anyway."</font>

Iuno wrote it down.

---

Amy had the pen in her hand and had written two letters of a name and stopped.

Iuno saw the two letters. Everybody at that table saw Iuno see them.

<font color=royalblue>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I know."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I'll say it, if you'd rather it came from me."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I've got it."</font> Amy put the pen down. She looked at the ceiling for a moment, and her jaw did something she had inherited without ever being taught. <font color=mistyrose>"Everybody I've got is on the bus."</font>

Mei, at the end of the table, went very still.

<font color=mistyrose>"It's fine. It's a *good* problem. Ten years ago the honest answer to box nine was a filing cabinet."</font> Amy picked the pen back up and rolled it between two fingers. <font color=mistyrose>"Rebecca."</font>

Rebecca's eyebrows went up. <font color=greenyellow>"Me?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You draw people. If it goes wrong I want to go on the wall."</font>

Rebecca opened her mouth. Closed it. Dragged her hand down her face and left it over her chin.

<font color=greenyellow>"...You get the good marker,"</font> she said.

<font color=mistyrose>"I want the green."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Everybody wants the green."</font>

---

Lupa's form came back with box nine empty.

Iuno set it on the top of the stack and squared the stack, and Lupa, from the doorway, said, <font color=indianred>"Say it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Blanks are disallowed."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Mm."</font> Lupa's tail moved once against the frame. <font color=indianred>"I wrote that part."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You wrote it for a charter in Septimont, about a different form, for people who had somebody."</font>

<font color=indianred>"I wrote it because blanks cost more."</font> She came in and put both hands on the back of a chair. <font color=indianred>"Give me till Thursday."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The stack goes in Wednesday evening. I can hold one page back to the morning."</font>

Chisa, who had spent the hour listening, folded her own form in half, and then in half again, and put it into her coat.

<font color=coral>"I'll bring mine back Thursday too,"</font> she said.

---

## III.

The stand was in the Third Ward under a run of vent-pipe that kept the whole alley eight degrees warmer than the street, which was why it had customers, and it had been serving the same four things for nine years.

Mei got there early and stood across the way, which she was bad at. She lasted a minute and forty seconds.

The woman at the end of the counter had a sheet of paper weighted flat with a chopstick rest and was copying.

Two bowls in front of her. One eaten. One steaming, untouched, set at the place to her left.

Mei sat down at her right.

*"Kuchiba Chiyo,"* said the woman, before she looked up. Clear, level, pitched for the middle distance — the voice of somebody who has spent a lifetime saying her own name into a room before entering it, so that a girl who cannot hold faces would always know who had come in.

Then she looked up. And the sentence had nowhere to go, and she put it down without any fuss at all, the way you set down a cup you have discovered is not yours.

*"Ah."*

<font color=moccasin>"Mei."</font>

*"I know."* Chiyo turned her sheet a quarter-turn on the counter, which was courtesy, and let Mei read it. *"You walk heavy at the heel."*

The sheet had seven names on it. Eleven times each, in an alphabet that was not her own, in a hand that had started the morning careful and had stayed careful all the way to the bottom of the page.

*Iuno. Lupa. Ciaccona. Cartethyia. Lynae. Mei. Amy.*

Mei looked at her own name eleven times.

<font color=moccasin>"There's eight of us now."</font>

*"She hasn't said the eighth one twice yet."* Chiyo picked up her chopsticks. *"I put them down when they come up twice. The first time could be anybody."*

<font color=moccasin>"Rebecca."</font>

*"Mm. She said it once, in November, with a face on."* Chiyo ate. *"I've been waiting."*

The stand's owner came past without being waved at and set a third bowl down in front of Mei, and went away again. He had been counting for a year, and had arrived at three some weeks before anybody told him to.

Mei looked at it.

<font color=moccasin>"I didn't order."</font>

*"He has a system. Don't interfere with it, he's very proud."*

They ate. The vent-pipe ticked overhead as something upstream of it changed shift.

<font color=moccasin>"You know why I'm here."</font>

*"You want a name for a box."*

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

*"Kuchiba Chiyo. Third floor, east wing, apartment eleven. I'll write it out for you afterward, because your people spell it wrong and then apologize, and I've decided I prefer the writing to the apology."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Thank you."</font>

*"That was the easy part of your errand."* Chiyo set her chopsticks across the rim of her bowl and turned on the stool to face her properly, which took some doing at sixty-three on a fixed seat, and which she did anyway. *"Ask the rest."*

Mei put both hands around the bowl. Warm ceramic. Steam on her knuckles.

<font color=moccasin>"I'm taking your daughter into a war."</font>

*"No."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Ma'am—"</font>

*"You're going to a war. She is going with you. Those are two different sentences and the difference is the only thing I have left that anybody can give me."* Chiyo's hands folded in her lap. The small precise sound of knuckles settling against knuckles. *"I have signed a piece of paper about my daughter once already. There was a line printed on it, under the signature, and the line said the signature was a formality. A man put a clasp on her throat in the same room and did the buckle with his thumb, like this."* She showed it. One motion, seated, unhurried, thirty years old and perfectly preserved.

Mei put her hands in her lap.

*"And then you came to my table."* Chiyo picked up her chopsticks again. *"That is the difference between you and him. It is the entire difference, and it is worth about as much as anything gets to be worth at my age, and I would like you to stop arranging your face."*

Mei arranged her face.

*"Better."*

They ate for a while. Somebody two stalls down was arguing pleasantly about the price of onions.

<font color=moccasin>"Can I ask about the second bowl?"</font>

*"It's Thursday."*

<font color=moccasin>"Right."</font>

*"She comes most Thursdays. When she comes it's hot and when she doesn't it goes cold, and I pay for it either way, and I have heard four different people be very moved about this and every one of them got it wrong."* Chiyo ate. *"It's a seat. A person needs somewhere she is expected. Expecting that starts when she walks in is a thing called luck, and luck charges differently."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Huh."</font>

*"You have a daughter."*

<font color=moccasin>"I do."</font>

*"Where's her seat?"*

Mei's chopsticks stopped an inch above the bowl.

<font color=moccasin>"Middle of the bed,"</font> she said. <font color=moccasin>"She claimed it. She announced the claim. There were terms."</font>

Chiyo made a sound at the back of her throat that was approximately a laugh and mostly an assessment.

*"Good. Keep it warm while you're away. Have the tall one sleep in it."*

<font color=moccasin>"...The tall one has opinions about the bed."</font>

*"The tall one will do it anyway."*

---

There was a man at the far end of the alley, well-dressed for the ward, standing at the tram board.

Mei clocked him at about the same moment she noticed Chiyo's left hand come off the counter and settle flat on the cloth-wrapped box beside her — a box roughly the size of a folded coat, tied in four places, that had been at her elbow the entire meal and that Mei had been keeping at the edge of her vision out of manners.

The hand stayed on it. The face stayed exactly where it was. The chopsticks kept working.

<font color=moccasin>"You want me to—"</font>

*"Eat your noodles."*

<font color=moccasin>"He's been there eleven minutes."</font>

*"Forty, last time."* Chiyo drank her broth. *"They stand very still and they read the board and they let four trams go. You learn the shape of it in a city where the lists go up on Tuesdays."*

<font color=moccasin>"Does Chisa know?"</font>

*"My daughter cannot see faces and can hear a man's shoes from a street away. She knows better than either of us."* The hand came off the box. *"Ask your priestess what she thinks it is. Then ask her what she thinks it wants. Those are two more different sentences."*

Mei turned around.

The tram board was showing an eastbound. The man had gone, in the deliberate wrong direction, and had been gone for some time.

---

Chisa arrived at eleven minutes past, and stopped four paces out, because Mei was in her chair.

<font color=coral>"Ma."</font>

*"Sit down. There's a third bowl, he counted."*

<font color=coral>"I can see it."</font>

*"You can hear it. Sit down."*

Chisa sat down. Mei got up and moved a seat over and had the extremely specific experience of being the wrong person at a table for approximately four seconds and then being folded in.

Chisa put her folded form on the counter and kept her hand flat on top of it.

<font color=coral>"There's a box."</font>

*"I've been told about the box."*

<font color=coral>"Lupa."</font>

*"Lupa,"* Chiyo agreed, without heat. *"She is under the impression that she is subtle. It's very restful. Give me the pen."*

Chisa unfolded the form. Chiyo read it — the whole page, top to bottom, at the speed of a woman who has been burned by fine print — and then wrote her own name in the box in a careful borrowed alphabet, and her address, and the relation.

She wrote the relation last, and took her time over it, and what she wrote was *mother,* and the whole word came out the same weight all the way through.

Then she put the pen down and turned to Mei.

*"Now. About the letter."*

<font color=moccasin>"Ma'am?"</font>

*"If it goes wrong, somebody writes to me. I'd like to say what's in it."*

<font color=coral>"Ma—"</font>

*"I have had one of these letters before, from an office, about a different thing, and it was four lines and two of them were about entitlements."* Chiyo's hands went back into her lap. *"I have spent twenty years in a sealed city writing a better one in my head. It's finished. Get out your pen."*

Mei got out her pen.

Chisa sat with her hands flat on the counter and her chin at the level everybody in a collar learns, and let her mother speak.

*"Write the day,"* said Chiyo. *"Write the hour, if you have it, and if you don't have it, write that you don't. Write what she was doing with her hands."*

Mei wrote.

*"Write the name of whoever was holding her. Spell it out for me. I will look it up, and I would like to get it right, because I intend to say it out loud for a long time."*

Mei wrote.

*"Sign it yourself, in your own hand, and put it into an envelope you have touched, and carry it to the post yourself."*

The pen stopped.

*"And then come to this table on a Thursday,"* Chiyo said, *"and tell me before the paper does."*

---

## IV.

Cartethyia sewed at the long table in the common room, past two in the morning, under the one lamp that had a decent bulb.

Ciaccona came down at twenty past with her lute and got as far as the doorway.

<font color=skyblue>"You're playing."</font>

<font color=teal>"You're up."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You're at three hours."</font>

<font color=teal>"You've been counting from the wrong end. I stopped at ninety and started again at midnight, which is a new day, which is a new ninety."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"That's the worst thing I have ever heard a person say out loud."</font>

<font color=teal>"It's arithmetic."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It's *fraud*, Cia."</font>

Ciaccona came in and put the lute against the wall and sat down, which was a concession, and looked at the table, which was a mistake.

There were six of them finished and standing in a row against the fruit bowl. Nine inches each. Felt bodies, yarn hair, buttons where buttons were needed. Grey undershirt and a tail. Blue coat, and one arm made from a chipped bead off a hobby stall on the eleventh terrace. Teal, with a lute the size of a thumb and a brace on the left hand stitched in white thread, which took a moment to find and then stayed found. A red string at the wrist and scissors built out of two straight pins bound with silk. A long coat and two gold seed beads for eyes. And a school jacket in the wrong blue with four letters of acid green on the shoulder.

The seventh sat in Cartethyia's lap with its face half-done.

<font color=teal>"Carte."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"They're just dolls."</font>

<font color=teal>"You made yourself last."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I know how I look. It's a *time management* thing."</font>

Ciaccona picked up the one with the crystal bead arm and turned it over. The stitching along the inside seam was minute and even and absolutely relentless, hundreds of passes, the work of somebody who had done this in the dark for twenty years, by feel, for an audience of dolls.

<font color=teal>"Who are they for?"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"The ones staying."</font> Cartethyia bit off a thread. <font color=skyblue>"Lynae gets four and Rebecca gets three, they've split it. So the ones they're waiting for should be somewhere in the house. Something with hands."</font>

<font color=teal>"..."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Don't do the face."</font>

<font color=teal>"I'm not doing a face."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You're doing the face where you're going to write about it."</font>

<font color=teal>"I'm doing the face where I'm going to write about it *well*."</font>

Cartethyia snorted and knotted off and set the seventh down, and reached into the basket at her feet, and brought out an eighth.

Ciaccona looked at it for a long moment.

It was a girl in a green coat with a hat, and on the brim of the hat there was a bird, stitched in three colors, about the size of a grain of rice.

<font color=teal>"...Who's that."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I don't know yet."</font>

<font color=teal>"Carte."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"The letter says a hat. It says her friends picked it. It says there's a bird on it."</font> Cartethyia turned it in the lamplight and adjusted the brim with her thumbnail. <font color=skyblue>"Somebody wrote to us. That's a whole person, sitting in a window with the shutters open on a windy night, and she's got a sister and a lake and a hat, and she's been waiting on a cliff at dawn for somebody she's never met."</font>

<font color=teal>"So you made her."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"So she's in the house before we get there."</font> A shrug, one shoulder. <font color=skyblue>"That's how it works. You make the thing and you say the name and then the dark has to go round it. It's a *procedure*, Cia. I ran it for twenty years and I came out with my own name on me."</font>

Ciaccona put her hand over her mouth and left it there for a while.

There was a ninth in the basket. Paper-pale cloth, cut and pinned and left there, the edges a fraction out of true where the pins had wandered. Blank at the head, blank at the front, waiting on somebody to be.

<font color=teal>"And that one?"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Can't finish it."</font>

<font color=teal>"Why."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Haven't got the name."</font> Cartethyia dropped it back in the basket with great care, the way you put down something sleeping. <font color=skyblue>"You need a whole name to sew."</font>

---

They did the forms after that, because they were both awake and the alternative was lying upstairs in the dark being awake there.

<font color=teal>"Cantarella,"</font> Ciaccona said, writing.

<font color=skyblue>"That's *mine.*"</font>

<font color=teal>"It's a name. There's enough of her to go round."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"She held my wrist across an entire sea, Cia, she gets to be *mine*—"</font>

<font color=teal>"She held your wrist across an entire sea in a boat I was rowing."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You were not rowing."</font>

<font color=teal>"I was morally rowing."</font>

They settled it the way they settled things, which took eleven minutes and involved a pastry, and at the end of it Cartethyia had written *Cantarella Fisalia, Ragunna* in her enormous round hand and Ciaccona had written something else entirely.

<font color=skyblue>"Who?"</font>

<font color=teal>"Hiyuki."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"...Oh."</font>

<font color=teal>"She's good at it."</font> Ciaccona blew on the ink, which was a habit from an older kind of paper. <font color=teal>"She's been good at it for a hundred years. She's got the bell and the shrine's got no lock on it and she rings it once for each and she doesn't hurry and she says the names *right.*"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"...Put me down for her too."</font>

<font color=teal>"You've got Cantarella."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You can have two."</font>

<font color=teal>"You cannot have two, it's a *box*—"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I'll write small."</font>

---

## V.

Amy came in with a piece of paper and shut the door with her heel, which was a tell, and Mei knew it was a tell because she had taught it to her by having it.

<font color=mistyrose>"Sit down."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'm sitting."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...We did rules."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"We did *mine.*"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"One. When you come back from something, you say a number, and it's a different number every time, so the thing that comes back can't fake being the thing that went."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Amy—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Two. Somebody's got a hand on you. I've got first refusal on the shift and everybody else rotates, and before you say it, yes, that's for me. I'm allowed. Iuno set the precedent, Lupa signed it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"In a battle line, kid, hands get busy, and—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Then it's a voice."</font> Amy kept her eyes on the page. <font color=mistyrose>"Chisa's frequency, or a channel, or Cia's four notes, or Lupa yelling. Something in your ear that's one of us. Rule two is a *hand or a voice.* I already redrafted it. That's the redraft."</font>

Mei sat back.

<font color=moccasin>"Okay."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Three. Chisa listens. Four. There's a clock and it's mine and the number is eleven, because I'm stealing Iuno's, because it's a *good* number and she's not using it while she's in the field."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"She'll be delighted."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"She's already delighted. She proofread it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...Of course she did."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Five."</font> Amy reached into her jacket and put something on the blanket between them.

Nine inches of felt. A school jacket in the wrong blue, sleeves run long, cut from memory. A radio the size of a thumbnail sewn into one hand. And on the shoulder, in four letters of acid green thread so small you had to carry it to the lamp: NYX.

Mei looked at it for a while.

<font color=moccasin>"Where'd she get the thread?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Carte's been sewing since the muster. She's done all of us. Don't go down there at night, it's like a shrine, it's *deeply* upsetting."</font> Amy's voice stayed level. Her hands worked at the cuff of her sleeve the whole time. <font color=mistyrose>"Rule five. She goes in your hands. Before anything, you fold your fingers. Then they're closed, and whoever's got the shift doesn't have to look at them open."</font>

The room was very quiet. Down the hall somebody ran water.

<font color=moccasin>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Say it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You watched them do this for you."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"For eleven weeks. At the foot of a bed. With a doll in her lap that did nothing at all,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"And I've had three weeks to work out where I picked up the habit that put her there."</font>

Mei picked up the doll. Nine inches of felt discovered, in her hand, exactly how much a thing that size can be made to carry.

<font color=moccasin>"Six?"</font>

Amy turned the page around so Mei could see it. Six was underlined twice.

*6. YOU DO IT IN FRONT OF US.*

<font color=moccasin>"...That's mine."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I know it's yours. I'm giving it back."</font> Amy folded her arms. <font color=mistyrose>"Because you made it a rule for the house and then you exempted yourself in your own head about four seconds later, and I watched you do that, and I know the exact face."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Kid—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You do the dishes."</font>

Mei stopped.

<font color=moccasin>"What?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Before you go somewhere you're not planning on coming back from, you do the dishes."</font> Amy's chin was up and her voice had gone flat in the way that meant she had rehearsed this in a corridor a number of times. <font color=mistyrose>"All of them. Including the ones in the drainer that were already clean. And you fold the blanket on the couch with the seam turned in, and you put the shoes side by side under the bench with the toes level."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...I don't do that."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You did it the morning you left the cabin. I came down at six and the kitchen was *perfect.*"</font> A breath. <font color=mistyrose>"I was nine. I thought it meant we were having company."</font>

Mei's hand was flat on the blanket and had stopped being a hand she was operating.

<font color=mistyrose>"So I told the house,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"Three weeks ago. Everybody's got it. Lupa's got Tuesdays."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Tuesdays for *what*—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"The sink."</font>

There was a long pause, and then Mei laughed, and it came out of her wrong and wet at the edges, and Amy sat down on the bed hard, the way she used to at nine, sideways, elbow first, and leaned into her mother's shoulder without any ceremony at all.

<font color=moccasin>"That's the most horrifying thing anybody has ever done to me."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Horrifying and practical."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...Yeah."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's the whole family. Rebecca's been saying it for months."</font>

They sat.

<font color=moccasin>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'm going to be afraid the entire time."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Good."</font> Amy stayed exactly where she was, against her mother's shoulder, with her eyes shut. <font color=mistyrose>"Be afraid. Bring me anyway."</font>

Lupa found Rebecca on the north wall at two, where Rebecca did her thinking, which she called tagging.

<font color=indianred>"Eleven months."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Huh?"</font>

Lupa held out an envelope. Sealed with wax, badly, the way somebody seals a thing when they have only seen it done.

<font color=indianred>"If we're not back in eleven months, you post it. If we're back, you give it here and you don't ask."</font>

Rebecca put the cap on the marker with her teeth and took it. Turned it over. There was an address on the front in Lupa's blocky hand — a street, a town, a country, and the top line left clear.

<font color=greenyellow>"Who's it to?"</font>

<font color=indianred>"Don't know."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"...You don't know."</font>

<font color=indianred>"There was a woman at the village edge, two summers, before dawn. Waybread, bandages, dried fish, a note about where the Discords were. Under a stone, always the same stone."</font> Lupa's tail moved once. <font color=indianred>"Three sightings. Every one of them agrees she was leaving."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"And what's inside?"</font>

<font color=indianred>"A thank-you."</font>

<font color=indianred>"And the note about the gullies. Mostly the note."</font>

## VI.

They did it at the kitchen table with the good lamp on, at one in the morning, with the letter flat between them and a sheet of blank paper beside it.

<font color=royalblue>"Read me the third paragraph."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You've read it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I've held it."</font>

Mei looked up.

Iuno turned the letter a quarter-turn on the table, toward herself, and looked at it with the flat professional attention of a woman who had governed a city-state in three scripts for thirty years, and then turned it back.

<font color=royalblue>"I have been carrying that page around inside my coat since the roof, and I have looked at it perhaps forty times, and it is a beautiful piece of setting, and I cannot read one character of it."</font>

The vent cycled.

<font color=moccasin>"...It's just words."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Read me the third paragraph."</font>

Mei read it. She read it easily, the way you read a sign you have passed every day, her thumb moving down the column without her looking at her thumb, and when she got to the end she stopped, and the stop landed where the sentence ended, which her mouth had known a beat before she did.

Iuno wrote it down as sound. Then she pushed the blank sheet across.

<font color=royalblue>"Now write me the word for *lake.*"</font>

Mei picked up the pen.

She put the nib down and drew one stroke, horizontal, left to right, clean.

Then her hand sat there.

She looked at it for a while, the way you look at a limb that has gone to sleep — with a kind of polite curiosity, waiting for it to be your own again. Then she set the pen down beside the mark.

<font color=moccasin>"Huh."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Do it again."</font>

They did it four more times, with four different words, and got four single strokes, each one first and correct and alone.

<font color=royalblue>"The crossing keeps what's made of sound,"</font> Iuno said, at last. <font color=royalblue>"That's the Row's own phrase, out of Mengzhou, about the shell. A tide is already a kind of wind. The sound comes through and the rest gets left at the gate."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's a shell."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That's a *rule*. And you fell out of the Etheric Sea onto a beach with nothing on you, and you speak a language that arrived with you and has no teacher anywhere on record, and your hand stops at the first stroke."</font>

Mei sat back and looked at the ceiling.

<font color=moccasin>"Say the rest."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"On the roof, after Amy came apart, you sang."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...You were on the stairs."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Third landing. Two flights down and out of the light, with a cold pot in my hands, for the whole of it."</font> Iuno reached into her coat and took out a folded paper that had been folded a long time and put it on the table. <font color=royalblue>"I wrote down what I heard. Phonetically, in my own script, badly, at four in the morning, and I have had it on me for three weeks and told nobody, and that is the second thing on tonight's list that I am confessing to."</font>

Mei opened it. Rows of syllables in Iuno's small precise hand, with stress marks over them.

<font color=moccasin>"I don't know where I got that song."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I know."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It just — my mouth had it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I know."</font> Iuno turned her cup. <font color=royalblue>"Ciaccona can't place the mode. <font color=moccasin>"So the war's your second reason."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"My reason for going is you."</font> Iuno set the cup down. <font color=royalblue>"My reason for *packing three weeks early* is a page I cannot read, in a city where several thousand people can. I'd like it noted that I have now disclosed both."</font>

Mei was quiet for a long time.

<font color=moccasin>"I leave,"</font> she said.

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It's the one fact anybody's ever been able to establish about me. It's in every file. I leave, and then I do something enormous, and then somebody has to go and get me."</font> She turned the folded paper over. <font color=moccasin>"So we go there, and somebody reads this, and it turns out there's a house. And a room. Kept."</font>

Iuno waited.

<font color=moccasin>"Chisa's mother kept a room twenty years,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"I sat at a counter this Thursday with a woman who taught herself a whole alphabet so that if a list ever went up she could read our names on it. <font color=royalblue>"And you'd like the stone left where it is."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'd rather find out with somebody standing next to me."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Then that's the arrangement."</font>

The lamp buzzed once and settled.

<font color=royalblue>"My mother left me one sentence,"</font> Iuno said.

Mei looked up.

<font color=royalblue>"That's the whole estate. The inside of a temple. A set of robes cut for a larger girl. And one sentence, which I have carried thirty years at full weight, the way you carry a stone somebody hands you on the way in."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Say it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"*A Priestess must never fall in love.*"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"How's that going?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Catastrophically. For thirty years."</font> Iuno picked up her cup. <font color=royalblue>"I intend to keep it up."</font>

*Tell her,* the High Priestess said.

*Not tonight.*

*Darling. There is a drawer, and it has a thing in it, and the thing has a date on it now, and you are sitting across from her doing arithmetic.*

*Soon.*

## VII.

She did her own form last, alone, at the table, at four.

Field 9. She wrote *Mei* and drew a line through it, because Mei was deploying. She wrote *Amy* and drew a line through it, for the same reason, and sat for a while with the pen on the paper making a small blot.

*Chiyo,* the High Priestess offered. *She'd take you. She's taking strays.*

*She has one letter coming already. Let her carry the one.*

*Lynae, then. Rebecca. There's a whole rota.*

*They're carrying seven.*

*So be the eighth. It is a box, darling. Boxes take ink and go in drawers.*

Iuno turned the pen over twice.

Then she wrote a name, and the relation, and the address, and every one of the three was a problem.

NAME: **Lilith.**

RELATION: *correspondent.*

ADDRESS: *Penrose-512. Last recorded heading: the Etheric Sea. Correspondence is received. A reply has been had.*

*The registry will reject that,* said the High Priestess.

*I'm the registry.*

*Ah.*

*It is the single convenient feature of the entire arrangement, and I have been waiting nine days to use it.*

She blotted it and moved down the page.

Field 12 read: **IN THE EVENT OF NON-RETURN, PERSONAL EFFECTS TO BE RELEASED TO —** and the space beneath it was clean all the way across, because whoever wrote this form had understood that possessions and grief travel by different roads.

Iuno wrote Mei's name in it without pausing, and then sat back and looked at the top drawer of the dresser across the room, where there was a small box she had made herself out of an offcut of the same crystal, and a slip of paper inside it with a date, which she had revised twice, both times earlier.

*Before,* she said, to the room.

*You said before.*

*I'm saying it again. It holds better the second time.*

## VIII.

Mei filed last, on the Wednesday, at the kitchen table, with six forms already stacked and squared under Iuno's hand.

She went down the page quickly, because most of it was the kind of question she found funny, and put *rope* in the occupation box, which Iuno left in.

Then Field 9.

She sat with it.

<font color=moccasin>"Everybody I've got—"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I know."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Lynae's got four. Rebecca's got three."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"They can carry eight."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

And then Mei's hand went down and wrote, in the box, in one unbroken movement at the speed of a signature, three characters.

They were in the hand of the letter.

They were beautifully formed. The strokes went in the right order, with the right weights, the way a hand writes something it has written ten thousand times and stopped seeing. The last stroke had the small trailing hook of somebody in a hurry who had learned to write in a hurry from somebody else in a hurry.

Mei looked at it.

She turned her hand over and looked at that too.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"What did I write?"</font>

Iuno came around the table and stood behind her chair, which was as much beside her as the room's geometry allowed, and looked at the box for a long time.

<font color=royalblue>"I've no idea."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Is it a name?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It has the shape of a name. Three characters, the first one set apart and the other two running together, and in every naming system I have ever studied that means a family and then a person inside it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Somebody in that city reads this hand."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Several thousand of them, at a conservative estimate."</font>

Mei put her palm flat over the box, and then took it away, and looked again.

<font color=moccasin>"Then I've got two reasons,"</font> she said.

Iuno photographed the page. She filed the original in the buff envelope with the other six, and folded the photograph into the lining of her coat, on the left, against the ribs, next to a folded sheet of syllables in her own small hand.
