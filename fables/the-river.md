# The River
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

---

## I.

The tournament began with Cartethyia putting Cantarella's purse on the table and Ciaccona taking it away before the drawstrings touched wood.

"We discussed stakes," Ciaccona said, holding the purse behind her back.

"I listened to the whole discussion. The chips represent money, so using money would save us a step."

"These little circles enjoy a full and independent life of their own."

Cartethyia leaned around her. "I brought small coins. We could set a modest limit."

"You once spent the winter lamp allowance on a mechanical swan because the vendor said it looked lonely. Cantarella entrusted me with emergency discretion, and this qualifies."

"The swan laid sweets. Everybody liked her."

"Six pieces of candy emerged before the grinding began."

Iuno reached across Cartethyia, took the purse from Ciaccona, and put it in the locked drawer beside the old maps.

"Tonight has one currency," she said. "Everyone receives two hundred chips. The winner receives the satisfaction of victory, along with whatever object Rebecca is making under the table."

Rebecca's hands stopped.

Eight people looked down.

She brought up a jagged crown cut from the oatmeal box, gold side outward, with **CABIN CHAMPION** written across the front in acid-green marker.

"I found scissors," she said. "This house rewards initiative."

Chisa looked at the crown, then at the scissors in Rebecca's hand.

"Those are mine."

"Your bag was very helpful."

"That sentence will shorten your life in several households."

Rebecca handed them back handle-first.

It had rained ice since lunch. The lake beyond the window had disappeared behind a grey rattle, and the cabin had drawn everyone inward by degrees: wet coats over chair backs, boots in a collapsing row by the stove, three pots going because Iuno believed every beverage required its own water. The big table still carried faint dents from the tabletop game. Amy had fitted the green cape drawing with a paper speech bubble that said **FOLD, COWARD**, and Mei had laughed so hard at it that everyone understood at once how her evening would go.

Lynae opened the card case.

The deck crossed her knuckles, divided into four clean streams, climbed one wrist, and came together in her palm with a soft, expensive whisper.

Amy sat forward. "Please teach me that tomorrow."

"I can teach you tonight, once the responsible adults become distracted."

"Put the queen back," Chisa said.

Lynae looked at her empty fingers.

Chisa held out her hand.

The queen of hearts appeared from beneath Lynae's cuff and landed in her palm.

"That was a table-security demonstration," Lynae said. "Your response time was excellent."

Iuno took the deck from her. "You may deal when your hands have accepted civilian government."

"My hands dispute its legitimacy."

"They can file an appeal after breakfast."

They drew seats. Lupa got the chair with its back to the wall through luck, divine judgment, or a private arrangement with the slips of paper. Chisa sat on her left. Ciaccona took the next chair and settled as though the table had paid admission to see her. Cartethyia sat beside her with the hand-ranking card Iuno had written in block letters. Rebecca straddled one corner, already shuffling her chips by sound. Amy counted hers twice, found two hundred both times, and looked faintly disappointed that arithmetic was still waiting for its opening move. Lynae lounged at the far end under formal observation. Iuno sat beside her.

Mei drew the last seat, on Iuno's right, directly in front of the couch.

"I used to play this with Amy," she said. "I know what I am doing."

Amy arranged her stacks by color. "You used crackers, and I was nine."

"You were a very advanced nine."

"You called every hand because you wanted to see the ending."

Mei looked around the table for support and found Cartethyia reading the same warning in her own soul.

"Stories deserve endings," Cartethyia said.

Ciaccona closed her eyes. "This is going to hurt me personally."

Iuno put the dealer button in front of Lupa.

"Texas Hold'em. Blinds begin at five and ten. They rise every twenty minutes. Resonance abilities stay out of the deck, Rebecca's optics stay at ordinary magnification, and Lynae keeps fifty-two cards in public circulation."

Lynae lifted one finger. "A skilled dealer can improve circulation by temporarily narrowing it."

"Every missing card costs ten chips."

"Your government has become punitive."

Chisa touched the red cord at her collar. "My hearing stays as it is. I can describe what I hear if that makes the field feel fairer."

Rebecca glanced at Lupa. "Can she hear a bluff?"

"She can hear you thinking about a bluff from the porch," Lupa said. "Your whole body starts an argument."

"Then everybody should make noise."

Ciaccona reached for the lute.

Eight voices stopped her.

She raised both hands. "This table fears the arts. Deal."

---

## II.

The first three hands established several facts.

Cartethyia needed to consult the ranking card whenever she held more than one pair, which happened twice. Amy could calculate the pot before the chips stopped moving and still took pleasure in recounting it. Rebecca folded a hand, received such an astonished cheer that she stood up and demanded everyone explain themselves. Lynae paid ten chips for producing the four of clubs from Cartethyia's hair.

Lupa played six hands and entered one. Chisa played five and won three small pots with the calm efficiency of someone clearing cups after dinner. Ciaccona narrated her folds as moral decisions and her raises as changes in the weather.

Iuno watched all of it.

Mei called.

She called Ciaccona with a pair of threes. She called Rebecca with king-high. She called Cartethyia through a board containing four diamonds, then turned over two hearts with the hopeful air of someone presenting travel documents at the neighboring border.

"Your hand was entirely hearts," Amy said.

Mei tapped the ace of hearts. "I had an ace."

"The flush had you surrounded."

"It could have become nervous."

"Lynae can terrify a deck through direct contact."

"You spent ten years as a radio signal, sweetheart. Keep an open mind."

Amy stared at her, defeated by maternal jurisdiction.

On the seventh hand, Iuno looked down at two queens.

Mei looked down at an ace and the remaining queen.

Iuno raised to thirty. Mei called before Ciaccona had finished folding.

"You can take a breath first," Iuno said.

"I took one while you counted."

"You used it to smile at your cards."

The flop came queen, eight, three, in three different suits.

Iuno put thirty-five into the middle.

Mei studied Iuno's face with tremendous seriousness. She studied the board. She checked her own cards again, where the ace and queen had remained loyal.

"I am calling you," she said, and pushed the chips in.

Around the table, several women experienced the same future.

The turn brought a seven.

Iuno gathered her remaining stack into one clean column.

"I am all in."

Mei's hand went straight to her chips.

Amy caught her wrist. "Ma, she has spent this entire hand building you a beautiful staircase toward a hole."

"She bluffs. I have seen her bluff."

"Governments have trusted that face and regretted it. You have top pair."

Iuno sat with her real hand folded over the crystal one and waited.

Mei narrowed her eyes at her.

Iuno's mouth moved by half a millimeter.

Mei pushed everything forward.

"Then I am calling."

The room made one collective sound, halfway between a groan and the collapse of a roof.

Iuno turned over the queens.

Mei looked at the three queens. She looked at her own queen, who had betrayed the household. The river came down as a harmless two.

"I had ace-queen," she said.

"One pair carried complete faith in my dishonesty," Iuno said. "Your tournament is over."

Lupa swept the chips toward Iuno. Rebecca gave Mei the solemn salute of a soldier seeing another soldier into a very avoidable grave.

"She led you there on a string," Rebecca said. "I have watched people follow glowing arrows into minefields with more suspicion."

"I thought she was making a face."

Ciaccona leaned across Cartethyia. "That is her face when she loves you and has also calculated your liquidation value. You see it every morning."

Iuno pointed at the couch.

"Go sit down."

Mei clutched the backs of two chairs and remained standing. "Iuno, please give me one more stack. I understand the game now."

"You understood each rule separately and assembled them into a disaster."

"I will play carefully. You can set conditions, and I will accept every one."

Iuno looked up at her. "Would you truly do anything for a second chance?"

"I would. Absolutely."

"Then go sit on the couch."

Mei's shoulders fell.

"That feels cruel."

"I folded your suffering into a mercifully short evening."

Mei went to the couch. She sat in the middle with her arms folded and released a sigh that moved the paper crown.

The tournament continued.

---

## III.

For twenty minutes, Iuno became unbearable.

She took forty chips from Rebecca with a river raise. She made Ciaccona fold a straight draw by asking whether she planned to compose an elegy for it. She caught Lynae's bluff with bottom pair and charged her another ten when the missing dealer button emerged from Lynae's sleeve.

Her stacks rose into a small colored city.

Mei watched from the couch behind her.

Iuno found two aces, raised, and won the blinds. She found a set of fives on the flop and watched six people abandon the hand before she placed a second bet. She opened with seven-two, continued into a dry board, and Cartethyia called her all the way to the river with a pair of fours.

"Why are you still here?" Iuno asked.

Cartethyia glanced toward the couch. "The atmosphere seemed favorable."

Iuno placed one final bet.

Cartethyia called. Her fours beat Iuno's seven-high, which looked embarrassed about it.

"That call stood on fog," Iuno said.

"Mei looked as if she had left the kettle on."

The table went quiet.

Iuno turned around.

Mei sat very still, cup held under her chin.

"What have you been watching?" Iuno asked.

"I have been watching the game."

"Choose a narrower answer."

Mei looked at the table, at the eight sets of cards, and back at Iuno.

"Your cards are closest, so I watch those."

Ciaccona slid down in her chair, laughing into both hands.

Iuno looked around the table. "You have all been using her face."

"The broadcast arrives free of charge," Lynae said. "We merely receive it."

"Your queens made her beam," Chisa said. "Your bad hands make her press her toes into the rug."

Rebecca pointed. "She does a funeral eyebrow when you bluff. The left one goes up and the right one starts grieving."

Amy had begun drawing a diagram on the back of an old receipt.

"Her expression predicts your hand strength with eighty-three percent accuracy," she said. "It reached ninety-one before she realized we were looking."

Mei put down the cup. "I have kept my mouth closed for every hand."

"Your eyebrows carry most of the traffic," Amy said. "Your entire head is involved."

Iuno turned her cards face-down and lifted the corners toward herself.

"Mei, look here."

Mei looked.

Iuno viewed the cards. Mei's eyes widened with involuntary delight.

The whole table folded.

Iuno placed the untouched cards in the muck.

"Far end of the couch," she said. "Face the wall."

"The window will reflect the table."

"Close the curtain."

Mei dragged herself to the far cushion and pulled the curtain across. She faced the logs with a dignity that would have carried more force if she had kept from peeking over her shoulder twice during the next deal.

Iuno found king-ten and raised.

The dark window above the kitchen sink reflected Mei's face beautifully.

Everyone folded again.

Iuno followed their gaze to the glass.

"This house contains too many reflective surfaces," she said.

"I can solve it," Cartethyia said.

She took the patchwork quilt from the back of the couch and dropped it over Mei from head to waist.

Mei became a floral hill.

"I need air," came her voice from inside.

Amy pulled one corner aside around her face. "The opening is generous, and the flowers fill your view."

"Come in here with me."

"I still possess chips, and love has reasonable limits."

The quilt settled.

Iuno dealt the next hand.

From under the flowers, Mei said, "I believe in you, Iuiu."

Seven people studied Iuno's face for the reaction. Her expression stayed perfectly still.

The High Priestess, somewhere inside, applauded the table.

---

## IV.

Lynae left the tournament under circumstances she described as judicial harassment.

The first penalty came from the queen. The second came from the dealer button. The third came when Rebecca discovered an extra ace tucked into the collar of her jacket and reacted by climbing halfway across the table.

Lupa caught Rebecca around the waist. Cartethyia rescued the chip stacks. Ciaccona saved the hot chocolate. Chisa sat through the whole event with her cards held above the flood.

"I put it there six hands ago," Lynae said. "Your situational awareness has room for growth."

Rebecca strained over Lupa's arm. "You planted evidence on me for enrichment?"

"You become vividly articulate under pressure."

"I am going to articulate your teeth into the stove."

"Save it for after the river," Lupa said, and set Rebecca back in her chair.

Iuno held out her hand. Lynae paid ten more chips.

"This tournament rejects your teaching methods," Iuno said.

"Everyone benefits from them. The whole table counts the deck now."

"Amy counted the deck from the beginning."

"She inventories stairs while climbing them. She represents a specialist case."

The fourth penalty came when Chisa heard the soft drag of a second card leaving the deck.

"Deal the top card," she said.

Lynae paused with two cards aligned under her thumb.

"I respect you immensely."

"The feeling costs ten chips."

By then Lynae had forty-three left. She doubled through Rebecca with a clean flush and celebrated honest play with such lavish innocence that Rebecca threw a pretzel at her.

Then Amy raised from the button. Lynae called. The flop came ten, six, four. Amy bet twenty. Lynae moved all in.

Amy stared at the board for a full minute.

"She wants you to use the numbers," Mei called from beneath the quilt.

"The couch has lost advisory privileges," Iuno said.

"My daughter deserves maternal guidance."

"Amy can survive a pair of cards."

Amy counted the combinations under her breath. Lynae leaned back and smiled at the ceiling, utterly loose, hands visible, sleeves rolled to the elbows under a rule introduced specifically for her.

"Your line represents a ten, two pairs, a set, and several draws," Amy said. "Your earlier play removes most two-pair combinations. A draw remains the largest group."

"You have explained a fascinating reason to preserve your chips."

"I have a pair of fours."

"Then folding sounds especially wise."

Amy pushed in the call.

Lynae turned over eight-seven. She needed a nine or five for the straight, or an eight or seven for a higher pair. The turn brought a two. The river brought another six.

Amy's fours and the paired board held.

Lynae looked at the cards. "You called an all-in with bottom pair."

"You constructed a story with too many adjectives."

"I liked you better before we met."

"Your sample size from that period seems weak."

Lynae went to the couch and lifted the quilt.

"Move over. The state has taken everything."

Mei made room. Within one hand they had arranged the quilt into a tent with sightlines along the floorboards.

Chisa looked over.

The tent collapsed by mutual agreement.

---

Rebecca lasted longer than anybody had predicted, which offended her almost as much as the prediction.

She folded. She raised in position. She gave up three bluffs after the flop and once released two pair when Chisa's river bet arrived with the weight of a safe falling down a stairwell.

"You had the second-best hand," Chisa said after she dragged the pot.

Rebecca's head snapped up. "You kept both cards hidden."

"Your fold was good."

"Keep your psychological operations away from me."

"That was a compliment."

"I know what you were trying, and it almost worked."

Lupa gathered the cards for the next deal. "Who taught you to fold?"

Rebecca rolled a chip across her knuckles. Her hands had grown quiet as her stack grew.

"Maine used to run games during long jobs. He said anybody could pull a trigger, and the hard part was sitting on your hands while someone tried to make you stupid. Dorio said he stole that line from a movie."

"It is still a good line," Chisa said.

"Yeah, well, he folded like garbage."

Three hands later, Rebecca went all in with two kings against Cartethyia's ace-seven.

"You can release those," Ciaccona told Cartethyia. "Rebecca has represented strength in every available language."

Cartethyia looked down at the ace. "This is the highest card."

"It currently has a seven attached and a dangerous future."

"The seven might discover a purpose if we stay."

"Cards follow arithmetic, beloved. We covered this."

Cartethyia called.

Rebecca stood up before the flop. "Finally. Somebody in this house has blood."

The flop brought ace, nine, nine.

Rebecca sat down.

The turn and river helped nobody. Cartethyia collected the pot while Ciaccona stared at the ceiling and revised several private ideas about providence.

"That was awful," Rebecca said.

"He came through for me," Cartethyia said.

"I respect it. I also hate it. Both are huge."

Rebecca joined the couch, where Lynae immediately tried to put the extra ace back in her collar. The chase went through the kitchen and returned with two cups, half a bag of pretzels, and Iuno's warning that the next collision with the kettle would end spectator beverage service.

The couch became louder.

---

## V.

At six players, the table still expected Iuno, Ciaccona, or Chisa to win.

Iuno had the largest stack. Chisa had the cleanest. Ciaccona had accumulated hers through a long campaign of remarks aimed at other people's confidence. Lupa sat fourth, quiet and watchful. Amy had recovered from calling Lynae and begun treating every pot as a paper she intended to defend. Cartethyia had the hand-ranking card propped against her cup and more chips than she could stack safely.

The expectations lasted eleven minutes.

Ciaccona raised before the flop. Cartethyia called. They saw a board of king, jack, eight, then a four on the turn and a two on the river. Ciaccona bet every street. On the river she placed half her stack in the middle and leaned back with the soft composure of a bard reaching the final line exactly on time.

Cartethyia looked at her cards. Ace-five. Her whole hand amounted to ace-high.

"You should fold," Ciaccona said gently. "I am telling you this as someone who plans to share a room with you after the game."

Cartethyia watched her for a while.

"You want me to admire this."

Ciaccona's expression stayed easy. "I would prefer that you retain enough chips to enjoy another hand."

"Your thumb keeps touching the same lute callus. You do that before the last verse when you think the room has wandered."

The room, fully present, leaned closer.

Cartethyia called.

Ciaccona turned over ten-nine. Her straight draw ended as a four-card sketch.

Cartethyia laid down ace-high and gathered the pot.

For once in her life, Ciaccona's next line arrived late.

Cartethyia's delight faded by a degree. "Did I make it unpleasant?"

Ciaccona looked at her, then at the exposed bluff, then at the woman who had listened to her play for years from bedrooms, roadsides, ruined squares, dormitory windows, and the other side of every door she had ever pretended was a stage curtain.

"You caught me performing at the dinner table," she said. "I am furious with pride."

Cartethyia brightened and pushed half the pot into a dangerously tall tower.

"That little callus betrayed you."

"Enjoy that sentence once."

Iuno eliminated Ciaccona on the next orbit with a higher straight. Ciaccona took the loss with grace until she reached the couch and learned Lynae had eaten the last pretzel.

The argument lasted through two deals.

---

Iuno's tournament ended on the river.

She held two aces. Cartethyia held king-queen, both spades. The flop came king, nine, two. Iuno bet. Cartethyia called. The turn brought a jack, and Iuno moved all in with enough force to make the little city of chips click together.

Cartethyia consulted the hand-ranking card.

"That card describes completed hands," Iuno said. "Your present situation remains unfinished."

"I have already spent most of my stack finding out."

Ciaccona came halfway off the couch. "That money has crossed the sea. Let it live somewhere else."

"These are independent little circles, according to you."

"They became money again for the duration of this emergency."

Cartethyia called.

Iuno showed the aces.

Cartethyia showed king-queen.

The river was a ten.

For a second, Cartethyia kept looking for the result on her card. Then Amy reached over and traced the run for her: nine, ten, jack, queen, king.

"You made a straight," Amy said. "You have eliminated Iuno."

Cartethyia shot to her feet. The table struck her thighs, six towers fell, and chips scattered through the room like bright hail.

"I actually won that!"

"The pot is yours," Iuno said from inside the weather. "Several distinctions remain important."

Mei came out from under the quilt and opened both arms. "Come to the couch, Iuiu. I saved you a place."

Iuno looked at her overturned stacks, at Cartethyia crawling under the table after chips, and at the couch where six people had arranged themselves into an unruly tribunal.

"What did the ten complete?" she asked.

Cartethyia's head appeared between two chairs.

"I knew it was a friendly card."

Ciaccona took both ends of the quilt and pulled them over her face.

Iuno went to the couch.

Mei folded herself against her side at once, warm and triumphant.

"I would have played better if you gave me another stack," she said.

"You are using my elimination as a parole hearing."

"The timing seemed favorable."

Iuno pulled the quilt over both of them.

"Your application is denied."

---

## VI.

Amy went out in fourth because the universe declined peer review.

She held ace-king against Lupa's queen-jack. The chips went in before the flop, Amy ahead by every measure she announced to the room. A queen landed immediately. Five cards later, Lupa's pair held.

Amy sat staring at the board.

"I had sixty-four percent equity."

Lupa stacked the chips. "The deck heard you and continued with its evening."

"I made the correct decision."

"The couch offers excellent conditions for another one."

Amy gathered her dignity and crossed the room. Mei lifted the quilt for her. Amy crawled in beside her mother and immediately began explaining variance to Rebecca, who listened for thirty seconds before asking whether variance could be punched.

That left Lupa, Chisa, and Cartethyia.

Cartethyia's luck became magnificent enough to seem vindictive. She cracked Chisa's two queens with a rivered flush. She called Lupa with bottom pair and found trips on the turn. She mistook a chip denomination, raised five times her intended amount, and watched both opponents fold winning hands.

Ciaccona grew steadily paler.

"Cantarella's treasury is alive because the rules saved it," she said.

"I appear to have a gift," Cartethyia said.

"You have walked through falling masonry because the stones kept missing. Your architectural career remains unaccredited."

"The masonry also felt encouraged."

Lupa waited.

She gave Cartethyia small pots. She folded when the hand-ranking card came up. She let the bright towers migrate across the table and watched Cartethyia begin every call with the same tiny forward lean, eager to stay inside the hand long enough to see what it became.

Then Lupa found a full house and bet it in three modest pieces.

Cartethyia followed every one.

When the last of her chips crossed the line, Lupa showed the hand.

Cartethyia checked her own cards twice. "I have two pairs."

"The board supplied one pair to everybody," Chisa said. "Lupa brought three more."

"That feels excessive."

"She waited three hours for that hand," Chisa said. "Patience built it."

Cartethyia went to the couch in third place, carrying her ranking card like a certificate. Ciaccona silently made room and wrapped one end of the quilt around her shoulders.

"I nearly won," Cartethyia said.

"You reached third through courage, affection, and repeated statistical assault."

"Next time we could use very small coins."

"Cantarella will hear about this in my morning letter."

"You always leave out the interesting parts."

"Tomorrow's letter will contain a diagram."

At the table, Lupa and Chisa counted their stacks.

They had almost the same amount.

The couch leaned forward as one body.

---

## VII.

Heads-up lasted forty-eight minutes.

It was the quietest poker of the night and the loudest room the cabin had held since the snow war.

Every fold drew judgment. Every raise brought six opinions. Rebecca stood behind the couch and called the action as though a building were on fire. Ciaccona supplied a low, dangerous chord whenever a river card appeared. Lynae began taking bets in pretzels on the length of each decision until Iuno confiscated the bag and reminded her that crumbs still constituted stakes.

Chisa removed the cotton Cartethyia had made her wear during the middle rounds. The concession had begun after Rebecca accused her of hearing cards sweat. With her hearing restored, Chisa tilted her head at Lupa's breathing, the click of her teeth, the small betrayals of muscle and blood.

Lupa put her tail on the table.

"You have been watching it all night," she said. "You might as well get a proper view."

The tail lay between the chip stacks, thick and red-brown, its tip moving once whenever Lupa folded and twice whenever Chisa raised.

"It has lied to me since the third round," Chisa said.

Lynae put a hand to her heart. "I taught it everything it knows."

"You tried to hide a card in it," Lupa said.

"The fur provides excellent retention."

Lupa had grown up dealing bent cards to other children in Caldara, where the deck belonged to the room and the child with the steadiest hands kept it through the week. Later there had been arena barracks, guards, loaded dice, and games played for bread ends, bootlaces, a place near the brazier. She offered this history in three sentences while shuffling and only because Rebecca had asked how a wolf learned to riffle a deck.

"You let us spend four hours calling you a beginner," Rebecca said.

"All of you filled the silence. I used the time."

Chisa smiled into her cards.

The blinds climbed. Stacks shortened. Lupa bluffed a large pot and Chisa called her with ace-high.

"Your heart ran faster when the river passed you by," Chisa said.

Lupa showed the empty hand. "It does that when I am having fun."

"You enjoyed that bluff very much."

"The next one will be even better."

Three hands later, Lupa made the same large river bet. Her pulse jumped in the same place. The tail gave one slow sweep across the wood and knocked the dealer button sideways.

Chisa folded.

Lupa showed another bluff.

The couch exploded.

Chisa rubbed one thumb over the edge of her cards. "You rehearsed the second pulse."

"I thought about Rebecca coming off the woodshed roof."

"That raised mine too," Rebecca said. "I looked amazing."

"You landed on my neck," Lupa said.

"The landing needed refinement."

The final hand began at twelve minutes past two.

Chisa held two tens. Lupa held eight-five.

The flop came eight, eight, ten.

Chisa had a full house. Lupa had three eights.

They checked it through.

The turn brought a five.

Lupa now had a smaller full house. Chisa bet. Lupa called.

The river brought the last eight.

Lupa held all four eights. Chisa still had tens full of eights, a hand strong enough to end almost any night.

Chisa placed a third of her chips in the middle.

Lupa went still.

Then her pulse climbed.

Chisa heard it. Everyone saw the tail give one slow, delighted sweep.

Lupa looked at the oatmeal-box crown on the stove. Rebecca had written the title crooked. Cartethyia had added two paper ears. Mei had drawn a tiny moon on one point during her imprisonment. It was ridiculous, and Lupa wanted it with a clean, bright intensity that surprised her body before it surprised her.

She moved all in.

Chisa listened.

The fast pulse matched the bluffs. The tail matched the pleasure. Lupa sat across from her with the face she wore before breaking a door, carrying someone out, or throwing herself downhill on a sled built for a child.

Chisa called.

They turned the cards over.

For three seconds, both women looked at Chisa's tens and Lupa's fourth eight.

Then Cartethyia picked up the ranking card and ran around the couch to verify it.

"Lupa wins with four eights," she said. "That is extremely excessive."

The cabin erupted.

Chisa stared at the four eights. Lupa stared at the crown. Rebecca climbed onto the couch. Ciaccona hit a triumphal chord. Amy began gathering the scattered deck before Lynae could improve it.

"You wanted me to hear your excitement," Chisa said.

Lupa leaned back and released a long breath. "The crown did that. My body handled the rest."

Chisa's smile arrived slowly and then all at once.

"That is the most embarrassing tell you have."

"Then claim it. You earned it."

Chisa took the oatmeal box from Cartethyia and placed it on Lupa's head. It slipped over one ear. The two paper ears sat between Lupa's real ones at uneven angles. Acid-green letters shone in the firelight.

Lupa looked grave, dangerous, and completely pleased.

"I will wear this through breakfast," she said.

"Breakfast ends at noon," Chisa said.

"Then everyone should wake up early."

---

## VIII.

The tournament ended at two nineteen and the couch refused to dissolve.

Lupa wore the crown. Chisa dealt herself three private hands and checked the final board twice, perhaps looking for a fifth eight. Cartethyia tried to retrieve Cantarella's purse. Ciaccona sat on the drawer. Rebecca and Lynae argued about whether planting an ace on someone counted as cheating once the planted ace stayed outside the active deck. Amy wrote **LUPA — 1** on the wall calendar and added **MEI — 9** beneath it before Mei could reach the eraser.

Iuno collected the chips by color.

Mei trailed her around the table.

"I think my elimination helped you," Mei said. "You became chip leader while I supported you from the couch."

"You exposed nine of my hands to the table."

"I supported you emotionally."

"Your eyebrows covered the whole room."

"You looked lonely after Cartethyia won."

Iuno put the last red stack in the case. "You had already built a quilt tent with the woman who hid an ace in Rebecca's clothes. I felt many things."

Mei leaned her hip against the table and smiled.

"Deal me one hand. Everybody else has finished, and I deserve a chance to show what I learned."

The room quieted by suspicious degrees.

Iuno looked at the couch. Seven people found deep interest in cups, cards, ceiling beams, and Lupa's crown.

She shuffled. She dealt two cards to Mei and two to herself.

Mei checked her own cards carefully. She took a breath. She waited.

Then she leaned across the table and peeked at Iuno's.

Her whole face lit up.

From the couch, seven voices began speaking at once.

"She has queens," Chisa said through the noise.

Iuno closed her eyes.

Mei sat back. "That could have gone better."

"Go sit on the couch."

"The tournament ended."

"The couch remains available."

Mei went, sighing, and seven people lifted the quilt to receive her.

Lupa's paper crown tipped sideways when the couch took Mei's weight. Chisa caught it before it hit the floor, straightened the paper ears, and set it back on the champion's head.

Iuno stood alone at the table with two queens, one empty chair, and an audience making enough noise to satisfy Rebecca for a week.

She smiled at her cards.

The couch saw that too.
