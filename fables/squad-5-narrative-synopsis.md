# Squad 5 — Narrative Synopsis

## The Observatory

Helios is new, but it rises on a Lahai-Roi still carrying the damage of the Void Storms. Roads between Startorch Academy and the Spacetrek Observatory are blocked, and the engineers need those roads open before they can get the parts to finish restoring the array. At the Observatory, Acting Captain Bridget briefs her reconnaissance squad over a cup of cold coffee. Leva is away again, doing work whose particulars Bridget keeps to herself. Teddy sees no reason a senior technician should leave her terminal for a routine clearance run. That leaves Kit, the fourth and newest Doll in Squad 5, whose room is full of hero plays and whose idea of usefulness is still a lone figure standing tall in a fight.

She borrows Leva’s outrageously modified motorcycle without asking. Bored by a road with nothing to shoot, she calls Bridget to complain, spots movement near Orbit Valley, and cuts the call to engage. The figures she mistakes for Tacet Discords are armed Royan Outcasts moving cargo. The moment somebody recognizes a Spacetrek Doll, an awkward encounter becomes a firefight. A disruptor wrecks the bike’s engine and takes out Kit’s shoulder. With an empty pistol and rifles aimed at her chest, she tries to talk down a man who assumes she has already called the Collective on him. Bridget arrives in time to knock him aside and gets hurt herself keeping the guns off Kit. Teddy identifies an Exoswarm nest and retrieves the smoking bike while Bridget uses the swarm’s eruption to get Kit out.

Bridget suspends Kit for three days and makes her responsible for the bike. Leva earns the latitude Kit envies by doing what she says she’ll do and checking in before a situation turns into a fight. Kit hears only that she has failed to earn Bridget’s trust. Then an anonymous sender congratulates her for dealing with the roadblock, says the plan was for Spacetrek to handle it, and promises they will meet again. Afraid that reporting the contact will give Bridget one more reason to bench her, Kit deletes the messages and calls them spam when Teddy notices the traffic. Teddy knows she’s lying. She also knows what three days alone with those posters will do to her. She stabilizes Kit’s shoulder and lets the questions wait. When Kit keeps working through the injury until she collapses, Bridget catches her and gets her to the medical unit.

Teddy calls Kit dead weight and is already at the workbench correcting her mistakes by the time she comes back from medical. The repairs take three days of bloody knuckles and exacting instruction. By the second day Kit can put the next tool in Teddy’s hand before she asks. By the third she knows to bring Teddy spicy instant noodles, and Bridget can leave beer and protein bars at the door without interrupting anything. After Kit takes the motorcycle around the perimeter with Teddy riding pillion, Bridget asks for another lap. Kit finally demands to know what would count as finishing the job. Teddy backs her on the need for a clear standard. The bike is operational, if still short of Leva’s standards, and Bridget welcomes Kit back. Then she sends both of them to clean the workshop.

Bridget has beer waiting when she takes the pair out to watch the Observatory unfold. Kit’s reckless run did help open the road, and the parts and Bridget’s reconnaissance brought the restoration forward a week. The consequences reach Haldareim just as quickly. An apparent frequency spike coincides with biomechanical Ironhoofs turning rampant across several settlements. Kit joins Bridget in the pen after the fence gives way and gets gouged in the side helping bring the animals down. The chief shepherd wants answers from the people whose equipment disturbed his herd. Bridget promises a frequency analysis within a day and gets Kit into Teddy’s hands for repairs. That evening she takes them out to watch the projected stars. Kit used to find the same constellations from her dormitory window when she couldn’t sleep. Teddy knows Bridget has a habit of feeding people and taking them somewhere quiet after a mission, and has watched plenty of them transfer, retire, or die. She was reassigned to Bridget. Staying is something she has done herself. In the hot spring beyond the trees, Bridget thinks about the photograph she keeps in her desk, about the dead and scattered faces in it, and about how quickly Kit could become one more.

## The Last Window

While the Observatory takes shape, Leva watches a group advancing toward the Exostrider from Bjartr Woods. The official stories about Mei’s companions leave out their part in classified incidents and the depths to which they have already gone for one another. To Leva, an old Squad 404 operative, they look dangerously like the Anti-Rain Team, drawn into the orbit of someone whose conviction makes the rest willing to jump. She recognizes a mother willing to follow her dead daughter into the dark. She has spent years mapping ways out for people who make that kind of choice. When she learns of a ten-minute Exostrider window involving several pilots, she sends the warning to Bridget and keeps watch.

The Collective rushes deployment without explaining the deadline to most of its workers. Kit runs supplies until the bike runs hot and logs every route. At six minutes to go, a relay goes dark. Teddy and Kit repair the severed connection, but the window closes while they work. In the corridor, they find Dhalifa confronting Sephira, a childhood friend who sabotaged the relay because she was afraid the Observatory would take Dhalifa as the last storm had taken their friends, their settlement, and Dhalifa’s voice. Dhalifa rebuilt the eastern array by hand because the people who should have been there to do it were dead. If the work does cost her life, she wants the choice to have been hers. Bridget, who lost three people on the deployment before Lahai-Roi, understands why Sephira thought preventing the attempt was the safest thing she could do. She tells her she was wrong and gives the two a chance to talk. Sephira gets fed and falls asleep under a borrowed jacket. Bridget files a technically accurate report that leaves her name out of it.

The next viable window, in three days, is the last anyone can predict. Teddy pieces together how much Bridget knew from the wording of the report, the location of Leva’s surveillance, and Bridget’s uncharacteristically private dinner. Bridget tells her the Collective is giving Mei’s group a chance to reach the Exostrider. Squad 5’s concern is making sure there is a way out. Teddy asks for a backup path independent of the access credentials she can’t account for, and Bridget hands her a keycard. They still keep Kit out of the loop. Kit, for her part, begins to understand how much she never asked about when she arrived after the storm and found everyone already at work.

Then the Collective orders Squad 5 to remove more Outcast roadblocks. At Gamma-7, Bridget walks toward the barricade alone and gives Kit a clearly bounded support role. Kit almost breaks position watching the rifles turn toward her captain. Teddy has time to see what Kit missed at Orbit Valley: the Outcasts’ leader has clean military equipment and is sending a running account over an old Spacetrek black-ops channel. Bridget realizes he needs them to start the fight for the record. She puts on her sunglasses and lets him run out of threats. He shoots one of his own men to force the confrontation. Only then does Bridget call Kit in. Kit and Teddy bring the bike down into the canyon together, cut off the man’s escape, and help take the survivors prisoner.

The mole calls himself Joric of Atuja. He claims the Outcasts meant to attack the Observatory’s primary conduits and that he needed the Collective to stop them. He is also the man who sent Kit the messages. Without the trail she erased, his claim of cooperation rests on the word of a man who has just shot a comrade. Bridget makes Kit take his statement and find corroborating evidence. Teddy objects to leaving her to stew in failure again and discovers Bridget expects her to spend the night helping. Kit’s first report buries what she has found in pages of testimony and shame. Teddy shows her how to match the time of Joric’s transmission against the hauler data and establish the connection to a Collective signal. The evidence shows somebody inside fed him information to stage the encounter. While they work, Bridget goes back to the canyon herself, fights off the Discords drawn by the earlier disturbance, and drags the wreckage out of the road. By breakfast the route is open, the evidence is secured, and Kit is still a member of the squad. Leva has seen the Royan chiefs yield to the Banat al-Rih, and Mei’s attempt is going ahead.

At the final deployment the command goes unanswered again. A dormant program buried in the relay has woken at precisely the promised window. Someone in the control room calls for the Doll to be plugged in. Teddy waits for Bridget’s look before going in, and Kit puts herself between Teddy and an engineer who keeps trying to lay hands on her. Bridget asks her to trust Teddy with the work. The technician isolates the program and breaks its hold, and the dishes swing into position at last.

When Teddy brings up the Stridergate feed, Kit watches Mei’s party cross impossible stone. A shell carries the presence of the dead idol Aemeath, Fleet Snowfluff, and its borrowed hand holds on to Mei’s as they go toward the place that took her. Someone promises to bring everyone home. Kit knows what victory is supposed to sound like from her plays, and she can hear how much more fragile that promise is. Bridget remembers when her own team numbered six and tells Kit to watch. Leva, closer to the gate, sees it swallow the group in light.

The barriers begin to fail. At the Research Institute, Mornye tries to contain the cascade while Buddy the Soliskin circles her in distress. In the woods, Elder Kaelen feels the Solistrees in pain and accuses the people working on the Exostrider of chaining their god to a corpse. Luuk Herssen works on the root network with golden ichor seeping through his glove. Tacet Discords pour through the breaches, and President Lucilla orders civilians toward the Riseway and calls on Septimont. Squad 5 is sent to Delta 3. Before the formal dispatch arrives, Teddy has already had word to warm the bike.

For eight hours the three Dolls work their way through collapsing streets. Bridget takes the weight of falling masonry while Kit pulls out a child. Teddy turns the maintenance bots into a defensive line and gets a broken courier unit moving again. In a flooded cellar, Kit finds a young Doll missing part of an arm who has been told to wait for somebody to come. Kit gets her on her feet. When a wounded Ironhoof calf charges blind through the street, Kit reaches for her pistols, hears Bridget tell her to use the modulator, and calms it long enough for its handler to reach it. The last barrier goes dark with twelve people still unaccounted for on Teddy’s manifest. Bridget holds the line against the oncoming Voidstorm until the last shuttle can get clear, then orders Kit and Teddy aboard.

Kit starts to obey. Teddy grabs her wrist to pull her up the ramp. Then Kit sees Bridget alone against the dark and goes back, knowing exactly whose order she is breaking. In Orbit Valley she had charged because she wanted to be the figure at the center of the fight. Here she wants to be beside the woman who is about to die buying everyone else time. She tells herself she wants to be in Squad 5 and fights alongside her captain until there are too many Discords to count. Then the sound of the stolen motorcycle comes through the storm. Leva scythes into the clearing on it, with Teddy alongside on a drone, and greets Kit by reminding her whose bike she touched. Bridget laughs. When Leva says she has struck a deal and wants them in Bjartr Woods, at the epicenter, Bridget gets on the back without asking for the terms.

Four witches are holding the last defense around the Stridergate. Herta cuts ranks of Discords out of existence. Black Swan brings down a Voidworm with a card. Viviane sees the barrier buckling, and Agrat bat Mahlat holds creatures suspended long enough for the Dolls to bring them down. Kit sees what all that power can do and feels the smallness of her pistols. Leva gives her a job with the drones. Kit holsters the pistols, marks targets, and keeps marking them as Teddy tears through her power reserves keeping the drones aloft. Bridget and Leva run through their ammunition. As the final wave crests, Teddy puts a trembling hand on Kit’s shoulder and tells her she did well.

Then the gate opens. Baldur, the Exostrider, steps through in Solistree gold. The Discords flee and the Voidstorm recedes. In the command center Lynae drives the stabilization sequence, Mornye sees the barrier hold, and Lucilla calls for medical crews as seven figures emerge from Baldur’s chest. Leva, accustomed to seeing conviction carry people only as far as the disaster, is quietly pleased that these particular idiots have made it out. She has also brought four cups of instant noodles. They eat together under real stars while the Solistrees go quiet. Teddy thanks Leva for coming. Kit can say only how glad she is that everyone is there.

Viviane approaches the four and calls them automata. Bridget tells her they have names, so the witch asks for them and learns each one. She has seen them make choices beyond the demands of their programming. Leva tells her they discarded that programming when they came to Lahai-Roi. Bridget calls the place theirs because they chose it. Later, when Teddy shuts down from overwork, Kit puts her head in her lap while the veterans go to talk. Luuk needed someone to hold in the woods, and Leva made her help conditional on a permanent roving assignment for all four. They can go home when they like. For Leva, that means people like Kalina and Lenna, whose names she seldom says. For Bridget, it means an end to the cycle of taking in people she can only hope to keep alive until they leave. Teddy wakes to dirt in her hair and protests that Dolls are assigned. Then she asks the question that matters more to her than the new terminal she wants or the overtime she still intends to bill. Are they staying together? Bridget tells her they are.

## Off the Manifest

The first thing Spacetrek does with the space Teddy leaves is send a new Doll to occupy it. TD-2 arrives between a crate of coolant and the mail, knows exactly where Teddy would put a toolkit, and explains that she was built from the TD baseline. At nineteen, Teddy had found the Institute turning her account of what thinking felt like into a product. They kept twelve of her forty pages and labeled her preferences, attachments, and pleasure in argument as noise. She destroyed three years of research to make herself unreplicable. Here is someone walking through the workshop on her architecture anyway, pleasantly accepting whatever tea comes out of the dispenser. The handover should take four days. Teddy makes it take nine.

When Dhalifa comes down to ask who will know the eastern array as Teddy did, TD-2 points to the complete logs. Teddy explains what it cost Dhalifa to make those logs and promises to leave her direct channel open, wherever she goes. TD-2 answered the sentence and missed the question. Teddy sees how well a Doll made from the Institute’s twelve pages can do the work. On the last day their workshop cat jumps onto the new bench. Teddy tells TD-2 to feed Freeloader at seven and log the food under pest control. TD-2 knows the entry would be inaccurate. Teddy leaves it to her anyway and tells her to find something other than her designation to sign when she writes.

Leva has secured custody of the squad’s neural recovery files along with the roving assignment. The Collective prunes memories from its snapshots and decides whether a Doll is worth restoring. Teddy sees the pruned version of herself as another spare and crushes the crystal in a vise. Bridget’s generation predates the current standard. Budget consolidation ended maintenance of her file nine years ago, and no one recorded when it finally degraded. The last person to be told that Bridget could die for good is Bridget herself. She laughs until her eyes water, thinking of the two squadmates who died before there were cradles at all. Leva buried her old name and lost her recovery point in Frankfurt. Kit keeps hers in a folded poster of *Last Stand at Beohr*. She knows anyone brought back from it would be a new person with her memories. She keeps it because she wants somebody to look after the earnest girl she was before Bjartr.

Kit turns up at the briefing room by habit on departure day and finds a dark table and a blank schedule. She asks Bridget what they do when nobody tells them. For the first time in thirty years Bridget can admit that she has no idea. Before leaving she goes to a shrine built after the crisis, where anyone can ring a bell for the people who kept reaching into the dark. She rings five times for the five faces in her photograph and leaves under Helios on its usual schedule. The Riseway brings the Dolls up into a real morning. Kit thinks the sun is late. Teddy wants to complain and has nowhere to file it. At the first unmarked fork, all four wait for an order nobody is coming to give. Leva, who walked away from a finished war and had to learn how to want things again, picks east on the grounds of coffee.

At a water stop, four convoy escorts have Kit’s face. One loves *The Lone Sentinel* down to the same moment the lanterns come over the hill. She calls the play a module from orientation. Kit learns that some four hundred KTs received the same hero plays to motivate them. Around the fire, Teddy can name exactly one who took Leva’s bike, lied about the messages, wrote that dreadful report, and turned around at a shuttle ramp because following the order would have felt worse than dying. The factory issued the first sentence. Kit wrote the rest, with stripped bolts and in crayon, in Teddy’s estimation.

At the junction freight depot, Bridget hears three wandering notes and sees Senna’s face. Eleven Dolls there have it, and the hum is standard S-series firmware, with an option to suppress it. Bridget asks the loading Doll to leave it enabled. She remembers how Senna directed the hum straight at her on cold watches, raising the volume until Bridget threw a ration bar and Senna caught it without missing a note. Senna was also the one who read BRG-7 as Bridget on a manifest and kept calling her that until it was her name. Bridget leaves an expensive beer on the rail and rides away humming under the engine.

Leva finds unseasonable frost along the road and follows it to Hiyuki, the retired miko who entered Baldur and built the shrine where Bridget rang the bell. She’s walking toward the restored city of Honami. The squad takes to walking the bikes beside her and learns the speed at which a road has details. At a fire, Bridget takes out the photograph and names the five people in it, including the one who went silent and got a bell rung for him wherever he might be. Hiyuki once took the names of the dead into herself as the work of her office. Now she listens, refills Bridget’s tea, and lets the names be said in company. Later Kit tells of an Outcast hijacking when she was a trainee with only a torch to hold out like a sword. Two clean shots from someone she never saw saved her transport. She tried out for reconnaissance four times because she wanted to be the sort of person who did that and was already gone by the time anyone came to thank her. Leva calls the shooter sloppy for failing to say hello. Bridget catches the twitch of Leva’s fingers against her knee and holds her tongue.

Teddy stays up to ask Hiyuki what she accomplished by burning the Institute’s work if TD-2 exists and is content. Hiyuki spent a century ringing possibilities out of her own future to fuel an office. By the end the bell showed her only a dark room, which she mistook for what awaited her. She now takes pleasure in deciding what goes into it. For TD-2, the danger would begin if a preference arrived and nobody was there to notice. Teddy thinks about the cat she left behind, the deliberately inaccurate ledger entry, and the invitation to choose a name. She writes to ask how Freeloader is behaving.

Discords attack a survey convoy on the way to Honami. Bridget’s shield is up, Leva is on a hauler roof, and Teddy’s drone is in the air before anyone gives an order. Kit gets the cook and chainmen behind Bridget and covers her squad. Hiyuki rings her pilgrim’s bell once and two creatures hesitate long enough for Leva to stop them. When the foreman asks who sent them, Bridget says nobody. Hiyuki tells him the matter fell to whoever was standing nearest. All that work is still there for Bridget to do, and the empty briefing room has no power to take it away from her.

At Honami’s first summer festival since the city returned, Hiyuki insists on waiting in line for red bean pudding. She has had thirty years to reach this particular evening. When her two bowls come, she gives the second to whoever is nearest, which turns out to be Kit. Her cold fingers linger on Kit’s machine-warm hands. Leva sends Kalina a souvenir spoon and a postcard signed L, then sits with her back to the crowd to watch the fireworks. When Hiyuki goes to stand beneath the great sakura and its one green branch, Leva knows to leave her the courtyard and be findable later.

The squad spends the rest of the season pulling convoys out of trouble, repairing a well for dinner, and choosing roads because they want to see where they go. Kit still cries at the lanterns in a hero play. At inns she sometimes sets the crystal on the sill so that spring-Kit can see the weather. When the first frost arrives, Hiyuki has a promise to keep. She must return below when it turns cold. Kit proposes an escort to the Riseway. They can call it a mission and take it on themselves. At the lift Hiyuki tells Bridget that all three of her Dolls can take another road whenever they want. She also knows what it is to leave a door open and have somebody come back through it. She and Kit make a date for the pudding stand next spring. As Hiyuki descends, Teddy’s terminal reconnects and a message comes through from the Observatory. Freeloader is misbehaving, her food has been logged as instructed, and she prefers the blue bowl. TD-2 has signed the message *Pekoe*, after the tea Teddy left behind. Teddy makes a folder for it. Then the four ride north to see what a real winter is like.

## Radio Nobody

Winter is quiet enough for Kit to notice how much time she has spent under the hum of a cavern. She takes to listening to Teddy’s shortwave by the inn stove. One night a stranded convoy escort with Kit’s cadence asks if anyone else is on. The relay answers with the four words it has for her. Regulation traffic only. Kit sits there until the fire goes out, thinking of a batch-sister watching the snow for help that won’t arrive till daylight. The squad can reach one convoy at a time by road. She asks them for a radio station.

Leva knows an abandoned broadcast tower. Teddy moves the useful parts of its old transmitter into a secondhand rover, and Bridget drills them until they can pack the mast in eleven minutes. Kit paints Radio Nobody on the door, remembering the man who asked who sent them. They go on air at the hour the convoys stop for the night. Kit plays fast music. Bridget plays long jazz sides for the watches and dedicates one to the friend who hummed through reloads. Teddy gives weather reports, including word when official road schedules are too optimistic. Leva folds warnings into the market news, and a driver eventually sends coffee because his convoy took the southern road on her advice and still exists. Escort Four asks for the fast song again through a chain of people willing to pass along an off-book request. Kit plays it twice and eventually gives her a whole hour.

The Collective tries jamming, tracking, and a cease order. The listeners turn tuning to a new frequency into a nightly game, and the station goes on air during the jammer’s own calibration break. Bridget reads the order in full before declining it. The former broadcasting authority was abolished, and nobody with a claim to the frequency has asked them to stop. When the dispute reaches President Lucilla, she reminds the Spectrum Office where its authority ends. The jamming stops. The Calibration Hour keeps its name. Down at the junction depot, a Doll designated S-11 hears Bridget talk about the three notes and learns that someone with her hum was loved and is still missed. She turns the canteen dial herself and leaves it there through a warning from the shift lead. In time she picks the name Java from a coffee tin and asks Bridget for the real reload song. Bridget finds the record she has never shared. On the night platform, eleven Dolls with Senna’s face hum along, each for the crew they share the shift with.

One night the transmitter puts a delighted voice on air without asking Teddy. Fleet Snowfluff used this frequency ten years ago, when she was one girl broadcasting unfinished songs from Lahai-Roi. She came back to find jazz, attack rock, market tips, and people listening for the voices on the roads. She gives the station her blessing and a song she has finally finished, *For the Watches*, with a second, older voice invited to sing off-key at the end. Kit, who has the bootlegs sorted by era, can hardly speak. Escort Four hears the song under the aurora with her gain turned up. The four make it their sign-off. By winter’s end a survey Doll is correcting Teddy’s forecasts on air, and Pekoe writes that the cat sits by the radio for jazz. Before they head south, Bridget tells the watches that the frequency stays theirs. She wants to hear about the small choices they’re making out there, right down to a name picked off a coffee tin.

## Telemetry and Tattoos

On the spring coast road, three days short of Honami, Kit gets stuck in front of a tattooist’s window. Spacetrek forbids Dolls to modify their plating to preserve its resale value. Kit likes the idea of making her chassis so particular that she’s the only person it suits. She has three lanterns rising over a hill inked where she’ll see them when she draws a pistol. Bridget puts five stars on the shoulder that has carried a rifle for thirty years, one for each face in her photograph. She declines a sixth. The five are already for her. Teddy prints the Institute reviewer’s word, *NOISE*, in the reviewer’s own lettering, over the diagnostic port someone would use to prune her again. Leva says she’s decorated already, but Kit finds *404 — not found* on the inside of the mechanical arm. Leva engraved it at the tower before Kit even made her pitch. Anyone looking for what she used to be can have an accurate status report.

The telemetry mesh beneath their plating has been sending the Collective their vitals all along, even after the transfer. The new ink makes the Directorate’s screens report damage, and the Acquisitions Department sends a dropship and a portable cradle to restore the four Dolls to baseline. The order bypasses the President entirely and makes no provision for consent. Pekoe sees the designations in the bulletin and forwards it with a recommendation to be difficult. With four days’ warning, Bridget weighs leaving and fighting it on paper. Kit sees what both approaches concede. Acquisitions expects to take them off a road in private. They have a station and a whole corridor that will listen.

When the six retrieval Dolls land, their leader reads from Order 12-M until the words “resale value” reach her own ears over the live microphone. Teddy shows the ink to the team’s camera. Down the coast, Java’s crew crowds the canteen radio. Escort Four listens from her cab. Pekoe and the cat listen at the workshop, and Aemeath keeps a way open in case the station needs one. Bridget has the discharge terms, which gave them custody of their platforms, and asks the leader to inspect her stars. Each cost more than the afternoon it took to put them there. Teddy has discovered that a consent setting was already built into Doll telemetry and set for them at activation. Over the same broadcast she sends an update that gives Dolls the choice to say yes, no, or ask. In the days that follow, the Directorate’s graphs go quiet as Dolls across the corridor start making their own decisions.

The retrieval leader is a BRG herself. She comes close enough to see Bridget’s stars and files what she sees. The modifications are voluntary, the chassis sound, and the order gives her no basis to proceed. The depot cheers. Kit asks the retrieval team if they want a song for the flight back. In the following days the tattooist’s book fills with Dolls wanting their own error codes, and the junction depot’s eleven identical faces acquire eleven different drawings. The officer who led the retrieval is reassigned pending review. When she shows up at the inn in civilian clothes, she asks Kit where to get ink that somebody will have to read before they can erase it. She wants *ASK* where a hand would go to check her pulse. All four walk with her to the shop.

## Dead Reckoning

The former retrieval leader keeps the designation BRG-9 when she sits for the tattoo. She has chosen to keep it. Escort Four, meanwhile, requests a coastal posting, takes a new rig and leaves her old call sign behind. She asks for the fast song twice on her last watch. Kit misses knowing where she is and leaves the last three minutes of her show open for whoever is driving. She could have Teddy find the rig. She tells her to leave it alone. If the radio gave people a switch, it applies to the people at the microphone too. The old marker is still on Kit’s map, with a dotted circle and room for whatever comes next.

Kit calls a meeting over dinner, fires the Collective as the authority that made Bridget captain, and calls a vote to hire her back. Bridget votes last. Her duties are a hot stove, an open door, and head counts. Her authority can be revoked at any meal. Teddy proposes that the squad ask before finding things out about each other or doing something on someone else’s behalf, and live with the answers. She is soon put to the test. Pekoe’s messages have gone from noting Freeloader’s preference for jazz to wondering which of them is keeping the other company. When the Institute sends a TD-3 trainee who asks what she is, Pekoe asks Teddy for help. Teddy says to teach her to solder, then secretly makes her a tuner. When the forwarding logs come under review, Teddy wants to hide Pekoe’s tracks. Instead she asks if she wants help. Pekoe declines. She forwarded information to people it concerned and has decided to stand behind it. Teddy goes back to the lathe.

Bridget asks for airtime of her own. Her fifth star went silent, and she has been holding that silence like a watch. He was the one who set her chronometer two minutes fast and called it optimism. She tells him over the radio that there is a kettle on the stove for him and he can take as long as he likes to answer. Then she hums Senna’s three notes. Two former squadmates write. One sends back forty credits and reports seeing the missing man keeping bees under another name at Kirsk. The other sends a tomato flower from Ragunna and has heard both that he went down with the relay in the crisis and that he was seen afterward. Bridget files both accounts, leaves the question open, and decides on Kirsk in autumn.

The Collective responds to unrecoverable Dolls by selling newer ones. Its revised package comes with three factory-installed preferences, and Teddy tells the new models over the air that the Institute has merely furnished them with a longer first sentence. Kit hears her own timing and warmth in an escort recruitment spot and spends an evening wondering what will still sound like hers. She takes stock of the withheld minutes, the lookup she asked Teddy to leave alone, all the nights of broadcasting, and the bruise from learning Leva’s two-shot drill. She climbs the mast because she wants the wind. The next night she greets the roads in the same voice.

By the time the squad returns to Honami, the one green branch has become a flowering quarter of the sakura. Hiyuki is on the stairs waiting for them. In the market, a younger KT with three crooked lanterns scratched into her arm asks Kit to tell her what to do with her life. Kit is tempted by how quickly the girl would take an instruction. Instead she asks what she last did without a task. The girl watched the sea for eleven minutes and logged it as calibration. Kit tells her to keep that receipt and teaches her to turn a salute into a crooked wave. A young dock Doll has copied Bridget’s stars and added one for herself, which Bridget tells her she gets to keep. A driver has seen a rig on the coast playing the bootlegs loud with one lantern painted on its door. Nobody checked who was driving. Kit is happy to file it as true. After the fireworks she sits with Leva and speaks of the two shots from cover that saved her transport. This time, when Kit says the shooter should have said hello, Leva does. Kit waves. Leva stays. That night she sends word about a coastal rifle match and gives Radio Nobody as her return address.

They draw up leave forms in crayon around a fire. Teddy is taking two weeks to deliver the tuner and see Pekoe, the trainee, and the cat. Leva wants to compete for prize money and feel recoil that owes her nothing. Kit wants to meet the people behind a repeater that sends the signal back with an accent and to argue antenna theory in person with BRG-9, who now keeps Relay One. Bridget is coming with her. She has also had an offer to teach new Collective intakes. The form finally has a field for consent. Bridget offers detached instruction on her terms, including an unsupervised fork in the road for the very first week. Before any of that, she’s going to Kirsk. They sign as witnesses and Bridget crosses out the line for an authorizing officer.

At the Riseway, Leva slips the Honami spoon into Teddy’s pocket for the trainee. Kit hugs Teddy goodbye and learns to count three without treating the fourth place as lost. Down below, Teddy rings Hiyuki’s bell for the twenty-eight pages the Institute discarded. Pekoe has the kettle on and has moved a workbench toward the spring light. Teddy sees that the light is better there. She sets out the tuner and the spoon, tells TD-3 they’ll get to metaphysics after soldering, and takes a bite on the thumb from a grateful cat. That night the three on the north road sign off, and Teddy, Pekoe, and the new trainee answer from the workshop. The trainee is a beat late saying “Nobody.” There is still room on the air for her.

## Fetch

Leva rides down the coast between the ocean and the silver ceiling of the Etheric Sea. She finds a Doll pulling a usable knee out of a hauler chewed up by the Dark Tide. The stranger wears parts she salvages, gives thanks to the wreck they came from, and can tell what Leva used to be at a glance. She’s Soppo, from the Anti-Rain Team’s orbit, out with a heavily rebuilt Dinergate named Ro. She starts to call Leva by the name she remembers from 404. Leva gives her the one she uses now. They are both headed for the same match, so Leva offers her a ride.

Soppo spent two seasons on Septimont’s Capitoline circuit as Spolia, an arena name for what the winner strips off the loser. A licensing office somewhere holds money earned by the silhouette on her soda cup. She knows how much fifty thousand people wanting the same thing of a fighter can change her, and how it felt to hear the announcer call her moves before she made them. The Slack Tide Invitational has imported the two-person format. Leva notices the sponsor in the small print, Spacetrek’s Advanced Acquisitions Group. She means to enter with her own money, for her own pleasure, and to give Lenna somebody to bet on.

Over a shared beer, Soppo sends her usual report to RO-635, Roro, who tells her to eat vegetables and says hello to Ro. The reply comes back faster than the relays ought to carry it and is stamped a tick before the message it answers. Later Leva hears a tick from the salvage satchel on the same beat as the light overhead. She turns on the radio and lets Soppo say hello to the roads. Kit, Bridget and Teddy answer in chorus. Soppo has always been afraid of being the last one at the door, and she leans into the sound when she learns Leva has a pack of her own.

At dusk a grey-haired driver in an old Chevelle passes them going north and raises two fingers specifically to Leva. Leva returns the gesture before she knows she’s doing it. Soppo says the woman smells like the bottom of the sea and looked at Leva the way a mirror does. At the ferry town Kit and Soppo make fast friends. Teddy notices that the Dinergate, supposedly a memento rebuilt by Roro, bears the marks of recent work from someone trained on rescue frames. Bridget sees a sole survivor’s habits in a Doll who still has a squad to send messages to. Hiyuki recognizes something from her old dealings with the dead, and later listens to the ticking in the satchel. At the market, Agrat greets Leva as the rifle from the gate. Over Mancala she tells Soppo she overpaid for what is in the bag and can come back to play when she knows what is hers to stake. She tells Leva that a wall can face either way, then hints that someone this close to the water might take an interest in the name Leva wears. She leaves without saying who.

At slack water the patterns in the Sea overhead look like language to Leva. Below, in a tide pool, Soppo sees stars she has known by name since activation and has never seen in the sky. For a moment the reflection has room for somebody else. They let the water settle. Soppo tells Leva that the last arena bout was registered with a vacant partner slot, approved by the Ephor, and her own logs confirm she fought alone. The footage shows a second fighter covering her blind turn. The crowd remembers both of them, and no camera has the partner’s face. Soppo is out looking for somebody she has never met. She has also sold a memory to a feathered woman who kept the same seat in the arena, and the hole it left is so smooth she cannot say what she gave up or decide whether the price was fair.

Leva has found a woman of that description in three files from three continents. She has watched improbable things happen around Mei, including the return of seven people from the place where the god stood up. She took the Observatory posting for pay, to give her people distance from her work, and to have a contract she had read before signing. She also wanted her family’s luck to sit downwind of whatever made those returns possible. An old pre-Lament relay was built around the Etheric Sea as if to contain it. Leva suspects the vault above them is a lid and wonders which side the builders meant to protect. Soppo knows the company finally ordered her whole product line shut down, yet she is here, still running errands for somebody she loves. She throws a pebble over the water. Somebody always gets to throw first, she says. The legs that run after it and the catch are hers. Leva knows how long playing a ghost can set the shape of a life. Soppo knows how well the arena monster still fits some nights. They sit on the wall together, two veterans used to people spending themselves for the ones they loved, finding out in company how to stay.

At the registrar’s table Leva reads the sponsor line again and puts *Leva* in the designation field. Soppo says she only works corners now, with a towel, water, and yelling. That is all Leva needs. Soppo signs and enters them as Spare Parts. Leva mails word home that she found a teammate. Down on the slip Kit’s laugh carries over the loading ferry, Teddy supervises, and Hiyuki holds something warm against the morning. Bridget counts heads and comes up over. The Observatory still has staff and Lahai-Roi lives under Baldur’s protection. The four who once waited there for their assignments now have people waiting for them along roads they chose, and a frequency that carries their voices even when they take different ones. At the waterline Soppo’s terminal chirps a moment before she thinks to want a message. Two reflections keep pace in the wet sand. She asks Leva to watch her run, whoever throws the next one. The road goes south between the seas.
