Mud.

Nobody had thought about mud.

Five days of frozen ground had trained every person on that vehicle into a set of assumptions about traction that were comprehensively wrong by the tenth morning, and at ten past nine the *Lucky Bird* put her middle axle into a soft verge on a bend above the fourth ford and sat down.

## +4

On the eleventh morning the country changed under them in about ninety minutes.

The forest thinned and stepped back off the road. The road acquired a surface — broken, patched, crowned, with a ditch cut either side by somebody whose job that was. There was a fence: four wires and a leaning post and a strainer that had been re-tensioned that season.

Then a field. Then a terrace, cut into a slope, with the last of the winter stubble on it and the water already standing in the lower bays.

Then smoke, a long way off, going up straight in the still air from something with a chimney.

Then a person: a man at the roadside with a barrow and a spade, who straightened up, and looked at the *Lucky Bird* going past with a four-metre ceramic angel kneeling on the back of it, and lifted one hand.

Mei, on the near bike, took a hand off the bar and lifted it back.
