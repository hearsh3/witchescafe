# An Impression of Wind
### being the new editions of one morning at the youngest gate, with the variants given entire
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

風雨如晦，雞鳴不已。既見君子，云胡不喜？
*Wind and rain, dark as dusk; the cock crows on and on. Now I have seen my lord, and my heart is glad.*
— the Book of Songs

黃梅時節家家雨，青草池塘處處蛙。有約不來過夜半，閒敲棋子落燈花。
*Plum-ripening season: rain at every house; frogs in every green pond. An appointment stands, and midnight passes, and the guest has yet to come; I tap a chess piece, idle, and knock the blossom off the lamp-wick.*

## The Woodblock Edition

*(cut in the standing grain, the way the city keeps what it means to keep)*

The willows on the west bank were three days ahead of the ones on the east. The letter had said so. The party had seen it from the pass, forty kilometres out and three thousand metres up, and had come down through cloud into a morning with the sun on it, and had then, with the whole bowl of the city below them going gold lamp by lamp as the switching was done by hand, stopped.

They camped at the last bend above the terraces, where the road holds its breath before the descent. The freight crews call that bend the kettle-lid. The clerk asked why and received, from four crews, four answers.

Where the wind rode on the road east, the editions part company. The Woodblock says she rode the rack with her own red kite, folded, the way soldiers sleep. The Movable-Type says she ran the whole road a day ahead, opening the weather like doors. The Burned Edition says she sat at the tailgate every evening while the language tape ran, and learned along with the truck the four sentences her golden-eyed cargo could say, and laughed — a laugh the size of a moth — at the same one, every time.

The raven declined to state which edition he rode in. He has a post. He kept it.

⁂

The last night went like this.

<font color=royalblue>"First light,"</font> Iuno said, at the tailgate, with the lamp on the map and the map already memorized by everyone present. <font color=royalblue>"She goes down at the fourth watch. We come down at first light plus one hour."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Plus *one hour.*"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Plus one hour."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You've timed us to the far side of the whole thing. You've timed us to *miss* it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I have timed us to arrive after it. Those are different acts and I have spent two years learning the difference."</font> Iuno folded the map along its old creases. <font color=royalblue>"I arrived at a shore early once. The earliness cost a courier her errand, and the debt has sat on my books ever since, at interest. This is the only coin that account takes. I am going to stand at the top of that path tomorrow and be late on purpose, with my whole attention, and you are all going to help me."</font>

The tailgate took a moment with that.

<font color=indianred>"Fine walls on that dam,"</font> Lupa said, to the dark, which was her way of ratifying a treaty.

<font color=mistyrose>"Okay, but the protocol still stands."</font> Amy had the folder out — her own manual, the better one she'd written. <font color=mistyrose>"Nobody sleeps alone in the dark where we can't reach. That's the rule. That's *my* rule, it's a good rule, Luuk countersigned it—"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Somebody has a hand on me the whole time,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"That's the whole appointment, kid."</font>

Amy looked at her mother. She looked at the night over the water, which had begun, at the eaves of the tarp, to pay very close attention.

<font color=mistyrose>"…Number going down."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Ten."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Come back different."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Always do."</font>

Ciaccona took the lute off its peg. Chisa's chin had been up for a minute already, tracking something along the ridgeline the way she tracks a footstep through a wall.

<font color=coral>"The valley's strung tonight,"</font> Chisa said. <font color=coral>"Something's holding the long note under it."</font>

<font color=teal>"Then somebody should give it the rest of the chord."</font> Ciaccona set her fingers, and played three notes — *da, da, da* — small, once, out over the terraces, the way you set a lamp in a window. <font color=teal>"Any three notes,"</font> she said, to the question Cartethyia had drawn breath to ask, <font color=teal>"with someone to play them for."</font>

The note stayed exactly where it was sung, the way notes do.

And the wind moved.

And this time, for the first time in the whole quarrelling history of the record, both of them pointed at the same shore.

⁂

Lupa walked her down. That satisfied the letter of pack law and most of its spirit. Where the beacon path gives out into the long soft rows of grass the keepers leave alone, Lupa stopped, because from there the night had a hand on Mei, and everyone on that hillside knew whose.

<font color=indianred>"Shout,"</font> Lupa said.

<font color=moccasin>"I'll be loud. It's my other whole thing."</font>

The maps call that stretch of shore the Gorges of Spirits. The fishermen call it the beacon shallows. Both names go on the sign; there was probably an argument. Mei went down through the grass with a shell against her sternum and a piece of Mengzhou lead in her pocket, and stood a while at the waterline with her boots in the reach of the smallest waves, talking quietly, the way she talks to weather, the way her Outrider mirror talks to it a hemisphere away — the day's absurdities, the road's inventory, a truck's worth of sleepers itemized with love. Then she lay down where the water could touch her hem, and put one arm under her head.

<font color=moccasin>"Okay,"</font> she told the lake. <font color=moccasin>"Your move."</font>

She sleeps anywhere, now that the house is full. Anywhere, trusting the building. That far from any roof, the trust had to go somewhere.

She gave it to the weather.

Far down, under the held stars, the lake turned over — slowly, with great courtesy — and the lamps along the crest wall guttered once, all together, and steadied. On the public stair, Keeper Pei felt it through the rail and answered the runner's question before it was asked.

"Wind all night, son. Log it."

He trimmed the lamp that lights the stair, though the oil was fresh and the flame good.

"Habit," he said. "You trim lamps for guests."

## The Movable-Type Edition

*(set fresh from the standing sorts, the way a new thing is made of old letters)*

Yangyang came off shift at the grey hour, the hour the fishermen call fish-belly white, when the water gives back more light than the sky has issued it.

The night had been quiet in the manner of an instrument being tuned. Twice on patrol she had stopped mid-street for a pressure that crossed the road and went on, businesslike, freighted; once, at the second bridge, the willows had leaned all together toward the lake, held it, and straightened, like a crowd craning at a gate. Her window, when she passed her own lane, stood as she had left it, the flute on the sill — and the note came to greet her the way it had greeted her every evening for a year, one low sound, their private *home again* —

— and it came out full. Round, warm, with the whole breath under it, the way it had sounded on festival night with two players on one instrument. A note with luggage. A note that had come home carrying something and wanted her to know it.

She stood in the lane with her hand on the gate and heard it to the end.

Then the wind crossed her cheek, took the brim of her hat — the little bird held its stitch — and tugged. Twice. With intent. The cadence of a sister's hand in a market crowd: *this way, now, hurry.*

Yangyang decided, the way she decided everything, all at once and permanently, and took the beacon stair at a run.

⁂

At the waterline, the wind came off the lake at the grey hour and crossed the shallows the way you cross a ward at night.

One strand of hair had strayed across the sleeper's mouth. She moved it — off the brow, back, the licensed gesture, the one handhold a wind has — and then she performed the second half of the gesture, the half that had waited twenty years and two printings for its turn, the half mothers do when a fever breaks: she stayed. Palm to brow, a pressure like a hand that decided long ago to be gentle, and in it, set down at last at its own address, the sentence she had carried across the dark between stories:

*Let her wake here.*

Mei surfaced the way she always surfaces, all at once, from the bottom, with one hand already out — the Fool catches things; it is the first fact about her — and the hand rose slowly, and open, and stopped beside her own cheek, palm curved around the pressure there, catching nothing, holding it. Gold came up under her lashes and found the morning.

<font color=moccasin>"Hi,"</font> she said.

The air over the water stood very still.

<font color=moccasin>"You're right on time."</font>

The wind spent a word. It came at the old rate, the way words come through a door open a finger's width, and every lamp on the crest wall leaned toward the lake to hear it.

"Found."

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font> Mei's voice did something and she let it. She sat up in the shallows with the water running off her sleeves, and she said the second of her four sentences of that country's language — the thank-you — in the wrong tone, at the wrong speed, the way you bring wildflowers: picked, crooked, yours.

A laugh the size of a moth. From nowhere. From everywhere at the rim of the morning.

The shell on Mei's sternum hummed its two seas. She closed her hand around it and said the rest plainly, in her own tongue, the way she makes all her deals.

<font color=moccasin>"Every wind is met once."</font> A breath. <font color=moccasin>"It's a good wind."</font>

> **CLERK'S NOTE.** *The Woodblock Edition states that the sleeper truly slept, as she sleeps everywhere, trusting the building. The Movable-Type states that she waited with her eyes shut. The Burned Edition states both, and calls them one act performed at two depths. On the question of which reached her first at the last — the palm of weather or the light of morning — the court declines to rule, citing precedent.*

⁂

<font color=moccasin>"What do I call you?"</font>

The water at the sand's edge dimpled. Lines drew themselves where the last wave had left the shore smooth and dark — a stroke, a second stroke, the start of a character, careful, like a student's first brushwork —

— and stopped, where it had stopped on a teacup a year ago and a hemisphere away.

And Mei's own hand was already moving.

It went down to the sand without consulting her, the hand that writes what it writes, the hand that has been finishing a stranger's address one character a season on kitchen tables and border forms, and it supplied the rest of the strokes at the speed of a signature, and sat back on her knee.

<font color=moccasin>"Huh,"</font> Mei said, to her own hand.

The character stood whole in the wet sand between them. Her fingering; its breath. Where one gave out, the other had carried, and what stood there was a third thing, wearing both of them.

Then Mei took the piece of Mengzhou lead out of her pocket — Iuno had put it there at the top of the path, with the whole instruction: *it holds; that's the entire job* — and pressed it flat over the character the way you press a seal, and held it while the third wave came in and took the sand back to smoothness around her wrist.

The lead came up bearing what a blank is for.

And the wind sat down.

The banners' whisper, the river's static, the smallest waves — everything settled at once, all around the two of them, into a stillness with a center, and the center was warm, and out along the bank the grass lay down in one long line, water to sleeper, like something walking in that weighed nothing.

> **CLERK'S NOTE.** *On what stood in the sand, the editions disagree. The Woodblock says an old name came back entire. The Movable-Type says a new name was made on the root of an old one — one radical from a school, one stroke from a signature. The Burned Edition keeps its counsel; see below. The founders on the Row, shown the lead, examined it in silence for some time and then asked to sit down. A quad is cast blank. This one now carries a face, in reverse, at striking depth. It is, they report, the beginning of a matrix — the thing you cast type from, against the day you want a word the cases have run out of. Nobody has cast from it. The court keeps it with the blanks.*

⁂

Yangyang reached the grass as the sun found the saddle in the eastern rank, and stopped, because ahead of her, at the waterline, the air was standing up off a stranger the way something stands that has been kneeling — unhurried, finished, light — and it went up the sky like a spark going home to its fire.

It crossed her cheek on the way. One touch, at the old spring pressure. Then the brim of her hat, once, gently — *yours now* — and the morning was only morning, with a woman sitting in it, soaked to the shoulders, laughing at the sky with her whole chest.

The pull under Yangyang's sternum — the one she had stopped explaining to herself years ago, the certainty, ridiculous, load-bearing, that somewhere a stranger was owed a seat at her table — swung once, all the way round, and settled, the way a needle settles when you finally stop walking.

She went down the last of the bank and into the shallows, boots and all, and put her hand out.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Slowly. The stones roll."</font>

Mei takes hands. She took this one, and came up out of the lake of Jinzhou into the first hour of the day, streaming, upright, met.

They stood there holding the grip a moment longer than the job required, which is a thing that happens on that shore, the record shows, in every printing.

<font color=moccasin>"Mei,"</font> Mei said.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yangyang. Mingting registry, Jinzhou posting."</font> The report voice ran out, the way it does. She looked at the horizon, and then at her own hands. A year of letters at a night window, a grammar book kept under a patrol log against an appointment she could never file, a seat wiped down at Panhua's every week for a guest of general description — all of it arrived at once and jammed in the doorway of her mouth, and what came through was:

<font color=yellowgreen>"…You're soaked."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah. Ask anyone. It's my whole origin story."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"There's breakfast at the stair foot."</font> Said fast. Said to change the subject. Said with the ears going pink under the hat.

<font color=moccasin>"Good,"</font> said Mei. <font color=moccasin>"Hungry."</font>

The fried dough was excellent.

⁂

They ate on the public stair with the fishermen, who made room the way that stair has always made room, and looked, and kept their counsel, and had the story dusted off for their grandchildren before the oil was cool.

Mei put the shell into Yangyang's hands somewhere around the second piece. She did it the way she does everything, sideways, mid-sentence, watch this. Yangyang held it across both palms like something asleep, and put it to her ear because that is what you do; some knowledge lives in the arm.

A tide. Long, patient, shore after shore of it. Under the tide, wind. And at the very bottom, faint as a lamp four rooms away, a second tide, older, in a different mother's rhythm — a sea from a printing where the same house had stood on the same lane and a woman with unhurried exact hands had wrapped this shell the way you dress a wound.

Yangyang lowered it. Her face performed the family craft, and the craft failed, and she wiped her eyes with her sleeve in the practical way, once each side.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Ma's work,"</font> she said. <font color=yellowgreen>"Both of them."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Keep it a while,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"It knows the address."</font>

Then Mei stood, faced the hillside, filled her lungs, and was loud.

<font color=moccasin>"FOURTEEN."</font>

Up at the bend, Amy lowered the field glasses. <font color=mistyrose>"She says fourteen."</font>

Lupa started the engine.

⁂

Iuno came down the path at the head of the family, one hour late, on purpose, with her whole attention, carrying the pot.

She had written the river level down for Mornye before she wrote anything else, because a promise outranks a sunrise; then she had allowed herself the sunrise; and somewhere in the middle of it, behind her sternum, in a small warm room with a fire in the next country over, a woman in blue silk had turned her glass a quarter-turn by the stem.

*Well, darling,* said the High Priestess of Septimont. *Jinzhou.*

*Yes,* Iuno answered, and the word closed a question two years old, and both of them let it.

The *Lucky Bird* came down the dam road at a ceremonial pace, apricot and tooth-yellow, four water drums on the roof, a red kite on the rack, a raven riding the air over the cab at parade height, on a current that kept the truck's pace down the whole descent, the way an honor guard keeps pace with what it honors. The tarp over the sample bed had worked one corner loose in the morning wind, and the corner of a white ceramic wing showed under it, and by noon the whole lakefront had decided, city-wide and without a meeting, that the foreigners had brought a votive, and approved.

A fisherman at the stair foot read the five black characters on the cab door, twice, the second time with his lips.

*"Good lintel,"* he said, to the truck, and went back to his nets.

The introductions happened the way weather happens. Amy arrived first, on the principle that she always arrives first, and stopped in front of Yangyang and looked at her with a broadcaster's full attention.

<font color=mistyrose>"You're the *letter.*"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"I posted several,"</font> Yangyang said. <font color=yellowgreen>"One learned to fly."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Okay,"</font> said Amy, to the sky, <font color=mistyrose>"I like her,"</font> and stuck out her hand.

Chixia arrived at the stair the way she always arrives, mid-sentence, off the night muster, a paper bag already going translucent with oil — *"—because if you're buying strangers breakfast at your age I want it minuted, Yangyang, I want a formal—"* — and stopped, and took in the stair: a wolf watching the noodle window with predatory scholarship, a knight of small size holding her own hand out of habit, a bard tuning to the gulls, a one-armed foreigner pouring tea with a handle turned guest-side out onto a flat stone, and a soaked golden-eyed woman laughing at something the air had done.

The bag changed hands four times in the next minute and came back empty, and Chixia announced to the stair at large that she had extra, and that she was a generous person, and Cartethyia translated this at eighty percent accuracy and one hundred percent delight.

Baizhi came last, with the incident ledger under her arm, and stood at the rail a while, watching the light on the water and the party on the stair with the expression she saves for results that replicate. She opened the ledger. In the cause column, in her small exact hand, she wrote *weather* — and then, for the first time in a year of underlinings, she struck it through, and wrote a different word, and closed the book on it.

Two women, hemispheres apart, have now made that same correction in that same column. The clerk sets it down without comment, twice, which is the clerk's way of commenting.

⁂

The kite went up at full morning.

Amy carried it to the parapet of the public stair, checked the spars the way crew checks crew, and let it up into the offshore air — and the string paid out through her hands, and then, at a height where the draft dies against the dam face, the kite kept climbing.

Red, against the grain of the wind, up the whole blue page of the sky over Jinzhou.

In Mengzhou everyone knows what that means. Jinzhou learned it that morning, shopfront by shopfront, the whole lakefront turning out to watch, and a child on the shrine street said *look* to the sky, and the sky, for once, was already looking back.

At the yamen, a window that is usually shut stood open.

And the towers of the youngest gate — on an order signed that morning at the Magistrate's own desk, under the doctrine that the first census after any dark is of who came back — beat once each as the kite passed along the wall. Tower by tower. Eight beats.

Eight beats, at full morning, for a column of one, entered in the garrison log as: *returning; counted.*

The ninth beat was left where it has always been left. But past the north face, where something has been counting down the frost months in tremors — eight days between them, then seven — the mountains that day kept a silence of their own, a full day's worth, and the war college has been arguing about it since, in two prefaces, across the stitching.

The wall's opinion is shorter. The wall swept under the parting-tree twice.

## The Burned Edition

*(recovered in part; the blanks stand where they stood; the court has ruled them admissible)*

What lies between stories is a dark that reads you. She had gone through it once with her doors blown open and paid the toll in name and word, and for twenty years the account had stood the way ruins stand: *the going was accepted, and the errand was refused.*

This is the chapter of the errand.

She came down to the water at the fourth watch and found the shore already holding its guest — delivered to the sand by her own appointment, wrapped in a coat of road dust, breathing the long even breaths of a woman who has decided, in defiance of all her training, to be catchable. The lake lay still and clear and took the last stars carefully, the way the old keep photographs. Far down, an ancient courtesy turned over and made room; it has held her confessions for two years at the fourth watch; it knows her weight to the feather.

She crossed the shallows.

She had rehearsed this crossing on the rims of two hemispheres. She had flown it toward a lamp once and come late, or early, or the pages burned; she had reached for a golden-eyed hand on a machine moon and come apart at the touch; she had learned, the hard way, which was the only way she had ever taken anything, that she could never be delivered by hand.

So she was delivered by reading instead. A letter, a year of steam off a rail cup, eleven words through a shell, one word a night, spent like principal. And now the last brushstroke of the delivery, the one the sentence had always ended with, at the address it had always carried:

*Let her wake here.*

The sleeper woke. The gold came up. The open hand rose and curved around her and held without closing — the grip the whole family is founded on.

"Hi," the woman said. "You're right on time."

She spent a word on that. She would have spent a name, a season, a self; the purse offered what it offered.

The woman answered in kind and then in the wind's own language, wrong tone, crooked, hers. And then, plainly, at the going rate of that shore, where every promise is made in small words at first light:

"Every wind is met once. It's a good wind."

She had asked the dark for exactly one thing, at a gate, to a garrison's back, in a voice a transcriptionist declined to describe. *Let it be a good wind.* The record will show the request was filed, and carried, and answered, and witnessed by a lake, a lamp, and a keeper's habit.

What she said back is the missing passage of this edition.

The sorts for it went off the Row years ago, bought out of every case across a single spring by a customer who paid in very old coin and wore a different face each visit. The court notes, for the register, that this spring the customer has begun, without explanation, to sell them back — one sort at a time, face down, at the price she paid. The blank stands, and holds the space, which is the entire job:

> ■■■■

⁂

The naming she watched happen to her the way you watch a fever break in someone else: from very close, making no sudden moves.

Half of her had gone into that first stroke on a teacup a year ago and stopped, apologizing, at the edge of what remained. This morning the other half arrived from outside her — out of a hand that has been writing an address it cannot read, one character a season, on tables and forms and the door of a truck — and finished her.

Her fingering. Its breath. A third thing, wearing both.

She had been unmade once at the rim of the world, gladly, with no road back. This was the other operation. The Row would call it *recast*. The troupe would call it *act three*. The family calls it *home*.

And the wind sat down.

⁂

She kept the once one heartbeat past the handoff. Letting go was, this time as every time, the delivery. Then she went up the morning like a spark going home to its fire, and took the long way along the wall, because for the first time since the crossing, everything she had was under one sky — one bowl of water and mountain, one evening's unhurried round.

An Outrider's window, first. The flute on the sill; their low note; she poured, and a girl with a hat on the bedpost carried, and they split the last of it half and half without discussing it, the way they have settled every account of the year.

Yangyang read the reply at that window, that evening, by lamplight, a much-folded page that had crossed a border in a coat pocket. What it says the court has seen, and declines to copy; some pages belong to their holders. The record admits only this much, because the lamp leaned and forgot to wander for the whole of the reading: the wind read it over her shoulder, every stroke, twice, and the paper is already going soft at the folds, the way paper goes when it is read the way letters deserve.

In the second ink, that night, under the year's first entry of the plum rains, the patrol log gained a marginal line:

*Log: clear. Wind: home. Guest: one. Seat: filled.*

And on a guesthouse roof above the east market, at the hour the lamp-grid goes gold from the north side first, a woman came up the ladder with two cups — her own coffee, cardamom, criminal strength, and a tea for the rail.

<font color=moccasin>"Local leaf tonight,"</font> Mei told the dark, setting it down on the tile. <font color=moccasin>"You're welcome."</font>

The air at the eaves came down to the rail.

It settled over the cup the way a guest settles who has finally been given the good chair, and the steam leaned all through the telling — Iuno and the border pole, Cartethyia's forty preserved plums, the angel's loose tarp corner, the exact pink of an Outrider's ears at the word *breakfast* — one way, steadily, like a flame with company.

Mei turned her cup on the tile, a quarter-turn, settling it.

<font color=moccasin>"Same time tomorrow,"</font> she said.

The steam off the second cup bent, once, the size of a nod, and stayed.

## Colophon

*entered at Jinzhou in the fifth month, in three hands and a second ink; the court keeps the register open*

**I. From the Wind-List of the Almanac Bureau, Eastern Copy.** The unfiled entry — **客風, the Guest Wind** — has been closed out by the keeper, forty years of blank ruled off with one stroke. Beneath it, new, in fresh ink:

**接風 — the Welcoming Wind.** *Fifth month, at dawn, off the lake. Arrives with guests; met with a meal, as the word requires. Stays.*

*Marginalia, two hands:*
*Filed under weather.* ~~struck~~
*Filed under family. — stands.*

*The Bureau notes it is the first wind on the list to have filed itself, and the first whose entry required the kitchen's concurrence. The city's tables have already taken up the custom. The hinge cup at Master Ping's steamed itself dry twice before noon and the chalk on the frame ran out at the lintel; the house has ordered a second frame.*

**II. From the Magistrate's office.** The runners' log for the night reads *wind, all watches* and is countersigned in Her Excellency's own hand. On the following night the lake dreamed forward, the first forward dream of the tenure: the Ministry of Rites has requested the contents and been refused. The office has, without explanation, doubled the kitchen budget, ordered lamp-oil in war quantities, and issued a standing instruction to the towers that any column counted returning is to be counted *whole, however constituted*. Beside clause C of the instruction, in the Magistrate's own pencil, small: *Rain before the fire, this time.*

**III. From the Plum Rain Troupe.** At the guest bar before that evening's *Maiden of Two Names* — doors thrown wide, house lamps dipped, one breath — a lamp at the back left, by the door hinge, relit of itself. The house went up before the overture on the strength of it. The master was found afterward in the property room, hat in hand, looking at the trunk beneath the third-act armor, which the night porter reports has stopped rattling entirely and now behaves like luggage. Asked what the lamp meant, the master put his hat on and answered from the doorway:

"Act three may begin."

Asked when the doors close, he answered from the stair:

"They don't. That's act three."

**IV. From Bookbinders' Row.** The lead returned from the shore is entered in the register as *quad, faced; matrix, probationary; do not distribute.* The founders cast nothing from it and take turns looking at it. At a venture, on the strength of the morning, one of the long-missing sorts was recut — and held in the mold, which no cutting of it has done in twenty years. The word was *hope*. The founders have let the sort for *fail* lie, on the grounds that nobody has asked for it.

The same week, a customer with an unfamiliar face inquired after these editions and paid in very old coin. She bought all three, read the Burned Edition twice at the counter, and left smiling — the founders agree on the smile and disagree about its temperature. She left behind, in payment or in promise, one sort, face down on the counter-cloth.

The house has not yet dared to turn it over.

**V. The register.** One member of the party, asked by this office which edition of the morning is the true one, said that both names go on the sign when the argument is finished but not over, and bought the clerk a lemon cake. The office has copied her ruling into the register, and eaten the evidence, and would like it recorded that it asked.

The letter is answered. The seat is filled. The wind was met once, at dawn, at the youngest gate, and the editions disagree about everything except this: the sleeper woke, the errand was delivered, and the world keeps such accounts without explaining them.

Whether *once* has ended, the editions also disagree.

The court, having read all three, is in no hurry to rule.
