# The Scenic Route

The gull opened its beak and spoke in Iuno's voice.

“Sweetheart, you've put your elbow in the butter.”

Mei looked down at her arm.

They were standing on a quay in Ragunna. Sunlight lay across the canal, a washing line crossed the street above them, and a woman at a stall was wrapping figs in paper. The air smelled of warm stone and the sea. Behind them, through a doorway edged with pale green light, Mrs Ren was still sweeping her yard.

Chixia leaned forward in her chair.

“Does it know any more?”

Ciaccona lifted a finger from her lute. The gull closed its beak.

“It knows a small mistake I made while arranging the harbour sounds. I'll attend to it.”

“Leave it until we've heard the rest. I've travelled a considerable distance for this.”

Her chair stood six feet from the guesthouse door. Lupa had carried it through with Chixia already settled in it, while Baizhi followed with the footstool and her folding chair. Now Chixia had her injured leg on a cushion, her crutch within reach, and three foreign countries ahead of her. She adjusted her cap to suit the occasion.

The gull hopped along the quay.

“I brought you a clean shirt,” it told a bollard.

Amy put both hands over her mouth. Beside her, the real Iuno considered the bird.

*We project beautifully in the open air, darling. Thirty years of addressing galleries has finally found its proper application.*

“The consonants are excellent,” Iuno said. “You have captured my concern about the laundry very faithfully.”

Ciaccona bent over the lute.

She had spent six afternoons on the realm, working at the cleared table beneath Cartethyia's new shelf. Downstairs, people had heard bells, surf, fragments of announcements, and once a very convincing horse. Cartethyia had held pages open and turned pegs when Ciaccona's fingers cramped. Mrs Ren had begun knocking before entering, after finding the space between the bed and the washstand occupied by half a bridge.

It had started with Chixia asking where they ate.

She knew about the tower. She had heard several accounts of the Great Agon, and Amy had described Lahai-Roi's underground sky with her arms extended until Baizhi moved a cup. Over lunch, Chixia wanted to know who chose the inns, how they washed their clothes aboard ship, and whether Lupa's description of Septimont's portions applied to ordinary customers.

Ciaccona had opened her notebook to find an answer. By evening, she had begun making a street.

Now there were four places, joined through the travelling verses of her unfinished epic, with stops chosen in company over supper. Chisa had asked for the street outside the bookstore. Cartethyia wanted Egla's bakery included. Lupa had given very particular instructions about the approach to the arena. Amy wanted to show them the view outside Lucky Bird.

Ciaccona had promised to bring everybody home before Mrs Ren needed the chairs.

She plucked a string. Along the quay, six gulls lifted their heads.

“Move over a little, sweetheart. You've taken the whole pillow.”

Mei sat down on a bollard and began laughing.

The woman with the figs walked through her own stall. Her apron followed a moment later, passing through the wood with a soft blue shimmer.

Baizhi took a notebook from her bag.

“I would like to record the sequence of your adjustments.”

“That makes one of us,” Ciaccona said. She listened with her right ear turned toward the water. “The voices have caught the wrong return phrase. Give me a moment.”

Yangyang stood beside Chixia, her loose cuff resting against the chair back. A small fold inside it lifted toward the gulls. She stroked the cloth once with her thumb, then looked across the canal.

“The bells are very clear. I can hear a second tower farther inland.”

Ciaccona glanced up.

“Behind the red roof. In the afternoon they used to ring six breaths apart. The nearer bell had a crack.”

They listened. The first tower sounded; the second answered, lower and rougher. Cartethyia smiled toward the rooftops.

Then a gondola came through the bakery wall.

Its bow emerged above a basket of loaves. The gondolier stood at the stern with an oar planted firmly in the floor. He wore an agreeable expression and continued rowing as the boat passed through the basket, the stall, and a very surprised Mei.

She looked down. A polished wooden seat extended from her stomach.

“Cia, I seem to have boarded.”

“Walk toward me. The things I made are still learning where your body ends.”

Mei stepped clear. The gondola continued across the street, entered a flight of steps, and rose through them diagonally until the gondolier's hat disappeared into a balcony.

Chixia watched its progress with professional interest.

“We've got a delivery cart that takes that approach to corners. I'd like to speak to this fellow when he comes round again.”

Lupa planted her hands on her hips.

“You tested this street yesterday.”

“I walked down it yesterday. Today I've added traffic.”

“We can see where the extra work went.”

Ciaccona sounded a low chord. The next gondola struck the quay with a hollow knock. Its bow stayed outside the stone.

“There. We've established a useful difference between water and pavement.”

Mei put one foot on the gangplank.

The boat shot across the canal.

It reached the opposite wall, sprang back, and hit the near wall at twice its former speed. Mei landed sitting in the stern. On the next crossing she passed them with both hands braced against the seat and her hair streaming straight behind her.

“I would appreciate a shorter route!”

Ciaccona damped a string.

The gondola stopped.

The canal kept going.

Water, reflections, three mooring posts, and a row of sunlit houses swept past the stationary boat. A woman shook a rug from an upstairs window as her house travelled sideways. Mei drifted by at a stately pace, looking over her shoulder at the piece of Ragunna she had brought with her.

Amy had folded against Cartethyia. Her laughter kept making a little squeak halfway through.

“Ma, keep your hands inside. You're coming up on the bakery.”

Ciaccona struck a sharper note. The houses returned to their foundations. The canal settled into its banks with a splash which left everyone smelling faintly of oranges.

Mei's gondola rested alongside the quay.

She stood carefully, tested the gangplank with a toe, and stepped ashore into Lupa's waiting hand. Then she looked back at the boat.

“That was lovely for about half a second. I could see all the way down to the bridge.”

“Sit with Chixia while I give that half-second some neighbours,” Ciaccona said.

Baizhi crouched beside the water. She dropped a pebble into it, watched the splash, and picked up another.

“The acceleration began at contact. I suspect your correction accumulated during each crossing.”

She dropped the second pebble. It hit the surface, rebounded, and went straight up.

Everyone watched it become a speck.

Baizhi drew a small arrow in her notebook.

***

They took the street route to Egla.

Ciaccona had compressed the journey into an archway covered in climbing roses. Chixia's chair travelled over the cobbles on a shallow disc of melody, its footstool keeping pace. Yangyang walked beside it. The others followed a little more cautiously than they had entered.

At the checkpoint, a guard stepped into their path.

His helmet was covered in pink and black squares. The same pattern ran down his shoulders and onto one glove. A square on his cheek blinked whenever he swallowed.

He looked at Cartethyia.

“I'm growing mushrooms under here. The damp has been very good for them.”

He pulled his own cloak over his head.

Mei leaned around the cloth.

“You used my explanation! I remember being quite pleased when that worked.”

Cartethyia reached up and lifted a corner of the cloak. Three mushrooms emerged from the guard's helmet, pushing through the little pink squares.

“He has evidence, too. We could have used some of these.”

“Keep walking,” Ciaccona said. “If I correct the speaker, I may have to find somewhere else for the mushrooms.”

Beyond the arch lay Egla's square. The light changed. Wind came warm across the fields, carrying the smell of grain and the sharper sweetness of Rosa's ovens.

Cartethyia slowed at the bakery window.

“Oh, she has the little tray out. Those are the ones with sugar on the edges.”

Rosa's likeness stood behind the counter, arranging lemon cakes. Cartethyia opened the door. A bell rang overhead, and from somewhere behind the shop came a pan being set down hard.

“That sounds like Gram,” Cartethyia said.

She went in smiling.

Ciaccona held a chord while the others followed. For a few moments, the bakery worked beautifully. Paper crinkled. Rosa turned a cake with the tips of her fingers so the prettiest side faced the window. Chixia asked about the lemon rind, and Cartethyia began explaining how the sugar caught in the little pieces.

Then Mei reached for a cake.

Her hand stopped six inches above the tray.

She tried from the side. Her fingers met the same smooth resistance.

Cartethyia put her palms around the empty air. She followed its curve, first upward, then down to the counter.

“Cia, I think you've given this cake the whole plate's worth of space.”

Lupa reached around them.

“Let me get under it. Both of you are pushing from the top.”

She lifted.

The invisible shape caught her beneath the chin and rolled her backward over a sack of flour. She landed on her bottom with a soft whump. A white cloud expanded from the sack, rose past her ears, and remained there in the shape of a very large loaf.

Lupa sat in its shadow.

“I have a fairly straightforward complaint about the cakes.”

Cartethyia was already crouching beside her, one hand offered. Lupa caught it by the forearm and came up laughing, then brushed at her trousers.

Each stroke made another flour loaf.

By the fourth, Amy was sitting on the floor.

Ciaccona lowered her lute against her hip.

“I can take us back to the yard. There is tea, and Carte bought the real thing yesterday.”

Chixia looked up from the counter. She had spent the last minute pressing a spoon against the cake's enormous invisible boundary.

“I'd like to keep going. I've got a comfortable chair, and I want to see what happens to Septimont.”

“You'll see it from the outer gate,” Lupa said. “I'm going in first.”

She rubbed a palm over her hair.

A final loaf rose from between her ears.

***

Septimont announced itself with forge smoke, sun-warmed basalt, and a crowd applauding an empty patch of road.

The applause swelled when Lupa stepped onto it.

She stopped. Several heads turned. A woman in the front row put two fingers in her mouth and whistled.

Lupa's ears lifted.

“That's the south approach,” she said. “People used to wait here after the afternoon practice.”

She raised a hand. The crowd cheered. Someone at the back called her name with the second syllable stretched into a howl, and she laughed, turning toward the sound.

Chisa heard the change in her steps as she went forward: the weight settling farther back, the pause before the turn. She remembered Lupa in the doorway with Mrs Ren's towel at her waist, showing how the blue coat moved.

“That's the walk you showed us,” she said. “You leave time for them to follow you.”

Lupa glanced back.

“They spent the whole morning standing here. You give them something to take home.”

Above her, a billboard lit up.

It showed Lupa holding a bottle of soda.

It also showed the violet coat Amy had drawn for Chisa, complete with the elaborate collar of feathers.

The billboard Lupa turned. Across the coat's back, in enormous black handwriting, appeared the words *ACTUAL BOOK*, with an arrow pointing toward her bottom.

Amy caught Chisa's sleeve.

“My drawing's in it. Cia, you put the whole sheet in.”

Ciaccona looked up at the advertisement. Her mouth opened a little.

“Carte used it to hold down the wardrobe pages while I was working.”

“I thought it would help you remember the cloth,” Cartethyia said. “We were all talking about it.”

On the billboard, Lupa took a long drink. The feathers swayed around her ears.

The real Lupa stood beneath it, examining the cut.

“The shoulder sits well. Amy, turn your drawing around when we get home. I want to see what you did at the back.”

“I can give you a matching one,” Amy said. “You've already got the advertising.”

Lupa put a hand on Chisa's shoulder and turned her gently toward the screen.

“Watch the hem when she moves. That's the opening I meant.”

Chisa watched the coloured shape widen at each turn. The coat followed the movement cleanly. Beneath her collar, she felt a small warmth that had very little to do with Septimont's sun.

Then every spectator acquired the coat.

Violet spread through the crowd in a great soft ripple. Feathers rose around a hundred necks. The woman who had whistled began applauding again, caught both hands inside her cuffs, and continued with muffled determination.

Mei ducked her face into Iuno's living shoulder.

“I want you to know I'm trying to behave. That man over there has got a feather in each nostril.”

Iuno's hand rested briefly against the back of her head.

“Take your time. I have found an equally absorbing woman beside the fountain.”

The woman was trying to drink through her collar. Each attempt turned her cup upside down. The water fell upward into her hat.

*At last, a reception worth attending.*

Ciaccona eased two notes apart. The crowd's coats faded back into tunics, jackets, and work clothes. The billboard retained its feathers.

“I'll leave that one for Chisa to examine,” she said. “The rest of the city needs its elbows.”

Lupa led them toward the arena.

At the entrance, a young attendant held a victory wreath at chest height. He bowed as Lupa approached, extended his arms, and placed the wreath on her head.

His hands returned to his chest with another wreath between them.

He bowed again.

Lupa caught his wrist.

“I've got mine. You can give the next one to Mei.”

The attendant's arm passed through her fingers. The second wreath settled on the first. A third appeared against his chest.

Amy stepped across the entrance line.

The attendant doubled his pace.

She stepped back.

He slowed.

She looked at Ciaccona.

“Oh, I know this one. Katya kept congratulating us whenever Ma went back for the chest. The doorway thinks we've arrived again.”

Lupa took a step away from the attendant. He glided after her, bowing at regular intervals. His feet moved beneath him with the brisk, pointless effort of someone walking on ice.

“Amy, bring your arrival somewhere else. I've got leaves going down my shirt.”

Amy moved farther from the entrance. The attendant continued.

Lupa backed toward the arena. He followed faster.

She turned and jogged.

He tilted forward until his body was almost horizontal, still bowing from the waist. A stream of wreaths came from his hands. Lupa rounded the first pillar with him level with her shoulder.

Chixia whistled.

The sound cracked across the approach with the authority of a busy junction.

Every spectator turned toward her.

The attendant shot past Lupa and came to a halt at Chixia's footstool. He bowed, extended his arms, and laid a wreath on her knee.

Chixia accepted it.

“Thank you. You've done a very thorough job. I'll take charge of distribution from here.”

She took the next wreath as well. Yangyang began passing them along the row of chairs and hands forming around her.

Lupa came back through the gate, pulling leaves from her collar.

“We used to have a man like that with the interview requests.”

Ciaccona lifted the phrase that governed the attendant. He settled into a bow and stayed there.

Sixteen wreaths lay on Chixia's lap.

Cartethyia put one on Ciaccona. The bard held still while she tucked it around the lute strap.

“You've been working all afternoon,” Cartethyia said. “You should have something too.”

The arena horn sounded.

In the open space beyond the gate, two enormous wooden training dummies slid into position. One had a spear. The other had the texture of a lemon cake, sugar sparkling along its shoulders.

Lupa turned toward it.

“I would like five minutes with that one.”

***

They spent eight.

At first, the cake dummy stood rigid while Lupa circled it, evaluating the reach of its invisible bulk. Mei went to the opposite side and tapped it with the end of a borrowed practice sword. The blow made the arena horn sound again.

Both dummies applauded.

The sword bent around Mei's wrist like a ribbon.

Chixia called advice from the front row while Yangyang held her growing collection of wreaths. Baizhi placed a folded piece of paper at the point where Lupa's foot had first met resistance. Then she moved it outward twice.

“Its boundary expands when the audience claps.”

Amy slowly lowered her hands.

Across the arena, the spectators continued applauding.

The cake dummy swelled. Its wooden feet stayed the same size.

Lupa looked toward Ciaccona, who had begun picking her way through the arena music with the expression of someone separating two necklaces.

“Give me a clear patch behind it. I'll try the weight from there.”

She ducked beneath the dummy's arm, put her shoulder against its back, and heaved.

The dummy tipped.

Its tiny feet left the sand.

For a moment it hung at an angle, sugar glittering in the sun. Then it spun upward, pulling Lupa with it by the front of her jacket.

Mei grabbed for her belt. Her hand closed on fabric just as the dummy completed its first rotation.

All three rose together.

They turned slowly above the arena, a cake, a wolf, and a woman holding a practice sword that now trailed beneath them in an elegant corkscrew.

Lupa's boots pedalled at empty air.

“Mei, let go of my trousers. You're taking them on ahead.”

“I've got your jacket as well. There are several layers involved in keeping you decent.”

Cartethyia stood.

Wind lifted her hair. Her laurel brightened, her body lengthened, and Fleurdelys stepped across the front row with her skirt sweeping over three discarded wreaths.

She reached up and caught the dummy by one ankle.

The rotating bundle stopped. Lupa dangled head downward at a comfortable distance from Fleurdelys's face.

“You may release the confection,” Fleurdelys said. Her deep voice carried to the upper galleries. “I have secured your retreat.”

Lupa unhooked her jacket. Fleurdelys lowered her into the sand, then presented a palm beneath Mei's feet.

Mei stepped down, looked up at the enormous dummy held overhead, and began laughing so hard she had to brace her hands on her knees.

“Carte, you look like you're taking the bins out.”

Fleurdelys examined the dummy. Then she drew back her arm and threw it over the arena wall.

A small, distant bell rang.

From somewhere in Ragunna, the gull asked after somebody's shirt.

***

The road to Honami opened beneath a sign advertising noodles.

Ciaccona checked the transition twice. Baizhi carried her chair through first, set it down, and sat to test the street. Chixia followed on her moving disc, with a wreath hanging from each armrest. Cartethyia had returned to her smaller body and was helping Lupa extract one final leaf from the back of her collar.

Beyond the sign, the air grew damp. Honami's buildings rose close on either side of the street, with shop awnings, cables, and coloured advertisements crowding the view overhead. Cherry petals travelled upward past a traffic light.

Chisa's steps slowed.

She turned toward the bookstore.

“The door stuck against the second tile. We used to lift it by the handle.”

Ciaccona laid her thumb against a string, holding the street at a quiet hum.

“I have the entrance and the first room. You can tell me how far you'd like to go.”

Chisa moved to the door. Her hand settled on its handle. She lifted as she pulled, and it opened with a scrape she felt through her wrist.

Inside were the shelves, the counter, the patch of floor where afternoon light came through the glass. A stack of magazines leaned against a table leg.

She stood in the doorway for a little while.

“The yellow one belongs on top,” she said. “Sumika kept going back to an article in it.”

Ciaccona changed a note. The yellow magazine slid upward through the pile and settled above the others.

Chisa touched the edge of its cover.

Lupa waited on the pavement, one hand resting on the door to keep it clear.

After a moment, Chisa turned toward them.

“I'd like to show Chixia the machine across the road. We got coffee there.”

The vending machine occupied a little recess under a blue awning. Ciaccona had rendered every button with exquisite care. Tiny drops of condensation stood on the picture of the iced coffee. A speaker above the coin slot played a cheerful four-note tune.

Chixia fished a coin from her pocket.

“Does it take these, or have I come abroad with the wrong money?”

“Press whichever button you like,” Ciaccona said. “I've already paid for the afternoon.”

Chixia selected coffee.

The machine clicked. Somewhere inside it, a can rolled down a chute.

A full-sized Iuno stepped out through the collection flap.

She wore a student's jacket and held a little paper form. Her head turned toward Mei.

“Please state your reasons for applying to Rabelle College.”

The real Iuno looked at Ciaccona.

“I should be interested to hear the answer. At the time, my principal qualification was arriving with luggage.”

Amy reached past the imitation and pressed another button.

A second Iuno emerged holding the same form. She stepped sideways into the first. Their hair occupied the same space, flickering between two shades of blue.

Amy bit her lip, considered the buttons, and pressed the one marked hot milk.

This Iuno arrived wearing the feathered coat.

“I wanted to know whether the choice mattered,” Amy said.

Baizhi wrote something down.

“It affects the clothing. We should preserve that observation.”

The three Iunos arranged themselves in a queue before Mei.

Mei looked from their forms to her partner.

“Should I mention family connections? It feels as though I've got a chance with the admissions committee.”

“Tell them about your attendance,” Iuno said. “They may enjoy a work of imagination.”

Chisa crouched beside the machine and put her hand under its little door.

“The can used to stick here. You had to lift the flap all the way and give the side a push.”

Ciaccona leaned close enough to see where she was pointing. She sounded a pair of notes. Chisa pressed the side panel.

The machine gave a metallic sigh and dispensed a coffee.

The three Iunos folded neatly at the waist, slid backward through the collection flap, and vanished. One feather remained against the grille.

Chixia took the can from Chisa.

“You remembered exactly where to hit it. How long did that take to learn?”

“Sumika got her first drink immediately. I spent several days choosing the same button she used, because she insisted it had a preference.”

Chisa looked at the blue awning. A petal passed its edge, rose another foot, and turned into a tiny loaf of bread.

“She enjoyed watching me try.”

Lupa leaned a shoulder against the machine.

“I'd have liked to see you making your case to it.”

“I gave it a great deal of useful information.”

Chisa's mouth moved at one corner. She took the can when Chixia offered it and turned it between her hands.

Across the street, a recorded version of Mei appeared beneath the traffic signal. She walked toward the crossing with both arms held straight out from her shoulders.

Her legs stayed together. She glided over the asphalt like a woman being carried on a tray.

Chisa turned at the familiar tread, then tilted her head.

“The footsteps are Lupa's.”

The likeness passed through a lamp post. On its far side, Mei's face changed to Cartethyia's. The stride remained audible beneath her motionless feet.

Amy followed it down the pavement, matching the rigid arms.

“This happened to Katya's horse after we put the winter saddle on. Ma rode it halfway up a church.”

Mei came alongside her daughter and extended her own arms.

“We reached the bell. I remember being very pleased with the view.”

Mother and daughter glided past Chixia with small, shuffling steps. The imitation turned at the corner, went through the wall of a pharmacy, and continued along the inside of its display window.

Chixia laughed into her wreath.

Yangyang had been watching the signal.

“It follows the pedestrian light,” she said. “Every time it turns green, she begins that section again.”

Ciaccona touched the melody governing the crossing.

The light turned red.

Mei and Amy froze with their arms extended.

For two breaths the street grew quiet.

Then Amy turned her eyes toward Ciaccona.

“You've caught the audience as well. I was planning to put my arms down before supper.”

Ciaccona winced.

Lupa came up behind Amy and tested an elbow. It moved easily under her hand, then rose back to shoulder height the moment she released it.

“The pose has got ideas,” she reported. “Try giving them something to hold.”

Cartethyia put the coffee can in Mei's hand. Mei's arm bent, brought it to her mouth, and extended again. The can stayed at her lips.

She looked sideways at Iuno.

Iuno removed it, took a small drink, and passed it to Baizhi.

“We shall discuss the convenience of this arrangement later.”

Ciaccona picked out the crossing tune more slowly. The traffic light turned green. Mei's arms dropped. Amy's followed, and both of them rubbed their shoulders.

“I'm beginning to understand the appeal of sitting on that horse,” Amy said.

Chixia guided her chair toward the road.

Its legs began running.

They were still wooden, with the same carved little feet Mrs Ren polished every week. They simply bent in the middle and took off, carrying Chixia across the crossing at the pace of a determined dog. The footstool galloped after her.

Chixia gripped the armrests. Her wreaths streamed behind her.

“Baizhi, I am continuing to follow your advice about staying seated!”

Yangyang reached the opposite pavement first. She caught the chair back as the signal changed, while Baizhi came after the footstool. The four wooden legs straightened. Chixia sat very upright, breathing through her nose.

Baizhi knelt, checked the cushion beneath her leg, and rested a hand over her ankle.

“Tell me where it pulled.”

“Mostly at my authority. The leg feels the same.”

Yangyang's cuff brushed Chixia's fingers. The cloth trembled with a quick current, and her cap slid over one eye.

She pushed it back.

“You enjoyed that. I felt you coming along for the ride.”

The cuff tucked itself against the chair arm.

Behind them, Ciaccona had sat down on the kerb with her lute across her knees. She stared at the traffic light.

Then her shoulders began shaking.

Cartethyia sat beside her. At first she watched Ciaccona's hands. When the bard bent forward with a laugh that came out almost silently, Cartethyia took the lute's neck and held it steady.

“Keep breathing, Cia. I've got this part.”

It took Ciaccona several attempts to answer.

“I gave her the walking accompaniment.”

She looked up at Chixia's chair. One of its feet tapped twice against the pavement.

That finished her for another minute.

***

Lahai-Roi arrived with a warm wash of gold.

Helios hung above the steppe. Beyond the restaurant forecourt, terraces of luminous grass descended toward crystal outcrops. The dome of the Observatory stood in the distance, its rim catching the artificial sunlight.

Amy went ahead to the low barrier beside the parking spaces.

“Come over here. It opens up when you get past the sign.”

Chixia's chair slid sedately after her. Ciaccona watched every inch of its journey.

Yangyang stopped beside Amy. Baizhi joined them, her notebook open against one forearm. For a while they looked across the terraces.

The grass moved in slow waves. The light warmed Chixia's cheek. She turned her head to follow a line of bright crystals down the hillside.

“You had this outside a chicken restaurant.”

“Ma kept stopping in the car park,” Amy said. “Sometimes we'd be halfway to the bikes when somebody noticed.”

Mei rested her forearms on the barrier.

“The food took a while to settle. I found a good place to do it.”

Ciaccona loosened her shoulders. The melody here was one she knew almost by touch. Her fingers settled along the neck, following the scar beneath the strings.

The Observatory began to turn.

A thread of light rose from its lens and struck the cavern ceiling. Bright fragments spread overhead, falling slowly across the false sky.

Cartethyia put a hand on Chixia's shoulder and pointed.

“There. Watch the blue ones when they reach the crystal.”

Chixia looked up.

One blue spark lengthened, flashed, and became a lemon cake.

Another followed it. Then a third.

The sky filled with cakes.

They descended in stately, luminous arcs, shedding sugar. A large one drifted past the Observatory and caught on its dome. Three smaller cakes struck the barrier and rebounded gently into the grass.

Lupa closed her eyes.

“That thing followed me across two countries.”

Mei lowered her forehead onto her arms.

Amy stood with a falling cake cupped between her hands. Its sugar cast little blue lights over her fingers.

“Carte, yours is coming true in bulk.”

Cartethyia took it from her. She tried a careful bite.

The cake rang softly between her teeth.

She considered the sensation, swallowed, and offered it to Ciaccona.

“It tastes like the high string feels. I think I prefer Rosa's.”

Ciaccona let the sky finish falling.

Behind them, an automatic door opened. A restaurant server came out carrying a tray. He walked to the centre of the forecourt and addressed the parked motorcycles.

“Your table is ready. Please come through while everything is hot.”

Every motorcycle fell over.

The server went inside.

The motorcycles stood up again.

Ciaccona stared at the door.

Chixia turned her chair toward it.

“I'd like to see the restaurant. Which table did you usually have?”

“Far side, near the window,” Amy said. “Lupa always wanted the end with room for her legs.”

She took Chixia's chair by the back and guided it across the forecourt. The others followed, stepping around the motorcycles as the server came out and invited them to dinner again.

Inside the restaurant was a table.

Beyond the table, the room dissolved into pale, closely ruled lines. The ceiling had four pencilled measurements and a grease mark. An unfinished arch occupied one corner, its stones ending beside the words *check this with Amy*.

Six chairs surrounded the table. A seventh place contained a music stand.

On the tabletop, Ciaccona's handwriting read:

*Supper. Everyone talked for ages. Continue with the Observatory.*

Chixia looked at the words.

Then she turned toward Ciaccona.

“I've been waiting all afternoon to hear this bit.”

Ciaccona moved her thumb across the lute's neck.

“There were several evenings. I've joined them for the view outside.”

“Which one had the best food?”

Lupa pulled out the chair at the end. It came away from the table with a satisfyingly ordinary scrape.

“The evening they brought the platter twice. Mei said we'd sort it out among ourselves.”

“We managed,” Mei said. She sat beside Iuno, leaving room for Amy at her other side. “There was a method by the end.”

“You put the bones on Iuno's bread plate.”

“She had the nearest empty plate. I thought we were sharing the arrangement.”

Iuno looked across the table at Chixia.

“I discovered my responsibilities when I reached for a roll.”

The pale lines beside the table deepened. A bread basket appeared. Behind it, part of the window acquired a frame.

Baizhi stepped closer to look at the new wood.

Ciaccona felt the change through her fingertips. She tried the next chord.

The server emerged through the unfinished wall and placed an empty platter on the table. He returned through the same gap. Beyond it, something began sizzling.

Amy looked at the platter.

“They put the sauce in a little dish shaped like a bird. Carte kept turning it so it could watch people.”

“It had a face,” Cartethyia said. “They'd gone to the trouble.”

A ceramic bird appeared beside the platter, its beak filled with sauce. Cartethyia turned it toward Chixia.

Yangyang brought Baizhi's chair over from the entrance. The room stretched to receive it. Chixia's chair settled beside the table, footstool tucked beneath. Yangyang took the space on her other side and laid her cuff along the armrest.

Ciaccona remained standing by the music stand.

Chisa reached between the chairs and moved the music stand aside. Then she rested her hand on the table in the space it had occupied.

“You sat here,” she said. “You kept moving when the light got in your eyes.”

“I was playing for part of it.”

“After we ate. You had sauce on your cuff, and Carte held it away from the strings.”

Cartethyia looked up from the bird dish.

“I remember that. You kept trying to look at the stain while your arm was still in the sleeve.”

Mei laughed and reached for Ciaccona's notebook, which hung in its little bag from the lute strap.

“May I have the page? I've thought of something your restaurant needs.”

Ciaccona let her take it.

Mei opened the notebook to the supper passage. Beneath the two sentences, she wrote with the pencil Baizhi offered her. Her handwriting crowded the margin.

The music stand became a chair.

It had a blue cushion, an unnecessarily high back, and a small uneven star carved into the wood. Chisa slid it back into place.

Ciaccona leaned over to read.

*Cia sits down long enough to eat while the food is hot.*

“You've made a very ambitious demand on the furniture,” she said.

Mei put the pencil down.

“Try it. We can change the cushion if it gets ideas.”

Ciaccona sat.

The chair held her comfortably. She rested the lute against her knee, and Cartethyia moved the platter farther from its body.

For a moment, Ciaccona listened to the room. Lupa was explaining the portions with her hands. Amy had begun drawing the rest of the restaurant on the blank part of the page, asking Chisa about the counter as she worked. Iuno moved the bread basket within Chixia's reach.

Ciaccona's thumb found the next note.

A lamp came on above them.

The restaurant acquired a ceiling, two more windows, and a slightly crooked picture of a chicken. The server appeared beside the table with a platter piled high enough that Lupa spread her hands in vindication.

Baizhi leaned toward the food, interested.

“Does the taste become more precise as the account develops?”

“We can spend the afternoon finding out,” Chixia said. “I want to hear how they got the second platter past this one.”

Ciaccona looked toward the window.

Outside, Helios had settled above the terraces. A cake remained caught on the Observatory. Along the forecourt, the motorcycles lay on their sides in an orderly row.

She left them there.

Lupa passed her a plate. Cartethyia held her cuff back while she reached.

***

Mrs Ren called through the doorway while Amy was describing the eating contest.

At the sound of their hostess's voice, everyone looked toward the same place. A rectangle of yard opened beside the restaurant window. The broom stood against the wall. The real kitchen smelled of onions and broth.

Ciaccona sounded the homeward phrase.

The restaurant thinned around them. Warm gold passed over the table, the platter, the bird-shaped dish. Chixia looked down as the wreaths on her chair became curls of sound and drifted away.

Then they were in the yard, crowded around Mrs Ren's borrowed furniture. The lute gave a final small vibration against Ciaccona's leg.

Baizhi checked Chixia's cushion. Yangyang carried the footstool inside. Lupa picked up the chair with its passenger, paused at the kitchen door, and waited while Amy turned it at the angle Mrs Ren preferred.

“How was the journey?” Mrs Ren asked.

Chixia settled at the table. Her cap had acquired a crease across the crown, and she smoothed it with her palm.

“I've seen four countries, won the Great Agon several times, and complied with every instruction Baizhi gave me about my leg. I'd like the big bowl, please.”

Mrs Ren looked at Baizhi.

“The leg is doing well,” Baizhi said. “The chair requires further observation.”

She set her notebook beside her place and went to wash her hands.

Ciaccona stayed in the yard long enough to put the lute away. Her fingers had begun drawing inward again. Cartethyia waited with the case open, then closed its clasps while Ciaccona flexed her hand.

From the kitchen came Chixia's voice, asking Mei where she had sat on the ship. Lupa began answering before Mei had finished pulling out her chair. Amy objected to some detail. Yangyang asked how they kept the cups steady.

Ciaccona reached for her notebook.

Cartethyia put it on top of the instrument case and took her hand.

“You can ask them again after supper. Come and sit where I can see your sleeve.”

Inside, Mei had left a chair between herself and Cartethyia. A clean bowl waited in front of it. Iuno reached past the bowl to set down a cup, using her living hand, and Ciaccona moved the spoon clear of her wrist.

Chixia leaned across the table.

“When you've had a few days to rest your hands, I'd like another trip. Yangyang says there's a harbour road you left out, and Baizhi wants to take a different pebble.”

“I have three measurements to confirm,” Baizhi said.

Ciaccona picked up her spoon.

“I'll put you both on the passenger list. Someone else can choose the transport.”

Chixia reached for the bread.

Her chair took one small step toward the table.

She lowered her eyes. Around her, the conversation ebbed.

The chair waited.

Chixia put her good foot firmly on the floor.

“I like sitting this far out,” she told it. “Give me room to eat.”

Under the table, four wooden feet shuffled back into place.

Ciaccona set her spoon down very carefully.

Amy lasted almost three seconds.
