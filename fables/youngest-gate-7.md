# Youngest Gate 7

## 夜話 — NIGHT TALK

### THE SAME TUESDAY, AFTER

## THE SHRINE STREET

Narrator: The harbour ward at midnight smells of wet stone and somebody's late frying, and the lamps are down to the low ones, which throws everything four feet up the walls and leaves the rest alone.

Amy and Cartethyia went ahead, forty metres and arguing, Amy walking backwards for whole stretches of it, which she can do now, and does, because for three years she could not.

Mei stayed on the step behind them with Yangyang and a ledger, so it was Lupa and Iuno for the first part, at a walking pace.

<font color=indianred>"Left side."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"…"</font>

<font color=indianred>"Eleven times since the door."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Nine."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Eleven. I started at the hatch."</font> Lupa's tail moved once. <font color=indianred>"You do it when you're deciding something. You did it four times at that table and three of them were while the Magistrate was talking."</font>

Iuno walked about six paces.

<font color=royalblue>"How long."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Second port."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You have said nothing for two hundred and eleven kilometres."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Mm."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Why."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Wasn't mine."</font>

A cat came out of a doorway, considered the wolf, revised its evening, and went back in.

<font color=royalblue>"Amy knows as well."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Amy's known since the landing. She watched you do it and she shut her mouth, which for her is a religious act."</font> Lupa stepped over a gutter without looking at it. <font color=indianred>"Chisa knows. Chisa knew before you did."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Cartethyia."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Cartethyia thinks you've got a knife in there."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"…Why does Cartethyia think that."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Because when she asked, you said *it's private*, and in Cartethyia's experience the private thing is always a knife."</font>

Iuno made a sound that had some laugh in it.

---

Narrator: They came up past the second bridge where the flute lives on a sill. The sill was empty tonight and the window shut, which Iuno noted and filed and kept.

She had come out of Panhua's last, because of the ledger, and Mrs Ren had taken it back off her at the door with the pages the right way round, and had held onto the other end of it for a second longer than the handover required.

*"Whatever's in the coat,"* Mrs Ren had said, in Huanglong, at conversational volume, to a woman she has known nineteen days. *"It doesn't get lighter."*

And then she had gone off up the lane on Little Guo's arm with her stick going and had said nothing else about anything at all.

<font color=royalblue>"Say the thing you are going to say."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Which one."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You have been holding one since the hatch."</font>

Lupa walked a while with her ears doing the slow sweep they do — front, side, back, front.

<font color=indianred>"Feed her first."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"…"</font>

<font color=indianred>"That's it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That is the entire counsel."</font>

<font color=indianred>"That's the whole thing. She's been up since the fourth hour, she's had a bad afternoon in a nice chair, and she's had four Moments today and I stopped two of them myself."</font> Lupa put her hands in her pockets. <font color=indianred>"She's full. Don't put another one on her tonight."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You are managing me."</font>

<font color=indianred>"I'm managing the room. You're in the room."</font>

*She has a point, darling,* said the High Priestess, from her lounge, with her feet up. *An unwelcome quantity of point.*

*Yes.*

*I did want to be present for it.*

*You are inconveniently unavoidable.*

*I have been practising a face.*

<font color=royalblue>"There is a second thing,"</font> Iuno said aloud. <font color=royalblue>"You have been holding two."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Don't make it a Moment."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That contradicts your first."</font>

<font color=indianred>"It doesn't."</font> Lupa stopped at the foot of the terrace stair and turned round, and in the low lamp her eyes did the thing they do, which is catch. <font color=indianred>"Feeding somebody's ordinary. Keep the whole thing that size. It's Tuesday. Do it on a Tuesday."</font>

Iuno stood on the bottom step and looked at her for about a second and a half.

<font color=royalblue>"That is better advice than I expected and I resent the whole of it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Mm."</font>

---

Narrator: Halfway up the terraces Iuno said the other thing, because she had decided at the second bridge that she would, and because she does that now.

<font color=royalblue>"You sold her interval."</font>

Lupa kept climbing.

<font color=royalblue>"You gave that woman a number that belongs to Mei's body, and you did it in a field, and you took a clause for it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Does Mei know."</font>

<font color=indianred>"She'll know when it's read out. It's in a day-book with her house in it and a date on it and my name under it, which is more than it was this morning."</font> Lupa took the last four steps two at a time and waited at the top. <font color=indianred>"This morning it was a thing four governments knew and none of us could point at."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That is a defence."</font>

<font color=indianred>"It's the reason. Defence is what you do after."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Was it worth it."</font>

Lupa looked out over the terraces, where the lamp-grid was down to its bones and the harbour was one long dark line with four riding-lights on it.

<font color=indianred>"Ask me in eleven years."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I intend to."</font>

<font color=indianred>"I know. That's why I gave you a number."</font>

They went up the lane where the guesthouse is. Mrs Ren's lamp was on over the door, because Mrs Ren leaves it on, because she was let into a house at eleven at night in a bad year and has had opinions about door-lamps ever since.

At the door Lupa said, <font color=indianred>"Fourth step from the top."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I am aware of the fourth step."</font>

<font color=indianred>"You forgot it on Sunday."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I did forget it on Sunday."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Mm."</font>

## THE OTHER LANE

Narrator: The Magistrate of Jinzhou walked home.

There is a chair. There has been a chair available to that office for two hundred years, and it lives in a shed behind the west court with a man whose whole employment is the chair, and it goes out perhaps nine times a year, and it stayed in tonight because nobody had been told to send it and Jinhsi had not thought to say.

Changli walked on her left, one pace back, which is where a Counselor walks, and which she had done for so long that walking anywhere else had stopped being available to her.

Sanhua walked four paces behind both of them and was, professionally speaking, a piece of the lane.

<font color=orangered>"Wen has the receipts. The strongbox goes into the second vault tonight and I have the tally in my coat; the second column came out four hundred and eleven short and I know where the four hundred and eleven is. It is a man who cannot read a schedule and has been carrying the difference in his own head for six weeks."</font>

<font color=gold>"Mm."</font>

<font color=orangered>"I will have him taught."</font>

<font color=gold>"Mm."</font>

<font color=orangered>"The seed-stock purchases went through at Schedule Four in nine villages and stuck in two, and the two are the two with the temple land, and I expected that, and I have a way round it that will take four days and offend a Deputy."</font>

<font color=gold>"Which Deputy."</font>

<font color=orangered>"The one you like."</font>

<font color=gold>"…Offend him."</font>

They went up the shrine street and turned at the top where the ward changes and the paving gets older and the lane narrows to the width of two people who know each other.

Jinhsi stopped at a shop window.

---

Narrator: It is Old Feng's, on the corner, and it shuts at five, and it had been shut for six hours, and the shutter is a half-shutter, so the top of the window shows.

The window is paper goods. Funeral paper. White and folded and stacked the way a stationer stacks: paper houses with paper doors, four to a rack; paper horses; a paper granary with a paper roof; and along the front of the display, in a row, at the height of a person's hand, eleven paper people, three inches high, white, folded, made to be burned.

Jinhsi looked at them.

In her coat pocket, against her hip, folded out of the blank margin of something, was a paper person three inches high that a woman with a cane had put into her hand at a doorway with the information that everyone gets one.

Changli stood at her shoulder and looked at the side of Jinhsi's face for the whole of it, which took about eleven seconds, and then looked at the lane, and then the eleven seconds were over.

<font color=orangered>"Feng's daughter runs the counter now. He has the back."</font>

<font color=gold>"…"</font>

<font color=orangered>"They do a good horse."</font>

<font color=gold>"They do."</font>

Jinhsi took her hand out of her pocket empty and started walking again.

---

Narrator: Fifty metres on, without any change of pace:

<font color=gold>"Move the fourth-hour thing on Thursday."</font>

<font color=orangered>"The Bureau of Waterways."</font>

<font color=gold>"Move it."</font>

<font color=orangered>"To when."</font>

<font color=gold>"Four in the afternoon is taken."</font>

Changli walked three paces.

<font color=orangered>"Taken by."</font>

<font color=gold>"A lane off the shrine street."</font>

<font color=orangered>"…"</font>

<font color=gold>"The oil's fresh at four. Nobody's in."</font>

<font color=orangered>"I will have to write something in the book."</font>

<font color=gold>"Write what it is."</font>

<font color=orangered>"The Magistrate of Jinzhou will attend a noodle house in the harbour ward at the ninth hour on Thursday for the purpose of eating fried food."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

<font color=orangered>"That will be read by four clerks and copied by two."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

Changli took out a small book with a pencil in the spine and wrote it down, walking, without slowing, in the hand she uses for the book, which presses hard on the down-strokes.

<font color=orangered>"…It is a good entry,"</font> she said, and put the book away.

---

Narrator: At the turn where the terraces start, Changli said:

<font color=orangered>"Your hand."</font>

<font color=gold>"It is fine."</font>

<font color=orangered>"You have had it shut since the third hour. You ate one-handed for four hours in a room with a physician in it."</font>

Jinhsi walked to the next lamp, and stopped in it, and opened her left hand.

Four marks, in a curve, at the base of the fingers, gone from red to the colour they go.

Changli looked at them for as long as it takes to look at four marks.

She did not ask what he had said. She has been Counselor seven years and she knows the difference between a thing a person is carrying and a thing a person is holding. You can take the first off somebody. Reaching for the second costs you the hand.

<font color=orangered>"Baizhi saw it."</font>

<font color=gold>"I know."</font>

<font color=orangered>"She wrote ninety-one and the word *near* and shut the book."</font>

<font color=gold>"…I know."</font>

Jinhsi closed her hand again, gently, the way you close a book you are still in.

<font color=gold>"Tomorrow,"</font> she said.

<font color=orangered>"Tomorrow."</font>

<font color=gold>"Where."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Your office. Door shut. No clerk, no runner, no Sanhua."</font>

Four paces behind them Sanhua registered her own name and continued to be a piece of the lane.

<font color=gold>"Will I be angry."</font>

Changli walked to the next lamp before she answered, which is a thing she does, and which Jinhsi has known about since she was fifteen and has never once mentioned.

<font color=orangered>"Yes."</font>

<font color=gold>"…Thank you."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Do not thank me for that. You will want it back by the third sentence."</font>

<font color=gold>"Will I still have a Counselor at the end of it."</font>

<font color=orangered>"That will be your ruling."</font>

<font color=gold>"That is a coward's answer."</font>

<font color=orangered>"It is the only honest one I have and I have had seven years to find a better."</font>

They went up two more terraces in a silence with a great deal in it.

At the gate of the compound the escort came out of the dark the way an escort does, and Deng was among them, on the correct side this time, having been spoken to, and wearing the expression of a man to whom the concept of light has been explained at length and with visual aids.

Changli stopped at the gate and stayed outside it.

<font color=orangered>"You ate a great deal."</font>

<font color=gold>"I did."</font>

<font color=orangered>"…Good."</font>

She went off down the terrace toward the second vault, with a strongbox waiting and four hundred and eleven in her coat.

---

Narrator: In the courtyard Sanhua stopped at the foot of the stair while Jinhsi went up, and said the only sentence she said all night that was not about a door.

<font color=lavender>"That was a good room."</font>

Jinhsi paused on the fourth step with her hand on the rail.

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

<font color=lavender>"…"</font>

<font color=gold>"There is a woman there who tells people to eat the fish. I would like her file."</font>

<font color=lavender>"She has one."</font>

<font color=gold>"…Of course she does."</font>

<font color=lavender>"Four pages. None of it is in Huanglong and two pages of it are a Septimont billboard."</font>

<font color=gold>"Bring it up on Thursday. Before the ninth hour."</font>

She went up. The lamp on the stair took her round the turn and let her go.

Sanhua stood in the courtyard a while with the memory of a crease in a paper shoulder, and then went and found Deng, and had a second conversation with him about light.

## THE SINK

Narrator: Yangyang came back at twenty to twelve, because she always does, and Panhua had the front bolted and both hands in a tub.

<font color=peachpuff>"Go home."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Mm."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"I said go home."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"I heard it."</font>

Yangyang hung her hat on the nail that is not a lie, rolled her sleeves, and took the rinse side, which is hers, and has been hers for a year, and which she has never once been offered.

They did nineteen bowls and the two big platters and the wok, which Panhua does herself and which nobody has ever been permitted to touch, in about twenty minutes, with the water going grey and being changed twice.

<font color=peachpuff>"Your foreigners eat like carters."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"They were carters for twenty days."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"The tall one had four bowls of the soup."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Ciaccona."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"She sang the donkey one wrong."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"She sang it Rinascitan."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"She sang it wrong."</font> Panhua put a lid on a pot. <font color=peachpuff>"The old woman had the tune."</font>

---

Narrator: At the end of it Panhua dried her hands and did something she has done perhaps four times in the year, which is stand still in her own kitchen.

<font color=peachpuff>"Fifty-two weeks."</font>

Yangyang went on rinsing.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Fifty-three."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"Mm."</font>

The tap ran. Out in the lane a shutter went over.

<font color=peachpuff>"You want me to keep the corner."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"…"</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"I'm asking because there's a shelf going up in the sixth month and if the corner's staying I'll put the shelf somewhere else."</font>

Yangyang set the last bowl in the rack.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Keep the corner."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"Right."</font>

And that was the whole of the conversation. Panhua went and got a rag. Yangyang stood at the sink with her hands in the water somewhat longer than the water needed.

---

Narrator: At the door, with a parcel that had been made up without discussion and put into her hands in a manner that did not admit of refusal:

<font color=yellowgreen>"How late did the lamp go."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"Which."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"The hinge one."</font>

Panhua looked at her.

<font color=peachpuff>"About a minute."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Over."</font>

<font color=peachpuff>"Over."</font>

Yangyang put the parcel under her arm and took out the ledger and wrote something on the step in the light of the door-lamp, in the second ink, which she has explained to nobody, and which Panhua has never asked about, because Panhua's entire theology is that a woman's second ink is her own business.

Then Yangyang said, <font color=yellowgreen>"Goodnight, Auntie,"</font> and went up the lane.

Panhua bolted the door. Then she went back through and took the third seat's bowl out of the rack, dried it, and put it up on the shelf beside the little stitched cloth bird, where it has been going for about three weeks now, which she has mentioned to nobody, including herself.

## THE TERRACE STAIRS

Narrator: Chixia had an event on the way home.

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Six years."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Yes."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Six *years*, Baizhi, and it's in the *remarks column*."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Yes."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Which is a column."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"It is a column."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"In a *file*. In a *building I work in*."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Volume."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"There is nobody on this stair."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"There is a man on the fourth landing who has been trying to sleep since the second hour and who is now going to be told about a goose."</font>

Chixia managed eleven steps out of pure discipline and then could not manage any further.

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"How did she know."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"She watched it."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"For how long."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"She said once."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"That's not a *length*."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"It is for her."</font>

They came up onto the third terrace where the wall gives you the harbour and the four riding-lights, and Chixia stopped to look at it the way she has stopped to look at it about nine thousand times.

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"I'm going in at six."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"To read the entry."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"…Yes."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Before anyone else is in."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Obviously before anyone else is in, I'm hardly going to be *seen* reading the — "</font> Chixia stopped. <font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"You're doing a face."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"I have one face."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"You've got the *other* one. You've all got an other one, it's a whole — "</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"The operation is at the fifth hour."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"I am not participating."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"You have the diagram."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"I am *holding* the diagram. That's custody. Custody's a whole separate — that's practically *legal*."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Mm."</font>

---

Narrator: Two landings up, Chixia said, in a different register altogether, the way she gets to things:

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"What are you going to write."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Ninety-one."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"And."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Ninety-one, resting, taken at the ninth hour after four hours seated. A recommendation about hours. A recommendation about meals. A note that the subject consented and a note that the subject was told she could refuse."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"That's it."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"That is the report."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Baizhi."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Mm."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"You saw something else."</font>

Baizhi went up four more steps at exactly the pace she had been going.

<font color=paleturquoise>"I see a great deal."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"That's a *yes*."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"It is a sentence about my profession."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"It's a *yes* — "</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Chixia."</font> Baizhi stopped on the landing. <font color=paleturquoise>"If I ever take your wrist in a public room, I would like you to be certain that whatever I find in it goes into a book that nobody reads without your name on the cover. That certainty costs something. What it costs is that you don't get to hear this one."</font>

Chixia opened her mouth.

Then she shut it, and went up two more steps, and said, <font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"…Fine."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Thank you."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"It's a *left hand*, though, isn't it."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Goodnight."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"IT'S THE LEFT — "</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"*Volume.*"</font>

---

Narrator: At Chixia's door, which is a door with four separate things nailed to it, none of them a nameplate:

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"She had sauce on her cuff."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"She did."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"And she just — pushed the sleeve back. Like anybody. Like a person at a table."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"Yes."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"Auntie Panhua brought the bottle down."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"I saw."</font>

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"I've eaten at that house four times a week for six years, Baizhi. That bottle's been down four times in eleven."</font> Chixia had her key in the lock and had stopped turning it. <font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"She didn't say anything about it. She just put it on the tray. Like it was — "</font>

She stopped, because the sentence was going somewhere she had no equipment for, and stood in her own doorway with her key half turned.

<font color=paleturquoise>"Volume,"</font> said Baizhi, gently.

<font color=lightgoldenrodyellow>"…I wasn't being loud."</font>

<font color=paleturquoise>"I know. It was the other thing."</font>

Chixia laughed once, wetly, and said something unrepeatable about the goose, and went inside.

Baizhi stood on the landing a moment and got out the notebook and wrote *91* and *near*.

Then she turned it over and went in at the back, where there are four pages in a different hand that she has shown to nobody in her life, and wrote one line on the fourth of them, and put the pencil through the loop, and went home.

## THE ROOF

Narrator: The guesthouse roof is flat, and has a low parapet, and gets the whole east market and about a third of the harbour, and has two cups on it most nights.

Ciaccona got up there first, with the lute, and sat down against the parapet, and did not play anything.

Chisa arrived four minutes later and stood.

<font color=teal>"I'm going to say it now."</font>

<font color=coral>"Mm."</font>

<font color=teal>"And you can't stop me, because Lupa's at the stairhead and I have counted the dumplings and she has run out."</font>

<font color=coral>"I have a dumpling."</font>

<font color=teal>"…"</font>

<font color=coral>"She gave me four. I have eaten one."</font>

<font color=teal>"You've been *holding three dumplings* for an hour and a half."</font>

<font color=coral>"Yes."</font>

<font color=teal>"For this."</font>

<font color=coral>"For anything."</font>

Ciaccona put her head back against the parapet and laughed at the sky for a while.

---

Narrator: Then she said it, because she had been carrying it since about eight o'clock and it had got heavier through the evening in the way an unsaid thing does.

<font color=teal>"A room like that makes a note."</font>

<font color=coral>"…"</font>

<font color=teal>"Sixteen people, one small space, low ceiling, wet plaster, and all of them going at once. That's a chord. It's a working chord — it's what a hull sounds like, it's meant to hold, it's meant to be *ugly* and hold."</font> Ciaccona had the lute flat across her knees and her palm resting on the boards of it. <font color=teal>"And every chord goes somewhere. That's the whole of it, that's the only law I've got. Strike one string and every string tuned to it sounds, whether or not anybody touched it."</font>

<font color=coral>"Mm."</font>

<font color=teal>"So who's tuned to that room."</font>

Chisa stood at the parapet with the lane below her and her scissors on her hip and her head at the angle it goes to.

<font color=coral>"The corner has two."</font>

<font color=teal>"Two."</font>

<font color=coral>"Two strings on the third seat. They do not go the same way."</font>

Down in the market a dog was working through its opinions and getting no support from the neighbourhood.

<font color=teal>"Where do they go."</font>

<font color=coral>"One goes up. Out at the seam over the door, up past the eaves, up past the weather. That one is easy. That one has been in the house since the fifteenth and it drinks the tea."</font>

<font color=teal>"And the other."</font>

Chisa turned her head north, along the line of the crest wall, where the towers sit at their intervals and the eighth is the last one that has a bell in it that anybody has heard.

<font color=coral>"North. Along the wall. It does not stop at the eighth."</font>

Ciaccona looked at the back of her head.

<font color=teal>"…How far past."</font>

<font color=coral>"I have not walked it."</font>
