She lifted.

<font color=coral>“You kept the scar.”</font>

<font color=skyblue>“The city has very low ceilings.”</font>

Lupa walked to the tunnel mouth. Heat rolled from her spear. Her ears followed Tollhide’s retreat until the impacts faded under the hill.

<font color=indianred>“Tollhide has settled somewhere ahead.”</font>

Chisa knelt beside the line her blade had cut through stone. She opened and closed the hinge once, listening for damage. The note remained whole.

<font color=coral>“It reached a chamber. The last step returned with open space around it.”</font>

Old Qiao found enough breath for authority. <font color=slategray>“That tunnel ends under the north shelf. We closed it after the survey bells started ringing underground. Thirty years ago, according to my predecessor.”</font>

Ciaccona looked at the tunnel. The blank ruled card inside her sleeve had grown warm.

On its lowest line, a faint grey mark curved like the beginning of a note.

She turned the card facedown against her wrist.

<font color=teal>“Tollhide has chosen a lair with favorable acoustics. I resent a monster with artistic instincts.”</font>

Lupa returned from the tunnel mouth and planted her spear in the dust.

<font color=indianred>“We eat while it cools down.”</font>

Old Qiao blinked at her.

<font color=indianred>“The wardens walk first. Chixia needs your count. We follow after the core is out.”</font> She pointed at the unbroken south path. <font color=indianred>“You bought the time. Spend it going home.”</font>

Qiao looked from the tunnel to his six people. He lifted the hand bell once, studied the crack running through its lip, and tucked it into his sash.

<font color=slategray>“There’s dry cedar in the survey shed. The quarry crew kept salt beside the stove.”</font>

Lupa’s tail swept hard enough to draw a line in the dust.

<font color=indianred>“You continue to be useful, old man.”</font>

---

The survey shed had three walls, half a roof, and a stove built from a retired ore drum. Lupa improved the fire until the other three had to sit close to the open side.

She laid pork in the iron pan. The marinade hit hot metal with a clean hiss. Scallions followed, then mushrooms, then the flat rice cakes along the edge where they could drink the oil slowly. Smoke climbed into the cedar branches and turned the whole abandoned quarry into a kitchen.

Ciaccona unwrapped the bandages from the top of her basket and found four enamel bowls underneath.

<font color=teal>“Panhua packed bowls as well.”</font>

<font color=coral>“She understands Lupa.”</font>

Chisa sat on the threshold with her blade across her knees. She wiped grit from the silver seam, added one drop of Ruan’s oil, and worked the hinge through three slow closures.

Cartethyia tended the camellia cutting. One of its leaves had folded during Fleurdelys’s transformation. She smoothed it between two fingers and turned the terrarium toward the sun.

<font color=skyblue>“You endured the charge bravely, Lady Camellia. I placed you behind a quarry wall for the loudest part, and that was excellent judgment from both of us.”</font>

One of Ciaccona’s Sylphs crept toward the pork.

Lupa tapped it on the head with a wooden spoon. It retreated behind Ciaccona’s boot and waited for weaker government.

<font color=teal>“You have wounded an emissary of art.”</font>

<font color=indianred>“Sylphs get fed after people.”</font>

She divided the pan into four bowls. Cartethyia received the crispest rice cake. Chisa received the mushrooms arranged where her spoon would meet them. Ciaccona received an extra strip of pork after her Sylph made a second diplomatic attempt. Lupa took what remained and sat with her back toward the tunnel.

For several minutes the hunt became chewing, crackling cedar, and the distant bells of Lianshui resuming their evacuation count.

Cartethyia ate one rice cake slowly enough to learn every edge.

<font color=skyblue>“Cia, the quarry song made its throat stumble. Did workers truly use that melody while moving stone?”</font>

<font color=teal>“Pieces survive at the fourth-terrace spice stalls. The modern version has acquired a verse about a handsome inspector and a cabbage. I planned to play the cabbage verse in the wrong mode until somebody became angry enough to teach me the rest.”</font>

<font color=coral>“You found the useful part before reaching the market.”</font>

<font color=teal>“A city’s beliefs have always been generous with their travel arrangements.”</font>

Lupa scraped caramelized bean paste from the pan and tasted it.

Chisa tilted her head toward her. <font color=coral>“Your tail has kept time since the clinic.”</font>

<font color=indianred>“We’re standing over old extraction lines.”</font>

<font color=coral>“The stone stayed quiet during lunch.”</font>

Ciaccona lowered her bowl to hide a smile. Cartethyia smiled freely.

Lupa pointed the spoon at the tunnel. <font color=indianred>“There are people below us packing their homes because that thing crossed a field. We finish the work.”</font>

Her ears stood high. Firelight moved in her eyes. Her tail struck the shed wall on the next beat.

<font color=skyblue>“Of course. The enjoyment remains entirely professional.”</font>

Lupa gave Cartethyia the last rice cake.

<font color=indianred>“Keep talking and you carry the pan.”</font>

Cartethyia accepted both.

---

They entered the extraction tunnel in single file.

Chisa led. The repaired blade stayed open around her body, one half above her shoulder and one near the ground. Every drip, pebble, footfall, and distant resonance returned through the steel. Ciaccona followed with two Sylphs ahead as green lights. Cartethyia carried the pan lashed to her pack. Lupa took the rear, where her fire covered every shadow and preserved Chisa’s hearing.

The tunnel descended through tool marks into older stone. Survey characters appeared on the walls, each one paired with a bronze nail. Many nails had been pulled. The empty holes led north in a line.

At the first branch, Chisa stopped.

<font color=coral>“It dragged the remaining horn along the left wall. The injured leg crosses its own track now. It is turning in a tight space.”</font>

Ciaccona touched the rock. A rhythm passed into her fingertips: scrape, step, three compressed beats.

Then another sound arrived beneath it.

A vast impact rolled through the hill from the north.

The bronze nails rang together.

The blank card burned cold against Ciaccona’s wrist.

Tollhide answered from the chamber ahead.

Its roar carried hunger, pain, and a clean thread of terror.

Lupa’s ears flattened.

Cartethyia’s hand closed around her sword. <font color=skyblue>“That sound reached the creature.”</font>

Chisa listened to the fading northern impact. It traveled through rock with an interval too broad for the creature waiting ahead.

<font color=coral>“Its pulse accelerated. The first path through Lianshui came south.”</font>

Ciaccona drew the ruled card and held it near a bronze nail. A second grey mark wrote itself beside the first. Together they formed the opening interval of a march.

<font color=teal>“There is a player beyond the ridge, and the countryside has begun resonating toward the instrument.”</font>

The tunnel breathed violet light.

Lupa rolled one shoulder and brought her spear forward.

<font color=indianred>“Then this one stops running through people.”</font>

They went in.

---

The final chamber had once housed the quarry’s survey bells.

Hundreds hung from the roof on green-black cords: hand bells, gate bells, tuning cups, cracked gongs, narrow bronze leaves marked with dates. Tollhide crouched beneath them. Its back filled the chamber. The broken shoulder plates glowed red along Lupa’s spear line. One tusk remained. The other side of its jaw leaked molten bronze onto the floor.

The violet core in its throat expanded.

Every bell overhead drew inward.

Ciaccona saw the chamber’s design in a single glance. <font color=teal>“The ceiling will carry its discharge back into the core if the bells answer together. I can give them one pitch for perhaps ten seconds.”</font>

Chisa heard three unstable supports around the monster’s chest. <font color=coral>“It shifts toward whichever plate takes force. We strike from three sides and leave the throat as the only open path.”</font>

Cartethyia set down her rucksack, the camellia, and Panhua’s pan behind a survey plinth.

Light rose around her.

Fleurdelys had to bow beneath the bell cords when she stood. She rolled one shoulder, then the other. The chamber filled with a series of deep, luxurious cracks.

<font color=skyblue>“The stretch continues to justify itself.”</font>

Ciaccona looked up at her. <font color=teal>“I’ll mention that in the casualty report.”</font>

Tollhide charged.

Fleurdelys met the remaining horn with both hands.

The chamber convulsed. Bells screamed out of tune. Stone fractured beneath her heels. She held the horn and walked the creature sideways one step at a time, steering its right shoulder toward Chisa.

Lupa ran along the wall. Fire caught each bronze nail behind her and turned the old survey line into a red constellation. At the final nail she kicked away from stone, spun above Tollhide’s back, and drove her spear through the plate on its left.

The core fled right.

Chisa’s blade closed on the right seam.

The core leapt upward.

Ciaccona struck the quarry song.

Every bell found the workers’ pitch.

The chamber became one bronze voice.

Tollhide released its shockwave.

The bells returned it.

Violet force collapsed into the creature’s throat. The fused metal burst outward in a ring. Fleurdelys tore the final horn from the jaw and flung it clear. Lupa wrenched her spear downward, opening the left plate to the spine. Chisa stepped through the bronze rain with her scissors wide.

The core had one path remaining.

It came toward her.

She heard Sumika’s red string move against her wrist. She heard Ruan’s new bearing carry the weight. She heard Ciaccona holding hundreds of bells inside one song, Cartethyia laughing through Fleurdelys’s deep breath, Lupa’s fire running with two beloved dead at either shoulder.

Chisa closed the blade.

Violet light divided cleanly.

Tollhide took two more steps because its body had already chosen them.

Then its front legs folded.

Its enormous head came down between Fleurdelys’s hands. She guided it away from Chisa and lowered it to stone.

Black resonance lifted from the plates in long ribbons. The basalt body opened into sound: irrigation bells, quarry hammers, boundary pylons, a bird’s last frantic wingbeats, and beneath all of them the northern march pressing south.

Ciaccona changed chords.

She kept the smaller sounds together while the march passed out of them.

Tollhide dissolved.

The survey bells swung above an empty floor.

One after another, they settled.

---

The Echo formed over the divided core as a small black-bronze image of Tollhide, compact enough to fit between two hands. It pawed at the air and shook a square little horn.

Lupa looked at the field terminal.

The Echo looked at Lupa.

Its tiny throat produced three indignant chimes.

Cartethyia had resumed her smaller shape. She crouched beside it, eyes bright. <font color=skyblue>“Oh! It has kept the most argumentative part.”</font>

<font color=teal>“Its loudest frequency won the inheritance.”</font>

Chisa held out the terminal. The Echo climbed into its field with a swirl of bronze light. A small classification wheel spun, failed twice, and displayed **PROVISIONAL OVERLORD: TOLLHIDE**.

Lupa picked up the severed horn. It weighed nearly as much as Cartethyia.

Her tail swept through the lingering dust.

<font color=indianred>“The contract requires proof of completion.”</font>

Ciaccona slid the blank card back into her sleeve. Two grey notes remained on it now.

<font color=teal>“Your civic dedication has become radiant.”</font>

<font color=indianred>“You can carry the other end.”</font>

They found the bronze fragments that belonged to Lianshui’s boundary pylons and wrapped them in the canvas. Chisa selected one thin strip from the broken jaw, tapped it against her repaired hinge, and kept it for Ruan. Cartethyia recovered Sir Whistle from her pack and blew one long, triumphant note toward the tunnel.

The chamber answered gently.

<font color=skyblue>“Sir Whistle has fulfilled his important office.”</font>

<font color=coral>“Chixia asked for three blasts when the route was secure.”</font>

Cartethyia blew twice more.

From far below, Lianshui’s field bells answered.

---

The lower terraces had completed their count by the time the hunters returned.

Forty-one households stood beside eleven wagons with their document boxes, bedding, cages, tools, and keys. Chixia had placed the injured driver in the first cart, where he could see the road. Old Qiao and his wardens sat on the bridge wall drinking water under Baizhi’s supervision.

Everyone heard the bronze horn before they saw who carried it.

Lupa and Ciaccona came down the cedar road with one end on each shoulder. Chisa walked behind them, her blade closed and whole. Cartethyia carried the iron pan against her chest and wore Sir Whistle around her neck.

Old Qiao rose.

He counted the four hunters with the same hand that had counted his wardens. When he reached Cartethyia, he touched the cracked bell in his sash, then bowed from the waist.

Cartethyia bowed lower.

Chixia met them at the blue marker. She inspected blood, gait, eyes, equipment, and the horn in one fast sweep.

<font color=lightsalmon>“Report while Baizhi checks you. I want the useful version first and the glorious version after supper.”</font>

Chisa offered her the field terminal. <font color=coral>“Six wardens recovered. Tollhide neutralized in the old survey chamber. The throat discharge used incorporated bell metal. A northern signal accelerated its behavior and directed its migration south.”</font>

Chixia’s hand tightened around the terminal.

The tiny Echo inside stamped one foot. Three chimes sounded through the casing.

Ciaccona drew the ruled card far enough to show the paired grey notes. <font color=teal>“That interval carries a march. I heard its opening in the string beyond Jinzhou’s eighth tower last night.”</font>

Chixia looked toward the northern ridge.

Behind her, the evacuation clerks kept counting. One called cart 304. A family answered. Another called 305. A child raised the household slate above his head.

<font color=lightsalmon>“Can you reproduce it for the Ranger relay?”</font>

<font color=teal>“I can give them the interval. The force behind it would require a larger and much uglier musician.”</font>

Chixia keyed her radio and sent the finding to the north posts.

Baizhi began with Lupa. She took the wolf-woman’s wrist, watched the pulse, examined the bruise rising beneath one collarbone, and looked at the tail still moving behind her.

<font color=tan>“Your temperature has risen another degree.”</font>

<font color=indianred>“I spent a fair amount of fire.”</font>

<font color=tan>“Your current expression accounts for part of the increase.”</font>

Lupa bared her teeth in a grin that made a nearby irrigation clerk take one respectful step away.

<font color=indianred>“Write down that I fulfilled the contract.”</font>

<font color=tan>“My report will prescribe fluids, food, and observation for two hours.”</font>

<font color=indianred>“We ate at the quarry shed.”</font>

Cartethyia held up Panhua’s pan. <font color=skyblue>“The seasoning survived beautifully.”</font>

Baizhi examined the pan as though it formed part of the medical evidence.

<font color=tan>“That satisfies one category.”</font>

---

The evacuation became a drill at four-fifteen.

Chixia stood on the bridge wall and told the people of Lianshui that the immediate road was secure, their houses remained available, and every family could return by terrace in reverse order. She also asked them to complete the route as planned, all the way to the wagon point and through the document check, because the next bell might carry a different answer.

Nobody cheered.

They finished the count.

Keys came back out of sleeves. Bedding returned to shoulders. The blue door opened again. The boy with the lamp carried it home with both arms.

Cartethyia stood beside wagon 311 and watched the fresh paint in the afternoon sun.

<font color=skyblue>“These wagons belong to the three hundred and twelve.”</font>

Chixia had climbed down from the bridge. Dust streaked one cheek. <font color=lightsalmon>“Any family whose road closes first receives a place. Changli’s office pushed the requisition through six days ago.”</font>

<font color=skyblue>“There are eleven here.”</font>

<font color=lightsalmon>“Thirty-four more are being fitted across the prefecture. We gain places one wagon, one spare room, and one cousin’s courtyard at a time.”</font>

Cartethyia laid her palm against the white number.

<font color=skyblue>“Then 311 has someone in.”</font>

Chixia followed her gaze. Her voice gentled and retained its carrying strength.

<font color=lightsalmon>“Today it held the Chen family’s quilts and a lamp. That counts.”</font>

They left the wagon in the sun and walked back to the bridge.

---

At the south arch, the auxiliary contract returned with four signatures, a medical annotation, and quarry dust ground into the fold.

Chixia wrote **OBJECTIVE COMPLETED** across the final line. Then she turned the page over and added the northern frequency report in smaller script.

The gate clerk stared at the severed horn occupying half his counter.

<font color=dimgray>“Does this enter municipal storage?”</font>

<font color=lightsalmon>“Boundary Works gets the bronze after Baizhi samples it. The black stone goes to Huaxu Academy. The terminal record goes to the Patrol Station and the Ranger relay.”</font>

Lupa kept one hand on the horn. <font color=indianred>“Panhua’s pan comes first.”</font>

The clerk found a supplementary property form.

Ciaccona leaned against the counter and watched his brush hover above a blank titled INCIDENTAL BENEFIT.

<font color=teal>“Write ‘four people had an excellent afternoon.’ The archive deserves one accurate line.”</font>

The clerk looked at Chixia.

Chixia’s shoulders began to shake. She pressed her lips together, preserved official dignity for almost two seconds, and laughed into the contract.

Lupa’s ears went back.

Cartethyia lifted the pan like a ceremonial shield. Chisa rested both hands on the closed grip of her scissors. Ciaccona drew a small star beside the dust mark on her blank card.

The clerk wrote **field cohesion satisfactory**.

It was the closest category he had.

---

Panhua received the pan at five-twenty and inspected its surface under the kitchen lamp.

<font color=sandybrown>“You let the bean paste catch at one edge.”</font>

Lupa leaned over her shoulder. <font color=indianred>“The stove was an ore drum and the monster was cooling.”</font>

<font color=sandybrown>“A weaker excuse would have failed.”</font>

She washed the pan, dried it over the second wok, and handed Lupa a bowl of hot oil with a brush.

Lupa heard the wok before Panhua told her. The oil had reached the thin, eager hiss that came just before scallions. She tipped the bowl at once. Green hit heat. Fragrance filled the kitchen.

Panhua gave one short nod and moved to the first wok.

The second belonged to Lupa for the evening.

She cooked enough for twelve.

Four portions went into taped containers on the high shelf for Mei, Iuno, Amy, and Yangyang. One portion went to the clinic driver. One went to Old Qiao, delivered by a patroller who complained cheerfully about the hill. Chixia ate standing at the counter while finishing the contract. Baizhi’s bowl waited three finger-widths away from the kitchen draught.

Chisa carried her bronze strip back to Ruan.

He heard the repaired blade enter the shop and set his file down.

<font color=peru>“How many closures?”</font>

Chisa placed the strip beside his hand. <font color=coral>“Thirty-one under load, seven through armor, twelve for inspection and cleaning.”</font>

Ruan added the figures.

The hinge opened and closed once between them, smooth as breath.

<font color=peru>“Tell me about the hard strike.”</font>

Chisa sat on the shop stool and described the quarry from the first delayed footstep to the divided core. Ruan listened with both palms flat on the workbench. When she reached the silver seam carrying Tollhide’s full weight, his fingers spread slightly against the wood.

She gave him the whole account.

Across the canal, Ciaccona played the quarry song outside Panhua’s.

She began with the old workers’ measure. A miner at the third table corrected her tempo before the eighth bar. A spice seller supplied the cabbage verse. Little Guo improvised a new line about a wolf carrying a horn through customs. The room took the song away from her and improved it immediately.

Cartethyia sat on the back step in Fleurdelys’s body because the courtyard had open sky and the kitchen lintel had already suffered enough. Her long legs extended halfway to the rain barrel. She ate a lemon honey cake one crystalline sugar grain at a time and hummed below the communal melody, off key with complete contentment.

Ciaccona adjusted one chord around her.

The little Tollhide Echo occupied a spare terminal on the table. Every time the chorus reached the quarry charge, it stamped one foot and chimed three times.

Lupa emerged from the kitchen with the next dish. Her tail moved in the song’s rhythm. Panhua saw it, Ciaccona saw it, Chixia saw it over the top of the contract, and Fleurdelys bent almost double to hide a smile behind her cake.

Nobody offered a verdict.

The market lamps came on by hand.

Beyond the eighth tower, too far for the tables to hear, a pressure moved through the northern ground.

The Tollhide Echo stopped chiming.

Its small head turned toward the wall.

Ciaccona’s blank card cooled against her wrist. The two grey notes darkened. A third space waited after them.

Chisa returned from Ruan’s shop and paused at the canal bridge. The vibration reached her through the paving: vast, measured, approaching the city through every creature already running ahead of it.

She crossed to Ciaccona and rested two fingers on the lute’s wooden rim.

The strings trembled beneath her touch.

At the kitchen door, Lupa set down the dish.

Fleurdelys lowered the cake.

For one breath, the four hunters listened together.

Then the pressure passed. The room’s song found its next bar. Little Guo came in early, the miner laughed, and Ciaccona caught them both inside the chord.

Lupa picked up the dish and carried it to the table.

<font color=indianred>“Eat while it’s hot. Tomorrow has its own teeth.”</font>

The Tollhide Echo stamped once.

Outside, Jinzhou’s lamps burned along the terraces, one human hand at a time.
