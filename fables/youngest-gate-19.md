# Youngest Gate 19 — The Returning Road

### *a reserve reassigned, eleven tables, and the last word of a wind*

---

The red aperture had carried Scar six hundred metres north.

It opened above a patch of dry grass behind the ridge, under a sky the colour of a bruise, and delivered him face-first into the ninth tail of Hsin of Mengzhou.

The tail had become a ring of amber iron around a circle of ground. It caught his wrists, ankles, and coat in five separate loops before he touched earth. A sixth loop took the blade hidden along his spine. The blade spun once in the air and planted itself beside Hsin's chair.

She had found a chair somewhere.

It stood on the ridge with all four legs level despite the slope. Beside it, Cristoforo's Sonoro Sphere rested in a smaller amber enclosure, its walls reduced to the width of a wardrobe. Cristoforo sat with his knees near his chest and watched Scar arrive with the attentive fatigue of a theatre director observing a late understudy.

Hsin poured tea from a field flask into a cup whose blue glaze showed hairline cracks.

<font color="#c86596">"That was a vigorous exit, and your landing requires work,"</font> she said.

Scar tested the tail at his wrists. Amber light tightened by the breadth of a thread.

<font color="#e9c2ff">"You have trapped me, fox."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"I made this cup four hundred and twelve years ago. The point survived its maker's impatience and three poor firings. You may address me as Sentinel while you remain inside my jurisdiction."</font>

The northern slope answered with three retreat whistles. Across the high ground, Fractsidus agents withdrew in pairs, carrying their wounded and burning the paper markers that had fixed Cristoforo's outer stage to the valley. Their red apertures opened low and narrow. Each aperture led farther from the road.

Scar turned his head toward them. Blood ran from the cut Lupa had opened beneath his shoulder and gathered at his elbow.

<font color="#e9c2ff">"Nivora will come for me before your tea cools."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"They are forty-eight steps behind you."</font>

Nivora crossed the ridge at a walking pace. They wore the same forgettable face they had worn at Panhua's dumpling stall, though the rain had washed one edge of it thin enough for something older to show at the temple. Two Fractsidus agents followed until the nearest guardian turned its head. They stopped. Nivora continued alone.

They looked first at Scar's wound, then at the amber loops, then at Cristoforo inside the smaller cell. Cristoforo raised two fingers in greeting. Nivora gave him the attention due a chair left out in weather.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Sentinel Hsin. I have authority to recover one of ours."</font>

Hsin drank her tea. A drop of rain reached the rim, paused there, and rolled along the outside.

<font color="#c86596">"His name would improve your opening."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Scar. He returns under my guarantee."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Your promise describes tomorrow. I am interested in the day after it."</font>

Nivora glanced down the southern road. Mei stood far below among the lamps, her gold dimming as the rear files of past soldiers entered the southern road. For a moment the old shape beneath Nivora's borrowed face became visible: a teacher watching a former pupil carry a lesson elsewhere.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Then we should speak at the proper scale."</font>

Hsin set down her cup.

Eight tails rose around the chair and closed like the petals of an amber flower. The ridge vanished behind their light. Sound reached the people outside as a soft pressure in the teeth.

Captain He Yuan, observing through field glasses from the reserve line, lowered them and checked his watch. The second hand crossed six marks. Twelve. Twenty-four.

At thirty-seven, the tails opened.

Nivora stood in the same place. Hsin folded a narrow red memorandum twice, hiding its written face, and sealed it with the impression of a fox walking beneath nine stars. The two negotiators bowed to one another at the exact depths required by age and authority.

<font color="#c86596">"Mengzhou will recognize the conclusion."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You may rely on my authority."</font>

The loops released Scar. He landed on one knee, caught himself with his sound hand, and reached for the blade beside Hsin's chair.

Hsin's shoe rested lightly on its guard.

<font color="#c86596">"Your responsibility begins here, and he leaves the blade behind."</font>

Nivora held out their hand. Scar looked at them, then at the fox, and left the blade in the ground. He took Nivora's hand and rose.

<font color="#e9c2ff">"The Rover was within reach."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You have opened that wound twice today, and Lupa's work deserves a physician. Walk with me."</font>

He went with them. On the far side of the ridge the surviving Fractsidus gathered around the pair, red cloths tied over white field dressings. They moved north by the quarry track. One aperture after another closed behind the last pair, each leaving a brief red line in the air and the smell of heated stone.

Lupa reached the crest in time to see Scar vanish beyond the second ridge. Her palms were blistered. A fresh dressing crossed her ribs, installed by Chisa while they climbed and already acquiring the loose, argumentative appearance of field medicine on Lupa.

<font color="indianred">"Scar left his knife behind. That may be his first sensible decision today."</font>

<font color="coral">"Hsin has placed a numbered evidence tag on it. She brought tags in her sleeve."</font>

<font color="indianred">"She packed for the paperwork as carefully as the battle. I should have expected that."</font>

Hsin lifted the blade by its tag and passed it to Si Ge. The guardian stored it in a compartment behind its shield.

Lupa studied the folded red paper in Hsin's fingers. Hsin met her eyes with the mild expression of a sovereign whose visitors had discovered a locked cabinet in the audience room.

<font color="indianred">"What did his release cost Nivora?"</font>

<font color="#c86596">"His organization found the price agreeable."</font>

<font color="coral">"That answer has six doors in it."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Mengzhou carpenters enjoy their craft."</font>

She tucked the paper into her sleeve. Below them, Cristoforo tapped one finger against the amber wall of his enclosure.

<font color="#fafadb">"My transport arrangements seem to have become a separate production."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"You will travel with the receiving column. A clerk in Jinzhou has prepared several excellent questions, and your breakfast will depend upon your answers arriving in complete sentences."</font>

Cristoforo settled back into his seat. He looked along the road at the people leaving his stage.

<font color="#fafadb">"The last spectator has left my house."</font>

Hsin picked up the cracked cup and poured the remaining tea over the place where his outer proscenium line had crossed the ridge. The chalk hissed and ran pale down the stones.

<font color="#c86596">"The city has set tables."</font>

The northern drum sounded once more.

The beat travelled beneath the valley, struck the eastern wall, and came back thin enough to rattle a buckle on an abandoned harness. The black banner gave one heavy movement around its pole. Its answering whistles had already gone north with the Fractsidus. Its soldiers walked south in their own names. The great cadence reached for a second beat and found only the clatter of stretchers, the creak of wheels, and Panhua's kitchen wagons descending the road.

Clouds began to lose their architecture.

The false crescent above the battlefield frayed at its lower horn. A shaft of morning entered through the gap and lit the wet face of the western cliff. Rain that had hung in the air resumed its descent. Drops struck helmets, stones, old banners, and the upturned palms of people who had spent a month watching water rise.

Sergeant Du stood under the ruined receiver mast and let three drops land on his knuckles. Each one broke against his skin and ran toward his wrist.

He took the bowl from his belt. Cord crossed it in six careful turns, holding the enamel handle against the crescent-shaped body. Rain gathered in the cracks, darkened the cord, and fell through.

Beside him Deng Rui watched the water behave.

The young signaller's field jacket still held the tear left by the claw. The Retroact gave his face the pallor of the northern watch, while the road gave his boots a fresh layer of ordinary mud. He touched the cover on the receiver. Waxed thread remained looped through the torn buckle where he had left it.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"You spent your last five minutes worrying that Tang would lose the needle."</font>

Rui ran a finger beneath the loop and found the needle secured there.

<font color="#9fbab7">"She would have charged the quartermaster for another one, and he would have made the rest of us listen to the complaint through supper."</font>

Du looked at the tied bowl. His thumb had pressed a permanent grey place into the cord.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Panhua is cooking at the shelf. I asked about pepper."</font>

Rui's smile arrived cautiously, as though the muscles had carried it through three wet years and wanted to test the ground.

<font color="#9fbab7">"You remembered the important part."</font>

They walked south together.

Along the old firing line, soldiers lowered weapons whose targets had gone. Armourers moved among them with chalk, marking split guards and overheated barrels. Medics cut wet cloth away from wounds. Names passed from runner to runner. The names arrived with water, blood group, blanket, broth, family contact, road assignment, and the hour each person had last slept.

Captain Bao sat on an ammunition box while Doctor Tang set her thumb. Bao kept her free hand on the breech of the western gun.

<font color="#609e47">"The recoil housing deserves inspection before anyone calls that piece roadworthy."</font>

Tang pulled the joint into place. Bao's sentence rose sharply through its final syllable.

<font color="#968873">"Your hand deserves eight hours in a sling. You may describe every mechanical flaw from a wagon."</font>

<font color="#609e47">"The limber gives me a clear view of the crew."</font>

<font color="#968873">"The wagon has a mattress."</font>

Bao considered the argument, glared at her restored thumb, and allowed two gunners to help her up.

At the reserve line, Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying stood over a map spread across the hood of a transport. Falling Rain lay bare along her left forearm. Its edge carried a faint grey sheen from the valley. She wiped it once with oiled cloth and returned the cloth to her driver.

Captain He Yuan approached at a trot. His seventy-two reserve soldiers waited behind him in clean formation, helmets fastened, ammunition full, eyes fixed on the road where the past had begun arriving.

Li moved three blue counters from the ridge to the southern road.

<font color="#cbd369">"Captain He, break your reserve into six receiving sections. First and second take the ambulances. Third takes water and blankets. Fourth carries the register cases. Fifth screens the eastern drop. Sixth remains close to the past soldiers and follows General Jiyan's pace."</font>

He looked at the counters, then at the long column below.

<font color="#b7c48d">"Their formations come from several campaigns. Some may follow old insignia when visibility changes."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Pair every current officer with a returning guide. Speak names before ranks. The road to Jinzhou has twelve kilometres, three washed culverts, and a hot meal at the south shelf. That is your mission."</font>

He Yuan saluted. The relief in his face lasted half a second, followed by the practical alarm of a captain whose tidy reserve had become an escort for nineteen hundred and seven dead soldiers.

<font color="#b7c48d">"I will need every cargo strap, four more lamp carts, and authority to empty the officers' tea wagon."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Take my transport as well. Driver Zhou will perform the required ceremony and then give you the keys."</font>

Zhou Lian, standing within easy hearing, held up the keys.

<font color="#8e9860">"Protocol requires thirty seconds, Grand Marshal. Captain, the left brake pulls downhill, and the rear cabinet contains her dry socks."</font>

Li looked at him.

<font color="#8e9860">"The ritual has twenty-six seconds remaining."</font>

He Yuan collected the keys. Within minutes the reserve formation dissolved into work. Rifle slings became litter straps. Ammunition wagons took blanket racks. Two gun crews unhitched their limbers and built seats along the sides. Blue reserve pennants came down; white escort lamps went up. A corporal used the Grand Marshal's map pins to label six water barrels by destination ward.

The past soldiers watched the preparations. Some drifted toward the insignia they remembered. Current guides met them there, gave their own names, and pointed south. Others had begun helping before any order reached them. A sapper from an older uniform crouched beside the third culvert and showed an engineer where the bank would shear under wagon weight. A cook dead twenty-two years climbed into Panhua's second cart, tasted the broth from a ladle, and asked for ginger.

Panhua handed him the root and a knife.

<font color="#f3b68f">"Slice it thin enough to see daylight. Anyone who argues may cook the next pot."</font>

The cook grinned and took the rear board as his station.

The sky lifted by degrees. Grey remained over the north, layered and deep, and a low bank of rain continued along the mountains. Above the south road, clouds separated into ordinary pieces with sunlight between them.

On the valley floor, Mei closed her hand.

Gold withdrew from stone to palm, from palm to the mark beneath her skin. The road kept the colour for several breaths, then returned to wet brown earth. Her knees folded. Iuno caught the back of her harness with her living hand and converted the fall into an untidy sit.

<font color="royalblue">"Your entrance into convalescence has damaged my wrist."</font>

Mei leaned against her hip and looked up.

<font color="#ffdead">"I can improve it. Give me another attempt."</font>

<font color="royalblue">"Your next exertion occurs on a mattress under medical supervision. I have counted sufficient acts of optimism for one morning."</font>

Amy arrived with the field kit bouncing against her thigh. The failed capacitor in her headset had left a black streak along one cheek.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Baizhi said both of you are going to the south shelf. She used your full names and gave me two sedatives, so the range of available choices feels wonderfully clear."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Which dose belongs to me?"</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"She labelled them by body mass. If you make me calculate while hungry, I may improvise."</font>

Iuno took the smaller ampoule from her. Mei watched this betrayal with an expression of personal injury.

<font color="royalblue">"You may complain during transport. Use complete sentences; our daughter has standards."</font>

Amy offered Mei her shoulder. Between them, they brought her upright.

The road south had filled with a slow, broad traffic. Guardians carried damaged guardians. Rangers supported past soldiers whose remembered wounds returned in patches whenever a cloud crossed the sun. Clerks moved beside them with boards tucked under their coats, recording the boundaries of each return. The walking wounded shared blankets with people whose skin held the chill of an earlier rain.

Jiyan and Geshu Lin remained at the rear.

Geshu had surrendered the spear to one of He Yuan's escort carts. His empty hand opened and closed as he walked, relearning its weight. Jiyan kept two fingers near the general's wrist. Every few steps Geshu glanced down at them with the patience of a commander enduring an earnest young medic.

<font color="#d4b896">"My pulse ended before your commission began."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"The road has introduced several administrative complications. I will document each one after Doctor Tang completes her assessment."</font>

Geshu looked along the convoy: old uniforms among new ones, carts loaded with weapons and quilts, a kitchen wagon trailing steam.

<font color="#d4b896">"Your reports must have become interesting."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Three annexes qualify as an ordinary morning in Jinzhou."</font>

Geshu laughed. The sound startled two former soldiers ahead of him. They turned, saw their general walking with a medic at his elbow, and continued south with their shoulders easier.

The south shelf had served as an assembly ground, a retreat line, and an emergency hospital during the plum rains. By midmorning it had become a dining room with poor walls and an excellent view.

The shelf curved beneath the southern cliff, broad enough for eleven long tables placed end to end. Tarpaulins stretched from rock bolts to wagon poles. Sunlight moved through the wet canvas in pale squares. At the outer edge, the land fell toward Jinzhou, and the city's north towers rose beyond twelve kilometres of washed fields.

Panhua's people had brought everything that could survive a road: rice porridge thick with shredded chicken, steamed river fish, pickled greens, eggs browned in tea, fried dough wrapped in yesterday's notices, ginger broth, and kettles large enough for a cavalry unit. Mrs Ren's quilts occupied two dry wagons. Each carried a scrap of cloth sewn to one corner with a household name, a ward number, or the words **FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK**.

Baizhi established three medical lanes through the tables and converted the fourth into seating after seeing how many people had arrived on their feet.

<font color="#a171d1">"Walking remains a symptom until I have examined the person doing it,"</font> she told a lieutenant who had tried to redirect three past soldiers toward the food. <font color="#a171d1">"They will eat during assessment. Bring bowls to this bench and place the register where I can read it."</font>

The lieutenant complied. Baizhi turned to Chixia, who sat on a supply chest with her trouser leg cut open to the knee.

The iron shard had gone through the outside of her calf and stopped against bone. Chixia had spent the last hour coordinating the field station around it, on the theory that a stationary captain could serve as furniture, dispatch desk, and morale problem at once.

<font color="#ff5757">"I have kept the leg very still, apart from the necessary running."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Your definition has entered my notes. Hold the rail while I remove this."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"Could you also put down that I showed exceptional restraint? The Patrollers have an annual review system, and certain supervisors read every adjective."</font>

Baizhi gripped the iron with forceps.

<font color="#a171d1">"I can support 'conscious.' Your performance during the next ten seconds will determine the rest."</font>

Chixia held the rail. She managed seven seconds in silence and spent the last three offering a detailed opinion of Scar's ancestry. Baizhi dropped the iron into a tray, irrigated the wound, and reached for clean gauze.

<font color="#ff5757">"Make the bandage red. It will match the cap."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Your blood supplies the first layer's colour. The outer wrap is white."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"Medicine has lost its sense of pageantry."</font>

The cap stayed on her head throughout.

At the second lane, Mei sat on an overturned bucket while Amy unfastened her harness. Iuno stood close enough to steady her and far enough to preserve the fiction that she was merely waiting for her own examination. Amber discharge had dried along the crystal at her shoulder.

Baizhi pointed across the shelf.

<font color="#a171d1">"Iuno, your coat is trapping moisture around the crystal. Remove it and place it on the dry bench. Mei, drink the cup Amy is holding. Amy, count her swallows."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"This family has become alarmingly regulated."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Five years ago you taught me that procedures are love wearing sensible shoes. You may admire your influence after swallow eight."</font>

Iuno shrugged out of the coat with Amy's help. Mei accepted it across her knees. The fabric was black, heavy with rain, one sleeve warm from the arm that had occupied it. She smoothed the lapel while Amy tipped the cup toward her.

One swallow. Two.

The gold ring lay in the hollow of Mei's right palm, where mud had dried around the facets cut from Iuno's own crystal.

Three. Four.

Mei folded the empty sleeve across the coat. Her fingers found the inner pocket, eased the ring beneath its flap, and pressed the cloth flat over it with her thumb.

Five. Six.

She turned the coat so the dry lining faced upward and laid it on the bench beside Iuno.

Seven. Eight.

Amy took the cup. Iuno lifted the coat by its collar and settled it across her knees while Baizhi examined the crystal. The ring gave a quiet click against the metal case in the pocket. Iuno's eyes moved once toward Mei.

Mei was watching Amy wipe the black capacitor streak from her cheek with a corner of gauze.

Iuno placed her living hand over the pocket and kept it there through the examination.

*An ordinary Tuesday,* the High Priestess observed from the ceremonial balcony in her mind. *Rain damage, battlefield medicine, and a private object returned to its proper jurisdiction. The liturgy improves.*

Iuno's mouth curved. Baizhi saw it and wrote down her pulse.

At the upper path, a convoy lamp swung once in the sunlight.

Yangyang came through the tarpaulin with one hand under her own elbow. Her uniform hung dark from her shoulders. The Wind rested inside her sleeve, too thin to lift the wet cuff, a small pressure close against her wrist.

Chixia saw her.

The cup in Chixia's hand struck the supply chest. Broth ran over her fingers. She planted her sound foot, forgot Baizhi's direct instruction concerning the other one, and made two rapid steps before pain reached her face.

Yangyang crossed the remaining distance.

Chixia took her by both shoulders, checked her face as though it were a dispatch whose seal she distrusted, and pulled her into an embrace broad enough to include the sword, the wet sleeves, and the exhausted weather gathered between them.

The Wind stirred.

It moved up through Yangyang's sleeve and crossed Chixia's back. The red cap lifted half an inch. Air pressed along its crown with exquisite care, following the seam, the brass badge, and the water-darkened brim. The cap remained bright, solid, warm from a living head.

<font color="#ff5757">"I brought fried dough in my medical satchel because the kitchen cart looked slow, and Baizhi confiscated it on grounds of sterility. There are six pieces waiting inside a folded casualty form. You get first choice."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"You have broth on your sleeve, and your dressing has begun leaking at the lower edge. Sit before Baizhi promotes you from patient to research subject."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Your pulse is fast. The secondary frequency has dropped below its previous measurable range, though it is holding a coherent boundary around the radial artery. Both of you will remain within reach while I prepare warm saline."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"You may inspect it. I have wanted your opinion since the twenty-seventh."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"Our room at Mrs Ren's still has the bed by the window. I claimed it for you before anyone with long legs developed ambitions. <font color="#667cf9">"My mother will ask how much ginger you take. She asks once and remembers forever, so choose carefully. Suisui has kept the blue curtains, and the western sill gets sun after breakfast."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"I can arrange a stable thermal field for the first nights. The clinic has a spare resonator, and its hum may help maintain coherence during sleep. Visiting hours become flexible when the visitor occupies the patient's sleeve."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"We have had a place ready for a month. Several people kept putting bowls at the empty end of the table, and Panhua started cooking five portions whenever four of us arrived. You can come through any door you like. We will learn which one creaks for you."</font>

The glass resonator sang.

The Wind spent the word it had carried out of the valley.

<font color="#94a5ff">"Home."</font>

<font color="#f3b68f">"The rice has reached its proper state. Anyone who waits for a formal invitation will receive the skin from the bottom."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"We should secure our portions before the dead discover army appetites."</font>

Red. Present. Heading toward lunch.

Shen surveyed the coins. She removed the nearest river stone and checked the first letter's fold against the first cushion.

<font color="#c5b79e">"Your aisle is narrow. Bring the boards through sideways, and keep the envelopes in their order."</font>

They laid one letter on each purchased seat and placed its half-copper over the fold. The first rows filled with broad household hands, cramped dictation, paper from four prefectures, and one letter consisting of six steady characters. The final envelope occupied a seat near the back door.

Ciaccona arrived carrying her damaged lute. Cartethyia brought the camellia. Chisa came with Lupa, her own posted letter already docketed at the north gate for the overseas mail. Amy, Mei, and Iuno took places in the aisle. Baizhi settled Chixia into a seat with room for her bandaged leg. Yangyang stood at the back door.

The Wind waited beside her.

Returning soldiers filled the remaining seats and stood along the walls. Citizens came in from the Row, still wearing kitchen aprons and printer's ink. Panhua held the final open place for Master Qu. He stayed by the stage with his watch.

At seven twelve, he turned the glass.

The second act began.

The stage remained empty. The nine pages carried their margins and their ruled lines beneath the lamps.

The house breathed.

A child asked for water. Someone passed a cup along the row. One of Geshu's artillerymen sneezed and received three handkerchiefs. Chixia shifted her injured leg onto Baizhi's bag. Lupa leaned her shoulder against Chisa's. Cartethyia set the terrarium where the camellia could catch the stage light.

Ciaccona kept her hands away from the lute.

The Wind entered through the back door, as it had during eleven years of domestic scenes. It passed over the three hundred and six letters. Each old coin cooled, then warmed. Paper edges lifted in sequence from the rear row to the front, a dry travelling murmur through the full house.

Yangyang walked beside it.

At the sixth minute, the Wind reached the stage. It paused before the blank pages. The two shells at Yangyang's throat answered with their two seas, one clear and one weathered. The sound entered the margins and left every line open.

At the eighth minute, the Wind turned toward the audience.

People sat there. Past soldiers, living soldiers, clerks, cooks, physicians, printers, a household from across four seas, and three hundred and six letters whose addresses had failed while their words endured in folded rooms.

Master Qu watched the final grains descend through the glass.

At nine minutes, the Wind left by the front door.

Yangyang went with it. Chixia rose on Baizhi's arm and followed. The rest of the house came out in its own time, carrying cups, coats, children, and the easy noise of people discussing where supper could still be found.

Master Qu sat in the middle aisle after the lamps came up.

Shen collected the letters. She left each half-copper on its seat.

<font color="#c5b79e">"They have attended. Your receipt should say so."</font>

Qu looked at the nine blank sheets.

<font color="rosybrown">"The act runs tomorrow as well. A paid seat remains available whenever its letter wishes to return."</font>

Shen folded the boards into the cart. The tin held all three hundred and six envelopes, still private, still in order, each one now entered in the theatre ledger as **PRESENT IN THE HOUSE**.

The old coins stayed on the seats inside the open house, a completed fee and a permanent audience.

---

Night settled gently over Jinzhou.

The mountain bank kept its clouds. A light shower crossed Ward Two after midnight, ran down every roof in the direction roofs expected, and moved out over the harbour. People opened umbrellas. Gutters filled. A vendor covered his peaches and continued counting change.

At the Bureau of Rites, Clerk Wen removed the plum-rain emergency notice from the public board. He dated its lower corner, rolled it around a wooden pin, and placed it in the seasonal archive. In the open space he posted the morning forecast: scattered mountain showers, clearing toward the coast.

On Bookbinders' Row, the morning forme waited on the stone with the returned thirty-one-year sort locked in place. Its first impression had dried cleanly above the press.

Farther down the same worktable, the unfamiliar customer's single sort remained face-down on the counter-cloth. Ink dust gathered along its shoulders. Every hand working the late edition passed around it.

In Mengzhou, a court drawer held the faced lead quad among the blanks. Its reverse-cut character waited at striking depth inside a wrapper marked **MATRIX, PROBATIONARY**. The founders kept its lead cool and its mould clean. Beside the drawer, a receiving hook stood ready for the sealed red strip travelling west in Hsin's sleeve.

At Mrs Ren's, Yangyang hung the two seashells from the bedpost beside the blue curtains. Under lamplight, the surviving cuts along the older shell's lip could be read as far as their missing pieces allowed:

*“Like moths to a ■■, all who hear the ■■ walk the road of ■■■■. To the last ■■■■ of the ■■■ left in ■■ world: may a journey unhoped-for, and fated to ■■, find its ■■■■ in the presence of ■■■.”*

The Wind slept in the curve between the shells. The current sea and the dead sea moved through the room at different depths. From the next bed, Chixia's breathing carried the soft obstruction of a face pressed too far into a pillow. Baizhi's resonator hummed on the sill. Yangyang had begun a letter to her mother on the desk.

At the north wall, Li Zhaoying remained awake.

Falling Rain lay bare across the parapet. Its sheath stood in the watch room behind her, dry and empty. The valley had disappeared into darkness beyond the fields.

Seventy-nine seconds passed.

A pressure entered the stone under Li's hand. The pulse had weakened to the depth of a distant drum heard through several hills. It travelled along the wall, reached the open sword, and gathered a single drop of water from the parapet.

The drop climbed Falling Rain's edge.

Li watched it reach the midpoint and hang there against the pull of the earth.

She rang the watch bell once. Captain He answered from the lower stair.

<font color="#cbd369">"Keep the escort lamps filled and the north kitchens on their night rotation. Send the next interval directly to me."</font>

<font color="#b7c48d">"The road remains quiet, Grand Marshal. We have six teams awake along the culverts."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Give each team breakfast before dawn. Hunger changes the pace of every army."</font>

He Yuan went to carry the order.

The drop reached the guard. Li closed her hand around Falling Rain and held its bare edge toward the north.

On the slate at her elbow, beneath **OVATHRAX — INTERVAL**, she wrote **EIGHTY-ONE SECONDS**.

Below her, Jinzhou kept nineteen hundred and seven new names under roofs. Baths steamed. Registers dried. Forty-one empty cups stood beneath an old guardian's shield. At Panhua's, the corner lamp leaned above a third seat, and the flame burned steadily in the weather after war.

Eighty-one seconds.

The Grand Marshal began the next count.
