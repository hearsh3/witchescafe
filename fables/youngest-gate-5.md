# Youngest Gate 5

## 手談 — HAND TALK

> *觀棋不語真君子，起手無回大丈夫.*
> *To watch a game and keep silence is the mark of a gentleman; to lift the hand and decline to take the move back is the mark of a great one.*
> — carved on the east face of the Jinzhou City Hall stair, worn to the depth of a thumbnail by four hundred years of people leaning there while they waited to be seen.

[COLORS: Mei=moccasin, Amy=mistyrose, Iuno=royalblue, Lupa=indianred, Jinhsi=gold, Changli=orangered, Sanhua=#bd9adf, Wen=gainsboro, Bo=wheat, Yangyang=yellowgreen]

## I. 提子 — LIFTING THE CAPTURED STONES

Narrator: The escort took him down the service stair, which has eleven turns in it, and the sound of the boots came up through the floor for a long time after the sound of him had stopped.

Amy counted the turns without meaning to. Eleven. She had been doing that since she was small and had given up apologising for it.

The sanctum looked like a room that had lost an argument. One reading table stood on three legs and a stack of tax rolls. The panel that had never looked like a door hung off its top hinge with the light coming through the gap in one long white bar, and the bar was full of dust, and the dust was still moving, because the air in this room had been somewhere else four minutes ago and had come back changed.

The Elysium had left a smell. Cold stone and burnt sugar. Amy's teeth ached from it.

Sanhua stood at the broken panel with her back to the room, watching the stair, and had held that position for six minutes.

Jinhsi stood at the high window with her hands at her sides.

Mei sat down on the edge of the three-legged table, which took her weight and complained about it, and looked at the Magistrate of Jinzhou for a while.

<font color=moccasin>"You're welcome,"</font> she said.

Jinhsi turned around.

She was nineteen. Amy had known the number for weeks and had filed it the way you file a number, and it had cost nothing to hold. Seeing it was a different transaction. The Magistrate had a smudge of plaster on her collar and a hand that had been closed for some time, and she was nineteen the way Amy was nineteen — which is to say from a distance, and only on the outside.

<font color=gold>"I apologise, Mei. I had presumed you would be familiar with such… games, given your past."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You weren't wrong."</font>

<font color=gold>"That is a separate matter from whether I was correct to presume it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font> Mei rubbed the back of her neck. <font color=moccasin>"It is."</font>

Amy crouched and picked the card up off the flagstones.

It had landed face down and stayed there. Black lamb, red slash, a drawing a boy made for his sister and then made into a flag. She turned it over twice, held it up to the bar of light, found nothing in it, and put it inside her jacket, because a thing like that left on a floor gets swept up by somebody who is paid four coppers a day.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma says you're welcome,"</font> she said, straightening. <font color=mistyrose>"I'd like the list."</font>

Jinhsi's eyes came round to her.

<font color=gold>"Which list."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Everything you knew before we walked in. In order. Starting from the day you left me a mangosteen."</font>

Sanhua's shoulders shifted a quarter-inch at the panel. It was the first movement she had made and it was a long way from approval.

Mei looked at her daughter and kept her mouth shut, which had taken four years and a god's dark to learn.

<font color=gold>"Sit down,"</font> Jinhsi said.

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm good."</font>

<font color=gold>"It runs to seven."</font>

Amy sat down on the flagstones with her back against a shelf-end, arms round her knees, and looked up. <font color=mistyrose>"Go."</font>

Jinhsi consulted nothing. She had the habit some people get, who read too much too early, of storing things in the order they will be needed.

<font color=gold>"One. That the Fractsidus had two apertures inside my prefecture in the third month and four by the fifth, and that the rate of increase was consistent with something other than reconnaissance."</font>

<font color=gold>"Two. That an Overseer designated Scar had been asking after Qichi through three separate intermediaries since the winter, one of whom is a fruit factor and has no idea what he is."</font>

<font color=gold>"Three. That Qichi's file was closed forty years ago in a manner I would refuse from a clerk of mine, and that the closing hand belonged to a man who is dead and whose widow receives a pension I have declined to stop."</font>

Mei's jaw did something.

<font color=gold>"Four. That the cassia leaf carried a Waveworn signature which would put your household in the highlands inside four days, and that a Ranger camp lay between you and the village, so that whatever you found would be found in company."</font>

<font color=gold>"Five. That he wants witnesses. He has wanted witnesses for eleven years. Every site he has left in this prefecture has been left legible."</font>

<font color=gold>"Six. That the Grand Library's fourth level has an approach absent from every plan filed with the Ministry, and that the plan it *is* on was copied in a scriptorium in the capital in a year when a man now working in my registry was a copyist there."</font>

Amy's head came up. <font color=mistyrose>"You left the door open."</font>

<font color=gold>"I left the door where it had always been, and I stopped correcting the copy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's leaving it open."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

Mei put her hands flat on the table either side of her and looked at the floor. The dust moved in its bar of light. Somewhere below, a drawer went shut, and a clerk who had heard nothing at all today walked down a corridor at ordinary speed.

<font color=moccasin>"That's six."</font>

<font color=gold>"Seven is what I failed to know."</font> Jinhsi's voice held exactly where it was, which was the tell. <font color=gold>"He can open a domain inside the sanctum. The wards on this room are four hundred years old, they are keyed to the lake, they have never failed, and I have the survey in my own file, and the survey is wrong. Sanhua and I were positioned for a man in a room. He took the room away."</font>

The bar of light moved a finger's width down the wall.

<font color=mistyrose>"So what was the plan,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"If we hadn't come back out."</font>

The pause had a floor to it. It went down.

<font color=gold>"…I would have written to your household."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's a letter."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's a letter, not a plan."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

Amy's hands were round her knees and her knuckles had gone the colour of the plaster. Mei watched them go and stayed where she was, because there was a version of this where she crossed the room and put herself between her daughter and a nineteen-year-old with a city on her back, and that version was the old one, and the old one had raised a girl who copied it perfectly all the way into a god's dark.

So she waited.

Amy breathed out through her nose and let go of her knees.

<font color=mistyrose>"Okay,"</font> she said. <font color=mistyrose>"Thanks."</font>

<font color=gold>"For which part."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You could've stopped at six."</font>

<font color=gold>"Six is a report. Seven is the truth, and you would have had it inside a month, because the survey is public and your mother reads floors."</font> A pause. <font color=gold>"And because I would have known."</font>

Mei laughed. It came out of her before she had sanctioned it, one short unlovely bark of a laugh, and it startled the dust.

<font color=moccasin>"Amy,"</font> she said. <font color=moccasin>"She answered."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I noticed."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"People don't."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I *noticed*, Ma."</font> Amy's mouth went sideways, which meant the worst of it had passed through. <font color=mistyrose>"I'm still putting it in the book."</font>

<font color=gold>"There is a book?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"There's a book."</font>

Sanhua spoke without turning round.

<font color=#bd9adf>"Third bell in forty minutes, Excellency. The Censorate at the third, the harbour levy at the fourth, and General Hou has moved himself to the fifth without being asked."</font>

Jinhsi looked at the window for the length of a breath.

<font color=gold>"Clear the afternoon."</font>

Sanhua turned round then. Her face performed no expression whatsoever, at speed.

<font color=#bd9adf>"All of it."</font>

<font color=gold>"All of it. Tell Hou he may have the fifth tomorrow, and that I will read the annex tonight, which will improve his argument and damage mine, and he will enjoy that."</font> Jinhsi crossed to the surviving chair. <font color=gold>"And send up the tea. The one in the green jar."</font>

Sanhua went.

Mei, who had spent a lifetime learning what it means when a person cancels a day, said: <font color=moccasin>"That's a lot of empty chairs."</font>

<font color=gold>"They will be filled tomorrow by the same people saying the same things with an additional day of feeling ignored, which improves the quality of their arguments by about a fifth."</font> Jinhsi sat. She put her hands on the arms of the chair and let them rest there. <font color=gold>"Also, I have been standing since the second watch and my legs have stopped being interesting to me."</font>

Amy snorted.

Jinhsi looked over at her, mildly startled, the way a person is startled by a sound in a house they believed was empty.

<font color=mistyrose>"Sorry. That was funny."</font>

<font color=gold>"…Thank you."</font>

## II. 打入 — THE INVASION

Narrator: Out of the east gate the road runs four kilometres of shade under the willows and then quits, and after that it is the plain, and the plain in the sixth month is a hammer.

Lupa smelled it from the last of the willows.

She stopped dead in the road with her ears going forward and her head up, and Iuno, who had been walking a half-step behind her for two days out of a habit neither of them discussed, stopped as well.

<font color=indianred>"They're cutting."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It is the sixth month."</font>

<font color=indianred>"They're cutting it *green*."</font> Lupa's nostrils went once, twice, and her lip came off her teeth in a way that had nothing to do with anger. <font color=indianred>"That's sap. Cut grain ripe and it smells like a warm floor. That's sap, and it's coming off about four hundred hectares."</font>

Iuno stood in the road with the sun on her crystal.

*<font color=royalblue>Ah. There she is.</font>*

*I had not finished being wrong yet.*

*<font color=royalblue>You finished at the tax office, darling. You have been walking for two days out of pride.</font>*

She had found it on Tuesday, in the harbour ward, in a remissions ledger she had opened for a completely different reason, in a hand so tidy it had made her scalp prickle. Eleven villages, sixty-one households, harvest-loss remission granted in advance of loss, on one day, under one chop, at a rate a fifth above assessed value. Advance remission is a thing a state does when it already knows. She had sat in a wooden chair with a goose on the other side of the door and read the column twice and understood that somebody in Huanglong had decided to lose the plain and had begun paying for it out of politeness.

<font color=royalblue>"How many people are in that field?"</font>

Lupa's ears moved through an arc.

<font color=indianred>"Sixty and change. Twelve of them are soldiers pretending they aren't. Four horses. One ox that hates its life."</font> A beat. <font color=indianred>"And a woman who's been standing in the same spot about an hour, because everybody else moves round her."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That will be her."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Mm."</font> Lupa started walking. <font color=indianred>"Out, two."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You have caught it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Everybody's caught it. Cartethyia counts the *bread*."</font>

They came up on it out of the heat-shimmer: an awning of oiled canvas on four poles, a trestle table under it, a strongbox with a soldier sitting on it who was reading a novel and doing it badly, three clerks, and a queue.

The queue was the thing. It ran forty people long and it stood calm. Iuno had spent thirty years reading queues — a queue is the most honest document a government produces — and this one stood with its hats off and its shoulders down and talked to itself in low voices about a wedding. Somebody had put out water. Somebody had put out a bench for the old, and the bench had been made that morning; the cut ends of the planks were still pale.

And in the middle of the field, in the sun, in a long coat the colour of a struck match, a woman held a paper umbrella over the head of an old man who was refusing to sign something.

Iuno stopped at the edge of the awning's shade and watched.

The old man's name was Bo. She learned that because the woman used it eleven times in four minutes and used it as a lever exactly none of them.

<font color=wheat>"Fifty-one years,"</font> he was saying. <font color=wheat>"I put the first row of that in with my father's hands on the drill. You want me to take money for it standing green. You know what taking money for it means."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Tell me what it means, Bo."</font>

<font color=wheat>"It means it's *lost*."</font> He had got past shouting some time before they arrived. <font color=wheat>"It means I signed it away. Fifty-one years, and the paper'll say the year I gave up."</font>

<font color=orangered>"The paper says *harvest-loss remission, advance.*"</font>

<font color=wheat>"I can read."</font>

<font color=orangered>"I know you can. You read the sluice notice to the whole village in the spring of the bad year, and you read it twice, because Widow Kang is deaf on the left."</font> The umbrella tilted a degree to keep the sun off his neck. <font color=orangered>"Clerk Wen."</font>

<font color=gainsboro>"Counselor."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Is there a schedule for seed-stock purchase for the resettlement wards?"</font>

A pause of exactly the wrong length.

<font color=gainsboro>"…There is Schedule Four, Counselor. Seed and stock, purchase at valuation, for the resettlement of displaced agricultural households."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Is Bo's standing crop seed-grade?"</font>

<font color=gainsboro>"It — "</font> Wen looked at the field. Wen looked at Bo. Wen was about thirty and had held this job for six days. <font color=gainsboro>"It would have to be assessed."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Assess it."</font>

Wen walked out into the sorghum and came back with a head of it in his hand and studied it for a long time, in the manner of a man doing arithmetic that has nothing to do with grain.

<font color=gainsboro>"It's seed-grade."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Then strike the remission line, and I thank you for catching it."</font> The woman in the red coat turned the umbrella so it covered him properly. <font color=orangered>"Bo is selling four hectares of seed-stock to the prefecture at valuation for the resettlement wards. It will be sown in the eighth month north of the lake, where the ground is heavier and will want a hardy strain, and the receipt names the strain and the grower."</font> A beat. <font color=orangered>"It goes into the record as a supply, Bo. Somebody eats it next autumn and the ledger says whose it was."</font>

The old man stood in the sun with his hat in his two hands.

<font color=wheat>"…Names the grower."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Names the grower. It is a requirement of Schedule Four and I have never once regretted it."</font>

He signed. His hand shook and he was furious about the shaking and he signed anyway, and Wen blotted it and stamped it and gave him the counterfoil, and the woman in the red coat said, <font color=orangered>"Thank you, Wen. That was the correct schedule and you found it faster than I did,"</font> which was a lie of the specific kind that costs the liar nothing and pays the other party for a year.

Bo went off down the row with his counterfoil held out in front of him like a bowl of soup.

Lupa, at Iuno's shoulder, spoke without moving her mouth much.

<font color=indianred>"She didn't lie once."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"No."</font>

<font color=indianred>"That's worse."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Considerably."</font>

The woman in the red coat closed the umbrella, turned, and looked directly at them across sixty metres of cut field, as though she had been waiting for the paragraph to end.

Then she came over, and up close she was younger than the coat and older than the face, and there were burn-scars up the inside of her left forearm shaped like feathers, worn bare, the way a person wears a thing she has decided to stop explaining.

<font color=orangered>"Registrar,"</font> she said. <font color=orangered>"Champion."</font>

Lupa's tail went still.

<font color=orangered>"Changli. Counselor to the Magistrate. I have wanted to meet you both for some time and I have twice found a reason to postpone it, which I would rather say now than have you work out later."</font> She looked at Iuno's stump, at the sun on it, at the four hours of afternoon left. <font color=orangered>"May I?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"May you what."</font>

Changli opened the umbrella and held it over the crystal.

<font color=royalblue>"…It does not burn."</font>

<font color=orangered>"It refracts. There is a woman in the dye-yards with a glass hand from an accident at the works. She loses an afternoon every summer to a headache that starts behind the eye on that side, and it took her physician four years to find it."</font> The umbrella held. <font color=orangered>"You may tell me to stop."</font>

*<font color=royalblue>Do NOT tell her to stop.</font>*

*I am aware of what she is doing.*

*<font color=royalblue>So am I, darling, and it is exquisite. Sit down, take the tea, and lose the first exchange gracefully, because we are going to need the second one.</font>*

Iuno stood in the shade of a spirit-bird umbrella in a field of cut sorghum and experienced, with great precision, the sensation of being read by a professional.

<font color=royalblue>"I have five facts and I would like to put them down at once,"</font> she said, <font color=royalblue>"because I am told I am tiresome when I lead up to things, and because it will save us both an hour."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Please."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"One. The berth held for my household at Mingting was reassigned overnight in a correct hand under a correct seal by somebody with the authority to do it, and it was you. Two. The Magistrate's three days of absence in the sixth month fell where they fell because you put them there. Three. The four objects given to my partner were the Magistrate's, and the week they were given in was yours. Four. Overseer Scar walked into a room in the Grand Library this morning because a survey of that room has been wrong for eleven years and somebody stopped correcting it. Five — "</font> Iuno stopped.

*<font color=royalblue>Say it.</font>*

<font color=royalblue>"Five. I would have done all four of those, in that order, with better paperwork, and I would have been wrong every time, and I am aware that saying so first is itself a manoeuvre, and I am doing it anyway."</font>

The cicadas went on.

<font color=orangered>"Yes,"</font> said Changli. <font color=orangered>"To all five."</font>

Lupa's ears went flat and came back up.

<font color=indianred>"That was fast."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Denial is a move that works once, and I would rather spend it in the winter."</font> Changli glanced back at the awning. <font color=orangered>"Wen. Two more cups and the pot, and put the strongbox in the cart before the sun gets on the lock; it swells."</font> Then, to Iuno, with the first thing in her voice that had any grain to it: <font color=orangered>"You found the remissions ledger."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Tuesday."</font>

<font color=orangered>"Tuesday,"</font> Changli agreed, and there was something in the way she said the day of the week that lifted the hair on Iuno's arm, and it was four days before Iuno worked out what.

## III. 目 — THE EYE

Narrator: The tea came up in a green jar with a chip out of the lip, and a pot mended twice with lacquer, and Jinhsi poured it herself, three cups, on a broken table, in a room with a door hanging off its hinge.

Amy took hers and held it and left it alone, which is what you do when you have decided to stay uncomfortable and are trying to be polite about the decision.

<font color=gold>"There is an annexe,"</font> Jinhsi said.

Mei's cup stopped.

<font color=gold>"To the record you read at the Academy. The First Resonator file. What you were given is the reading copy. It is redacted at eleven points, the redactions were made in the ninth year of the second reign, they are lawful, and I would make most of them again."</font> She set the pot down. <font color=gold>"There is an annexe. It is sealed under the Magistrate's hand and it holds the eleven. Three Magistrates have had the key. Two of them left it in the drawer."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"And you."</font>

<font color=gold>"I have had it four years and I have taken it out twice."</font>

Mei put her cup down on the table, very carefully, on the flattest part.

<font color=moccasin>"Why."</font>

<font color=gold>"Which twice?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Why haven't you read it."</font>

Jinhsi looked at her, and it was the flat look, the one she keeps for true things, and the plaster dust was still on her collar.

<font color=gold>"Because the moment I know, I have to write it down."</font>

The bar of light had reached the floor and lay across the flagstones with a shelf-shadow in it like a bar of music.

<font color=gold>"I am an office, Mei. If I open that annexe and find in it what I believe I would find, then by the eleventh of the seventh month there is a memorandum in the Censorate, a query from the Ministry of Rites regarding the naming of a shrine, a request from the Board of War for the disposition of an asset, and a letter from the capital. By the ninth month the Grand Marshal, who takes an interest in your household that he has been at no pains to hide, has a policy about you."</font> A pause. <font color=gold>"A rumour I may decline. A fact I must forward. That is the entire difference, it is the whole of my authority, and it works in one direction."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"So you left her a puzzle box."</font>

<font color=gold>"I left her a route that did not run through me. The archive at the Academy is open to any registered resident with a reader's chit and Baizhi's countersignature. Whatever your mother now believes about herself, she assembled it out of a fruit, a leaf, a sweet, a piece of clockwork and four days of her own judgement, and there is no line anywhere in my registry saying the Magistrate told her."</font> Jinhsi turned her cup a quarter-turn on the table by the rim, and settled it. <font color=gold>"The key is in the third drawer of the desk in the room below this one. You may have it. I will leave, and I will come back when you send for me, and I will ask nothing about what you found."</font>

Mei sat with that.

Then, with no part of her mind attending to it, still looking at the far wall, she reached out and turned her own cup a quarter-turn on the table by the rim, and settled it.

The room went very quiet.

Jinhsi's eyes were on the cup. Mei's eyes had come down to her own hand as though it belonged to a stranger who had reached across her.

Amy saw both of them see it. Amy, raised in a hall of mirrors by a city that loved a mystery, who could have said four different clever things and had one of them halfway up her throat, put her teeth together and let it pass.

Nobody said anything.

After a moment Jinhsi took a mouthful of tea, and set the cup down, and left it where it landed.

<font color=gold>"The third drawer,"</font> she said. <font color=gold>"On the left. The brass one sticks."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"…Not today."</font>

<font color=gold>"Very well."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You're not going to ask."</font>

<font color=gold>"No."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'm telling you anyway, because otherwise I'll chew on it all week."</font> Mei picked at a splinter on the table edge. <font color=moccasin>"If I open it in this room, with you standing in it, it stops being mine. It turns into evidence. Somebody writes it down, and then it's a *thing about me*, and everybody in this city gets to hold an end of it before I've had one afternoon to be sad about it on my own."</font> She flicked the splinter away. <font color=moccasin>"So I'll open it somewhere with worse chairs."</font>

<font color=gold>"That is correct."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Which part."</font>

<font color=gold>"All of it, and I would have said it less well."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"She talks like that on purpose,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"It's a whole technique. She's been doing it to customs officers for a month."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It works on customs officers."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"It works on *everyone*, Ma, that is my entire point — "</font>

<font color=gold>"The mangosteen was a mangosteen."</font>

Mei looked up.

<font color=gold>"You will have decided it meant something. Everybody does. The sugar pearl was a vaccine, and I chose it because the coughing epidemic of the fourth reign was ended by a woman with a laboratory, a wheelbarrow and nine assistants over two winters, and I wanted that fact in your pocket before anybody offered you a cheaper story about how plagues end. The cassia leaf was a warning wearing a clue's coat. The sundial was the route."</font> Jinhsi's hands lay flat on the chair arms. <font color=gold>"The mangosteen is in season eleven days. It was the ninth. You had come two thousand kilometres in a truck. I wanted you to have one before the season shut."</font>

Amy made a small strangled sound.

<font color=mistyrose>"You put a *snack* in a spy puzzle."</font>

<font color=gold>"I put a puzzle around a snack."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Amy ate it."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Amy ate it in *four minutes* and Amy regrets nothing."</font>

<font color=gold>"Good."</font>

Mei laughed, and this time she let it run, and the dust moved.

Then, because Mei has always been able to walk from one register to another with no step in between, and it has frightened cleverer people than anyone in the room:

<font color=moccasin>"The pearl worked differently than you meant it to."</font>

<font color=gold>"Explain."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You meant it as a vaccination record. In a village under a lake we found a girl's diary. She'd been told her death would end a cough."</font> Mei's voice went nowhere at all. <font color=moccasin>"It had already ended. Somebody with a wheelbarrow ended it two years before. They kept killing people for it anyway, because by then the killing was the part that worked."</font>

Jinhsi held completely still.

<font color=gold>"…Her name."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"A-Ling."</font>

<font color=gold>"A-Ling,"</font> Jinhsi said, and it landed as a filing rather than an echo. <font color=gold>"She goes into the record beside the ledger that priced her. Her own words, her own hand, with the ledger's page facing. That is a doctrine of this office, it has a number, and I will give you the number tomorrow when I am able to think."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno already started the paperwork."</font>

<font color=gold>"Of course she did."</font>

Narrator: Amy had been watching the Magistrate's left hand for eleven minutes.

It had been closed since before the escort left. Closed the way you close a hand around something you have not decided what to do with. Jinhsi had poured tea with the other one and turned a page with the other one and had kept the left one shut all afternoon.

<font color=mistyrose>"He said something to you."</font>

The room's temperature held and something in it moved.

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Privately."</font>

<font color=gold>"Yes."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"About the Sentinel."</font>

Mei said, <font color=moccasin>"Amy — "</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm not asking her to tell me."</font> Amy set her cup down on the flagstone beside her, untouched, gone cold. <font color=mistyrose>"I'm telling her I clocked it, so she knows she's being obvious, and so that when she keeps it she can keep it on purpose instead of by acting."</font>
