She is asked to dictate what happened.

What happened: a country made a form for the losing of a girl. What happened: the form had a box for the instrument, and the girl checked the box, because the girl had been taught penmanship. What happened: the form authorized one personal item of sentimental value. Sentiment, in the Republic of Ragunna, is a scheduled exception. Sentiment is the thing the bureaucracy permits you to carry after it has taken everything that works.

She carried the lute. It was her father's. Its neck had been broken by a playwright and mended, broken by a god and mended. She carried it onto a ferry that smelled of tar and oranges, and on the ferry she did not play.

## MELPOMENE &nbsp; &nbsp; TRAGEDY

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

*Pilgrim's Sail, out of Ragunna. Wind fresh from the west. One aboard.*

*Singing.*

&nbsp;

— from the ledger of Tammaro, lamplighter, Cape of Last Fingers

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

She went to find the record of his dying. She walked to the headland where the coast pilots turn back. She climbed the tower. The lamplighter showed her the page.

One line. Lamplighter's script — the letters formed the way they teach you, in case the log ever matters to a court.

The date. The wind. One aboard. *Singing.*

He was singing when the glass lost him. The wind was from the west, so the sound went out to sea, so what the lamplighter had was posture. The chest. The open mouth. A man at the rail, performing to an audience of salt.

&nbsp;

She climbed down to the rocks at slack tide. She was twenty-five. She had the Forte. She asked the sea for her father.

&nbsp;

*What the sea gave:*

Everything.

&nbsp;

All of it. At once. Ten thousand griefs, forty generations, every ship, every name called across water since names began, undifferentiated, fortissimo. One vertical chord. Total memory. The sky's size.

Her father in it somewhere.

&nbsp;

She came to on the rocks with blood in her left ear and a lamplighter hauling her up by the collar.

&nbsp;

The lamplighter said: *The water's a grave, girl. Graves keep everything and give back nothing. That's what makes them graves.*

&nbsp;

The Order edits. Keeps what power needs, burns the rest. Memory as property.

The sea keeps all of it, forever, equally, until keeping and forgetting are the same weather. Memory as flood.

Both of them are oblivion.

&nbsp;

So what is between them.

&nbsp;

*She* is between them. A singer chooses. Out of everything, this verse. Out of everyone, this name. Selection, with love — which is all a song is, when the varnish is taken off. The third memory. The sung memory: partial, biased, warm, alive. Because someone with a heartbeat decided it mattered and spent breath on it.

&nbsp;

*Breath.* From the body. Through the body. The body is the instrument and the instrument is the body and the sound passes through both the way a name passes through a border: changed. Carrying what it carried. Lighter by one country.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

She wrote him a song. She wrote him a hole.

> *He carried a key and they gave him the water.*
> *He stood at the rail and he sang out the leaving —*
> *— and here the verse waits —*
> *what he sang, the gulls keep.*

The hole was the center of the last verse, where the melody climbs and stops on the open fifth. A door, ajar.

She took her own father's death-song into the taverns and played it badly. On purpose. Fumbled at the door of the hole, every night.

Every night, a fisherman or ship's cook or harbor widow leaned in and fixed it.

Filled the hole. Told her what a man like that would have sung.

&nbsp;

In Varrena, a sailor gave the hole a verse where the man shares his last water with the gulls. *A singer knows his audience.*

Down the coast, a caulker's wife added a blessing for the ship. *Bless the wood, boys — the wood is innocent.*

In Lastra, a woman with flour to her elbows gave him an argument. A whole verse. On a point of music theory.

On the difference between a passacaglia and a chaconne.

*And wins.*

&nbsp;

She had never heard their name.

&nbsp;

A year on, a one-eyed tinker in a town she had never played was singing it.
Three verses she had never written.
The water verse had grown a second half.
There was a new one where the gulls follow the ship three days, out of respect.

She stood in the doorway with her hood up and asked where the song came from.

The tinker shrugged the shrug of the entire oral tradition.

*Everyone knows that one.*

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

Confiscate it, Fenrico. There is no manuscript. There is no author.

Arrest everyone.

## CALLIOPE &nbsp; &nbsp; EPIC POETRY

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

*From the running mock-epic of the pack, kept in a stitched notebook with a star in the margin where the truth is, in the hand of the witness who was there*

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

CANTO THE FIRST — *in which a woman with no name is pulled from the surf by a priestess with no right to keep her, and the keeping begins*

&nbsp;

There was a girl with nothing. She came out of the water the way all of them come out of the water — fourteen times the water, fifteen counting this one — wearing the one outfit the sea permits, which is a name two sizes too big.

A woman on the shore pulled her up by the wrist. The woman was blue silk and mathematics.

She said: *It's Mei, okay? You're called Mei. Now keep up.*

&nbsp;

She kept up.

&nbsp;

CANTO THE SECOND — *in which the witness is assigned, and the witness assigns herself, and these are different labors*

&nbsp;

She was sent by a merchant to observe. She observed. She made notes. She filed notes. She reported: the girl is fast, reckless, sincere past all tactical usefulness. She has a habit of catching people. She will catch anything. She catches falling fruit off the vendor's cart, and the vendor does not pay her, and she does not notice, because the catching is the whole compensation.

The notes were professional. The observer filed them. The observer kept a second notebook.

In the second notebook:

> *She laughed the whole way down. Both times. I have recorded this. It is unnecessary data. It is the only data I have filed twice.*

&nbsp;

The merchant paid for intelligence. The bard kept the second notebook for herself. This is the doctrine in its infancy: a song with a hole in it belongs to whoever mends it. But first there must be the witness, and the witness must decide: *this.* Out of all of it. *This one.*

&nbsp;

She was asked, later, why this girl.

She could have answered: because she catches. Because her grief is loud and her joy is louder and she fights like the openings are gifts. Because she apologized to a practice dummy. Because she smells like moonflower some mornings, which means the blue silk visits her at midnight and leaves her worse. Because she keeps everyone's name in her mouth like a coin she is afraid to spend.

She said: *Carlotta sent me.*

She did not say: *and I sent myself, and these are different labors.*

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

CANTO OF THE PRIESTESS — *in which the mathematics are revealed, and the water is the work*

&nbsp;

A priestess kept a girl on a fixed schedule of cold tea. Arrive late by design. Cold tea teaches waiting. Waiting binds.

The girl waited three hours. The tea went cold. The priestess drank it anyway.

The girl said: *make it hot next time.*

The priestess said she would.

*WHY DID I SAY THAT.*

&nbsp;

Here is the truth the priestess committed to thirty years of paper: she chose the future where the most people lived. She always chose it. One hundred and seventy-three. The number ran like a ground bass under everything she touched.

She filed a girl under EXPENDABLE. She wrote beside her name, in ink pressed hard enough to emboss the reverse:

**I can't.**

&nbsp;

A bard reads frequency. A bard reads what the body does while the mouth is lying. The priestess's body did this: it followed the girl home, rooftop by rooftop. It stood at her window in the rain. It raised its hand. It withdrew.

*Knuckles: split. Stone: fine. Discrepancy noted.*

&nbsp;

A love story told in the passive voice is still a love story.

A love story told in ledger entries is still a love story.

A love story told in filed exceptions — *she likes apples. Irrelevant. Filed anyway* — is a confession the author believed she had hidden inside bookkeeping, and the bard is here to tell the bookkeeper: the books do not balance. They have never balanced. The surplus is her. She is the remainder in her own arithmetic, and the remainder is love, and she filed it under *irrelevant*, and the file is the loudest thing in the archive.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

CANTO OF THE WOLF — *in which the fire is too big, which is the correct size*

&nbsp;

*Dictation. Repeat after me.*

&nbsp;

A child of seven stands in a ring behind a tannery. Fire climbs her arms.

The village looks at the burning girl the way they look at the autumn wool when the weighing comes out heavy.

That is the day a price forms around her. She feels it settle like a garment being fitted. She stands still for the fitting.

&nbsp;

The teacher says: Stand still.
The pupil stands still.

The teacher says: You are an asset.
The pupil: ________

&nbsp;

A woman on a tape says, to herself, before a bout: *you love this, you love this, they came to see you, don't — please — let me not disappoint them, please, just this once more —*

The roar takes her.

The wolf watches the tape forty times. On the fortieth, she begins to talk back.

*You drop your shoulder before the lunge. There. Again. Someone should have beaten that out of you in a season.*

&nbsp;

The woman on the tape is dead. The woman on the tape is the first colleague who stays.

&nbsp;

A bard knows: a dead woman who stays is a song. A song you finish is a tomb. Beautiful. Sung. *Shut.*

But the wolf does not finish her. The wolf talks to her fire instead. The fire is where the dead woman lives now.

*We hunt together. Even the dead. Especially the dead.*

&nbsp;

The bard writes, in the margin of the mock-epic:

> *Her tail keeps the census. Her fire is inhabited. She carries them or she drops them, and she has never dropped anyone. She builds the fire too big on purpose, so everyone has to sit close. I have written this as comedy. The comedy is load-bearing.*

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

CANTO OF THE MAIDEN — *in which a girl is sealed, and the dark is survived by naming*

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

Twenty years. A tower. One hundred dolls, named.

&nbsp;

The dark said: Give me your name.
The girl said: Ser Pincushion. Lady Thimble. Captain Crescent. Dame Buckle.

The dark said: Those are not your name.
The girl said: I have one hundred names and they are all mine and you may have none of them.

&nbsp;

Twenty years. A held breath. A sealed building has a sound.

&nbsp;

When the puppeteer skimmed those years — *boring, nothing happens, next* — the girl's first instinct was to agree. She looked at herself sewing in the dust and thought: *she's right. You're a held breath. A rock in a field.*

Then she broke the puppeteer's furniture.

But the agreement came first. That is the truth she owed, and she paid it, in a dormitory, over stolen tea, to a bard who understood the cost: that boring is the vault, that the tedious is where everything true gets kept, that a god skimming twenty years of dust and yawning is a professional endorsement from the vault's own enemy.

&nbsp;

The bard writes:

> *Nobody warned her you could build the lifeboat so well you never sail. She sailed anyway. She sails flat, out of key, into intervals outlawed in every tuning on every coast. I played along. I found the wandering phrase where it lived, put a floor under the illegal interval so it stopped being an error and became a feature. She hums on purpose now. I have created a monster. The monster rhymes.*

## URANIA &nbsp; &nbsp; ASTRONOMY

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

*The false stars come on across the cavern ceiling in their programmed constellations, each one named by a subcommittee, each one wrong.*

&nbsp;

A sky that admits to being built is ahead of most institutions she has known.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

She is underground. She has been underground for months, inside a kneeling god, beneath an engineered sky, in a city that rehearses its weather at 14:00 and apologizes over the announcement system.

Above them all, a girl's heartbeat in the god's chest. Below them all, a gate.

Between: the bard. Holding three notes she will not play.

&nbsp;

*Examination. The pupil is asked to identify the interval.*

*The examiner sounds: D — A — D.*

*The interval: a fifth, ascending. A fifth, descending. A return.*

*Da — da — da.*

*Three notes of a song interrupted thirteen years ago. Three notes of a girl at the bottom of a god.*

*The pupil holds them. The pupil does not play them.*

&nbsp;

Questioned: Why?

A song you finish is a tomb.

Questioned: And a song you keep?

The verse is still waiting. The door is still ajar.

&nbsp;

A woman in a tower kept a hundred names so the dark could not take hers. She knows what holding a door open with your own body costs.

She said: *You become the door.*

She said: *In every story you told me tonight, the mending happens in company.*

She said: *Your plan for Amy's three notes is to hold them alone, in the dark, in a box, forever — in the one pair of hands in the world most qualified to know better.*

&nbsp;

The pupil does not answer. The pupil holds the notes. The false stars tick.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

Then.

&nbsp;

The barrier is failing. The clock is dead. The lute is shattered — the god of ending broke it, the way a playwright broke it, the way a god broke it before — and the bard has nothing. The bard has three notes and a broken instrument and a body with cracked hands and blood in one ear from a detonation in a public square, and she has been carrying these three notes for years, in a box behind her sternum, and the box was a lifeboat and the lifeboat was a door and the door was her body and the body is giving out.

&nbsp;

*Ciaccona plays the three notes as a road.*

&nbsp;

*Da — da — da.*

&nbsp;

They were never fading. They were waiting to be played with someone else.

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

*Dictation. The pupil is asked to translate. The original is in a language the pupil has carried since birth but was never taught in school.*

&nbsp;

Original: *a hand found my shoulder in passing the way punctuation finds a sentence.*

Translate into: ________

&nbsp;

Original: *the toast levy was collected without violence.*

Translate into: ________

&nbsp;

Original: *a girl who spent a decade in the dark ate three bowls of rice like a public statement, and the whole room pretended that was normal, because pretending a miracle is normal is how you teach it to stay.*

Translate into: ________

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

The pupil cannot translate.

The pupil submits: these are already in the original language. The original language is a kitchen at six in the morning with a wolf-warm silence and an earl grey that has been stolen so many times it has become community property. The original language is a hand on a shoulder and a toast and a girl eating as if eating were an argument and winning. The original language is: *home.*

Home is the standing appointment. The gap kept open. The two cups on the desk, one good, one a lid, and the negotiation over which goes where, which between them is a whole treaty.

&nbsp;

The examiner notes: the pupil has not translated.

The pupil notes: translation implies the existence of another language. There is no other language. There is this one. The one spoken in the kitchen. The one filed under: *weather.*

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

She is asked: who sings the bard?

Nobody sings the bard. The bard is the frame.

*Frames that demand paintings embarrass everyone at the exhibition.*

&nbsp;

So if I wanted a Ballad of Ciaccona to exist. I would have to make it myself.

Out of whatever I happened to know. With my musical training.

Which consists of you wincing at me for two years.

And it would be rough. Holes in it. Enormous holes. Structural.

&nbsp;

*Oh, the bard came out of Ragunna with a key inside her lute —*

&nbsp;

You cannot rhyme *lute* with *truth*. The vowel is doing all the work and the work is bad.

Sounds like a hole in the verse.

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

She is asked: what is the ground bass?

The ground bass is: a short line in the low voice, repeating, while everything above it changes.

A passacaglia and a chaconne are almost the same dance. Both are variations over a ground. Two hundred years of fighting about the difference.

Her grandmother named them one each — her father and herself — one generation apart, to keep the argument going.

&nbsp;

The ground bass of this story:

*Leave the hole in the verse. That's where everyone gets in.*

## TERPSICHORE &nbsp; &nbsp; CHORIC DANCE

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

*A study in the instrument of the body.*

&nbsp;

The lute is strung with gut. The gut is from an animal. The animal was alive. The strings remember being alive in the way that all material remembers: when you draw a bow across gut, the sound the gut makes is the sound of a body remembering its body.

The lute's neck was broken and mended. The seam runs along the fingerboard, raised, so that the left hand, playing, reads the scar with every shift. The thumb traces it. Back and forth. A braille of damage.

The player's hands crack in cold weather. The knuckles split along the joints. She plays through it; the keys turn pink at the edges; the audience does not notice; the audience is attending to the sound, which is the point, which is the whole economy of performance: what the body spends so the ear can receive.

&nbsp;

The body:

The left ear, damaged. The detonation in the square — the Threnodian's frequency — left a ringing she has filed under *furniture.* It does not impair the work. She tunes to the right ear now. She has always tuned to the right ear. The left ear is where the ringing lives, and the ringing is not silence — it is the sound of a public square in which fifty thousand people witnessed a saint's corruption and a bard lay concussed, blood at her ear, while a priestess pulled her onto a chakram and a woman's hand clamped over hers: *Don't you dare.*

The throat. She opens. The air passes through. She does not think about the passage; a singer who thinks about the passage is a singer who has remembered that the body is a series of tubes and membranes, and the membrane is tissue, and the tissue can tear, and the voice is the most voluntary of the involuntary acts — you must choose it and then forget that you chose it, the way you must choose to breathe and then forget, or the breathing stops, or the breathing becomes performance, which is a different death.

The hands. They are her father's hands, in the sense that hands belong to lineage. His mixed varnish. Hers mix the air. The resin and the frequency. The house reeked of his work every spring. Hers reeks of weather.

&nbsp;

A body is an instrument someone else built and the player inherits.

A body is a lute with the builder's name inside the soundhole, readable only when the strings are removed and the eye is pressed to the opening and the light is held at the correct angle, which is the angle of total surrender.

&nbsp;

She does not look inside the soundhole.

&nbsp;

She plays.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

*Examination: physical.*

*The pupil is asked to demonstrate the mechanism of singing.*

*The pupil opens her mouth.*

*The diaphragm contracts. The lungs expand. The air rises through the trachea and passes through the vocal folds, which approximate — close — and vibrate at a frequency determined by their tension and mass. The vibration produces a fundamental tone. The resonating chambers of the pharynx, the oral cavity, the nasal cavity, shape the harmonic series above the fundamental. The tongue, the teeth, the lips articulate. The jaw releases. The soft palate lifts.*

*The sound exits the body.*

*The sound enters the room.*

*The room shapes the sound again: its walls, its ceiling, its furniture, the bodies in it — each body another resonating chamber, another shaping — until the sound that arrives in the listener's ear is not the sound that left the singer's mouth. It has been touched by everything between them.*

*The pupil demonstrates.*

*The examiner hears: a lullaby.*

*The lullaby goes:*

> *Sleep, little oar, the sea rocks your bed.*
> *The sky trims the lamp at your mother's head.*
> *Two hands on the cradle, one salt and one bright —*

*The examiner notes: the third line contains a doctrinal irregularity (see Index of Forbidden Harmonics, Schedule A, sub-schedule vii, "Dual Theologies"). The pupil should be aware.*

*The pupil is aware. The pupil learned the verse from a seventy-year-old woman with hands like tree roots in a grain-town tavern, who believed she was teaching a clumsy beginner her heritage. She was.*

*The examiner notes: the Conservatory does not recognize unregistered pedagogical encounters.*

*The pupil notes: the Order burned the library. They missed the nursery.*

## POLYMNIA &nbsp; &nbsp; SACRED HYMN

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

The sacred, she has learned, lives in the places the auditors skip.

A lullaby survives because it is beneath confiscation.
A lamplighter's ledger matters because it is tedious.
A tinker in a town she has never played knows her father's song because songs with holes in them cannot be arrested.

&nbsp;

The sacred is: Fiora, patting her arm. *You play like my granddaughter. Come back tomorrow and I'll teach you the rest.*

The sacred is: Tammaro, logging every ship that rounded the headland for forty years, and when she asked him why, he gave her the entire theology of his life in three words: *Someone should.*

The sacred is: a registrar in a country town, writing a child's birthdate as the previous day's, because a registrar's first duty is to the truth and his second duty is to know when the truth needs help.

The sacred is: an acolyte in a town square, watching a dead saint walk through her own hometown, saying *I didn't see anything*, and walking away without reporting it.

The bard called her the best musician in that square. Present company included.

&nbsp;

*Because she was dosing.* Song is medicine, and medicine is dosage. Play the truth too loud and the people nearest it burn. Too soft and the auditors win. The work is the middle register. The work is: *how much light, for whom, at what hour.*

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

*From the running mock-epic, stitched notebook, last entry of the day, star in the margin where the truth is:*

&nbsp;

She sings everyone. Mei has a whole cycle that keeps rearranging itself. Lupa's tail has opinions in iambs. Cartethyia gets a full canto with structural holes she refuses to repair. The ewe that tried to eat a boot has a stanza. The stanza scans.

The priestess is a fugue — subject, counter-subject, codetta — the form in which one voice pursues another through every key and finds, at the last page, that the pursuit was the composition.

The girl with the scissors gets a pavane, slow and stately and precise, because a face-blind girl who reads people by gait and voice and habit is a girl who reads truer than anyone in the pack, because faces are where people keep their lies.

The pilot gets a page of white. White like light. The copyist's hand, copying days from a journal she memorized, is the same exercise the bard learned from Fiora and Tammaro: *someone should.* The pilot wrote weather the way the devout write saints' names. The bard practices it back.

The girl who came home from the bottom of a god gets three notes and a bodyguard. The three notes became a road. The road brought her back. The bodyguard is the bard's body, which stood between the notes and the silence for years, which held the door open by being the door, which spent itself — hands, ear, throat — so the verse would still be waiting when the girl found her way to it. The bard calls this economy. The mock-epic calls it love.

&nbsp;

And the bard?

&nbsp;

Nobody sings the bard. The bard is the frame.

&nbsp;

Until a country girl with dirt under her nails and an illegal interval and a total absence of shame stands in a dormitory at midnight and begins.

*Oh, the bard came out of Ragunna —*

Rough. Enormous holes. Structural. Sung badly — which is, the bard is given to understand, the correct technique.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

*Attestation.*

&nbsp;

She attests:

She was sent by a merchant and she sent herself. She carried a key and a creed and a lute with a scar on its neck. She sang the truth in squares where it cost other people, and she learned the cost, and she learned to dose. She played badly on purpose, and the world poured into the holes she left and mended them in versions she will never hear. She stood in a doorway and tipped a tinker for her own father. She walked in the wrong direction out of a town so that an uncle she will never name could go on living under a name she will never know. She held three notes for years and played them as a road and the road brought a girl home. She lost the hearing in her left ear to a saint's detonation and she tunes to the right one now. She sang individual griefs back to a god, one at a time, until being witnessed became more interesting than being ordered. She has a running mock-epic with a star in the margins. She has a lot of griefs and one face. She has one cry per grief. She keeps a copper bell and a key and a stolen thermos of stolen earl grey.

&nbsp;

She attests: the whole doctrine runs on company.

A finished song has an owner. An owner can be arrested.

But a song with a hole in it belongs to whoever mends it.

Which is everyone.

Which is the only congregation that cannot be excommunicated.

&nbsp;

---

&nbsp;

She is asked to dictate.

She opens her mouth.

&nbsp;

*The last lesson.*

&nbsp;

The teacher says: ________
The pupil writes: ________

&nbsp;

The teacher has been taken. The teacher has been singing to gulls for twenty years. The teacher left a creed composed under arrest, in four seconds, with the guards at the door — the truest thing he had, in the most memorable shape he could build.

The pupil carries the creed. The pupil has spent a lifetime finding out what it means.

&nbsp;

The pupil writes, in the examination booklet, in the space where the answer goes:

&nbsp;

*Leave the hole in the verse.*
*That's where everyone gets in.*

&nbsp;

She puts the pencil down.

She picks up the lute.

She does not close the book.
