# SPOLIA

*spolia*, n. pl. — the spoils. Also, in the trade: old stone taken out of a ruin and set into a new wall, where it goes on holding something up under a different name.

---

**Colours.** Lanze Reiter = `lightsteelblue`. Ronin = `plum`. Sparrow (archer, hired) = `sandybrown`. Bell (mage, hired) = `darkseagreen`.

---

## THE ARM

The cyclops had come down with one arm under it, and getting the arm out was going to be the evening.

They had tried the obvious thing first, which was pulling. Then they had tried the second obvious thing, which was pulling harder, and Sparrow had gone over backwards into the wet grass with the wrist-strap still wound round her fist and lain there a while looking at the sky.

<font color=sandybrown>"Right. So it's a lever situation."</font>

<font color=plum>"Mm."</font>

Ronin had already gone for the pole. She came back with a length of ash out of the treeline, stripped it on the walk, and had it seated under the shoulder before anyone had finished agreeing.

Lanze Reiter put her boot on the fulcrum and her weight on the shaft. The body came up about a hand's width, the smell came out from under it all at once, and Sparrow made a sound like a kettle.

<font color=sandybrown>"*Ohhh*, that's — no. No. That's a personal insult. That's an insult aimed at me specifically."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"It has been dead for some hours."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Auntie, I'm aware. I was there. I helped."</font>

Bell set her staff down in the grass and knelt by the freed arm with her hands on her knees, and looked at it for a while in the way that people look at something they have decided to be careful with. Then she said, to nobody in particular:

<font color=darkseagreen>"May I touch it?"</font>

Sparrow stopped mid-complaint. Ronin's head came round about ten degrees.

<font color=plum>"It's dead."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Yes."</font> Bell kept her hands on her knees. <font color=darkseagreen>"I would still rather ask, and there are three of you, and one of you may mind."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Nobody minds. *Nobody* minds. It's a hill with a face."</font> Sparrow flapped a hand and went off toward the treeline. <font color=sandybrown>"Grey, tell her nobody minds."</font>

Lanze Reiter had gone down on one knee at the ankle, and did not answer, because she had found the rope.

---

It was around the left ankle, above the joint, and it had been there long enough to have gone into the flesh and had flesh grow up around it. Old cord, three-strand, tarred once and the tar long since gone grey. The free end had been cut with something sharp and cut *cleanly*, which is a thing you do on purpose.

The knot was a picket hitch. Two round turns, a bight through, the standing part dogged back on itself so a pull tightens it and a hand can still throw it off in the dark, one-handed, in a hurry, wearing a gauntlet.

She had tied nine hundred of them. She had been taught it by a man who counted out loud while you did it and stopped counting when you got it right, and she had got it right on the eleventh day, and the number he had reached before he stopped was six.

She looked at it for about two seconds.

Then she coiled the cord off the ankle, worked the knot loose with her thumb without untying the shape of it, and put the whole thing in her pack.

Ronin, at the other end of the animal with the pole still in her hands, watched her do it.

---

The hoard came to four coins, a boot, and something that had once been part of a cart.

<font color=sandybrown>"Four."</font> Sparrow held them up and fanned them. <font color=sandybrown>"Four coins. It was eleven feet of solid personal problem and it was worth four coins. I want you all to understand that I put nineteen arrows into that thing."</font>

<font color=plum>"Eighteen."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"I *put* nineteen in."</font>

<font color=plum>"You have eighteen."</font>

Sparrow looked at her quiver. Then she looked at the field, which was long and dark and full of grass with a dead cyclops in it, and her whole face changed shape and settled into something patient and awful.

<font color=sandybrown>"Right,"</font> she said. <font color=sandybrown>"Start the fire without me."</font>

---

## NINETEEN

The others got the fire going in the lee of the outcrop while Sparrow walked the field.

She did it properly, which nobody had expected. She started at the body and went outward in arcs, feet placed, sweeping the grass flat with the side of her boot, and she said the number out loud each time she found one and dropped it in the quiver, and by the second circuit the numbers had stopped being cheerful.

<font color=sandybrown>"Fourteen. Fifteen. — Sixteen, and that one's bent, that one's kindling."</font>

Bell watched from the fire with her hands in her sleeves.

<font color=darkseagreen>"Is it the cost?"</font>

<font color=plum>"No."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Then I do not know what it is."</font>

<font color=plum>"Neither does she. That's why she's still out there."</font>

Ronin took the hatchet and went for wood, up the slope where the ground broke into roots and old stone, and forty paces in she found the seventeenth arrow.

It had gone through the grass and into the seam between two roots and snapped about a third of the way down the shaft. The fletching was intact. The head was intact. It was, by any standard Sparrow used, an arrow.

Ronin crouched over it with the hatchet across her knees.

Down in the field Sparrow's voice came up, small with distance, going *seventeen, no, that's a stick, that's a stick, that's a—*

Ronin picked the pieces out of the root.

It took her about four seconds.

Then she put them up her sleeve, took an armful of deadfall, and went back down to the fire.

---

## THE FIRST WATCH

The drop came about an hour in, all four of them at once, the way it does.

Sparrow had been standing up to demonstrate something and sat down mid-word and stayed sat. Bell's hands came out of her sleeves and lay palm-up on her knees like they had been put there by somebody else. Ronin's shoulders came down two inches and stopped fighting the harness. Even the grey plate went quiet — a long ticking as it cooled, and then nothing.

Lanze Reiter sat with her back against the rock and her helm off beside her on the stone, and her hair was flat with sweat that ought to have evaporated an hour ago, and she looked at the fire.

Then she said:

<font color=lightsteelblue>"At 2214 the archer opened at forty metres."</font>

Sparrow raised her head off her arms.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Forty is long for a cyclops. The first shaft went into the shoulder seam, above the strap, where the plate rides up when the arm comes back. That is the correct target. Most take the eye at that range, and at that range the brow covers the eye, and the shaft goes into bone and does nothing."</font>

A pause.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Nineteen shafts. Fourteen in the seam."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"…I aim for that,"</font> Sparrow said, to the fire, in a completely different voice, and then caught herself and put the other voice back on. <font color=sandybrown>"You hear that, Already? *Correct target.* Get that engraved on something."</font>

<font color=plum>"She keeps a book."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"— yeah, that's the horrifying part."</font>

Lanze Reiter went on. She had not stopped for the interruption; she had put it in the report.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"At 2219 the mage came forward to eleven metres and held there for the duration."</font> Her eye came round to Bell. <font color=lightsteelblue>"Eleven metres is inside the club. It went over you twice. You held."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"I was where I could reach you."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

She turned her head back to the fire and kept going. She gave them the whole thing. She gave them the second knee going, and the eleven seconds it spent on one leg before it committed to falling, and the direction it chose to fall in and why that direction was lucky and how much of the luck belonged to the ground. She gave them Ronin's shield angle at 2223, which had taken a downward blow at forty degrees off vertical and shed it into the hillside instead of into an arm, and which she described in more detail than the blow itself. She gave them a note about the grass being wet, and what wet grass does to a shield foot, and how none of them had slipped, which she appeared to consider remarkable.

It went on for a long time. Nobody had asked for any of it.

Sparrow lay on her back with her boots to the fire and listened with her mouth open. Bell listened the way you listen in a building where listening is what the building is for. Ronin fed the fire, and turned a stick when it needed turning, and did not say a word, and watched her.

When it finished, it finished in the middle. Lanze Reiter stopped, and looked into the fire, and it became apparent that she had run out of things she had written down and had reached the part she did not have a form for.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"It was a well-run engagement,"</font> she said.

<font color=sandybrown>"It was *rad*."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"…Yes."</font>

---

<font color=sandybrown>"Okay. Okay okay okay. Serious question."</font> Sparrow rolled onto an elbow. <font color=sandybrown>"How many of these have you done? Ballpark. Twenty? Am I insulting you? Fifty?"</font>

The pause went about two seconds.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"This is the first."</font>

Sparrow laughed. Then she stopped laughing in stages, checking each of the other two faces on the way down and finding no help in either of them.

<font color=sandybrown>"The first *cyclops*."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The first of any of them."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"— Right, sure, but you've got — "</font> Sparrow gestured at all of her, the plate, the sword, the pack, the several centuries of finish worn off the pauldron in the exact place a strap goes. <font color=sandybrown>"How old *are* you?"</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Four."</font>

Sparrow waited.

Bell said, gently, <font color=darkseagreen>"Units?"</font>

Lanze Reiter put another stick on the fire and did not answer, and after a moment Sparrow started talking about a man in Bakbattahl who had sold her a bad bowstring, and the fire went on, and the field outside the light was very large and quiet.

---

## THE PAPER

Sparrow needed tinder around midnight and started going through the pack that was nearest, which was Lanze Reiter's, because Sparrow had been raised by nobody and had taken from that a policy of total property communism which she applied without malice in every direction including her own.

She came up with a folded page, soft at the creases from carrying.

<font color=sandybrown>"This going in?"</font>

Lanze Reiter looked at it.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"…Read it."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Ohhh, is it a *love* letter."</font> Sparrow was already on her feet. She held it up to the firelight, cleared her throat, and did a herald: chest out, chin up, a voice pitched to carry over a market.

<font color=sandybrown>"*To the bearer of the mark, greeting!*"</font>

Ronin's head came up.

<font color=sandybrown>"*The office of the Captain of the Guard requests your attendance at the Stardrop Inn, at your convenience, regarding a matter of the succession which cannot be committed to paper —*"</font>

The herald voice went out of her about there. She read the rest of it at a normal volume, faster, the way you read something you have realised you are holding.

<font color=sandybrown>"— *come alone or come with everyone. B.*"</font>

The fire did what fires do.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Read it again."</font>

Sparrow's eyes went from the page to the woman in the grey plate and back to the page. She was loud and she was quick and the two arrived at the same conclusion at the same time, and to her lasting credit she did the whole of the working in silence.

Then she sat down on the stone, put the page on her knee, flattened it with the side of her hand, and read it again from the top. Slowly. Properly. In her own voice, with the punctuation where the punctuation was.

When she got to the end she said, in exactly the same tone she had used for the rest of it:

<font color=sandybrown>"It's the old orthography for *succession*, that one. Two characters, and the second one's been out of use since before my last three owners. Nobody reads it. I only know it off cargo seals."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Noted."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"S'a stupid alphabet anyway."</font> Sparrow folded the page along its own creases, put it back in the pack, and put the pack back exactly where it had been, which she had never once done with anything. <font color=sandybrown>"Grew four extra letters in three hundred years and threw two away and kept the ugly one. Learn it in a week. Offer stands."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Noted."</font>

---

Bell said, after a while, <font color=darkseagreen>"They are offering you a throne."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"It's a casting call."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"That's the same — "</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Role offered."</font> Lanze Reiter picked up her helm and turned it over and started going along the strap with her thumb, checking the stitching, which she had checked twice already since the fight. <font color=lightsteelblue>"No action indicated."</font>

Sparrow made a delighted noise and fell over.

<font color=sandybrown>"She filed it. She filed the *crown* — Already, are you getting this down, somebody's offered her a country and she's put it in the tray — "</font>

<font color=plum>"I heard."</font>

Ronin was looking at the pack.

She had seen the signature at the bottom of the page from three feet away, upside down, in firelight, because that is what she is for. And she had seen the other one — the one in the log, on the ninth page from the back, in the section Lanze Reiter keeps folded in on itself so the paper has worn through along the fold — the mark at the bottom of a requisition for winter forage, dated in a system that ran out four hundred years ago.

The same B. The same little kick on the downstroke, like a man signing quickly and enjoying it.

Ronin has known about this for eleven days.

She has decided, twice now, to say something about it in the morning.

---

## THE SECOND WATCH

Sparrow took second watch because Sparrow could not have slept if you had paid her, and Ronin took it with her because sleeping was optional and Sparrow talking to herself for three hours was a thing that could be prevented.

They sat on the outcrop above the fire with the field below them going silver where the dew came in.

<font color=sandybrown>"Eighteen."</font>

<font color=plum>"I know."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"It's the count. I want that on the record, because Auntie has it down as the money, and Auntie is very kind and very wrong."</font> Sparrow had her chin on her knees. <font color=sandybrown>"You put nineteen out, you bring nineteen back. Every time. That's the whole discipline. Everything else is aiming, and aiming is easy, anyone can aim."</font>

<font color=plum>"And when one goes."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Then there's one of mine out there in the dirt doing nothing."</font>

The dew came up the field.

<font color=sandybrown>"There was a fella hired me out of Bakbattahl for a season. Nice enough. Wept a lot. He used to say the count was superstition."</font> She picked at the fletching of the top arrow with her thumbnail. <font color=sandybrown>"And I'd go, sure. Sure it is. And then I'd go out at night and find it."</font>

---

Much later — the fire down to a red bed, Bell asleep sitting up against the rock the way she does — Sparrow said, out of a long nothing:

<font color=sandybrown>"You ever hear yourself?"</font>

<font color=plum>"Say more."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Recruitment cylinder. Bakbattahl, in the arcade off the smith's row, they've got the wall of them and they run all day."</font> She was still working the fletching. <font color=sandybrown>"I'm walking past and there's this voice going *and there's always room for one more at the fire!* — big finish, lift before the last word, little laugh at the end. And I stopped. Because that's my laugh. That's the exact one. I've been doing it since the day I switched on."</font>

<font color=plum>"…"</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Package two point one. Warm, quick, good with children."</font> She turned the arrow round and started on the other side. <font color=sandybrown>"Cost extra, apparently."</font>

Ronin let it sit.

<font color=plum>"And you kept the laugh."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Course I kept the laugh."</font>

<font color=plum>"Why."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Because if I put it down I want to know what's underneath, and I've decided not to find that out tonight."</font> She said it fast and easy, the way she said everything, and then she added, at the same speed, <font color=sandybrown>"Or this year. Ask me in the spring."</font>

---

Ronin sat with that for a while and found, in the way that you find you have been holding something, that she was going to say it.

<font color=plum>"At the engagement."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Mm."</font>

<font color=plum>"When it turned. I moved before—"</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"*Ohhh*, hang on, hang on — "</font> Sparrow sat bolt upright and pointed down the slope with the arrow. <font color=sandybrown>"That's a *fox*. That is a fox and it is going *directly* for the cyclops. Do you know how long that's going to take it? It's going to be at that all winter. Look at it. Look at its little face. It's got no idea what it's committed to."</font>

The fox went about its business.

<font color=plum>"…Yes."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Sorry. You were saying."</font>

<font color=plum>"It'll keep."</font>

---

Somewhere in the third hour Sparrow, half asleep, decided that the pawn beside her had been going by a category for long enough, and produced a name for her, and the name was ridiculous.

<font color=sandybrown>"— because you've always *already* done it. That's the thing about you. Wood's cut, pot's on, pole's under the arm, and nobody's said anything yet. It's unnerving. It's a real problem for me personally."</font>

<font color=plum>"Mm."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Already. That's you. G'night, Already."</font>

She was asleep in under a minute, sitting up, with eighteen arrows in the quiver against her knee.

Ronin sat the rest of the watch with a broken arrow up her sleeve and left it there.

---

## THE THIRD WATCH

Bell woke at exactly the hour, which she does, and came and sat down at a polite distance with her staff across her knees.

Lanze Reiter was awake, and had been awake for the whole of the second watch, sitting against the rock with her helm in her lap and her thumb going along the same six inches of strap.

<font color=darkseagreen>"May I look at your side?"</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"There is no injury."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"It went over you at 2226."</font> A small pause, and then, with the faint pride of a new speaker of a language: <font color=darkseagreen>"By your own count."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The plate took it. There is no injury."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"May I look anyway."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"No."</font>

Bell put her hands back in her sleeves and sat with that, and after a moment she said:

<font color=darkseagreen>"Thank you."</font>

The strap stopped.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Explain."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"I have asked eleven thousand and some times."</font> Bell's face stayed where it was. <font color=darkseagreen>"Every hand, every field, every road. I ask, the answer comes back yes, I do the work, I go on to the next. Eleven thousand yeses."</font> She looked at the fire. <font color=darkseagreen>"At about the four hundredth I understood that I was making a noise before the work, the way a kettle makes a noise before the tea, and I went on making it, because it was the only part of the procedure that was mine."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"…"</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"You said no."</font> She turned her hands over in her sleeves. <font color=darkseagreen>"It was a question after all. Eleven thousand of them were questions, all along, the whole time. I did not know that until this evening."</font>

The fire went down a notch.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Eleven thousand is a large number for your service."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Yes."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"How long have you been out."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Three weeks."</font>

---

The fire went a while on its own. Then Bell said:

<font color=darkseagreen>"There is a woman in the winter. In a doorway. She is holding my wrist and she is doing something to my arm and she is being kind about it, and she has not asked me, and I did not know then that asking was a thing that could be done, so at the time it was only weather."</font> She said this in the same even voice she used for everything. <font color=darkseagreen>"It is a very old winter. There are pines. I have never seen a pine."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Whose is it."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"I do not know. It came back with me."</font> A pause. <font color=darkseagreen>"Several things came back with me. That is the one I use."</font>

---

Later she said, without any change of position:

<font color=darkseagreen>"There is something in you that finished a long time ago and is still going."</font>

Lanze Reiter looked at the fire.

<font color=darkseagreen>"I do not have the word for it and I am not asking for the word."</font> Bell adjusted the staff on her knees. <font color=darkseagreen>"I am telling you because you will want to know that it is legible from eleven metres, and nobody in this camp is going to say so twice."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Noted."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"May I say one more thing."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"The ground moved twice tonight. Once at the second hour and once about eight minutes after."</font> Bell looked out at the black field. <font color=darkseagreen>"Nobody else marked it. I have decided that this means my legs are new and I am wrong. I would like to say it out loud once anyway, so that it has been said."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"It has been said."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Thank you."</font>

She got up, bowed slightly, went back to her rock, and was asleep sitting up inside a minute.

---

## BEFORE LIGHT

Ronin came and sat down where Bell had been.

The fire was nearly out. The east was doing the thing it does an hour before it is willing to admit anything.

Lanze Reiter had the rope out of her pack and across her knees, and was working the knot back into it. She had it four-fifths of the way there. Two round turns, a bight through, the standing part dogged back.

<font color=plum>"You've had that out three times."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=plum>"It's a picket hitch."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

Ronin waited.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"You tie it in the dark, one-handed, gauntleted, at the end of a day when your hands have stopped working properly."</font> The thumb went round the bight and drew it home. <font color=lightsteelblue>"You tie it to a stake, and you tie the other end to a horse, and then you sleep, and the horse is there in the morning."</font>

<font color=plum>"And the last time you tied one."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Four."</font>

The fire ticked.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Somebody picketed that animal. Above the joint, so it could take weight and it could not slip the cord. They cut it free with a good edge, cleanly, which is a thing you do when you have decided something and have time to do it properly."</font> She turned the coil over. <font color=lightsteelblue>"The cord has been in the flesh a long while. Longer than the animal has been on this side of the river."</font>

<font color=plum>"Someone owned that."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Someone owned it, and fed it, and let it go, and put my drill sergeant's hitch on its leg to do it, and that hitch went out of the world with a country that has no name left in the register."</font>

She stopped.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"That is the whole of it. The assessment line is empty."</font>

<font color=plum>"And you want it full."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

She put the rope back in the pack.

---

Ronin looked at the lance where it lay against the rock with the rest of the kit, wrapped, the shaft clean, the finish unworn where a hand would go.

<font color=plum>"You could have used it."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"At 2211 the animal was in the open and coming on and I had thirty metres of level ground. Yes."</font>

<font color=plum>"And."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"And it is a horse weapon. It works because a horse is already going, and the horse has decided, and the rider does the aiming."</font> Lanze Reiter turned the helm over in her lap. <font color=lightsteelblue>"On foot I would have to decide first, and then run, and by the time I arrived it would be a very heavy pole."</font>

Ronin let that be a joke, since it was almost one.

<font color=plum>"So it wants somebody already going."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes. That is the design."</font>

---

The east got a little worse about admitting things.

<font color=plum>"There's something I —"</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"At 2214 you moved before I did."</font>

Ronin stopped.

Lanze Reiter was looking at the fire, and her voice had not changed at all, and she went on in the register she uses for grass conditions and shield angles and the number of shafts in a shoulder seam.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The archer opened at forty metres. The mage came up to eleven. You were inside the reach at four seconds and I was inside at seven, and the three seconds are mine, and I have been looking at them since."</font>

The fire had nothing left in it worth calling light.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"It was correct."</font>

Ronin sat very still.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"You went because the two of them went, and the two of them went because nobody ever told them it was optional."</font> She set the helm down on the stone beside her. <font color=lightsteelblue>"I want that entered. It is going in the day's page. It is going in in ink."</font>

<font color=plum>"…"</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"You had something."</font>

Ronin turned the broken arrow over once inside her sleeve, where it had been for six hours.

<font color=plum>"It'll keep."</font>

---

## THE RIFTSTONE

They came down to the road stone a little after sunrise with the dew all over everything and the four coins split four ways, which took some doing and produced a great deal of comment.

Sparrow got up, stretched, swore at her own spine, and counted the quiver on reflex the way she had counted it eleven times since midnight.

She got to nineteen.

She counted it again.

She looked at the field for about as long as it takes to decide something. Then she looked, for about a second and a half, at Ronin, who was doing up a strap and kept doing up the strap.

Then she slung the quiver, and said, in exactly her ordinary voice:

<font color=sandybrown>"Miscounted."</font>

<font color=plum>"Ah."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"In the dark. Adrenaline. Happens."</font>

<font color=plum>"Mm."</font>

Sparrow shouldered past her on the way to the stone and hit her, once, quite hard, in the arm with the flat of her hand, and kept walking.

---

Bell stopped in front of Lanze Reiter and stood a moment with her staff held in both hands.

<font color=darkseagreen>"May I say a thing before I go."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"You gave the report last night and nobody had asked you for it."</font> Bell's face did what it did, which was very little, in a way that was somehow warm. <font color=darkseagreen>"You said where each of us stood and what it cost and you got all four of us into it, and you did that for an hour, and I have been in nine parties and no one has ever told me where I was standing."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"You were at eleven metres."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"Yes."</font> She bowed. <font color=darkseagreen>"I know. That is what I am thanking you for."</font>

---

Sparrow, at the stone, turned round and walked backwards for the last three steps.

<font color=sandybrown>"Right! Grey. Alphabet. Week. Offer stands, it's a *standing* offer, that's what standing means — "</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Noted."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Auntie — the asking's slow. Everyone's going to tell you so. Keep doing it."</font>

<font color=darkseagreen>"I intend to."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"*Already* — "</font> Sparrow pointed at her with two fingers, walking backwards, grinning like a door coming open. <font color=sandybrown>"Nineteen."</font>

And then the stone took them, the way it does, without any ceremony at all, and the road was empty, and there were two of them again.

---

Somewhere else, that morning or a morning very like it, an archer arrives home to somebody who has been waiting up, and puts down a quiver, and starts talking about a woman in grey plate who does an after-action report like a saint's calendar; and a mage arrives home to somebody else and says that she was told no, once, and has been turning it over ever since. Both of them come back annotated. Neither of them will ever find out what they did.

Ronin knows this. She stood at the stone and watched it happen and thought about it in full, once, and put it down.
