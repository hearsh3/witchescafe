# THE BATTLE BENEATH THE CRESCENT

## ACT I — BEFORE THE SPEECH

Narrator: Cristoforo raised two fingers.

Across the northern lip, six Fractsidus spotters lowered their sighting glasses together. Their lenses caught a pale crescent of sky, then the south shelf, then the people gathering around Jinhsi's field seal. Thin copper wires ran from each blind into the rocks. The wires belonged to recent manufacture. The powder beneath the shelf had been packed by hand.

Nivora watched the wind test one wire and pass over it.

<font color=violet>"You asked for the first edition,"</font> they said. <font color=violet>"The first edition opened with survivors describing confusion. Your charges look very organized."</font>

Cristoforo kept his fingers raised. Below, Jinhsi stepped onto a flat stone between Jiyan's command board and the first waterproof register case. A clerk offered her a speaking horn. She accepted it, checked the mouthpiece for rain, and glanced once toward the black banner.

Nyx Ravenhollow stood behind the center in white and gold, five meters of restored Startorch armor with eleven feather blades folded down her back. The acid-green name across her shoulder showed clearly from the ridge. Her lance rested point-down beside Amy, dead emitter planted in the mud. Amy's shell carried the relay and the operator's access; the machine waited with paired blue eyes on the valley.

<font color=lightyellow>"Confusion requires preparation,"</font> Cristoforo said. <font color=lightyellow>"Chaos left unattended settles into a mess, and a mess teaches the audience to look away."</font>

Scar rolled his right shoulder. His eyes tracked Qiuyuan at the forward berm.

<font color=lightpink>"Your western team set their range twelve paces short."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"They set it exactly where I requested."</font> Cristoforo smiled at the valley. <font color=lightyellow>"A wound near the road draws the hand that keeps reaching for everyone. Watch where she goes."</font>

Jinhsi lifted the speaking horn.

Cristoforo brought his fingers down.

The south shelf struck first.

Stone kicked upward under the medical track. A flat orange flash opened inside the earth, bright enough to lay every body on the ridge against the eye in black outline. The pressure reached them a fraction later. It hit Mei through her teeth. Her jaw snapped shut; the edge of her tongue split, and the world became a high metal scream.

The speaking horn left Jinhsi's hands.

Chixia hit her at the waist and carried both of them behind the command board. Iron pellets crossed the space where Jinhsi had stood. Three punched through the map rack. One entered Chixia's calf and stopped against bone. Her leg folded as they landed.

The second charge tore open the west berm.

Master Gong Mi had been turning toward the first blast. A slab caught him across the back and drove him face-first into the cradle road. Engineer Deng Hao reached for him and took a stone splinter through the throat. His hands went to the wound. Blood ran between his fingers in two quick sheets.

The third blast burst above the column.

It came from a clay shell buried high in the eastern wall, packed with nails, type sorts, and clipped lengths of wire. San Jie's shield crossed half the distance before the shell opened. Nyx turned with it. The center caught the blast. Fragments drummed against both machines; the outer spray passed around their edges and entered people. One feather blade tore loose from Nyx's lower rank, spun over the command board, and buried itself upright in the west berm.

Amy felt a blow under her left ribs. Air vanished from her chest. She landed on the register case with her mouth open and her body searching for the next breath. Ciaccona struck the ground beside her, one hand trapped under the lute. The instrument gave a low, sick chord.

Iuno's crystalline stump rang like a struck glass. Pain climbed through the missing limb and filled the shoulder, cold and electric. Her knees touched stone. The field map skated past her living hand.

Mei reached for Amy.

The red tether at her wrist jerked hard from the other direction. Iuno had wrapped the line around her fist.

<font color=royalblue>"Your daughter has Ciaccona beside her. Take my shoulder and stand up with me."</font>

Mei's hearing returned in pieces. The sentence reached her through water. She saw Amy push one palm against the case, saw Ciaccona's free hand catch the back of Amy's harness, and obeyed.

Jiyan's whistle cut across the ringing.

Two short calls turned the Rangers inward. A long fall sent the relief sections below the berm. His voice followed, roughened by dust.

<font color=lightblue>"Third Relief, cover the medical track. Fifth Engineers, lift from the hips and clear Gong Mi's chest. Sun, close the middle. Du, give me the north wall."</font>

Sergeant Du had blood down one side of his face. He crawled to the observation slit, pressed his cheek against the stone, and raised two fingers.

<font color=burlywood>"Movement in both cuts. Thirty or more coming low, with another rank holding the ridge."</font>

Jiyan wiped grit from his mouth. His lower lip had split against his teeth.

<font color=lightblue>"Let the front rank reach the marker stones. Shields stay angled for the high guns."</font>

Amy pulled the felt Mei from her inner pocket. Her fingers folded around the little black-yarn head and gold buttons. Ciaccona shifted behind her and laid a steady hand between her shoulder blades, injured fingers curled safely against her own palm.

<font color=mistyrose>"Going in. My number is fifty-seven, and Nyx has lost one lower flight blade."</font>

Iuno found the field clock in the mud and struck its lever.

<font color=royalblue>"The bracket is entered. I will call each minute through your relay."</font>

Chisa angled her head toward Amy's breathing.

<font color=coral>"Her frequency is moving left and upward. Ciaccona has the shell."</font>

Amy's face emptied. Her lungs continued their even rhythm while her weight settled into Ciaccona's hand.

Nyx Ravenhollow's blue eyes flared.

The machine tore her lance from the mud. Ten flight blades opened in two ragged ranks, their jets climbing into a blue roar. Damaged servos dragged at the left shoulder; Amy rolled the joint until the catch released with a crack that belonged to metal.

<font color=mistyrose>"All right,"</font> Nyx said through her external speakers, voice huge enough to cross the shelf. <font color=mistyrose>"My family carried me four hundred kilometres for this. Somebody point me toward the people shooting at our paperwork."</font>

Jiyan lifted his spear toward the northern cut.

<font color=lightblue>"Break their high line and keep your emitter below the ridge. Our wounded lie beyond it."</font>

The Fractsidus came through the dust in two curved lines.

Their coats formed a stolen assortment. Quarry brown, Ranger grey, printer's black, a medic's faded blue: clothing chosen to cost the eye an extra heartbeat. Each fighter wore a white crescent stitched inside the left cuff. The curve flashed whenever a hand rose to fire.

Their first volley struck the ropes.

Broad-headed bolts cut relief lines where they crossed the berm. Hooked blades followed, thrown low to catch ankles and tethers. One buried itself in Captain Lan Shuo's thigh and dragged her off her feet. Her section caught her before the second pull took her over the broken edge.

Lupa planted her spear through the hook's chain.

The next pull tore the weapon sideways in her hands. Leather burned across her palms. She locked the shaft under one arm, put both boots against the rock, and felt two Fractsidus fighters hauling from the smoke below.

<font color=indianred>"Chisa, I have tension running northwest. Give the chain somewhere expensive to go."</font>

Chisa heard the links scrape over three kinds of stone. The chain passed beside Lan Shuo's breathing, under a Ranger's raised shield, through a metal eye hammered into the lower cut. Behind it, two sets of boots dug in while a third approached with a short axe.

Her scissor-blade opened.

<font color=coral>"Keep your weight for three more steps. Their anchor sits behind the second marker."</font>

Lupa gave ground. One pace. Two. Her shoulder pulled forward inside its socket. At three, Chisa closed the blades on the eye bolt.

The rock anchor came free. The chain whipped downhill, carrying its two handlers into their own advancing line. Lupa followed the released weight and drove her spear through a coat, mail, and the muscle beneath. The impact traveled up her arms. The fighter screamed into the dust at full human volume.

She pulled the blade free. Blood made the grip slick beneath her burned palms.

Around them, war became close enough to smell.

Powder smoke carried salt and bitter charcoal. Open blood smelled of wet iron. A Ranger vomited behind his shield after watching Deng Hao drown through the hole in his throat. Orderly Han pushed past him on both knees, caught Deng's wrists, and forced them down so she could pack the wound. Blood filled the dressing before her second fold.

Baizhi arrived with You'tan gliding above her shoulder and her medical case striking her knee at every step. She put two fingers against Deng's neck, watched his eyes, and moved to Master Gong Mi.

Engineer Pei looked up from the dead man's side. His face had grey dust pasted to it by tears.

<font color=plum>"Deng Hao has died. Keep his tag attached and move his body inside the shield line. Gong Mi is breathing under compression, and four people can lift the slab on my count."</font>

Pei stared at her for half a second. Then his hands found the slab.

<font color=plum>"Set your feet wider. Your back has already taken enough load today."</font>

You'tan's pale field settled over Gong Mi's crushed shoulder. Baizhi counted. Four engineers lifted. Gong Mi came free with a sound pulled from somewhere below speech. His right arm lay at an angle that made Pei look away.

<font color=plum>"You may scream,"</font> Baizhi told him as she cut the sleeve. <font color=plum>"It will help me track your breathing while I reduce the pressure."</font>

He screamed when she straightened the arm. He screamed again when the splint tightened. Both sounds traveled across the shelf, plain and useful.

At the command board, Jinhsi pressed both hands around Chixia's lower leg. Blood welled between her thumbs. Chixia had gone white around the mouth.

<font color=lightsalmon>"Magistrate, I had a better opening for your speech. There was a joke about the tax goose and everything."</font>

<font color=honeydew>"Save it for the dressing station. I require a full account, including the goose."</font>

Jinhsi pulled the field scarf from her own neck, folded it into a pad, and leaned her weight over the wound. Chixia's fingers clawed furrows through the mud.

<font color=lightsalmon>"That hurts enough to qualify as attempted murder."</font>

<font color=honeydew>"Your complaint is entered. Keep pressure here while I find Baizhi."</font>

Chixia caught Jinhsi's wrist before she rose.

<font color=lightsalmon>"The shooters waited for the horn. They wanted you standing in that exact patch."</font>

Jinhsi looked at the iron pellets buried in the map rack, then at the flat stone where her address had begun. Her first page lay in the mud. A fragment had pierced the sentence beneath the title.

<font color=honeydew>"Then they have studied ceremonies more closely than government."</font> She tucked the field seal deeper inside her coat. <font color=honeydew>"I can work from the ground."</font>

The northern line opened fire again.

Yangyang heard the shots travel through the Wind before her ears received them. The Welcoming Wind tightened around her shoulders, gathered dust and rain, and pushed sideways. Most of the volley bent with it. Three rounds kept their line.

Yangyang drew at the hip. Her first stroke turned one shot into the stone. The second grazed her upper arm and spun her half around. The third struck the register case beside Amy's quiet body.

The waterproof lid jumped. One hinge sheared away.

Two Fractsidus fighters cleared the lower marker. One carried a hooked knife. The other held a clay flask with a burning cord in its neck. Ciaccona hooked her boot through the case strap and kept her other hand against Amy's back.

Nyx's eyes turned toward them.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ciaccona, drag my body south by the harness. I can reach the case from here."</font>

Ciaccona rolled onto her injured hand, hissed, and brought the lute across her body. The trapped fingers answered with a wet pulse of pain. She played with the other hand: one hard descending run that gave the air weight.

The flask carrier stumbled as the sound struck his knees. His arm kept moving. The bottle left his fingers.

Cartethyia hit it in midair with the flat of her sword.

The clay burst against the rocks. Burning pitch spread over her right sleeve and the glass wall of the camellia terrarium. The little plant vanished behind black smoke.

Cartethyia dropped the pack, smothered the sleeve against her breastplate, and saw the pale leaf curl behind the soot-streaked glass.

Her next breath came out low.

Light unfolded around her in one clean surge. Fleurdelys rose from the small armored girl, one hand already closing around the hooked-knife fighter's chest.

<font color=skyblue>"Thou hast scorched a leaf entrusted to my keeping,"</font> she said. The deep voice shook grit from the register lid. <font color=skyblue>"Thy present review proceeds poorly."</font>

She threw him through the remains of the eastern blind.

Ciaccona clutched her hand against her stomach and looked at the terrarium.

<font color=teal>"She still has a green stem. Take that as your cue to keep the second case intact."</font>

Nyx's gauntlet closed around the case and lifted it clear of the burning pitch. Fleurdelys gathered Ciaccona and Amy's shell under one arm, keeping Ciaccona's palm planted through the move. The two giants met back to back for one step, white ceramic against radiant armor.

<font color=skyblue>"Fourteen centimetres remain a trifling distinction,"</font> Fleurdelys said.

<font color=mistyrose>"The official measurement stands, and I have the paperwork to prove it."</font>

The ridge gun fired.

Its heavy round struck Nyx below the named shoulder and drove her sideways. Static burned through Amy's awareness in the shape of a ruined joint. Nyx's left arm dropped; the register case began to slip from numb mechanical fingers.

Fleurdelys caught its lower edge. Together they lowered it behind San Jie's heel.

Nyx raised the lance in her right hand. The emitter opened at its licensed field setting, eight meters of blue cut to a narrow spear. She drove it under the ridge gun's mantlet and lifted. Wood, iron, and two firing braces came apart. The crew scattered from the emplacement as the weapon rolled into the cut.

<font color=mistyrose>"The permit covers this setting,"</font> Nyx announced, left arm hanging at her side. <font color=mistyrose>"Auntie filled in every box."</font>

The Fractsidus line bent around them.

Cristoforo had given his fighters three destinations. White chalk on their inner cuffs marked the register cases. Red marked Jinhsi. Gold marked Mei's right hand. Their crescent narrowed as every surviving operative obeyed a private mark and the shelf compressed them toward the middle.

Mei saw the shape from inside it.

The straight road opened ahead of her again. Through six bodies, over the broken berm, across the valley floor: reach the ridge, reach the man with two fingers raised, end the direction at its source. Her mark lit so brightly that the tendons of her hand shone through her glove.

Iuno leaned her crystalline shoulder into Mei's back.

<font color=royalblue>"Your feet have chosen the ridge. I need your eyes on the two fighters entering from our right."</font>

Mei turned.

The first blade passed close enough to move her hair. She caught the wrist, stepped through the fighter's balance, and drove him onto Iuno's raised knee. Bone broke in his forearm with a small, intimate crack.

The second fighter fired from an arm's length away.

Mei put her marked hand over the muzzle. Gold struck the charge as it left the barrel. Heat punched through her palm. The shot crossed the web between her first two fingers and tore a strip from the side of her hand.

Pain reached her elbow in a white rush. Her grip opened.

The fighter saw it and lunged.

Iuno's Moonwheel caught him behind the knee. He fell into Mei's shoulder. All three went down among broken map stakes. His knife entered Mei's coat and stopped against a rib. She felt the point scrape bone as he pushed.

Iuno caught his collar with her living hand and pulled until the fabric closed across his throat.

<font color=royalblue>"Move your hips left, Mei. I can give you the knife arm for one breath."</font>

Mei shifted. The red tether went taut between them. Iuno used it to draw the attacker's elbow across his own chest. Mei struck once under the jaw. His teeth met with a click, and his body sagged across her.

She pushed him aside and stayed on her knees. Blood ran from her palm, down the wrist, and into the tether knot. The shallow knife wound burned each time she breathed.

<font color=moccasin>"That ridge looked easier from here."</font>

Iuno sat back against the fractured board. Her crystal shoulder continued to hum from the first blast.

<font color=royalblue>"Your tactical imagination has always benefited from supervision."</font>

*<font color=royalblue>We are bleeding in mud beside a strangled cultist, darling. Try to look less pleased about being correct.</font>*

<font color=moccasin>"Stay close while I get up."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I have invested heavily in that arrangement."</font>

They rose together.

On the forward berm, Qiuyuan listened past the guns.

The Fractsidus assault carried layers: boots across scree, bowstrings, wet powder, pain voiced in every pitch the body could make. Beneath them moved one familiar cadence. Scar favored the outer edge of his left foot before a burst of speed. His coat buckle struck his belt on the fourth step. He breathed through his mouth when the old wound under his ribs tightened.

Qiuyuan turned his wrapped sword across his body.

Scar came over the berm smiling.

His blade hit the cloth around Qiuyuan's steel and cut through six layers. Qiuyuan received the force through his burned hand. Fresh blood opened under the dressing. He yielded half a pace, found Scar's next step in the vibration, and drove the iron pommel into the old rib wound.

Scar's breath broke into a laugh.

<font color=lightpink>"Mingting wrapped your sword and called the matter settled. They keep finding new ways to deserve you."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"Your people have placed themselves between wounded soldiers and their road south. I am occupied by the choice you made today."</font>

Scar's second cut took the bamboo flask from Sergeant Du's belt twenty paces away. The cord parted. The flask spun through smoke.

Qiuyuan heard Du reach. He heard another Fractsidus blade move toward the same point. He drew one handspan.

Steel cleared cloth with a dry whisper.

The attacking blade split at the base. The bamboo flask landed in Qiuyuan's open hand. Medicine struck the stopper and stayed contained.

Scar had already moved toward the command board.

Qiuyuan slid the flask inside his coat and followed at a measured run. His Forte dragged heat through the injured hand. Each heartbeat pushed more blood into the wrap.

Hsin watched the crescent close from San Jie's shoulder.

The guardian's shield had taken a bowl-shaped dent. One sensor cluster hung by its cable and tapped the armor with each turn. Engineer Luo Jiang shouted steering corrections from the collar platform while another crew member worked a manual crank beneath him.

Hsin rose. Her nine tails streamed through powder smoke like pale banners.

<font color=thistle>"San Jie, these guests have mistaken a curve for a trap. Turn your left foot inward and teach them geometry."</font>

The heavy platform stamped.

Rock split under its heel. The eastern half of the attacking crescent lost its footing as the shelf tilted. Rangers drove into the gap with shields shoulder to shoulder. Lieutenant Sun took a bolt through the meat of his upper arm and kept his shield locked against Bao's. Bao's bound thumb slipped inside the grip. She changed hands and leaned harder.

Jiyan entered the line beside them.

His spear moved at waist height, built for crowded ground. The butt struck a knee. The blade opened a coat from hip to sternum. A third Fractsidus fighter caught the shaft under one arm, and Jiyan stepped in close enough to put his forehead into the man's nose. Cartilage gave. The man dropped his grip and reeled into Sun's shield.

<font color=lightblue>"Bao, take three living names and move the western case behind San Jie. Sun, your arm gets a dressing when the rotation arrives. Captain He, advance the relief clock by thirty seconds."</font>

Captain He Yuan looked at the sand timer. Half its upper bulb remained full.

<font color=darkseagreen>"The next section has spent four minutes in reserve."</font>

<font color=lightblue>"They will spend the fifth minute on their feet. Bring them through the middle."</font>

The reserve came forward fresh enough to lift its shields. The Fractsidus had run uphill through smoke and their own fallen. Their breaths had shortened. Their crescent met a line that changed faces while keeping its shape.

The attack began to peel away.

Scar reached Jinhsi at the broken command board.

Chixia lay behind it with one hand clamped over her calf. Jinhsi stood over her, field seal in one hand and a Ranger's short sword in the other. The sword sat awkwardly in her grip. Her feet were steady.

Scar slowed, taking in the ruined speech, the blood, and the Magistrate in mud-dark field clothes.

<font color=lightpink>"Cristoforo promised me a proclamation. He said you would tell the valley what Jinzhou means."</font>

Jinhsi's gaze passed over his shoulder. She saw Qiuyuan approaching through the smoke. She saw Yangyang moving along the inner line with blood down her sleeve. She saw Baizhi wave a stretcher toward Gong Mi.

<font color=honeydew>"Jinzhou currently means six medical cradles, a damaged west road, and a relief section arriving behind you."</font>

Scar's smile widened.

<font color=lightpink>"That will disappoint him."</font>

<font color=honeydew>"He may file a petition during office hours."</font>

Chixia fired from the ground.

Her first shot grazed Scar's hip. The second shattered the stone beside his foot. He turned with the third, coat flaring, and Qiuyuan's wrapped blade met him across the retreat line.

Their weapons struck once. Scar gave the ground a last glance, measuring the shelf as though he meant to remember every body on it. Then he dropped backward over the broken berm. Two Fractsidus fighters released smoke pots below him. Grey swallowed the cut.

The northern ridge answered with a horn.

Fractsidus operatives disengaged in pairs. One dragged a woman whose leg bent beneath the knee. Another carried the fighter Lupa had speared, his blood leaving a broken line over the rocks. Rangers fired until Jiyan lowered his hand.

<font color=lightblue>"Keep the marked roads clear. The wounded on that slope remain inside recovery range."</font>

Sergeant Du looked toward the retreating coats.

<font color=burlywood>"Some of those wounded belong to them."</font>

<font color=lightblue>"Doctor Fei's eastern team takes anyone who reaches the marker stones. Record custody after breathing is secure."</font>

The firing thinned. Each final shot arrived separately enough to make bodies flinch before it struck.

Nyx knelt beside the command board. Her jets faded in sequence. Ciaccona kept her palm between Amy's shoulder blades while Chisa listened from six paces away. Iuno watched the field clock.

The machine's light went out. Amy's fingers tightened around the felt figure.

<font color=mistyrose>"Twenty-four. My side hurts, my mech's shoulder is jammed, and Fleurdelys still owes me fourteen centimetres."</font>

Ciaccona's breath left in a short, shaky laugh.

<font color=teal>"Seven minutes and twelve seconds. Your entrance ran long, your exit landed clean, and I am charging the machine for a new hand."</font>

Amy opened her eyes. The first breath caught under her bruised ribs. She looked up at Nyx's slumped white body, counted ten feather blades, then checked Ciaccona's swelling fingers.

Then the shelf held the sounds people made after battle.

Someone called for a mother with the earnest confidence of a child. Someone else laughed until the laugh turned into coughing. Gong Mi asked three times whether his hand remained attached; Baizhi answered with the condition of each finger while Orderly Han cut away his glove. Lan Shuo swore at the hooked bolt while two medics sawed through the shaft. Chixia tried to sit up, saw her own blood spread under her boot, and lay down again under Jinhsi's palm.

Amy found Mei beside the command board.

She saw the torn hand first. Her own side hurt with every breath. She crossed the distance anyway, caught Mei by the wrist above the wound, and turned the palm toward the light.

<font color=mistyrose>"I have one bruised rib and a working relay. This hand needs Baizhi before you volunteer it for another lesson."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I was planning to use the other one."</font>

Amy's eyes lifted.

<font color=mistyrose>"Try that joke again after she closes the hole."</font>

Mei leaned her forehead briefly against Amy's. Their breathing found the same pace, one shallow from bruising, one guarded around a scraped rib.

Iuno reached them with Ciaccona's injured hand supported against her forearm. Her crystal stump had developed a dark line near the shoulder where the blast resonance entered it.

<font color=royalblue>"The family count stands at seven present. Nyx is grounded and recoverable. Cartethyia is small again, furious, and guarding a singed plant. Lupa requires both palms cleaned. Chisa has acquired three prisoners and one cut across the cheek."</font>

Chisa approached at the end of a red tether, guiding two Rangers who supported a Fractsidus prisoner between them. Blood traced a level line from her cheekbone to her jaw. Her attention moved over the household by breathing and gait.

<font color=coral>"Ciaccona favors her left foot because her right hand hurts. Amy's inhale catches near the bottom. Mei has blood on the tether. Iuno's crystal changed pitch."</font>

Lupa came behind her, palms open and raw, carrying the second waterproof case under one arm.

<font color=indianred>"The pages stayed dry. Save the full inventory for the physicians, Chisa."</font>

<font color=coral>"You are holding your spear with two fingers."</font>

Lupa looked at her hands.

<font color=indianred>"Put me on the list, then. Baizhi can complain while she cleans them."</font>

Ciaccona cradled her swelling fingers and glanced toward the ridge where the smoke pots drifted apart.

<font color=teal>"Cristoforo spent powder, people, and surprise to place us on six marks from the old field map. He got four. The western case crossed the center on the hospital porters' path."</font>

Jinhsi heard her. She had Chixia's blood to both wrists. The speaking horn lay crushed beside the first line of her address.

<font color=honeydew>"General, give me the field return."</font>

Jiyan read from the board as reports reached him.

<font color=lightblue>"One dead confirmed: Engineer Deng Hao, Fifth Engineers. Seventeen wounded on our line, three critical. Two allied platforms damaged and mobile. Both register cases remain with the column. West cradle road is blocked for twenty paces. The lower route can carry hand litters. Third Relief holds the east. Enemy losses observed at nine down, with five inside our recovery markers and four removed north."</font>

Jinhsi knelt beside the ruined speech. She turned the page over. The reverse held clean paper apart from one blood print at the edge.

She wrote Deng Hao's name first.

Chixia watched from the ground, sweat cooling on her face.

<font color=lightsalmon>"You still owe them the inspiring part."</font>

Jinhsi looked across the shelf.

Pei and three engineers carried Deng's body behind San Jie's shield. Orderly Han kept one hand over his tag so it stayed with him. Captain He reset the relief clock. Yangyang and the Wind gathered scattered pages from the mud, each sheet delivered to Amy by its corner. Nyx knelt over them with one arm hanging and ten flight blades folded. Cartethyia held the terrarium against her chest and counted its green leaves aloud for Ciaccona. Qiuyuan stood while Du wrapped his hand again. Hsin had climbed down from the guardian and was helping Luo Jiang pull the broken sensor cluster clear of its wheel.

Jinhsi set the page on the command board.

<font color=honeydew>"They already have their instructions. The city can hear the account when the road carries it home."</font>

Above the northern lip, Cristoforo applauded once.

The sound carried strangely well.

Nivora studied the south shelf through a sighting glass. Their borrowed face had settled into stillness.

<font color=violet>"You wanted the Lady to run uphill. She stayed with her tether. You wanted the Magistrate upright at center. She is writing a casualty return in the mud."</font>

Cristoforo lowered his hands. Pleasure warmed his expression.

<font color=lightyellow>"The actors have brought private habits to public roles. That is where a production begins to live."</font>

Scar climbed back onto the shelf with blood darkening one hip. He wiped Qiuyuan's grit from his blade.

<font color=lightpink>"You lost the surprise. Jiyan kept both roads, and the cases are still moving."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"Act One has achieved its proper ending."</font> Cristoforo looked toward the crescent of pale sky above Norfall. <font color=lightyellow>"Everyone now understands that the floor is real."</font>

On the south shelf, the Retroact rain began to fall upward.

Blood lifted from the stones in red beads. Powder smoke drew into straight grey curtains. Deng Hao's tag rose from Orderly Han's palm and pointed toward the valley.

Qiuyuan turned his head.

Across the floor below, one thousand nine hundred and seven pairs of feet settled into their marks.

Geshu Lin lowered his weapon from the memorial angle.

The black banner snapped once.

The dead advanced.

## ACT II — THE COMPANY REMAINS VISIBLE

The dead crossed the valley at parade speed.

Their boots struck ground that remembered the weight and gave back impressions: cratered shell paths, supply ruts, the turn at the boulder where the mules had always shied. The Retroact rain drew these roads upward from the mud as silver lines, until the valley floor held two maps — the living terrain and the remembered one — and the phantom army marched on the second. Geshu Lin walked at their center in ceremonial armor, each plate catching a moon that had set three years earlier. The black banner above him moved in a wind that had died with the soldiers carrying it.

One thousand nine hundred and seven figures in formation. Their faces held the quality of memorial bronze: correct features pressed into a substance that declined to move on its own. Their weapons swung at the cadence of the second act's closing march — three drumbeats, a held space, a final beat — and where each foot landed, the suspended rain overhead rippled outward in concentric rings, as though the dead were walking across the underside of a lake.

The phantom column struck the outer markers.

Jiyan's whistle went twice, short and low.

<font color=lightblue>"All sections, hold the paired line. Report every voice you hear and the voice that answers it. Nobody crosses the berm alone."</font>

Rangers locked shields in twos. Bao leaned her bound thumb against the grip and held with the rest of her hand. Du called names down the line, one section at a time, and each name came back with the small inflection of a person confirming that they were the one who had said it. The paired rule ran along the shelf like a hand closing.

The first phantom rank met the forward berm.

Their spears came down together, and where the blades touched stone the Retroact rain flared with a sound like a wet finger drawn across the rim of a bowl — high, sustained, pulling at the ear until every closed memory wanted to open. Lupa's flame guttered inside her chest. A Ranger on the western station turned his head toward a gap in the smoke where sunlight lay along a remembered rail.

Du caught his shoulder.

<font color=burlywood>"Lian. Name the six in your section and tell me what Wei is carrying in his left hand."</font>

The Ranger blinked. His mouth opened. Then:

<font color=burlywood>"Lian, third section. Wei is holding the signal cord because his fingers are too cold to let go of it."</font>

The phantom rail dissolved. Rain closed the gap.

<font color=lightblue>"Open the cases."</font>

Amy unlatched the first waterproof lid. The hinge Cartethyia had caught from the fire pot held. Inside lay the register in four bound quires — names in the first column, personal details in the second, true roads in the third — set by six rival printing houses in a single night. The ink smelled of iron and lamp oil and the particular sourness of a compositor who had worked through his dinner.

Yangyang opened the second case. The Wind arrived at its pages before her hands did, pressing flat the top sheet and reading downward at a speed that carried dust off the margins.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Begin at the sixth company, third platoon. The Wind will carry the names forward."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Copy. Relay is open on all channels."</font> Amy's voice came through Nyx's speakers, stripped to broadcast clarity. <font color=mistyrose>"Cartethyia, the field is yours."</font>

Cartethyia knelt beside the forward berm with a satchel of paper figures — one hundred and nine of them finished, three hundred and eighty-two carrying a name and a detail, the remainder holding a name alone. Her injured thumb was bandaged around a bamboo splint that Baizhi had tied with the stern economy of someone who expected it to be used immediately.

She laid the first figure on the stone.

<font color=skyblue>"Shen Ke. North battery, signalman. Recovered from the west sinkhole road on the second day."</font>

She spoke the detail the way a person names a dish she is serving: directly, with care for the portion.

<font color=skyblue>"He hated pears. His family cooked them in a separate pot."</font>

The paper figure's ink darkened. Along its chest, where Cartethyia had written the name, a thread of warmth climbed through the fibres and the character 沈 lifted from the paper in a pale amber glow — the temperature of a lamp left on, or a pot kept warm.

In the phantom ranks, sixty paces out, a signalman's arms rose slightly from his sides. The flag he carried had been a stage property; now its weight surprised him. His head turned an inch to the east — toward the sinkhole road, the true one, which ran along the base of the opposite shelf from where the play had placed it.

The chorus pulled him back. His arms came down. The flag straightened. But the inch remained.

Ciaccona set her fingers on the lute.

The first note fell between the third drumbeat and the held space, a note placed exactly where the march had no room for it. The second note climbed an interval that belonged to a cooking pot and a pair of hands sorting pears out of a bowl. The third carried the weight of a signalman's flag in a real wind rather than a staged one.

The march stumbled.

Twelve phantom soldiers in the sixth company lost a step. Their formation opened by the width of one arm, and through the gap the suspended rain showed what lay behind the performance: a remembered encampment, exhausted, muddy, held together by meal rotations and the particular argument about whether the supply road turned east or west at the second ridge. Cookfire light, orange and unsteady, broke across the phantom armor like a crack in porcelain.

Cartethyia placed the second figure.

<font color=skyblue>"Mi Renshu. Captain, second company. She rescued moths from washbasins with a cup and a postcard, even on campaign."</font>

The paper figure's warmth rose through the name. Among the dead, a woman near the black banner looked down at her empty hands. Her gauntlets had been drawn from a painting of the battle hung in a school corridor; underneath them, her actual hands remembered the rim of a cup and the dry brush of wings against a card. The phantom gauntlets wavered.

<font color=skyblue>"Fan Yuelan. She salted soup with her left hand and threw dice with her right."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Wen Tao. Accurate roads, fantastical chickens."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Peng Rui and Peng Shan. Brothers. The tag cord tangled because they carried one tag between them."</font>

Each name, spoken with its living detail, drove a hairline fracture through the chorus. The phantom formation held its shape the way a sheet of ice holds over moving water — structurally intact, and beneath it, something entirely different. Ciaccona placed a note inside each fracture and widened it by a semitone.

Mei felt it through her marked hand.

Gold climbed her fingers without being asked. The warmth ran up through the branching mark, past the torn web between her first two fingers where Iuno's field dressing held, and reached toward the suspended rain. Where her light touched a hanging drop, the drop turned — slowly, as if waking — and inside it appeared a scene too small and clear to be an illusion: a young man asleep against a supply crate with a half-eaten plum balanced on his knee. A woman counting ammunition with her lips moving. Two soldiers sharing a blanket on frozen ground, arguing about whose turn it was to fold.

The drops multiplied. Each one that caught the golden light became a lens showing a life at the scale of a kitchen, a bedroll, a letter home. They hung above the valley floor like a chandelier made of ordinary days.

<font color=moccasin>"They're in there."</font> Mei's voice carried the raw quality of someone watching a search produce results. <font color=moccasin>"Under the armor. Under the march."</font>

Iuno put her living hand on Mei's shoulder.

<font color=royalblue>"Your light is extending past the forward berm. Pull your hand back six inches and sustain from there."</font>

Mei pulled back. The golden field steadied. The drops held their images.

Across the shelf, Qiuyuan tilted his head.

Beneath the phantom army's layered frequency — stage directions compressed into destiny, memorial applause become a permanent audience, school recitations smoothing away every rough edge of the living voice — he found what he had been listening for since the valley opened. A young man breathing hard through a split lip. The texture of real anger, the kind that belongs to someone standing between a weapon and three farmers because the farmers are behind him and the weapon is in front and he has already decided which direction he faces, without flourish, without considering the pose.

Qiuyuan drew one handspan of blade from the cloth.

The steel carried his Forte's charge into the suspended rain. The listening frequency moved through the field of hanging drops, past the school recitations, past the playwright's revisions, past the memorial incense and the actor's projection, and found the seam between the performance and the man. A whisper of true voice, buried under three years of other people's versions of his final speech.

The burned hand paid for it. Blood opened beneath the dressing and ran along the blade's flat. Qiuyuan held the frequency for four seconds, then released it and pressed the wrap against his thigh.

<font color=sandybrown>"The general is aware."</font> His voice came thin and precise. <font color=sandybrown>"He has been aware. The performance does not silence him. It drowns him in company."</font>

Hsin, on San Jie's shoulder, paused with a candied hawthorn halfway to her mouth. Her nine tails, which had been arranged with the studied elegance of a scroll painting, went still.

<font color=thistle>"Aware and compliant."</font> She ate the hawthorn and considered the distinction. <font color=thistle>"That is a cage even I have struggled to build. The man hears himself and walks anyway, because walking is what the room expects."</font>

<font color=sandybrown>"The room has been cheering for three years."</font>

<font color=thistle>"Yes."</font> Hsin's ears turned forward. Something had interested her. <font color=thistle>"And your girl with the paper scissors is trying to change the programme mid-show. Fascinating. Presumptuous. Almost certainly fatal in the third act."</font> Her tail swept once, slow. <font color=thistle>"I approve."</font>

Cartethyia heard none of this. She was placing figures.

Her hands had developed a rhythm: draw from the satchel, read the name, speak the detail, set the paper on the stone. Each figure stood three inches high on its fold, the way she had learned in twenty years of dark, because a named thing keeps its shape only if it can stand on its own. Fifteen figures. Twenty-three. Thirty-one.

<font color=skyblue>"Gao Shun. He clicked a chopstick against his bowl when he wanted another serving. His mother heard every time."</font>

In the phantom company, a soldier's hand twitched — the small involuntary motion of someone reaching for a utensil that should be beside his bowl. The ceremonial armor on his forearm cracked along the wrist joint and through the crack showed cloth, a stained sleeve, the evidence of a meal taken at speed.

The black banner pulled.

It was a physical thing, the pull. The wind that moved the banner was the wind of a stage effect — bellows concealed beneath the boards, three stagehands working a rotation — and it drew every separated figure back toward center with the gravity of a closing scene. Shen Ke's signalman turned away from the true sinkhole road. Mi Renshu's hands closed over their phantom gauntlets. Gao Shun's chopstick-twitching fingers went rigid at his side.

Ciaccona fought it.

She abandoned the march and struck one chord, hard, the kind of chord that belongs to a room at two in the morning when someone has finally said the true thing. The lute's voice crossed the suspended rain and the drops shivered, and inside each one the small domestic scenes — the plum, the blanket argument, the letter — trembled as though the wind had found them.

<font color=teal>"The banner rewrites the ending every eight bars."</font> She wiped sweat from her jaw with the back of her wrist. <font color=teal>"I can keep the details alive in the spaces between the beats, but I need more voices or I'll lose the fractures when the chorus closes."</font>

<font color=coral>"The Wind is carrying names at sixty per minute."</font> Chisa's head was angled toward the field. <font color=coral>"The figures respond faster when the voice has a personal frequency attached. Strangers' names hold for one to two seconds. A family member's voice holds for six."</font>

Amy understood the math before Chisa finished the sentence.

<font color=mistyrose>"Relay to Jinzhou. All channels, all wards. Families with a name in the register: speak your detail into the nearest receiver. One sentence. What you remember. The channel is open."</font>

She had been running a city from a radio for ten years. The architecture went up in her hands like something she had built before.

The first voice came from ward four.

An old woman, speaking carefully into a receiver held by a ward runner at the corner of the east market: <font color=darkkhaki>"My husband carried a photograph of our granddaughter inside his canteen lid. He showed it to everyone who sat beside him at mess. He replaced it twice because the steam ruined it."</font>

The second came from the harbour: a boy of twelve, shy, with his mother's hand on his back. <font color=antiquewhite>"My da was scared of geese. He used to go the long way around the tax office."</font>

The third, fourth, and seventh arrived together, overlapping on the relay, carried by the Wind across the valley at the speed a word travels when someone has been holding it for three years.

In the phantom ranks, three more soldiers separated. Their armor cracked. Through the gaps, warmth showed — human warmth, the kind that has a temperature and a color, the kind that can be measured in the way a partner learns to measure fever by pressing her lips to a forehead.

Cartethyia began to cry. She went on placing figures. The tears fell on the paper and the ink held, because she had used the good ink, the kind Bookbinders' Row sets for documents intended to survive handling.

Yangyang read from the register in her flat procedural voice, one entry at a time, and the Wind carried each name forward with a pressure that felt like an open palm resting briefly on a shoulder. The dead received their names and shuddered. Some turned an inch. Some looked down at their hands. One man in the rear of the formation sat down on the ground, cross-legged, the way he had sat outside his tent, and the soldiers beside him had to flow around him like a stream around a stone.

The formation's geometry began to deform.

What had been a crescent — tight, theatrical, following the stage blocking of the seventh-edition march — became something more confused. Soldiers whose names had been called walked at angles that diverged from the choreography. Their remembered roads pulled them toward the real terrain, the west sinkhole, the supply path, the turn at the boulder. The chorus still held them, still sounded their boots in unison, still carried them toward the valley's central stone where Ovathrax waited. But each called name made the chorus work harder.

Jinhsi moved to the forward berm.

She carried the field seal in one hand and a single sheet of paper in the other. The sheet held a copied line from the first-edition prompt book, written in Clerk Wen's careful hand: 將軍回顧。全連可見。THE GENERAL LOOKS BACK. THE COMPANY REMAINS VISIBLE.

She held it up, facing the valley, the way a magistrate holds a ruling: flat, visible, offered to the room rather than imposed on it.

<font color=honeydew>"General Geshu Lin."</font>

Her voice carried the weight that had held ward meetings, budget shortfalls, expelled Chamber presidents, and a city that answered eleven questions at once. It was the voice of a person who had written casualty returns in mud and meant every name on them.

<font color=honeydew>"The direction was removed from your script three years ago, and I am restoring it. Your company remains visible. They are here. Shen Ke is carrying a flag on the wrong side of the valley because the play put him there. Mi Renshu is looking for a moth she cannot find because her gauntlets are painted on. Gao Shun wants another serving and his hand remembers the chopstick. They have been behind you the whole time. They have names and roads and the details their families have been carrying. Your city has been asked, and your city has answered."</font>

The field seal caught the golden light from Mei's hand and threw it forward.

The black banner shuddered. One corner tore where a remembered wind met a present one and found they were pulling in different directions. The phantom army's march broke into two tempos — the staged closing beat and something underneath it, ragged, human, the cadence of tired people walking home.

Geshu Lin's head turned.

The motion was small. A degree, perhaps two — the same fraction Cartethyia's named figures had achieved, one at a time, across the whole formation. But this was the general. The axis of the performance. When his head moved, every phantom soldier's head moved with him, because the play had built them into one body with one face.

They looked back.

The company was there. Shen Ke with his flag at the wrong angle. Mi Renshu with her hands open. Gao Shun sitting cross-legged on the ground. Fan Yuelan with her left hand raised as though reaching for a salt dish. Wen Tao, whose phantom gauntlet had cracked along a line shaped exactly like a chicken's neck. The Peng brothers, shoulder to shoulder, with a single tag between them catching the rain's amber light.

Three hundred and twelve named dead, standing slightly apart from the chorus, their faces warming into something closer to firelight — unsteady, particular, alive in the way a candle is alive.

The remaining fifteen hundred and ninety-five stayed in formation. Their armor held. Their march continued.

But three hundred and twelve had looked back, and the play's blocking stopped here, at the moment the company was seen.

Ciaccona left the fourth beat open.

The silence lasted one and a half seconds. No drum filled it. No stage direction covered it. The dead stood inside that silence with their own names in their ears and their own details showing through cracked armor, and for one and a half seconds they stood free of the performance entirely.

Cristoforo leaned forward on the northern ridge.

His face held the expression of a director watching an understudy discover something the lead had missed. Pleasure, genuine and unperformed. Beside him, Nivora studied the field through a sighting glass.

<font color=violet>"She is using your production against you."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"She is using the production's own grammar against the production."</font> Cristoforo folded his hands. <font color=lightyellow>"The restored direction, the open beat, the personal details disrupting the rhythmic unity. It is structurally exact. The Magistrate has read her prompt book."</font>

Scar watched the formation deform. His hip wound had stiffened.

<font color=lightpink>"Three hundred and twelve are turning. The other sixteen hundred are yours."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"For now."</font> Cristoforo's smile widened. <font color=lightyellow>"The families are speaking into receivers. Every ward, every channel. They have connected the audience to the stage. Within twenty minutes the separation will reach the middle ranks."</font>

<font color=lightpink>"Then close the beat."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"And lose the most interesting production I have staged in forty years?"</font> He watched Mei's golden light play across the suspended drops. <font color=lightyellow>"She chose to bring the city. She chose to restore the company. She chose to leave the ending open. She wrote those directions herself."</font>

His fingers tapped once against his thigh. A conductor marking time.

<font color=lightyellow>"We built Geshu Lin a solitary ending because the Lady Arbiter's legend proved that solitary endings work. She has answered with a crowded one."</font> He watched Jinhsi hold the field seal at the forward berm, mud on both wrists, Chixia's blood still visible on her coat. <font color=lightyellow>"I would very much like to see what the Threnodian makes of a battle where every fighter insists on performing in company."</font>

Nivora lowered the glass.

<font color=violet>"You are describing an audience, Cristoforo. An audience for the god."</font>

<font color=lightyellow>"Yes."</font> His pleasure did not dim. <font color=lightyellow>"And they have brought chairs."</font>

Below, Hsin watched the three hundred and twelve named dead stand in the open fourth beat with an expression that had narrowed from amusement to something older.

Her tails curled behind her in a configuration her crews had seen precisely twice: once when Xuanfang's eastern wall had held for nineteen hours, and once when a child had solved one of her wagers by giving the right answer to the wrong question.

<font color=thistle>"San Jie."</font>

The guardian shifted its weight. Luo Jiang looked down from the collar platform.

<font color=thistle>"Your ancestors fought beside that man. When the fourth beat closes, his formation will either hold or scatter. I will need your voice to carry the detail registers into the middle ranks, where the families' transmissions cannot reach."</font>

Luo Jiang stared at her.

<font color=thistle>"You have loudspeakers mounted for tactical communication, Engineer. I am repurposing them for a recital."</font> One ear turned toward the valley. <font color=thistle>"A lived lesson, never forgotten. I should very much like to see what a thousand people sound like when they remember at the same time."</font>

She smiled. Nine tails settled behind her like a spread hand.

The fourth beat was closing.

The drum gathered itself in the valley's stone. Ovathrax's frequency pressed upward through the foundation, through the old Tacet scars, through the remembered roads, reaching for the final beat that would seal the performance and fix every phantom soldier in the march forever.

Ciaccona held the open space. Her injured fingers ached against the strings. Sweat ran into her eyes.

Cartethyia placed the eighty-ninth figure.

<font color=skyblue>"Deng Rui. He wanted the pepper out of his bowl because his stomach always gave him trouble before a march."</font>

Somewhere in the formation, a young man who had died pulling a sergeant clear of a remembered stair turned his face toward the south shelf. His phantom armor ran with cracks. Through them, a private named Rui looked at the living army with the expression of a person who has been walking for three years and has just remembered that he left the stove on.

The drum pressed.

Ciaccona's strings hummed under the weight. The open beat trembled. Three hundred and twelve named dead stood in the silence, and the silence was shrinking.

From the south shelf, carried by four guardian loudspeakers and one Welcoming Wind, the voices of Jinzhou reached the middle ranks.

A daughter described her father's habit of checking every lock in the house twice, and then checking them again, and then sitting down and asking whether he had checked them. A wife explained that her husband's left boot always wore faster because he dragged his foot when he was thinking. A grandmother said her grandson carried a stone from the river in his coat pocket for a year because a girl had given it to him, and the girl had moved away, and the stone was all he had of the conversation where she said she liked the way he listened.

The middle ranks cracked.

Armor peeled from two hundred more soldiers, revealing the cloth and leather and patched canvas of people who had carried stones in pockets and dragged their left boots and checked locks twice and then sat down. They separated from the chorus and stood in their own shapes, at their own heights, with their own particular way of holding a weapon that said everything about whether they had wanted to be soldiers or had simply been the ones who showed up.

Five hundred and twelve named dead. Looking back. The company visible.

Mei kept her hand open. The gold held steady. Iuno stood beside her, watching the drops, counting the separated figures, filing the shape of what was happening with the silent thoroughness of a woman who had spent thirty years reading other people's documents and had finally, after everything, found one worth writing.

The drum pressed harder.

The beat had to close. The performance demanded it. Ovathrax demanded it. The whole weight of a play staged for three years in schoolrooms and memorial halls, printed and reprinted until the blocking became geography, pushed against the open space where Ciaccona held her chord.

The chord held.

The next twenty minutes would decide whether it kept holding.
