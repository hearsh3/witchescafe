# The Step

*After Youngest Gate 19. The evening before.*

---

The market below Mrs Ren's guesthouse was shutting early. A vegetable seller Changli had watched on three other evenings folded his awning with a knot that would hold against weather and release with a single pull. Each time she had seen him tie it, the knot was identical.

She climbed the stairs. She carried nothing. She had considered persimmons from the corner stall where the woman knew her face, and then stood in the laneway for a while, considering what it meant that the offering was the first thing she had planned.

The front room's light was on. Through the wall she could hear the household — Amy's voice explaining something about a boot, a low sound from Lupa that could have been agreement or could have been the noise a wolf makes when she has been asked a question she considers optional. Somebody's bowl scraped a table. A laugh scattered wide. Cartethyia, most likely.

Iuno sat on the step below the guesthouse door with her tea set balanced on her knee. The crystalline stump rested against the door frame, catching the market lanterns in slow colored arcs. Her face had the focused emptiness of a person listening to a room behind her while thinking about a different room entirely.

She looked up when Changli's shadow fell across the step.

<font color=#5881fe>"You're here for the conversation."</font>

Changli stopped two steps below. <font color=#cfdb29>"I had considered beginning with the weather."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"The weather is that the wind has spent her last word and the rain will come in four hours. My crystal has opinions about the pressure."</font> Iuno lifted the stump from the frame. <font color=#5881fe>"Now you can proceed to the thing you came to ask me."</font>

Changli descended the remaining steps and stood level with her. The dust from the afternoon's work was still on her cuff — the unsurveyed shed, the silkworm trays, a stranger's daughter handing her a cloth without being asked. Up close, Iuno looked tired in the particular way of someone who has spent a day standing with the posture of a person who intends to keep standing.

<font color=#cfdb29>"You were at the gate when the column came through."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"I was counting. Wen closed the entry at one thousand nine hundred and seven."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"I checked the copy."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"Sit down, Counselor."</font> Iuno lifted the teapot with her one hand. <font color=#5881fe>"I have a spare cup."</font>

Changli sat beside her. The stone still held the last of the afternoon's warmth. Iuno poured with a motion so settled by repetition that it had the quality of landscape — something shaped over years until it stopped being a skill and became the shape of the ground.

The tea was Earl Grey.

<font color=#cfdb29>"Tomorrow morning I intend to sit across from Jinhsi and tell her what I have done."</font>

Iuno's hand paused on the teapot's lid.

<font color=#5881fe>"You told me you would tell her. Then a siege happened, and a march, and a battle, and a column of nineteen hundred people on a road in the rain."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"Several things happened."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"And you found urgent work in every one of them."</font> Iuno set the teapot down. <font color=#5881fe>"You are very good at finding the thing that has to be done between yourself and a conversation you have been postponing."</font>

Changli looked at the tea. The cup was Iuno's spare — smaller, without the gold rim.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I have written my resignation. I have considered the room, the time of day, the distance I will place between my chair and hers. I have imagined what she will say, and I have prepared for several directions the morning could take."</font>

She turned the cup by its rim — the quarter-turn, the gesture she shared with Jinhsi or had taught her or had arrived at by the same route at the same table — and caught herself doing it, and stopped.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I have run out of preparation. That is a thing that has never happened to me before."</font>

Iuno studied her over the rim of her own cup.

<font color=#5881fe>"You haven't come here to ask me how to deliver difficult news. You've told ministers their appointments were over and generals their deployments were fiction."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"Those men were willing to be angry with me. Their anger had a shape I could work with."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"And hers will come in a shape you can't."</font>

Changli's thumb found the hairline ridge in the cup's glaze where the potter's brush had once paused.

<font color=#cfdb29>"When she was nine, she brought me a petition about the kitchens. Three words misspelled, one article cited from the wrong schedule. The petition was excellent. I could see exactly what she intended, and I could see the fourteen officials between her desk and the one where the petition needed to land."</font>

The old pride arrived — bright, specific, entirely earned.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I spent the next fourteen years making sure her words reached the right desk at the right hour. I cleared obstacles from her path when they would have stopped her. I placed them there when I decided she was ready. Every lesson had a schedule. Every difficulty had a perimeter I had drawn."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"And the chronology."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"And the chronology. I altered the prophetic record to stop a Council inquiry that would have removed her from office. I kept the originals in a room above hers for seven years. I sat with her while she brought me her doubts, and I accepted every one while I held the thing that would have answered her worst question."</font>

She took a breath that came in shorter than the one before it.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I told you this at the spillway, that afternoon. You looked at me and you saw it before I finished the sentence."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"I saw it because I have done it,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=#5881fe>"The inventory is different. The practice is the same."</font>

Changli set the cup on the stone between them.

<font color=#cfdb29>"Then tell me what comes after."</font>

---

Iuno held up her crystalline stump. The market light passed through the crystal in a slow spectrum.

<font color=#5881fe>"I kept the woman I love on the wrong side of a continent. There was a wind moving east — a signal she was meant to receive — and I filed it under the wrong column in a ledger. The filing was clean. The entry was procedurally correct. I used my title and my education and every authority I had to make sure the classification would survive review."</font>

She turned the stump so the light shifted.

<font color=#5881fe>"Mei stayed west. Where I could manage the information she received. Where I could decide which part of the crisis reached her and which part I absorbed on her behalf."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"And it came anyway."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"It came to both of us, separately, for months, while the filing sat in a drawer."</font> She set her arm down. <font color=#5881fe>"The drawer was very well organized. I'm good at drawers."</font>

The line had the sound of a joke that drew blood and meant both.

<font color=#cfdb29>"When did you tell her?"</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"In a hallway. I had rehearsed the hallway. I had considered the light, the hour, whether her face or mine would be in shadow. I wore my best silk, because thirty years of the seminary had convinced me that presentation was a form of care."</font>

She picked up her own cup and held it between her hand and her crystal.

<font color=#5881fe>"She said she hadn't forgiven me. Then she asked if I would keep trying with her. I had prepared for the first sentence. The second one left me standing in my silk with my rehearsed voice and nothing in my hands."</font>

The wind passed through the half-inch gap in the front room's window and lay along the step between them. It carried nothing. It had spent its vocabulary that afternoon on one word, and it would have to travel to Mengzhou to find another. The air cooled by three degrees where it passed.

Changli's thumb pressed against the ridge in the cup.

<font color=#cfdb29>"And then."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"Then Lupa put food in both our bowls, and we ate while the conversation sat between us with all its edges still showing."</font>

Iuno turned toward her.

<font color=#5881fe>"Three days later, Mei handed me a cup without looking at my hands first. She told me where she was going that afternoon and walked out without turning to see what I would do with the information. She started trusting me again before I had earned it, out of habit, out of instinct, out of whatever it is in her that reaches for people and refuses to be taught otherwise."</font>

She set her cup beside Changli's on the step.

<font color=#5881fe>"I wanted to tell her to be more careful. I could see her reaching for me, and I knew what my hands had done, and I wanted to say something about how she ought to guard the things she gave me. That was the first moment — the real one — where I understood what I had actually taken from her."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"Her ease."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"The freedom to reach for somebody without weighing it first. I had spent months deciding what she could handle, and the cost was that every time she reached for me freely, I could feel the weight of what I had intercepted."</font>

Inside the guesthouse, a door closed on the upper floor. The fourth step from the top announced someone descending — Lupa, by the weight — and then the stairhead settled again.

---

Changli felt the evening air against the scars on her wrist where the cuff had slipped. She pulled it back into place.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I know how to enter a room so the conversation moves where it needs to go. I can set a distance so the person across from me feels safe to speak. I can read a face and know which sentence to offer so the afternoon arrives where it has to arrive. That office lives in my hands whether I send for it or not."</font>

She folded the cuff flat.

<font color=#cfdb29>"Tomorrow morning she will see every piece of it. She will look at the chair I have placed, and the envelope I have written, and the distance I have measured, and she will know that I measured it."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"She will."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"And every skill I have will be making the room worse."</font>

Iuno set her hand flat on the stone beside the two cups.

<font color=#5881fe>"Then let her see it."</font>

Changli looked at her.

<font color=#5881fe>"You will arrange the room. You will set the chair at the distance your hands choose. You will place the resignation where she'll find it and sit there in your trained voice with your pressed cuff. You will do all of it because forty years of reading rooms lives in your posture whether you dismiss it or not."</font>

The crystal caught the market light and scattered it across the stone.

<font color=#5881fe>"Sit in the arrangement you've made. And let her see the woman inside it — the Counselor of Jinzhou with her pressed cuff and her prepared answers, terrified that a girl she taught to read petitions is about to find out how many of those lessons were timed."</font>

The singing inside the guesthouse had gone soft — Ciaccona, perhaps, or a melody passed between sisters that wandered and arrived late.

<font color=#cfdb29>"My creed has been the same since I was twenty-six. I choose the hour at which the question is asked. Every conversation I have conducted has landed at the moment I calculated the other person was ready to hear it."</font>

She turned her hands over and looked at them. The feather scars ran pale along her wrist into the fold of the cuff. The ink stain at her first knuckle was from the night's drafting.

<font color=#cfdb29>"I have been choosing her hours for fourteen years. Tomorrow she learns about the clocks."</font>

Iuno stood. She gathered the tea set by feel — her cup, the pot, the lid, each into its cloth pocket with the sureness of a woman who had packed this set in harbors, in field stations, in hallways where the High Priestess had just told an ambassador something he would be recovering from well into the following week.

She paused with the spare cup in her hand.

<font color=#5881fe>"You asked me once what the blank card meant. In the weiqi game, the one I gave a name to."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"A pass."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"You give up your turn. You put your stone down and you hand the board to the other player, and you sit there while they decide what the next move is."</font>

She tucked the cup into the cloth.

<font color=#5881fe>"When I told Mei, I had chosen my hour too. I had the hallway, the silk, the light, the voice. And then she answered, and every piece of the rehearsal was behind me, and the conversation was moving on her time, and I had to decide whether to reach for the board or let it go."</font>

She set her hand on the door frame.

<font color=#5881fe>"I let it go. It was the most frightening thing I have done in a career that once included addressing a city under siege from a balcony in a nightgown."</font>

<font color=#cfdb29>"And the board went where you feared."</font>

<font color=#5881fe>"The board went to a breakfast table. It went to an argument about whether tea counts as a meal. It went to a Tuesday in a rented room where the window will never latch and a wolf on the stairhead has opinions about my tea service."</font>

She opened the door. The household sounds came through — the singing, a yawn, Amy saying something about tomorrow.

<font color=#5881fe>"The board went somewhere ordinary. That was the part I hadn't prepared for."</font>

The door closed behind her. The step held the damp ring where the teapot had been and the two cup-prints on the stone and the fading warmth where two people had sat.

***

Changli stayed on the step.

The market below had gone fully dark. The lantern seller at the corner was pouring the last of his oil into a single lamp on the edge of his table. She had watched him close on two other evenings. He would set the lamp down and walk away, and the flame would burn until it burned through, and in the morning he would trim the wick, pour fresh oil, and the lamp would be ready for another day.

She thought about Jinhsi's face at the gate when the column came through. The relief had been so large and so completely undisguised that Changli had felt it from across the road, through two lines of returning soldiers and the noise of the ward officers calling names. Jinhsi had looked toward her, and the look was the one Changli had been receiving since the girl was eight years old — the look that said *you are the person I understand in this room*.

Tomorrow morning, that look would carry something else in it. She had spent seven years knowing it would change. She had spent seven years finding work to do between herself and that morning.

The work had been real. Some of the best of her career — the silkworm assessment, the firebreak across eleven villages of sorghum, the Schedule Four seed-stock purchases, the berth at Mingting. All urgent, all necessary, all capable of occupying a woman who could not afford to sit still long enough to think about what was in the drawer upstairs.

The lantern seller set his lamp on the table's edge and walked south without looking back. The flame steadied in the gap he left.

She stood. The stone had left a cold line across the back of her legs.

Tomorrow she would walk into the paper room. She would set the chair on the far side of the table, because that was where her hands would put it, and her hands had forty years of practice at choosing the distance. She would place the resignation beside the inkstone. She would sit in the arrangement she had made and let Jinhsi read the years.

Inside the guesthouse, Iuno said something in a low voice, and Mei's laugh came through the wall — brief, warm, the kind of laugh produced by a remark about a cup or a day or a small ordinary thing that had pleased her. A bowl moved on the table. The fourth step from the top announced someone going up.

Changli tucked the cuff back into place over her left wrist. The feather scars had warmed where she'd held the tea, and they ached softly, the way a joint aches when the weather is about to change.

She went down the steps.

The hour would come on its own.
