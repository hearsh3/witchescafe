# Youngest Gate 14A–15 — Narrative Synopsis

Household four-one-one was warm against the rain, but no one inside mistook warmth for safety. Beyond Jinzhou, Norfall Valley had become a theatre of the dead. Ovathrax’s drum was sounding faster with every interval, the Retroact rain was raising an army from a battle three years past, and Geshu Lin stood beneath a black banner in the posture history had chosen for him: the solitary general facing north while his company disappeared into darkness. Two more people had died the previous night. An entire company remained caught in the performance, and even the woman remembered as the wind was gone. Mei and her household had discovered the trap’s shape, but not yet the road that would bring everyone out of it.

Mei sat beneath the window with her damp coat against the wall and her marked hand open on her knee. The golden mark could feel the pull toward the center of the coming battle. It offered her the old answer it had always offered: enter alone, reach the heart of the danger, and become the single figure on whom every life depended. Four hundred years earlier, when she had been the Lady Arbiter, that answer had made her effective. It had also taught the world to erase everyone beside her. Now she could sense how Norfall meant to draw her in, yet she could not see a door on the far side for the others.

Iuno named the problem without softening it: Mei knew the trap, not the exit. Amy concluded that something was missing. Ciaccona found it in the theatre’s first prompt book, where an original stage direction had been removed: **THE GENERAL LOOKS BACK. THE COMPANY REMAINS VISIBLE.** Later editions had cut the messenger, shortened the officers’ reports, turned the general north, and cleared the company during his speech. Chisa understood that the erased direction was the door. Cartethyia, speaking as herself rather than sheltering inside the knightly grandeur of Fleurdelys, understood the work behind it. Someone had persuaded the dead that they ceased to matter once their usefulness ended. The living would have to persuade them otherwise.

Lupa refused to let that discovery become a clean solution. She set down her flask and asked whether that was really all. Even if they found an exit for those still trapped, the two who had died the previous night would remain dead, as would the company already lost and the woman who had been the wind. The dead had purchased this hour of thought, and no restored direction would return them. Lupa’s anger was not cruelty. She had learned to count the person a strategy spent, and she would not allow cleverness, even Mei’s, to make absence abstract. When she challenged Mei to admit the cost, Mei did not defend herself. She accepted that Lupa was right.

Mei also refused the answer that guilt tried to impose. She would not stand alone in the doorway. Having one person hold the passage for everyone else was what had brought them to this crisis, and even if she possessed the power to restore the dead, she would still choose to sit beside the people she loved rather than erase them from the decision. Yet the choice hurt. She asked whether remembering the fallen gave her any right to live while others fought and died.

Lupa answered that remembrance conferred no right at all. Rui had not possessed a right to die; he had possessed forty years, a bad stomach, and a sergeant whose belt needed grabbing. Death had come from the material facts of a moment, not from some moral exchange. Chisa uncovered the fear beneath Mei’s question: whether remaining with her household was the same as choosing to let others die. Amy, who had once spent a decade holding a door because it had needed holding, said it was not. One person in one place was not the arithmetic that kept people alive. Iuno then asked the only question that could move them forward: did Mei trust them to stand in the rooms where she was absent?

Mei did. She trusted the household around her, and she trusted the people already holding the frontline. That trust did not erase the dead; it changed what the living were permitted to build. Cartethyia turned the paper figure in her hands. Ciaccona plucked one note and heard that the room’s air had shifted. Chisa wound the red string at her wrist. Lupa spoke the current drum interval—three minutes and thirty-one seconds—so urgency could not dissolve into comfort. Together they resolved to discover who had erased the direction and put the company back into the scene.

They scattered into the rain. Iuno returned to City Hall, where Jinhsi and Changli were still divided by a paper wall and by seven years of concealed truth. Lupa and Chisa took the north route. Ciaccona went to the theatre; Cartethyia went to the camellia cutting and the paper figures. Amy lingered until Mei sent her on. Then Mei found Nivora at a dumpling stall in the harbour ward.

The Grand Architect of the Fractsidus wore a face designed to be forgotten, but the patient, ancient attention behind it was the same presence Mei had known as Triss and later as the Charon who ferried them through the Dark Tide. Nivora’s repeated changes of face could not disguise the pattern: they sought proximity to Mei, tested her isolation, and built lies out of true material. This time they had ordered dumplings without hunger and waited beneath a lamp that burned longer than it should.

When Mei asked why they had altered the play, Nivora admitted learning from the best—from the Lady Arbiter herself. The original direction had allowed Geshu Lin to look back and see his company. Nivora’s ending forced him to face north alone because Mei’s own legend had proved how powerful that image could be. The Lady Arbiter had crossed herself out of every page, left her company behind, faced the dying sky, and won. Mei replied that she had been wrong. Nivora called her effective, but when Mei refused to confuse effectiveness with rightness, something unperformed briefly entered the shapeshifter’s voice. Nivora agreed that they had never been the same.

Mei placed a dumpling directly before Nivora and told them either to eat or explain what they had been awaiting. Nivora took one bite. They had waited for someone to take up the pen, and now they were satisfied. They would not say whose pen they meant. Mei answered with the restored direction: the company remained visible. Nivora warned her not to follow. Mei said she would not—this time—and let the Grand Architect disappear into the rain.

Amy emerged from the alley after Nivora left, damp and caught spying. Mei teased that she was learning bad habits from her aunt, ruffled her pink hair, and accepted that her daughter had seen everything. Amy could not decide what to make of an immortal shapeshifter accepting a cold dumpling from her mother. Mei did not pretend she knew, only that she was allowing the encounter to settle. They walked uphill shoulder to shoulder to find Iuno and Jinhsi.

In the work room, Jinhsi, Changli, and Iuno received Mei’s report with different kinds of caution. Iuno and Amy had seen this pattern before, but Jinhsi and Changli had to weigh Mei’s strange cordiality with the leader of the Fractsidus against the intelligence she brought. Nivora had erased the first direction and fixed Geshu Lin as the lone general. They had wanted to see whether Mei’s household would take up the pen, and their satisfaction proved only that a move had ended, not that the game had become safe. Jinhsi observed that the Fractsidus never staged a production without an audience. Amy understood that Jinzhou itself was the audience.

Changli saw the strategic concession hidden inside the encounter. A player surrendered a stone only when another move mattered more. Nivora had withdrawn pressure from the archive, the sanctum, and the city without extracting all they could. The Grand Architect had conceded this territory on the board because attention had shifted north, toward Norfall, the phantom army, and the shortening drum interval. The picture of a general facing north alone had not been constructed only for Geshu Lin. It waited for Mei as well.

Amy told her mother she would not go alone. Mei did not resist the promise. Changli, though her authority had been suspended and her resignation remained unsigned, turned immediately to the practical window Nivora’s withdrawal had opened: the shelter count, the grain, and the eastern gate all had to be secured within twelve hours. Jinhsi accepted her help without pretending their personal breach was repaired. The paper wall remained between Magistrate and Counselor, but the work was still the work. Mei told them to sleep while they could.

Jinzhou interpreted sleep as another collective task. Clerk Wen found campaign cots in the lower records room, Panhua brought bathhouse blankets, and Lupa wrote a rota before anyone with a seal could transform exhaustion into a test of virtue. Mei tried to surrender her first ninety minutes to the casualty return. Lupa threatened field authority over her ankles, Jinhsi found legal precedent for the order, and even Changli’s shadow beyond the paper wall appeared ready to cite it. Mei finally lay down. Amy curled beside her around a relay headset until Iuno removed it and took the other side, her crystalline stump against Mei’s shoulder blade. Mei laughed once and fell asleep at the end of the sound.

The rota held because everyone submitted to the same rule. Cartethyia slept after the casualty boxes arrived. Ciaccona spent part of her forty minutes insisting musicians followed a different clock. Chisa rested by the door and woke at each drum with her hand on her shears. Lupa slept last, still in her boots. Jinhsi attempted to work through the night until Panhua placed food over her shelter return and Wen moved the ward meeting into the sleeping room. When the citizens arrived with chairs and found their Magistrate asleep beneath a bathhouse blanket, they set the chairs down quietly, and a carpenter repaired her cot while she rested. The city’s leaders were no longer permitted to prove devotion by becoming unusable.

The drum struck again after three minutes and nineteen seconds. Cups chimed, guardians lowered their shields, Mei’s mark warmed beneath Iuno’s palm, and thousands of metal type sorts shifted together in Bookbinders’ Row. Jinzhou used the interval to breathe, then resumed the work of restoring the company.

Bookbinders’ Row stayed lit through the night. Six rival printing houses surrendered their feuds long enough to set one casualty register from the original return, seven theatre scripts, letters, pension claims, menus, complaints, school copies, and family memories. The first forme carried ranks and names. The second placed one personal detail beside every name. The third carried the true roads by which each person had served, fallen, or been recovered. Cartethyia cut a paper figure for every completed entry, writing the name across its chest, the detail along one arm, and the road down one leg before standing it on Jiyan’s field map.

She asked families for what official history would discard. Luo Jiang of the second gun crew filed splinters from cheap chopsticks while people talked. Shen Ke, a north-battery signalman recovered from the west sinkhole road, hated pears so much that his family cooked them in a separate pot. Fan Yuelan salted soup with her left hand and threw dice with her right. Wen Tao drew accurate roads and fantastical chickens. Two sisters untangled the identities of Peng Rui and Peng Shan from the knot in a recovered tag cord; one brother received the tag, while the other remained present in formation with recovery open. Amy managed the receivers and demanded one fact an official account would waste. Ciaccona gave each name its own spoken measure and each detail a different silence, creating a march that a thousand mouths could learn only by listening to one another.

The Fractsidus attacked the work because individual memory broke the chorus they needed. Seven names vanished from a wet proof even though the paper retained the pressure of the type. Chisa recognized that the forme had struck the names and the ink had departed afterward. The rear lamps died one by one. Lupa intercepted a hooked tool in the dark, Chisa cut off the route to the window, and they trapped a Fractsidus compositor wearing a printer’s apron strung with type sorts filed blank. A resonance pin burst above the first stone and spilled black rain, but Ciaccona held it inside a low chord long enough for Master Liao to cover it with a tray.

Other operatives crossed the roof, yet Lupa refused to abandon the witnesses and composing stones in pursuit. Keeping the room rather than chasing the enemy cost her, but it obeyed the truth she had forced Mei to face: the people present mattered more than the glamour of a hunt. The prisoner repeated the corrupted direction that the company cleared during the speech. Cartethyia answered by lifting Captain Mi Renshu’s blank figure. A young relative remembered that the captain rescued moths from washbasins with a cup and a postcard, even while on campaign. Cartethyia wrote the moth onto the figure. Ink crept back into Mi Renshu’s name, and seven families began speaking at once until all seven erased entries returned.

The Row changed its practice immediately. Every forme received two readers, every proof a hand copy, and every name a detail belonging to the person beyond rank. Duplicates traveled by separate roads, family witnesses carried impressions home, and children wrote names into costume linings. Even a blank had to remain visible, labelled with its source and the hour it was found. By the next shortened interval, the first register held six hundred and eighty-four names and the second held six hundred and eighty-four lives in their working clothes.

At the south shelf, Jiyan remade military doctrine around the same refusal of solitude. The field board declared that one voice called, one answered, and a third kept the road. Every advance left another set of footprints beside it. Every runner knew the runner behind them. Officers gave orders from the ground occupied by those expected to obey. Sergeant Du held Bao; Bao held Lieutenant Sun; gun crews, shield bearers, and engineers entered as named groups whose members could release one another when phantom hands reached through their harnesses.

The dead tested those bonds. Captain Zhou rose beneath the western gun with sunlight on his old armor and pointed Bao toward the bridge where he had died. Du caught her harness and made her name the six living people in her squad, their ammunition, their cover, and the road home. By the sixth name, the false sunlight had become rain and the gun stood on the shelf again. Jiyan took Bao’s exposed post while Lieutenant Sun moved with him, proving that rank did not exempt the general from the paired rule.

The four guardians of Xuanfang heard old crews calling them forward in remembered command tones. Hsin answered by making the present engineers state their names. Their voices moved from platform to platform; the guardians shared their weight, closed their shield locks, and set the lifted foot back inside the line. Hsin reminded the ancient machines that although their ancestors had installed good manners, their current crews had climbed the ladders that morning. The cores changed key and attended to the living.

Qiuyuan then heard six Fractsidus frequencies moving beneath the phantom artillery, each wrapped in an absence where identity should have caught. Jiyan did not answer with a heroic charge. He let the infiltrators follow a Ranger withdrawal into a space built from Jinzhou movement, Xuanfang shields, Qiuyuan’s hearing, and Hsin’s refusal to welcome armed guests. One operative activated a speaking seal in Geshu Lin’s cadence, declaring that the final line belonged to whoever reached him first. The voice carried phantom applause, and the entire dead formation turned toward Jiyan.

Jiyan replied with names rather than rivalry. He called Shen Ke and the pears cooked separately; a phantom signalman broke formation and raised an invisible flag. He called Mi Renshu and her moth postcard; a woman near the banner looked down at her empty hands. Qiuyuan used the Qiongqi token’s resonance to find the seam in the stolen authority and split the speaking seal without unwrapping his sword. Bao and Du took the operator together while Sun held their return lane.

A seventh infiltrator, hidden even from Qiuyuan at first, attacked Hsin from behind. Her tails caught the knife and swept an engineer to safety before she seized the attacker. The captive dissolved their borrowed face through a dozen dead soldiers drawn from memorial prints, forcing the guardian crews to see fathers, teachers, officers, and a son. They kept the shields locked. The forty-seven-second skirmish ended with seven prisoners, two damaged shields, Bao’s dislocated thumb, and a resonance burn on Qiuyuan’s listening hand, but all twenty-three living participants returned either to the line or to treatment. When Jiyan noted that the valley had offered Qiuyuan a private verdict, the swordsman refused it. A private verdict would only repeat the first error; he had come to testify in company.

Within the phantom ranks, seven named dead now stood slightly out of place. That fraction of separation held.

While soldiers defended the shelf and printers restored the dead, the city worked on the rooms behind its shelter number. The remainder fell from two hundred and fifteen to eighty-three not by decree but door by door. Apprentices moved pepper sacks and received receipts describing every tie. The bathhouse opened more places after the night-soil route proved dependable. The theatre offered its rehearsal room under guard. The tax office admitted eleven people while its resident goose appropriated a quilt. The west gallery of City Hall became sleeping space while clerks worked between bedrolls.

Every threshold carried conditions that the city recorded rather than overruled: a grandmother needed the ground floor; a household required cooking space free of sesame oil; quarry workers needed quiet during daylight; shrine exclusions traveled with their families; a frightened boy needed his cousin within reach at each drum. Iuno’s mark for consent with a stated limit appeared eighty-nine times, and each limit held. When an innkeeper withdrew her offer because the government could not guarantee coal past dawn, Jinhsi returned the key and paid her inspection time. Because public power already possessed schools, halls, yards, and offices, the innkeeper’s household retained its own threshold. She later returned with brazier bricks and freely offered the room through noon.

Changli worked under suspension rather than outside it. When twenty-seven ferry laborers remained under canvas because one form called them a crew while the census called them residents, she did not use the registry channel she no longer controlled. She asked Wen for the current rule, waited for it, and applied it. The laborers received the school gymnasium, the ferry office received a bill for neglected ropes, and even the two dogs traveling with the night team entered under the caretaker’s written terms.

The remainder fell, then rose when five people arrived from a village whose footbridge had failed in the Retroact rain. Jinhsi confessed that she wanted to see zero before leaving. Wen reminded her that a register preferred accuracy to morale, and she told him always to give accuracy the last word. By dawn, the count stood at sixty-one. The register remained open because safety was not a triumphant number; it was a continuing set of obligations to people whose conditions could change.

The political wound between Jinhsi and Changli also stayed open. At dawn, Jinhsi completed thirty-one pages of account from eleven years of backward returns, seven years of altered dates, secret copies, diverted runner maps, Luo Zemin’s disclosures, the order protecting his daughter, and Changli’s resignation. When Jinhsi asked what Changli believed she would have done with the first truthful return, Changli admitted that she had expected the fifteen-year-old Magistrate chosen by Jué to study every implication and then treat the Council’s fear as evidence against herself. Changli had selected an established record for the girl and called that choice protection. In doing so, she had also forced Jinhsi’s office to investigate a false chronology for seven years, distorting staff, budgets, and every conclusion drawn from the silence.

Changli accepted liability for every consequence she could trace and for those that emerged later. Jinhsi refused either instant pardon or easy rejection. The Decree Council would judge the official answer; her personal judgment required a longer room than that morning allowed. The appointment for that room remained scheduled.

Jinhsi then announced that she would travel with the field column. Geshu Lin had died under Jinzhou’s command and received three years of replies written safely away from him. The Magistrate would carry her office to the valley, ask what the city owed, and return the answer to the eleven ward tables that had each demanded to know who would govern in her absence. She appointed Changli Acting Magistrate, not because trust had been magically restored, but because Jinzhou needed the woman who knew all eleven wards, seven ministries, the north compact, and the habits of every office arguing over survival.

The appointment bound Changli’s strengths inside public limits. Wen held the prophetic key and countersigned every order concerning Jué, the Mingting registry, or Luo Zemin’s household. Private requisitions needed ward concurrence. Stores, casualties, shelters, and roads would be read aloud every second hour. All eleven ward meetings continued. Jinhsi gave Changli confidence in her present work while preserving the future account. Changli signed, accepted the civil seal, and promised to keep the tables running.

As soon as the seal passed, Mei asked who would stand close enough to hear Jinhsi’s answer in Norfall. The formation assembled around the question. Jiyan commanded the entry. Mei’s household held the center’s exits. Qiuyuan listened beneath the performance. Hsin and her guardians covered the shelf. Rangers carried the true roads. Yangyang carried the Wind’s terms. Jinhsi carried the authority to ask and the obligation to bring the answer home. Amy demanded exact relay distances because Mei drifted toward people who needed catching and Iuno predicted around corners. Iuno required two recall voices for every entrant, one in the field and one in the city, so a stolen voice could never possess the whole grammar. Lupa added two physical handholds—a rope, belt, wrist, shield edge, spear haft, or coat seam. Chisa offered red tethers that opened only when the holder deliberately chose. Law, witnesses, muscle, thread, roads, relays, and the listening city became one formation.

The theatre tested it before the valley could. Ciaccona removed the audience’s expected fourth beat from the seventh-edition march. Ovathrax supplied it after one minute and forty-one seconds, sending phantom rain through the boards and doubling the actor who played Geshu Lin with the memorial general. Cartethyia placed the named figures around him. Each personal detail disrupted the perfect heroic rhythm. Fantastical chickens and separate pear pots gave the march uneven feet, and the phantom general turned his head by one degree.

Jiyan reported that the details loosened individuals, but the black banner still held the formation’s destination. The first-edition actor showed Jinhsi what later versions had discarded: a southern messenger brought the relief order, the officers reported their sections, and Geshu looked back before answering. Successive playwrights had called the order redundant, shortened the scene for schools, and eventually given the general a cleaner ending by giving his company darkness.

Jinhsi restored the first movement with every figure in its true place. The messenger entered. Officers reported the west road, medical tents, fallen bridge, and reserves delayed by their own maps. When the general looked back, he saw not a chorus but people doing separate work: a signalman with flags, a captain with a moth cup, brothers disputing a blanket, a cook shaking dice. The actor could breathe from his own chest and speak the old line: if they advanced, they advanced by sections; if they returned, they returned by name.

Geshu Lin’s phantom voice struck the relay. Relief, he said, had spent three years arriving; they must bring the city that signed it. Darkness crashed through the theatre. Fleurdelys emerged from Cartethyia to catch falling scenery. Chisa sewed black rain into the orchestra pit. Lupa guarded the receiver. Mei caught the actor before the memorial pose dragged him into its mark. Amy refused to close the channel and ordered the field to answer outward by touch. Du, Bao, Sun, the guardian engineers, Qiuyuan’s medical orderly, and even Hsin’s nine tails supplied living witnesses in sequence. Lights returned one row at a time. The actor demanded that the messenger remain, and Ciaccona preserved the open fourth beat so the dead, when called, could choose their own answers.

The last shelter cases reached verified thresholds soon afterward. Grandmother Ge, her daughter, a twelve-year-old boy, and a tribute-loving donkey named Minister took the final stable and adjoining room under written terms. The official remainder reached zero through 4,112 separate entries, not through erasure of outstanding needs. Temporary arrangements, broken stoves, missing mattresses, and the goose’s captured quilt stayed attached to the return. Six minutes later, a daughter was born to the couple married in the weavers’ hall. Wen reopened the register, entered one resident—name pending, housed—and raised the cohort to 4,113 while the unhoused remainder remained zero.

The soldiers’ register could not reach the same completeness. Of 1,907 entries, 1,431 carried a name, detail, and road; 382 still awaited a personal detail; sixty-one required review of variant names; thirty-three preserved only a unit, recovery place, and a bordered human space. Cartethyia wanted to keep cutting until her injured thumb failed. Amy made her accept the bandage and reminded her that incompleteness did not bar anyone from the journey. The figures traveled together, finished and unfinished. Yangyang and the Welcoming Wind chose the remaining clear road and divided the two waterproof cases between several hands. The Wind pressed over the thirty-three blanks like an open palm, then released them into the company.

Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying arrived with medical wagons, shield couplings, infantry, engineers, a mobile kitchen, and an amended order. Jiyan retained field command. The entry column would carry Jinzhou’s civil authority, Xuanfang’s sovereign undertaking, ISA testimony, the full casualty register, and the first-edition roads, while the Grand Marshal took responsibility for the outer shelf. The objective was no longer to spend a heroic force against a monster. They would separate the dead from the performance, maintain a named southern road for every living entrant, resource each withdrawal through relief, preserve every dead soldier’s ability to answer apart from the chorus, expose the Fractsidus control structure, and deny Ovathrax the completed cue.

Jinhsi forced the Grand Marshal to name the limits under which people could be spent. Li Zhaoying answered with complete rolls, recovery obligations, extraction routes, relief rotations, and her own driver Zhou Lian as the man carrying her way back. She left any surviving Threnodian remainder under civic process rather than predetermining its fate. She also admitted that she had signed the assessment three years earlier declaring the losses acceptable. Jinhsi promised evidence capable of revising it, including all thirty-three damaged spaces. The Grand Marshal promised that headquarters would read every line before moving the next order.

Baizhi imposed equal precision on Qiuyuan’s body, limiting his amplified listening medicine and placing the flask, authorization, dressing, and medical return line in separate hands. Hsin left Old Seven in Jinzhou with the boy, the repaired bridge, and forty-one cups of tea, because a sovereign ought to leave one good argument behind. The automaton waited beneath a paper sign reading **FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK**.

Before departure, the city fed the column at eleven tables. Soldiers ate beside printers and Rangers beside the families whose details they carried. Mei checked bandages, stole and returned one of Amy’s greens, leaned against Iuno, and drank Earl Grey hot enough to survive the crisis. Amy assigned Mei the field call *Fool* and gave her two return calls, *Ma* on Amy’s channel and *Mei* on Iuno’s. If the performance swallowed her into a grand title, they would insult her back to the road with the résumé of the solitary hero she no longer wished to be.

Lupa checked every red tether and arranged the household in pairs inside larger triangles. Rope would tell them where bodies were; voices would tell them where people were. Any disagreement had to be spoken at once. Cartethyia packed the camellia terrarium, whose new leaf had listened to all their plans and therefore belonged on the journey. Ciaccona tightened its strap herself.

At Jinhsi’s table, each ward delivered a final question, resource, promise, or personal detail. An old man gave her the chopstick his dead son Gao Shun had clicked against a bowl whenever he wanted another serving, believing his mother had not noticed. Jinhsi promised to carry the knowledge that she had heard every time. Each ward sheet left **WHO RETURNS** open, but wrote beside it the thresholds the city could offer: beds, hot water, isolation rooms, translators, witnesses, storage for willingly surrendered weapons, and household terms. The field would bring back names, needs, and chosen destinations; the eleven rooms would answer.

At the north gate, Changli wore the Acting Magistrate’s plain robe with the civil seal inside her sleeve. Wen held the prophetic key. Jinhsi arrived in field clothes with Gao Shun’s chopstick secured beside the city’s maps. Changli adjusted the strap across Jinhsi’s shoulder with the care that had survived the failure of her methods. She promised to support the road home. Jinhsi placed a hand on her scarred wrist and told her to have the next room ready. Their account was neither forgiven nor abandoned. It had become another return obligation.

The column formed by names. At the shelf, Jiyan’s command joined the Grand Marshal’s layered relief, Hsin’s guardians, Qiuyuan’s listening line, engineers, medics, and paired runners. In Jinzhou, Mei’s household gathered around the registers. Yangyang and Amy shared one case with the Wind between them. Lupa checked tethers; Chisa listened to breathing; Cartethyia counted every complete and incomplete figure; Ciaccona held the unfinished measure; Iuno poured the last hot tea into Mei’s field flask. Every forward element had a protecting section, and every protecting section had relief. The battle that demanded one hero received a diagram crowded with hands.

North beneath the black banner, Geshu Lin waited in ceremonial armor with his wound arranged for an audience. Named soldiers already stood a fraction away from the chorus. The old pressure caught in the banner met the Welcoming Wind at the shelf, and the record recognized the impression. Jiyan sent his sections forward by voice and whistle while the battle drums remained covered. Sergeant Du spoke each section into motion, Lieutenant Sun answered from behind, and Bao carried the western road against her bound thumb.

Jinzhou opened the north gate, and the departure column walked through at the careful pace of people carrying paper. Citizens along the wall marked every living name, its departure time, and the names of those beside it. When Mei passed the eighth tower, the golden mark pulled toward Norfall and once more offered the straight line: one woman, one sword, one center. Her tether tightened softly. Iuno stood on her right. Amy and Ciaccona carried the case behind her. Lupa, Chisa, Cartethyia, Yangyang, the Wind, Jinhsi, the Rangers, and 1,907 entries occupied the road around her.

Mei matched their pace.

Then she looked back. Changli stood under the gate beside Wen, Chixia, eleven ward runners, and a city that had changed during the night. Twelve hours earlier, its exhausted leaders had been divided by secrecy, its displaced citizens had been compressed into an unfinished number, its fallen soldiers had been trapped inside rank and chorus, and Mei could feel only the entrance to a solitary trap. Now all 4,113 displaced residents had verified roofs with their conditions still visible. The eleven tables remained open. Every lamp along the wall burned. The recovery yard set out cups for the next shift, including an extra row facing north, and Panhua’s kitchen kept a stockpot steaming for people who had not yet arrived.

The Wind lifted a page from the register and carried it ahead. It held a soldier’s name, a true road, and room for an answer that no playwright, god, general, Magistrate, or Arbiter would supply on that person’s behalf. Fifty-seven seconds of silence neared its end. Geshu Lin waited with his weapon raised, but he no longer waited for a lone hero. A company approached with its dead named, its living tethered, its authority divided, its exits guarded, and its road home supported by an entire city.

Behind them, Jinzhou kept the water hot.
