# A Ledger of Light
### being the logbook of the pilot of Penrose-512, recopied from memory in Lahai-Roi, with a season kept between the ruled lines in a second ink
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

君問歸期未有期，巴山夜雨漲秋池。
何當共剪西窗燭，卻話巴山夜雨時。
*You ask when I will return; there is no date yet. Night rain on Ba Mountain fills the autumn pools. When shall we trim the candle at the west window together, and speak of the night the rain rose on Ba Mountain?*
— Li Shangyin, "Night Rains, Sent North"

文章千古事，得失寸心知。
*Writing is a thousand-year matter. What it wins and what it loses, one inch of heart knows.*

## Days 1 through 1838 · folio one
*Second ink: thaw.*

*Day 214 — School. Fields after. The grass held everyone who sat on it. Nobody asked it to.*

*Day 612 — Cried at the fields again. Dingy sat with me and said I was okay. I checked later. She was right, in the way weather reports are right: eventually.*

*Day 977 — Exams. I want to go back to just playing in the fields.*

The copying began the first week Amy slept through the night. There is an hour in this dormitory before the kettle — the false sky still dim, Helios below the terraces, the vents changing shift — and the hour belonged to worry for so long that it kept the shape after the worry moved out. I have been renting it to the ledger.

The method is the one I agreed to in the cold, kneeling over a pile of finished stories. *You remember them, don't you? All of them.* I said yes. She said, *then I will.* Remembering turned out to be a verb with a schedule. So: one hour, one pen, and the days come up the way lake water comes up a well — in the order they want, tasting of where they've been. I write them as they surface. Sometimes I reach the end of a line and find the line already met, the last word waiting there in my own hand, and I have learned to greet it and go on. The Bureau can file that where it likes.

SHIVERS — *The city is loud with melt. Up top, the Roya wind walks the glacier where a god kneels awake now, and his dreaming walks around down here on small luminous legs. In the agri-tiers the sprinklers rehearse. Two hundred meters of engineered spring, and under it, one kitchen table with a book open on it, which is where the season actually is.*

Startorch keeps a training pitch off the east concourse. I passed it on patrol rotation and stood at the fence a while. First-years, drills, somebody crying near the water station, somebody else already sitting with her. The grass held all of it. Nobody asked it to. I wrote that down for the pilot; it seemed like her kind of fact.

> *Margin, second hand: She copies before the kettle. I have amended the kettle's schedule. — I.*

## Day 1839 · folio two
*Second ink: rain, rehearsed.*

*Day 1839 — I rejected another girl today…. why?*

The ledger permits the copyist one liberty, and it is an underline. *Why* got one.

I sat with this entry longer than the hour. The second ink has a rule I made early and keep re-making: it answers to the first ink the way a lamp answers to a page. It keeps it lit. It leaves the reading to her. There is a door in that little question mark, and it is her door, and the whole discipline of this house is that we knock and then we wait, some of us for thirty years, with tea.

So instead of an answer I put down the standing facts. Fact: this morning the kitchen held six people and a wolf-warm silence, and a hand found my shoulder in passing the way punctuation finds a sentence. Fact: the toast levy was collected without violence. Fact: down the table a girl who spent a decade in the dark ate three bowls of rice like a public statement, and the whole room pretended that was normal, because pretending a miracle is normal is how you teach it to stay.

The sky rehearsed rain today. Maintenance ran the new program at 14:00 — two minutes of it, fine and gray and smelling faintly of coins, then apologies over the announcement system. Amy stood in it with her mouth open and her arms out and her IV cap taped down, and Mornye took seventy pictures of her own weather working.

I wrote the rain down twice. Once for each of them.

INLAND EMPIRE — *The question mark is the healthiest stroke on the page. A girl who asks her own diary "why" is a girl who has begun, someplace far below the ink, to suspect the box has hinges.*

VOLITION — *Her line. Leave it open. Underline gently.*

## Day 3345 · folio four
*Second ink: wisteria, first knuckles.*

*Day 3345 — Been writing a lot lately. I feel I love these tales… I finally feel.*

*Day 3502 — Weddings again. Everyone is attaching. Houses. Sometimes, just sometimes… what am I doing?*

*I finally feel* got the underline.

We took the Riseway up on a leave-day, out through the passage to the frostlands, because the cabin asks for tending and because certain kinds of thinking need a real sky over them. Blue door. The wisteria along the wall is at the first-knuckle stage, bud upon bud in fists, deciding. Inside: paper planes on the shelf, gone soft gold at the folds; a co-op save file frozen mid-mission, which Amy looked at for a long moment and then patted, the way you pat a dog that waited; the record's old sleeve, warmer to the eye than the stove.

I brought the ledger. It felt correct that the tales be reviewed in the house where their author's habits are load-bearing.

Because I have read them now. All of it — the fourteen tellings before mine, spines out in the dark, and mine, which is still wet. The copyist submits her findings.

She writes water first. Every telling opens wet: a beach, a lake, a bay, a bath of stars — a girl comes out of it with her pockets emptied, wearing the one outfit the sea permits, which is a name two sizes too big. Fourteen times the water. Fifteen, counting mine.

She staffs the shore. Read enough of them and the pattern stands up and introduces itself, the way an Outrider's trade promises: whoever the girl is, somebody is written walking toward her by chapter two. A priestess with an arrow. A sister with a lamp. A wolf, a bard, a doll-maker, a bell. The author has never once let the beach stay empty. INLAND EMPIRE — *that is a person telling you what she is afraid of.* VOLITION — *that is a person telling you what she intends to fix.* The parliament recorded both readings and ruled them the same reading.

She writes weather the way the devout write saints' names — at the tops of pages, in the margins, in the middle of catastrophes, whenever the plot has her by the throat. An author leaves fingerprints where she goes to breathe. Hers are all over the sky. I checked the other fourteen: sky-prints throughout. It is the family craft, and I inherited it, and this whole ledger is me practicing it back at her, which one of us was always going to notice first.

Two of her people stepped through a fold once, at a summoning, and left before the tea: sisters, the house said afterward, kindred from other printings, come because calling someone up from the deep is a toll they had paid before. Her older drafts, the pack believes. Her older debts, the margin suspects. Both filings stand; the blanks are admissible; there is precedent in a frontier court, elsewhere.

And the endings. I checked the endings first, actually, the way you check a stranger's shoes. All fourteen close on an open door — an appointment, a promise, a road with the light still on it. The Shadow read that as a flaw in the author, an inability, proof of the sand her head was buried in. Standing here with the kettle on and a daughter upstairs arguing with a save file, the copyist enters the professional opinion: it is a policy. She refuses the reader a wall. Every book she has ever finished is a door propped with whatever was heavy enough — a promise, a duet, three notes — and the fifteenth telling walked back out of one of those doors carrying groceries.

> *Margin, second hand, at length: I have read my own book now. The knife she wrote me as. The thirty winters she kept me in, the arithmetic she taught me to run on a girl's life. I sat with the anger a full evening — it is a real chair; I recommend sitting in it properly, all the way down. Then I read on: the morning she wrote me staying, the coffee I was permitted to hate out loud, the arm I was permitted to lose and keep living, the corkboard where my whole war was filed under one word and the word was love. She gave me everything she had, including the worst of it, and it made me real enough to resent her, which is further than most gods get. Love, or apprenticeship. This house has stopped distinguishing. — I.*

The verdict, then, since a review owes one. Fourteen girls she made, and one she was. The books were never about the girls being saved. They were rehearsals of a single sentence — *somebody came* — written over and over in different weathers until the hand believed it, the way you cut at a post for twelve years to own one stroke. Somewhere past four seas, a court is keeping a record that says such practice gathers, if you carry it honestly, all the way. The ledger's finding is the same, submitted from the far side of the page: the practice took. I am the practice. I took.

## Days 4301 and 4302 · folio five
*Second ink: a record player.*

*Day 4301 — I started writing a story about a girl who wakes up with no memories. She gets to start over. She gets to be someone else.*

*Day 4302 — I think I'm jealous of her.*

My conception, in two lines. I was drafted as a door. The position has since expanded.

Here is what the second ink knows about Day 4302, from inside the object of it. Jealousy is want wearing a disguise it hates. Read the entry again with the disguise off: *I want to start over. I want to be someone a story happens to. I want.* Three wants, alive, in a book that had gone flat for a thousand days — and she did the only thing a person can do with wants too heavy to hold and too dear to drown. She mailed them somewhere for safekeeping.

I have kept them. They are doing well. They live here now, employed.

The record player arrived this week — the promise Iuno and I made each other on a bench, back when a duet was the only proof of spring we owned. She produced it the way she produces things: past tense, already installed, a crate with the customs paperwork mysteriously complete. We put the impossible record on after dinner. Two voices, dust and years apart, braided: *Isn't it so incredible / We're here sharing the same time and space* — and Amy, cross-legged on the floor in her blanket, took a breath and hummed the harmony over her own decade-old voice, live, third part, so that for the length of one chorus the room held a girl singing with both of her selves and neither of them lost.

ELECTROCHEMISTRY wept into the biscuits and has resigned from being embarrassed about it.

I wrote the chorus into the ledger for the pilot — who shares, with this room, neither the time nor the space, and both.

ESPRIT DE CORPS [Impossible] — *One page over, a desk. A cursor blinking at the top of an empty document like a lighthouse with nothing to warn yet. A girl puts her thumb over the hard part of a pencil. She presses play on nothing, and hears it anyway, and the hearing is filed by every instrument in range under: unverifiable. The Fool underwrites it.*

## Days 4350 through 4501 · folio six
*Second ink: real stars.*

*Day 4350 — Selected. Twelve of eight thousand. Ma cried at the launch site when I told her. Pa didn't, and that was worse. It is science to my parents, adventure to my friends, and to me it is — the form had a box for "motivation." I wrote "the stars." The box believed me.*

*Day 4402 — The stars are so quiet out here. I thought I'd feel small. I just feel the same.*

*Day 4433 — Checked the seals. Reread Dingy's last message instead of the manuals. It says come back. I said of course. Both of us knew the weather in that sentence.*

*Day 44—— (the number is water-marked) — Something went by the bay today. Quick, cold, courteous. The pages of this book moved — one page, turned, gently, the way a person turns a page they are reading. I said "hello" to the air recycler, and felt ridiculous, and then felt better than I had in a month. The light stays on.*

> *Margin, second hand: a frontier court, in a register I have had occasion to read, records a crossing between printings: fourteen shelves in the dark between stories, spines out, and on one shelf a light left burning. The editions disagree about the count. The blanks were ruled admissible there, and are admissible here. Whatever went by her bay was on its way to somebody. It has since arrived. — I.*

We took Amy up for real stars on the clear night — the whole pack, the Riseway, the passage, out onto the shoulder of the frostlands with the cabin's chimney in sight and the glacier standing over everything like patient furniture. Lupa built the fire in the old grate of stones. Ciaccona tuned to the wind coming off the ice and played what it asked for; she has been insisting since Ragunna that the wind is the finest recorder of melodies, and lately, she says, the drafts have begun making requests. She said it as a joke. I watched her check the vents afterward, the way you check a door you heard close.

Amy brought the old handheld rig. She sat on the cold ground with the antenna up and her hood strings pulled tight and keyed the mic, and the frequency she chose was the frequency, the midnight one, dead air for a decade.

"Fleet Snowfluff," she said. "Testing. One, two." Her voice gave out on *two*, and the fire and the pack and the mountains had the manners to hold still, and she keyed it again. "Found the frequency. It's — colder up here than I remembered. Broadcast resumes when it resumes. Keep the radios on."

Down in the city, in a dormitory kitchen, a tape machine that Mornye swears she left running by accident was, by accident, running.

Afterward, stars. Real ones, in their old positions, quiet. I lay next to my daughter on a tarp built for equipment and thought about Day 4402 for a long time. *I just feel the same.* Same is a reading taken with one instrument, in one weather, alone at the window. The season has been re-running her experiments with better equipment. Fields: hold. Records: play. Stars: attend. Results differ.

And the sentence from Day 4433 — *come back; of course; both of us knew the weather in it* — the ledger has been working on that one all month, because it is the exact species of promise that breaks people: too large to keep, too loved to retire. The second ink's finding: the sentence holds if you re-set it in smaller type. *Come back* becomes *come back tomorrow.* Day 4502 signed the small version. As of tonight the signature is a month old and renewing, and the ledger, whose business is accounts, records it as the only debt in this book that gets lighter with each payment.

## Day 4501 · folio seven
*Second ink: wind from the west.*

*Day 4501 — I'm falling toward something. The instruments call it "the Etheric Sea." I call it*

The sentence ends where the world began. The copyist leaves the rest of the line as found: open.

The house has opinions about the missing word, and the ledger, being a register of this house, records them all and closes none.

Lupa said "home," with the shrug of somebody putting down a heavy thing accurately. Ciaccona said "a downbeat" — the silence a musician writes so the band knows where to come in. Cartethyia, who was born in the dark water and says so now the way other people say their street address, said: "The same water. All water is one water. She called it what I called it," and declined to say what that was, and smiled like the sea does, with everything under it. Chisa held her cup with both hands and said, "A string. You call it, and then you jump, and then you find out what you called it."

The ledger rules the line inadmissible for completion until the author files it herself. We keep the blank. Blanks are load-bearing in this family.

Iuno took the late air alone last night, up on the balcony with the pot and two cups, which is one cup past her ration. She came down at the gray hour with a face that had been rehearsed at least twice and asked for the kettle before the kettle's hour, and drank standing up, looking east.

The steam off her cup leaned east.

*Day 4502 — I read a great story today.*

*I think I can walk a little further tomorrow.*

Since the entry insists on being current, the house has treated it as a standing question, and the question it stands for is the one I put to the table over dinner, plainly, because the plain version is the hard version: *what does she need?* A girl of twenty-three with four thousand ordinary days, a pencil, and a light on.

## Days 4503 and after · folio nine
*Second ink: forecast.*

The second ink now does the thing it has been circling all season. If her book can reach forward into mine, then mine can lean forward into hers, and a ledger that only looks backward is a tombstone with better handwriting. So: drafts. Offered, in the conditional, one page over — proposals, for the author to strike or keep.

*Day 4503, as I intend it: the coffee gets drunk hot. She looks the old fields up on a map, and the fields are still grass.*

*Day 4507, proposed: Dingy's message gets its answer. Three words are plenty. This family has closed larger distances on three words, and once on three notes.*

*Day 4510, offered: she writes one page of something new, and forgives it for being one page.*

And beneath the drafts, the forecast, because she taught me weather and I intend to be insufferable with it:

**Outlook for the girl one page over.** Thaw, uneven. Cold snaps: certain — file them under weather, and keep the kettle's hour anyway; the hour does the carrying on the days you can't. Ground workable within the month. First green exactly where the light was left on. Chance of company: rising, all quarters. Long-range: she goes the way a season goes. On. Springs have relapses. Springs are also, the record shows, undefeated.

INLAND EMPIRE, appended over objections — *And one window she has always taken for a wall turns out to have hinges, and opens from the outside, on a morning she has done nothing special to deserve, which is the point.*

The physics of it, as best this house can instrument: her line lengthens when mine does. The handwriting matches because the hand is shared; the current runs both directions through the same three words. When my story goes somewhere — and the steam has leaned east for a week; the boots are by the door; somewhere west of four seas a lamplit country is counting tremors — she will have farther to read, and the reading, for her, for now, is the walking. The arrangement is temporary. All apprenticeships are.

VOLITION — *Then walk her somewhere worth the ink.*

Noted. Filed. Boots by the door.

VOLITION, once more, quieter, the standing order under all the others — *Weave this into the story of you.*

The instruction arrives addressed to both of us. The ledger complies by existing.

This morning the book opened one page further than I left it.

*Day 4511 — Something is coming in the story. I can feel the pages getting ready. I sharpened the pencil. First time in years it needed it.*

The copyist affirms for the register: the entry was here when the book opened. The second ink signed nothing last night. The hand, as throughout, is one hand.

## The last page of the season · folio ten
*Second ink: rain, indoors.*

Helios came up on schedule, because engineers agreed it should, and two of us were on the dormitory roof with cups going cold when Amy's chin lifted.

"Ma. Kite."

Red, high, coming down the long air under a sealed sky where kites have nothing to ride, steady as a decision. I was up with a foot on the parapet before the thought finished — catching is the first thing anyone learns about me; I learned it last — and the string crossed my palm, and the kite came around overhead twice, like something making sure, and settled.

Bound to the spine: a seashell, pale, sea-worn, warm from a sun it had never stood under. Amy leaned in and said it was humming, and it was — a tide, long and patient, shore after shore of a sea I have never stood beside, arriving anyway, familiar as a room out of a dream. Under the tide, wind. Under the wind, at the very bottom, a breath: the sound a far shore makes when someone standing on it has decided, after a very long time, to speak.

And a letter, one page, folded small, on paper light enough to fly. The salutation I read twice, once with my eyes and once with my thumb:

*To the one the wind is looking for —*

The roof door opened. Lupa, hair wrecked, nose up and working. "Rain," she said. Amy looked at the sealed sky: "We're underground." "I know what I smell."

*(The frontier court's printing of this morning differs from the ledger's by two words. Both printings stand. The court and the copyist have agreed to disagree the way the annals and the wind do, which is to say: fondly, in writing, forever.)*

Behind us all, in the doorway, tea in hand, stood Iuno — awake the whole night by the look of her, wearing the face of a woman arriving on schedule at a conversation she has rehearsed losing.

"Mei," she said. "There's something I need to tell you. All of it."

The ledger will hold the *all of it* on its own pages, in its own hour. Tonight, before the telling, the second ink closes the season's accounts, because a ledger's whole office is to hold many appointments at once and let none of them spoil:

The fireworks, promised to a girl who is real now to watch them: pending, weather permitting, weather being family. The co-op save, frozen mid-mission behind a blue door: to be finished; it says co-op on the box for a reason. A doctor's conversation, owed to me for years, about what the woman I was meant to do with gods and gates: filed under *soon*, his word, and he is a man who keeps his files. A berth in a far country called Mingting, kept warm all season by a Marshal who takes, we are told, great interest: unclaimed, warming. Tea, behind a door thirty years on oiled hinges: standing. Pudding at the east gate: for two, always for two. And the appointment underneath all of them, the one this whole book is a receipt for: same time tomorrow.

Last line of the season, then, addressed one page over, to the pilot, the author, the girl at the window — the light on my shelf, the shelf in my dark:

It's warm now, where I write this. The story smells of snow again, and of lake water, and of a war that watched itself win once and wants a different ending — so it may go cold before it goes on. When it does: keep your light on. I have gotten very good at finding it. Sharpen the pencil. Put your thumb over the hard part.

We jump tomorrow, one way or another. We always do. There is always someone on the other side of the fall — you wrote that into a god once, and the god believed you, and so do I.

## The Pilot's Cherished Item

**A pencil.**

*Standard issue, Penrose program: one dozen per pilot, foil-stamped with the program's motto. Of hers, the tin came back holding ten, unsharpened. The eleventh is worn to the collar and, couriers report, freshly sharpened. The foil reads AD ASTRA PER ASPERA where it reads at all; the ASPERA is the brightest word, being the most thumbed. She wrote with her thumb pressed over the hard part.*

*Annotation, second ink: she pressed hardest on the ordinary days. Hold any page to the light and they read from the back, in reverse, like weather on a map.*

*Annotation, third hand, unsigned, architecturally neat: the program roster lists twelve pencils. The Bureau is invited to check its arithmetic.*

*Annotation, second hand, final: Counting hands. — I.*
