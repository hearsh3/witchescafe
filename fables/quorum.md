# Quorum
### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

天時不如地利，地利不如人和。
*The season's favor gives way to the ground's advantage. The ground's advantage gives way to people in accord.*
— Mencius

---

## I.

The kite lay along the parapet with its tail wound round the spar, and the string was still warm across Mei's palm where it had crossed.

Amy sat on the low wall with her knees up. Lupa stood at the roof door with her hair wrecked from sleep and her nose working at an underground morning that had suddenly acquired weather.

Mei turned the page over once, and then again, and got as far as the salutation.

<font color=mistyrose>"Out loud."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Please."</font>

There were ten years in the *please.* Ten years of a girl in a place with no sound in it, holding three notes because they were the last thing anybody had said to her. Mei sat down on the wall beside her daughter and started at the top.

> *To the one the wind is looking for —*
>
> *I have tried several names for you and they all belonged to someone else, so I will do without.*
>
> *Our city sits in a bowl of water. The willows on the west bank come out three days ahead of the ones on the east and nobody has ever explained it, and every spring the whole city argues about it as though the argument were new. There is a dam above us. At dusk the lamp-grid lights from the north side first, and if you stand on the crest wall you can watch the gold run down the terraces the way something poured runs down a step.*
>
> *I am an Outrider. I write the incident ledger for my patrol. This season I have written the word* weather *nineteen times.*
>
> *Third watch, the eleventh: a Discord's claw turned aside by a gust, indoors, in a stone room with the door shut. Fourth watch, the nineteenth: a rope bridge over the gorge held through a squall, and the porter swore on his mother that the wind leaned on the knot. A beam came down in the market and an Outrider was standing where it landed, and then he was standing four feet to the left, and he could not tell me how.*
>
> *My sister wrote to me from home. She sweeps the courtyard where I trained. There is a post there that I cut at for two years and left unmarked, and one morning after wind she found a new cut across the old scars — one stroke, clean, three inches deep, the grain parted like water — and the wooden sword standing at the post's foot with its edge to the east. She says a guest visited. She says the guest left a mark my teacher would have liked.*
>
> *I go up to the northeast cliff at dawn when I come off shift. My friends chose my hat for me and there is a bird stitched at the brim, and some mornings the wind pulls at it going past, familiar as a hand, and I say* manners *out loud to the open air like a woman with somewhere to be.*
>
> *I have left my window open on windy nights. I do not know what I am ■■■■■■■ for. I think you do.*
>
> *The Magistrate kneels at the water and asks it about the year ahead and the water shows her the year behind — a library burning that is standing whole across the square from her own window, floodwater at second-floor shutters on streets that have been dry a decade, a red cap turning in a current. The Ministry files this under her grief. I have read the file. It is a very tidy file.*
>
> *The water is showing us a year that belongs to somebody else. The patrols write* weather *in the cause column. I am writing to you because weather is not a cause. It is a warning, and warnings are for the people who can still ■■■■.*
>
> *If this reaches nobody, I will have spent a season and some paper. If it reaches you, then I have been standing on a cliff at dawn for a reason, and I would like very much to be told which.*

Mei folded it along the crease it came with.

The Solistrees hummed their morning down below. Helios came up on schedule because engineers agreed that it should.

<font color=indianred>"Read the blanks."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"They're blank."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Read them anyway."</font>

Mei looked at the two gaps in the type — clean rectangles where the founders had begged the reader's pardon — and her mouth moved and produced nothing, twice, exactly.

Iuno was in the doorway with a pot and two cups, and she had been there for a while, and she had not slept.

Lupa's nose went once toward her, and then again, and her ears came forward.

<font color=indianred>"You've been making two."</font>

Silence on the roof.

<font color=indianred>"Three weeks. Every night, the guest service, hot, and one cup comes back full and cold and you wash it standing up."</font> Lupa's tail moved once against the doorframe. <font color=indianred>"I thought it was grief. People do stupid things with cups."</font>

Iuno set the pot on the parapet.

<font color=royalblue>"Sit down,"</font> she said, to all three of them. <font color=royalblue>"This takes a while and I'd rather do it once."</font>

---

She told it standing.

The balcony past midnight. The steam leaning east against a vent that draws west. The second cup poured hot, handle turned outward, guest-side, and left there. The voice coming through a door open a finger's width, buying every word out of a purse that was going to zero.

*Ovathrax.* The Threnodian of War. Coming to the youngest gate.

The audit thirty years old, in a basin, with clear sky in every direction and wind on the water for one hour, leaning east, and one line written under it in a young priestess's hand: *Weather.*

And the correction the wind had spent nearly her last coin on. *Her to choose.*

When Iuno finished, the pot had gone cold and Amy had both arms around her own knees and Lupa had not moved at all.

Mei stood up. She crossed the roof to the door, and her hand went to the hook by it where the bike keys hung — six sets, brass tags, hers on the second peg — and closed around them.

Everyone watched her hand.

Mei stood there for eleven seconds with her fist round the keys and her back to the roof.

Then she hung them up.

She turned around and put her palms flat on the doorframe on either side of her, the way you brace a thing that wants to move, and Iuno saw exactly how much it cost and would be thinking about it for years.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"How long does honest take?"</font>

Iuno considered it the way she considered load-bearing questions.

<font color=royalblue>"Four days. Done properly."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Take five."</font>

---

## II.

The rule came at the table that evening, before anyone had eaten, and it landed badly, which Iuno had budgeted for.

<font color=royalblue>"Before any of the rest of it. Nobody speaks about it tonight."</font>

<font color=teal>"Excuse me?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The letter goes on the table. The shell goes on the table. Everyone reads it, and then we go to bed, and tomorrow morning we start."</font> Iuno set the pot down. <font color=royalblue>"That's mine. It's my tolerance and I'll say why."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Say why."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Because Mei arrives at decisions in the gap between two heartbeats, and she is nearly always right, and that is the problem."</font> Iuno kept her eyes on the pot while she said it, which was its own courtesy. <font color=royalblue>"If she says it tonight, every one of you agrees inside four seconds. Tomorrow you'll each be able to tell me what you decided and none of you will be able to tell me when."</font>

Cartethyia opened her mouth.

<font color=royalblue>"Cartethyia. When did you decide?"</font>

Cartethyia closed her mouth.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"On the roof,"</font> said Lynae, from the counter. <font color=palegoldenrod>"She wasn't even on the roof. She heard about the roof."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"That's an *unkind* thing to say out loud."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"It's an accurate thing to say out loud, which is the more expensive kind."</font>

Mei had her chin on her hand at the end of the table and had said nothing since she sat down.

<font color=moccasin>"I'm in,"</font> she said. <font color=moccasin>"On the rule. Not the — I'm in on the rule."</font>

<font color=indianred>"You're agreeing to be muzzled."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'm agreeing that when I open my mouth in this house it does something to the room, and I'd like the room to get a night off from it."</font>

Lupa's ears went flat, then came forward.

<font color=indianred>"...Fine."</font>

<font color=teal>"Say it in a normal voice."</font>

<font color=indianred>"*Fine.*"</font>

---

The house stayed up, and it stayed quiet, and it turned out those were two separate skills.

Lupa cooked for eleven. Nobody counted out loud. There was enough left over to fill three containers and she filled them and stacked them and stood looking at the stack with her hands on the counter for a long moment before she put them in the cold store.

Cartethyia sewed at the long table under the lamp with the good bulb, and what she was making went into her lap whenever anyone came through, and she did that eight times with a perfectly clear face, which was worse.

In the practice room Ciaccona played something in D that kept turning over on itself and refusing to land, and the timer on the sill went at eighty-nine minutes, and she took her hands off the strings on the beat, mid-phrase, on the wrong note, and sat with them in her lap for a while and then put on the brace.

Chisa went up to the drying terrace with a mug that went cold in her hands and sat on the reinforced crate with the collar in both hands, turning it, thumb finding the three-line wave punched by hand into the inside of it, and put it back on her hip at some point and stopped noticing she had.

Lynae did arithmetic on the back of a delivery slip, in pencil, and crossed out a number four times, each one smaller than the last.

Rebecca drew on the north wall until the marker gave out and then went and got another marker.

At three in the morning the kitchen light was on.

Amy came down for water and stopped in the doorway.

Her mother was at the sink, washing one cup. It was already clean. She washed it, and set it in the drainer, and took it out of the drainer and washed it again, and set it back, and squared it with the others so the handles all pointed the same way.

Amy stood in the dark of the corridor with her hand on the frame.

Then she went back upstairs without her water, because the rule was that nobody spoke about it tonight, and she kept the rule, and it cost her more than it cost anybody in the building.

---

## III.

They built the thing at the kitchen table over four hours the next morning, which is how this family builds everything.

<font color=royalblue>"Same as the last one,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"Rules. Owned. Named for whose limit they are."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"The last one was about me."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The last one worked."</font>

Amy took the pen off her, wrote *RULES* at the top of the back of a lab printout, underlined it twice, and handed the pen back, which settled the question of whether the format was agreed.

<font color=royalblue>"One. Mei speaks last."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yes."</font>

<font color=indianred>"That fast?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"She's right."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Say the reason out loud. It's load-bearing."</font>

Mei turned her cup a quarter-turn on the table.

<font color=moccasin>"Because if I go first, this table agrees with me in four seconds and then seven people ride to a war they never actually chose, and I'll have done to all of you what got done to me."</font> She set the cup down. <font color=moccasin>"And I'd know. Every day. For the rest of it."</font>

Chisa's head turned very slightly toward her, and turned back.

<font color=indianred>"Two's mine."</font> Lupa put both forearms on the table. <font color=indianred>"Nobody says their answer in this room first. You say it once, alone, to one person, and that person writes it down and seals it and doesn't tell."</font>

<font color=teal>"Why."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Because I go in four seconds and the room goes with me."</font> Her jaw worked. <font color=indianred>"I've watched it. Septimont, twice. Somebody good stands up and says yes, and eleven people who were still thinking find out they already agreed. That's weather. Somebody gave it a name that sounds like a vote and everyone went home pleased with themselves."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You've never called yourself weather before."</font>

<font color=indianred>"I've never been sober about it before."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Accepted. Amendment: the person who wrote it reads it out at the table."</font>

The kitchen stopped.

<font color=teal>"...Say that again."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You give your reason to one person. At the table, they read it. In their voice. To everyone."</font> Iuno wrote it down as she spoke. <font color=royalblue>"So that nobody in this house gets to *perform* the most important sentence of their life. Every one of you is a performer. Three of you are professionals."</font>

<font color=teal>"That's the most hostile thing you have ever done to me and I've watched you do paperwork."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Object to it and I'll strike it."</font>

Ciaccona looked at the table for a while.

<font color=teal>"...Keep it."</font>

<font color=coral>"Three."</font> Chisa had her hands flat in front of her. <font color=coral>"I listen. Voices do a thing at the third syllable when the reason underneath is different from the reason on top. I can hear it in all of you and I've been hearing it for a year and saying nothing because nobody asked."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"...Okay, that's *deeply* upsetting—"</font>

<font color=coral>"I can call anyone. Any time. Once, out loud, and then they get to answer or refuse, and the refusal goes in the record."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Who calls you?"</font>

Chisa was quiet.

<font color=coral>"Amy."</font>

Amy's pen stopped.

<font color=coral>"You cannot verify yourself from inside,"</font> Chisa said. <font color=coral>"You wrote that. You're the only other person at this table who's been unable to."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"...Yeah, okay."</font>

<font color=teal>"Four."</font> Ciaccona held up her braced hand. <font color=teal>"Anybody can change their answer. Right up to the moment the wheels turn on the loading dock. And when they do, the rest of you say *okay* and then you shut up about it forever."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Forever's a long—"</font>

<font color=teal>"Forever."</font> Her voice went somewhere flat and old. <font color=teal>"I nearly turned myself in once to keep a torch off the rest of you. Four people talked me down and every single one of them was kind about it, and I've been carrying the kindness ever since like a bill. If somebody at this table looks at that letter and says *I can't*, they say it once and it costs them nothing and it never comes up again."</font>

Cartethyia's hand went under the table and found hers.

<font color=skyblue>"Five,"</font> Cartethyia said. <font color=skyblue>"When you give your reason, you say what it costs the person on your left. Not you. Them."</font>

<font color=indianred>"That's cruel."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I spent twenty years doing arithmetic about people I couldn't see, and I got every single sum wrong, and I'd have got them right if anybody had made me say them out loud to a face."</font> A shrug, one shoulder. <font color=skyblue>"So. Five."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Six."</font> Amy put her pen down. <font color=mistyrose>"The people staying get a vote. Same weight. It counts."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"We're staying. That's the definition of not—"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"For ten years I was the thing everybody was deciding about."</font> Amy said it evenly, chin up. <font color=mistyrose>"There were meetings. There were *files.* Nine people sat in a room and decided about a girl who wasn't in it, and every single one of them loved me, and it was still the worst thing that's ever happened to me and I include the void."</font>

The kitchen absorbed that.

<font color=mistyrose>"Everybody in this house votes,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"Nine."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Six stands."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Seven's mine and it's boring. Numbers before feelings. I do the estimate first, and once it's on the table nobody argues around it — you can argue *with* it, you can call me wrong, you can't pretend I didn't say it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Granted."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Eight."</font>

Everyone turned. Rebecca had her feet up on the chair rung and her arms crossed.

<font color=greenyellow>"Before you go, every one of you writes down one true thing about every other person here. One line. Doesn't have to be nice. Has to be true. I keep them."</font>

<font color=teal>"Why."</font>

Rebecca looked at the table for a second.

<font color=greenyellow>"'Cause I drew my dead off memory and I got some of it wrong,"</font> she said. <font color=greenyellow>"And the wrong parts are the ones I think about."</font>

Eight went on the list.

## IV.

Lynae did the estimate standing up, at the whiteboard, with a marker, in fourteen minutes.

Transit: three weeks by the Mingting route if the Marshal's berth was still warm, and it was, and she had a name and a favour and a date. Materiel: what they carried, which was a great deal, and none of it resupplied past the passage. Intelligence: one letter, one shell, and a dead woman's word relayed through a priestess at two in the morning. Medical: one physician with a rolling stool and a dish of sweets, who was staying, and a list of four bodies on the deployment roster with recent surgical dates on them.

Terrain nobody in the room had walked. A garrison whose doctrine they'd never read. A Sentinel they'd never met, who was nineteen, and who was being remembered by something she had yet to meet.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Posture: seven, on the roster I've been assuming, which is a guess and which I will redo inside an hour if you hand me a different one. Seven, into a theatre nobody at this table has walked, against a full-class Threnodian that took a city and won."</font>

She capped the marker.

<font color=indianred>"Number."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You don't want it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Number, Lynae."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Then somebody promise me it stands. I give you a number and it stays where I put it, and nobody in this room massages it in the next four days because it went somewhere ugly."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It stands."</font>

Lynae gave the number.

The ventilation changed shifts.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"And now the professional disclosure,"</font> she said, into it, <font color=palegoldenrod>"which is that my number is garbage."</font>

<font color=teal>"You just made us *promise*—"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"It stands *and* it's garbage, those are both true and you'll have to hold them at the same time like adults."</font> She put the marker down. <font color=palegoldenrod>"I've priced this table four times. Ragunna. Septimont. The Plains. The gate. I was correct once and I've been wrong three times in the same direction, and there's a word for a man who keeps betting the same way against the same horse and it's not *analyst.*"</font>

<font color=coral>"So what's the real number."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"There isn't one, because you people keep doing things that aren't in the model."</font> Lynae sat down on the edge of the table. <font color=palegoldenrod>"Which is a very nice thing to say about your friends and an absolutely worthless thing to plan on. So take the ugly one. Plan on the ugly one. And then go and be unmodelable, and I'll do the paperwork afterward and complain."</font>

---

Rebecca went next, and she did it sitting down, and it took two minutes.

<font color=greenyellow>"You want the other estimate?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Go on."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"The room."</font> She turned her marker end over end. <font color=greenyellow>"After. That's my number, 'cause I'm the only one here who's had the whole thing happen at once."</font>

Nobody moved.

<font color=greenyellow>"You keep setting the table for the number,"</font> Rebecca said. <font color=greenyellow>"That's the thing. Not the big stuff — the big stuff you brace for. It's that you put out seven and then you're standing there holding two plates and your hands are still doing it, weeks later, months, and every time you go *oh, right* like it's news. Every time. Like brand new information."</font>

Cartethyia had gone very still, which from Cartethyia was a siren.

<font color=greenyellow>"And the noise in a place changes."</font> Rebecca shrugged. <font color=greenyellow>"That's all. That's my estimate. I'd be a hypocrite if I was saying it to stop you — I've done dumber for worse and I'd do it Tuesday."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Rebecca."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Yeah?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Thank you."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Don't."</font> She stood up and stretched. <font color=greenyellow>"Here's my ask. Before you go, I get an hour with each of you and you sit still and I draw you. Alive. Sitting in a chair being annoying, with your face doing the thing it does."</font>

<font color=teal>"...Ah."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=teal>"So the reference exists."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"So I'm working off a chair and a Tuesday, and off nothing else, at four in the morning, forever."</font>

---

## V.

Chisa assigned the pairs, by ear, and refused every appeal.

<font color=teal>"This is a *disaster*—"</font>

<font color=coral>"You'd tell Cartethyia the tidy version and she'd believe it, because she loves you and she's an optimist and those are the same disease."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"HEY—"</font>

<font color=coral>"Lupa gets you. She has never once believed anything you've said on the first pass."</font>

<font color=indianred>"That's true."</font>

<font color=teal>"...Fine."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Who do I get?"</font>

<font color=coral>"Amy."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"She's *nineteen.*"</font>

<font color=coral>"She's nineteen and she has spent ten years listening to people be brave at her through a wall. Take it up with the ear."</font>

---

Iuno got Cartethyia, which she had planned for approximately nine seconds before Chisa said it, and which she had been dreading since.

They did it on the drying terrace at eleven at night with the laundry moving in the warm vent-draft.

<font color=skyblue>"Okay. Go."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I'm going. That's the answer."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"That's the answer everybody's giving, and Chisa's going to hear it and make a face. Do the reason."</font>

Iuno held her cup with both hands, real and crystal, and looked out at the reservoir where the lights were breaking up in the water.

<font color=royalblue>"I'm going to be very good at it."</font>

Cartethyia waited.

<font color=royalblue>"That's the reason. The letter is true, and the woman on the balcony, and the girl on the cliff, and any one of them would hang on the front of this beautifully and every person at that table would take it."</font> She turned the cup a quarter-turn. <font color=royalblue>"I have spent eighteen months learning to ask before I move. It has been the hardest and best work of my life and I have done it badly on eleven separate occasions and been corrected each time by people at this house who love me, and I have kept at it, and I am proud of it, and I would like that noted."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Noted."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"And a war is a room where asking gets people killed."</font>

The laundry moved.

<font color=royalblue>"Somebody will have a wall coming down and four seconds to decide who's under it, and the four seconds will be mine, and I will be *magnificent.*"</font> Iuno's voice stayed exactly level, which cost the same every time. <font color=royalblue>"And I will be good at it every day for three months, in front of everyone, and it will work, and they'll thank me. And when we come home I will have to put it down again."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"...And you don't think you can."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I think I'll be able to make an extremely persuasive case for keeping it."</font>

Cartethyia sat with that for a while, and then she put her mug down on the concrete and did the thing nobody expects Cartethyia to do, which was to stop being loud.

<font color=skyblue>"Do it out loud,"</font> she said.

<font color=royalblue>"That's the whole doctrine of this house, and I wrote half of it, and I'm telling you it will not survive contact—"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Then it fails out loud, in front of us, where somebody can grab your arm."</font> She picked a fallen sock off the concrete and folded it, badly. <font color=skyblue>"I was very good at mine, too. Best there's ever been at mine. They put me in a *window*, Iuno. Coloured glass, four metres tall, sword up, and every single person who knelt under it thought they were being kind."</font>

Iuno looked at her.

<font color=skyblue>"Nobody ever grabbed my arm,"</font> Cartethyia said. <font color=skyblue>"That's the entire difference between my twenty years and your next three months, and it's standing in a kitchen downstairs arguing about pastry."</font>

She took out the paper Iuno had given her, and wrote it down, in her enormous round hand, and folded it, and sealed it.

<font color=skyblue>"Also I'm putting *magnificent* in."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You will not."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It's a direct quote. I'm a *witness*, Iuno, I have *responsibilities*—"</font>

---

Mei got Rebecca, and Rebecca made her come to the north wall to do it, because Rebecca was not going to be walked to.

<font color=greenyellow>"So."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"So."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Big hero's gonna say she's going 'cause the letter came to her."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...That's what I was going to say."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Yeah, well, I got told to write down a true thing, and I'm gonna get graded by a woman with scissors who can hear people lying, so."</font> Rebecca sat down on the ledge and swung her boots. <font color=greenyellow>"Try again."</font>

Mei stood there a while with her hands in her pockets, looking at the acid-green sprawl of Rebecca's wall.

<font color=moccasin>"If I stay,"</font> she said, <font color=moccasin>"I have to find out who I am when nobody needs saving."</font>

The vent machines turned over somewhere.

<font color=moccasin>"I got my daughter back three weeks ago. I've slept through the night four times in my whole recorded life and three of them were this month. And every single morning I get up and there's nothing to walk toward, and I have been calling that *peace* out loud in this house since spring."</font> She kept looking at the wall. <font color=moccasin>"And then a kite came down through a sealed sky and my hands were on the keys before I got to the second line."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"And?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Relief."</font>

Rebecca whistled, one note, low.

<font color=greenyellow>"That's the worst reason I've ever heard for anything."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Like top three. I've met *mercs.*"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"Okay."</font> Rebecca got out the paper and a pen with her teeth. <font color=greenyellow>"Say it again slower, I write like a drunk."</font>

She wrote it. Then she stopped and chewed the cap.

<font color=greenyellow>"Can I put a question under it?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"The rules say one line."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"The rules say one line *from you.* This one's from me."</font>

Mei thought about it.

<font color=moccasin>"Go on."</font>

Rebecca wrote a second line, and folded it, and sealed it, and put it in her jacket on the left side.

<font color=moccasin>"What was it?"</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"You'll hear it at the table with everyone else,"</font> Rebecca said, <font color=greenyellow>"like a *big girl.*"</font>

---

Lupa got Chisa, which surprised nobody and which Chisa had done on purpose, and they did it on the roof of the machine shop where Lupa could see the exits.

## VI.

<font color=coral>"Anything. It's for the sound."</font>

<font color=coral>"Ma—"</font>

*"You have taken something. I'd like to take something."*

<font color=coral>"Ask."</font>

*"The band."*

The clocks went on being four separate opinions.

<font color=coral>"No."</font>

At six the collar chimed.

The bedroom door was already open. Her mother was standing in it.

She had been standing in it since five fifty-five, because she had been getting up at six for twenty years, and because she had done the arithmetic on a daughter who had refused something at a table the night before and had chosen to stay over anyway.

<font color=coral>"Sixty-one."</font>

*"Mm."*

Chiyo went and put the kettle on.

<font color=coral>"Rebecca."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Nobody else in this house makes that noise."</font> Mei looked round the table. <font color=moccasin>"Every single one of you has decided to be careful with me and it has been extremely restful and I have been eating it with a spoon since spring."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's—"</font> Amy's voice went. She got it back. <font color=mistyrose>"You know what you're doing. You know what you're putting on me."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I do."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's — Ma, that's the whole *weight*, that's the exact thing you've spent three weeks trying to keep off me—"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It's the only honest answer I've got, and we agreed to spend five days on honest, and I've been sitting here for four of them with my mouth shut listening to people I love describe me accurately."</font> Mei's throat did something complicated. <font color=moccasin>"You can refuse. Rule four. You say *I can't* and it costs you nothing and it never comes up again."</font>

Amy sat with it.

The Solistrees hummed. Somewhere down the corridor a door opened and closed.

<font color=mistyrose>"...Okay,"</font> Amy said.

Mei let out a breath she had been holding since the first morning.

<font color=mistyrose>"It's a trade, though."</font> Amy's chin came up, wet-eyed and absolutely level. <font color=mistyrose>"You take mine too. If I say stop, you stop. If you say stop, I stop. Both ways or neither."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...That's a terrible deal for you."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm a terrible negotiator, I get it from my mother."</font>

Lupa put her face in her hand.

<font color=royalblue>"Show of hands,"</font> Iuno said.

Nine hands went up.

## IX.

Mei found Lynae on the loading dock at three in the morning, because Lynae conducted all her important business at three in the morning on loading docks, and it was a personal brand, and she defended it.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I've already decided."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I know."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Days ago. Before the table. I sat down and worked out what my life is for at the moment and it turned out to be a nineteen-year-old with half her ink and a candidacy she hasn't decided whether she wants."</font> Lynae was looking at the false stars. <font color=palegoldenrod>"So whatever this is, I've saved you the speech."</font>

Mei got out the folded printout of a message that had come from a President's communicator disguised as a makeup mirror, and put it on the rail.

Lynae read it twice.

The second time, her hand came up and pressed flat on the rail.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"...She's on campus."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"She's been on campus the whole time. She was at the funeral. She was at the *gate.*"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"And she's watching Sigrika."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"She's got a name for her."</font>

Lynae read the last line again.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"How long have you had this."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Since before the kite."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Five days of *honest*, Mei. Eight rules. A woman crying over a *form.*"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"And this sat in your jacket through every hour of it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"If it goes on that table, Chisa stays, Lupa stays, and Amy volunteers to stay, and the three of them are wrong and I'd have let them do it anyway because I'd have been outvoted by people who love a girl I also love."</font> Mei kept her eyes on the rail. <font color=moccasin>"So I held it. That's the whole confession and I've got no dressing for it."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You built a machine for five days to keep yourself honest and then you went round the back of it."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yes."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"...At least you didn't make me *find* it."</font>

She folded the paper along its crease, very neatly, and gave it back, and then stood there with both hands on the rail and her head down for a while.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"So you've just turned it into a job."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"...Yeah."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I had a *reason*, Mei. A whole one, mine, that I picked out on my own like a grown adult, and it was the first thing in eleven years I've done for a person instead of a fee."</font> Her voice stayed level and went thin at the edges. <font color=palegoldenrod>"And now it's an assignment. Now every time I sit on her floor and make her eat something I'll be on post."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"That is a *rotten* thing to do to a person."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It is."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You could have left."</font> Lynae turned round. <font color=palegoldenrod>"You could have got on the bike on Thursday and let me have the clean version for three months. I'd never have known. Nobody would have told me."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno had that as the recommended option."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Iuno's got *sense.*"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno wrote it out, and read it back to me, and then took her own pen and put a line through it,"</font> Mei said. <font color=moccasin>"And said the word *weather.* And I didn't ask what she meant and I'm fairly sure I know."</font>

Lynae laughed, one breath, and it came out wrong.

She held her hand out.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Give me the file."</font>

Mei gave her the file.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I can't tell her."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Mm."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Because a girl who knows she's being watched changes how she walks, and the thing watching her reads walks."</font> Lynae put the file inside her jacket. <font color=palegoldenrod>"So I'm going to spend three months lying to somebody I'd take a round for, which is the exact thing this entire family exists to be against, and which I am doing, tonight, with my eyes open, because the alternative is worse. Do you hear how that sounds."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I've said it in this house four times."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"And how'd that go for you?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Badly. Twice it was still the right call and I'd do it again."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"...Right."</font> Lynae rubbed her face. <font color=palegoldenrod>"Rule. Somebody tells her. There's a date on it and the date is real and it's written on something."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Name it."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"The day you lot come back through that gate. Whoever's still standing walks straight past the soup and up to that girl and tells her the whole thing, start to finish, including that I knew."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Done."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Write it down."</font>

Mei wrote it down on the back of the folded printout, in the dark, on the rail, and signed it, and Lynae watched the pen the whole way through.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You know that's a promise about a day neither of us can guarantee."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"That's the *point*, isn't it. That's why you're all like this."</font>

Mei put the pen away.

<font color=moccasin>"Come back and we'll find out."</font>

## X.

They talked at two in the morning in a kitchen with one lamp on, the way they always did, because the day had been long and neither of them had anywhere left to be.

<font color=moccasin>"Question."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"If the table had gone the other way."</font>

Iuno turned her cup a quarter-turn.

<font color=royalblue>"Ask it properly."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"If nine hands had stayed down. If Lupa had said no and Chisa had said no and Amy had said no and the whole room had looked at that letter and said *we've done ours.*"</font> Mei had her chin on her hand. <font color=moccasin>"What would you have done?"</font>

The lamp buzzed once and settled.

<font color=royalblue>"I'd have stayed."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Mm."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You don't believe me."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"No."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Neither do I."</font> Iuno set the cup down. <font color=royalblue>"I would like very much to have been the woman who would. I have been working toward her for eighteen months and I have got about sixty per cent of the way and I intend to keep going, and if you had asked me on that fifth morning I would have said *I'd have stayed* out loud with total conviction and I'd have been lying to us both and I'd have found out somewhere around Mingting."</font>

Mei nodded slowly.

<font color=royalblue>"Your turn."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Mine's worse."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I'd assumed."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'd have gone."</font> Mei said it to the tabletop. <font color=moccasin>"Alone. On the Thursday, early, on the bike, with the kitchen clean and the blanket folded and a note on the table saying something warm and completely useless."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"...Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's what the five days were for. That's the whole reason I asked how long honest takes."</font> She looked up. <font color=moccasin>"I wanted it to be theirs so badly that I built a machine to make it theirs, and I did that because I already knew what I was going to do if it wasn't. So the machine's real and the rules are real and every single one of them is load-bearing, and underneath it there's a woman who was going to go anyway."</font>

The vents turned over.

<font color=royalblue>"Yes,"</font> Iuno said.

<font color=moccasin>"That's all you've got?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You've just described building an honest procedure around a dishonest core, in full, to the one person alive who did that professionally for thirty years and put a *city* on top of it."</font> Iuno picked her cup back up. <font color=royalblue>"So: yes. And also that the procedure worked, and you're the one who told me about the core, unprompted, in a kitchen, at two in the morning, five days early."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's grading on a curve."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Everything is grading on a curve. The trick is knowing whose."</font>

Mei laughed into her hands.

They sat with the lamp.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"When you said you'd break the rule. At the table."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Was that true, or was that you buying credit for later?"</font>

Iuno looked at her for a long moment.

<font color=royalblue>"Both,"</font> she said.

<font color=moccasin>"...Okay."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You may hate that."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I don't."</font> Mei reached over and took the crystal hand, the one that catches the lamp and throws it around the room. <font color=moccasin>"You said it in front of everybody. Eighteen months ago you'd have said it to me at three in the morning on a balcony after it was too late to matter, and you'd have been kind about it, and I'd have thanked you."</font>

Iuno's thumb moved once against her palm.

*Tell her,* the High Priestess said.

*After Mingting.*

*You said before.*

*I said before we go. Mingting is before we get there, and Mingting has a river, and there is a specific bridge, and I have looked it up.*

*...Oh, darling. You've picked a* location.

*I have picked a location.*

*Thirty years of contempt for sentiment and you have picked a* location.

*Go to sleep.*

## XI.

The vote was taken again on the last morning, formally, because Iuno insisted that a thing agreed in the heat of a Wednesday be re-agreed in the cold of a Thursday, and because two people had been given four days in which they could have changed their minds at no cost, and neither of them had used it, and Iuno wanted that on paper.

Nine people stood in a kitchen at six in the morning with their coats on.

<font color=royalblue>"All those going."</font>

Seven hands.

<font color=royalblue>"All those staying."</font>

Two hands.

<font color=royalblue>"All those in favour."</font>

Nine.

<font color=indianred>"That's the same question three times."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It is three different questions and they have all been answered the same way, which is the outcome I have spent five days trying to make possible and about which I will now be insufferable for approximately a decade."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"She's crying."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I am filing."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"She's crying *and* filing."</font>

<font color=teal>"That's her whole thing, Rebecca, keep up."</font>

---

They went out at seven, on six bikes and one mech transport, with a woman of sixty-three standing at the dormitory steps beside a girl with half her ink, because Chiyo had come down for it and had brought a flask nobody had asked for.

Lupa's hand was on the mech's shin plating for a second on the way past, which nobody mentioned.

Cartethyia's bag had eight small heavy shapes in the bottom of it and a ninth wrapped separately in tissue, unfinished, which she had refused to explain to anyone.

Chisa had an hour of her mother's voice on something the size of her thumb, in her inside pocket, on the left.

Amy had a felt woman in a long coat with two gold seed beads for eyes, and a set of rules with her mother's signature on the bottom, and a mech with her own fourteen-year-old handwriting on the shoulder.

And Mei went last through the gate, which she had never once done in her life, because the arrangement now was that she goes last.
