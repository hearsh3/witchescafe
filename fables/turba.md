## ITEM 1 — ~~MEET HER AT THE GATE~~ ~~AT THE SHORE~~ WHEREVER SHE IS

*0552.*

The grip lasted a moment longer than the job required, and then it ended, and Yangyang stood in the shallows of the lake of Jinzhou in her boots with her patrol coat wet to the third button and nine prepared sentences in her mouth, all of which arrived at the door at once and jammed there like a crowd at a gate.

<font color=yellowgreen>"…You're soaked."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah. Ask anyone. It's my whole origin story."</font>

The morning went on happening. The gulls went on with it.

Somewhere behind Yangyang's sternum, a needle that had been swinging for years had stopped swinging, and the effect of this on her ability to construct a sentence was total.

*Nine,* she thought. *There were nine of them. I wrote them down.*

She had. She had written them down over a year at a night window and had gone over them the way you go over a route, and at least two of them were good, and one of them was so good that she had struck it out twice.

<font color=yellowgreen>"There's breakfast at the stair foot."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Good. Hungry."</font>

That was the whole of it. Off it went, up the bank, at a walk, in wet boots, with the sun coming over the eastern rank and the beacon lamps going out one by one behind them as a keeper worked down the wall with a pole.

Yangyang did a headcount out of pure trade reflex.

One.

She had been doing headcounts for six years. It is the first thing on the patrol card and it is done on entering, on halting, and on leaving, and she had done it at every gate, every lane end, every cellar mouth, every bridge, for six years, and she had never once in her life done a headcount of one and had it come out feeling like a full column.

Behind them, at the waterline, the air stood up off the sand and went along the bank, and out on the grass a long line lay down, water to path, like something walking that weighed nothing.

Yangyang looked back at it once.

The brim of her hat lifted, and set down, and the little stitched bird held its stitch.

<font color=moccasin>"Is it doing that a lot?"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"It has never done that at all."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Huh,"</font> said Mei, to the sky.

## ITEM 3 — THE DOUGH AT THE STAIR FOOT

*0631.*

The public stair comes down the crest wall in four flights and gives out on a landing wide enough to be a street, and there are two dough stalls on it, and one of them opens at five and the other opens at six and the argument about which of them is better has been running since before the flood.

The first stall was open. The second stall was raising its shutter.

The fishermen made room the way that stair has always made room, which is by nobody moving and there suddenly being room. Somebody's grandson was sent up the steps for a second stool and came back with a crate.

Mei sat down on the crate with a piece of fried dough in each hand, dripping, soaked to the shoulders, wringing lake water out of her own hem into the gutter, and made a small pleased noise that the entire stair heard and understood.

<font color=yellowgreen>"The oil is fresh at this hour."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You can taste it."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"By nine it has been through four batches and there is a—"</font> Yangyang stopped, and reached for a word she had drilled in a grammar book eleven months ago at a night window with the shutter open, and found it sitting there, filed, waiting, as though it had known. <font color=yellowgreen>"—a heaviness."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah. Heaviness. That's the word."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Which stall is this?"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"The first."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"And which one's better?"</font>

Yangyang looked at the second stall, which had its shutter up now and its oil coming to heat, and heard herself deploy sentence number five out of nine, in the wrong order, on the wrong day, at a stall she had never intended to be standing in front of.

<font color=yellowgreen>"The first stall opens earlier and the second stall is better."</font>

Mei thought about that with her mouth full.

<font color=moccasin>"So we've got somewhere to be tomorrow."</font>

The oil went on ticking in the pan. Down the landing, a man was arguing with his own net.

Yangyang looked at the lake and said, in the report voice, at normal volume, in the manner of a woman reading out a mooring depth:

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yes."</font>

---

They talked, for about an hour, about the following:

Oil temperature. The correct thickness of the dough. Whether the second stall's advantage is the oil or the hand. The price of both, which is the same and has been the same for nine years by a private agreement neither stall will admit to. The gulls. A particular gull. Whether the particular gull is the same gull as the one Yangyang has been seeing since spring, which Mei took a firm and entirely unfounded position on within four seconds and then defended for six minutes.

Also: the road.

<font color=yellowgreen>"How was it?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Long."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Kelvet to here is two hundred and eleven kilometres."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Nine days."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Mm."</font>

Mei tore a piece of dough in half and passed the larger half over without looking, which is a thing she does, and which nobody in the world has ever successfully declined.

<font color=moccasin>"You knew that off the top of your head."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"It is on the schedule card at the post."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Four thousand kilometres away."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"…The post road runs a courier twice a week."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Uh huh."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"And the courier stops at the second landing."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Uh huh."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"And I am generally at the second landing on those days."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Sure."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Because of the patrol pattern."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Very thorough,"</font> Mei agreed, gravely, and ate the dough, and let it lie, and Yangyang sat on a public stair with her ears going hot and discovered that she was, unexpectedly, having an extremely good time.

---

Somewhere around the second piece, Mei took the shell off her sternum and put it into Yangyang's hands, sideways, mid-sentence, the way she does everything.

It went across both palms like something asleep, and Yangyang put it to her ear because that is what you do, and the arm knows it before the head does.

A tide. Long, patient, shore after shore of it. Under the tide, wind. And at the very bottom, faint as a lamp four rooms away, a second tide in a different mother's rhythm — a sea out of a printing where the same house had stood on the same lane, and a woman with unhurried exact hands had wrapped this shell the way you dress a wound.

Yangyang lowered it. Her face performed the family craft. The craft failed.

She wiped her eyes with her sleeve in the practical way, once each side.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Ma's work,"</font> she said. <font color=yellowgreen>"Both of them."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Keep it a while. It knows the address."</font>

Yangyang held it in both hands for a moment.

Then she put it in the inside pocket of her patrol coat, which is the pocket the trade keeps for orders, and buttoned it, and looked out at the water, and asked what somebody in her position asks.

<font color=yellowgreen>"How many of you are there?"</font>

Mei stood up on the crate, faced the hillside, filled her lungs, and was extremely loud.

<font color=moccasin>"FOURTEEN."</font>

Down the whole landing, forty fishermen went on with their nets without breaking stride, because the stair has heard everything.

<font color=yellowgreen>"…Fourteen."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Six,"</font> said Mei, sitting back down. <font color=moccasin>"Plus me. It's a whole thing, I'll explain it later, there's a rule."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"There is a rule about how many of you there are."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"It's about *numbers.* Different rule."</font> Mei licked oil off her thumb. <font color=moccasin>"You'll get it."</font>

## ITEM 7 — TELL CHIXIA

*0744.*

She did not get to tell Chixia.

The towers had beaten eight along the wall for a column of one at full morning, which the whole barracks heard and none of the barracks could account for; a runner had come off the yamen stair at speed with an order signed at the Magistrate's own desk; a keeper had logged *wind, all watches* and countersigned it; and somewhere in the middle of all that a fisherman's boy had told his aunt, who told the noodle window, which told the muster yard, which is faster than any of the official channels and has been for eighty years.

So what arrived at the stair foot was Chixia, mid-sentence, off the night muster, with a paper bag already going translucent with oil.

*"—because if you're buying strangers breakfast at your age I want it minuted, Yangyang, I want a formal—"*

She stopped.

She took in the stair.

A wolf watching the noodle window with predatory scholarship. A knight of small size holding her own hand out of habit. A bard tuning to the gulls. A woman with an enormous folded blade across her back, sitting perfectly still with her chin up. A one-armed foreigner pouring tea, on a flat stone, with the handle turned guest-side out. A girl of about nineteen with her boots up on a bollard, conducting some kind of interview with a fisherman by pure gesture. And, sitting on a crate in the middle of it, soaked to the shoulders, laughing at something the air had just done to a menu board —

*"…Yangyang."*

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yes."</font>

*"Yangyang."*

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yes."</font>

*"There's* seven *of them."*

<font color=yellowgreen>"I am aware."</font>

*"You didn't say* seven."

<font color=yellowgreen>"I have not said anything at all yet. It is not eight in the morning."</font>

Chixia looked at the stair for another second and a half.

Then she made a noise like a kettle achieving its purpose, and crossed the landing in four strides, and stuck the paper bag directly into the nearest available hands, which belonged to Cartethyia.

*"Auntie Panhua did the crispy thing again. I got extra."*

<font color=skyblue>"Oh! What—"</font> Cartethyia looked in the bag. <font color=skyblue>"OH."</font>

The bag went from Cartethyia to Amy, who took two and reported the contents on general principle. From Amy to Lupa, who took one, ate it in a manner that ended all conversation within two metres, and said something short in Septimontian that Ciaccona declined to translate and Cartethyia translated anyway at approximately forty percent accuracy and a hundred and ten percent enthusiasm. From Lupa to Chisa, who took one, turned it over once by feel, and said <font color=coral>"Rice flour and something,"</font> and ate it, and was correct about the something.

The bag came back empty in under a minute.

Chixia looked at the empty bag with an expression of naked, undisguised joy.

*"I have* extra," she announced, to the entire stair, holding up nothing. *"I am a* generous *person."*

<font color=skyblue>"She's got extra! And she wants everybody to know she's generous!"</font>

<font color=indianred>"Tell her the extra's gone."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I'm not telling her that."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Tell her I said thank you."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"…You want me to say *thank you.*"</font>

<font color=indianred>"I want you to say it fast and then change the subject."</font>

---

Introductions took eleven minutes and Yangyang lost track twice.

She had a system. The trade gives you one: face, height, gait, weapon, position in the line. She deployed it, correctly, in order, on seven people who would not stay in the order.

Ciaccona introduced herself and three other people, two of whom immediately contradicted the introductions.

Cartethyia introduced herself twice, the second time at three and a half metres, which caused two fishermen to sit down and one gull to leave the province, and then apologised, and then did it once more because a child on the stair had asked nicely.

Amy stuck out her hand and said <font color=mistyrose>"You're the *letter,*"</font> and Yangyang said, <font color=yellowgreen>"I posted several. One learned to fly,"</font> and Amy said <font color=mistyrose>"Okay, I like her"</font> to the sky, and something in the arrangement of the day shifted onto a footing Yangyang would spend the rest of it trying to identify.

Lupa shook her hand, looked at her for two seconds longer than the transaction required, said <font color=indianred>"Good coat,"</font> and went back to the noodle window.

Chisa did not shake her hand. Chisa listened to her for a moment — to her breathing, to the specific weight of her boots on wet stone, to the small sound her sword harness makes when she turns her head — and then said, <font color=coral>"You've been awake since yesterday afternoon,"</font> in a level voice, without any particular emphasis, and turned back to the water.

Yangyang stood there.

<font color=yellowgreen>"…That is correct."</font>

<font color=coral>"Mm."</font>

Six.

She counted again. Seven.

She counted again, because the bard had moved, and got six, and then found the bard behind her, and got seven.

<font color=royalblue>"They do that,"</font> said Iuno, at her elbow, with a cup of tea in her hand and the handle turned outward. <font color=royalblue>"I have been counting them for two years. The number is stable and the process is a nightmare. It helps to count boots."</font>

---

Baizhi came last, with the incident ledger under her arm.

She had walked down from the clinic at a pace that was, for her, a run, and she arrived at the rail at the top of the last flight and stopped there, and did not come down for some while.

From the rail you could see: the whole stair, the party on it, the light coming up off forty kilometres of water, and, at the head of the dam road, an apricot-and-tooth-yellow truck at a walking pace with a raven riding the air over the cab.

Baizhi opened the ledger.

She had a year of it in there. A Tacet Discord's claw turned aside indoors. A rope bridge that held through a squall because the wind leaned on the knot. An Outrider shoved clear of a falling beam by air that arrived with intent. A cliff face that came down on Third Squad and opened a corridor in itself and held it open, and held it, and held it, until the last man cleared it.

Cause: *weather.*

Underlined, in her own hand, one hundred and four times.

She looked down at the stair.

Then she took out the second ink, and struck the word through, and wrote one character above it, and closed the book on it, and went down the steps to be introduced to seven foreigners and a wind.

---

She got to Yangyang last, on purpose.

*"Sixteen questions,"* she said.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Now?"</font>

*"Over the course of the day. I will space them out and I will make several of them sound like small talk, and you will notice, and you will let me, because you have owed me a set of answers since the equinox and we both know the sum."* Baizhi adjusted her glasses. *"Also, you are on your feet at the end of a night shift with your coat wet to the third button and you have eaten nothing."*

<font color=yellowgreen>"I have eaten a piece of dough."</font>

*"A piece."*

<font color=yellowgreen>"…Two."</font>

*"Sergeant Mo has struck you off the roster for two days,"* said Baizhi. *"He did it at six twenty. Nobody asked him. He wrote* leave, compassionate *in the column and then crossed out* compassionate *and left the box half empty, which is the most emotional document that man has produced in nineteen years, and the clerk has photographed it."*

Yangyang stood very still on a public stair in the sun.

<font color=yellowgreen>"…What did he put instead?"</font>

*"Nothing. He left it blank."* Baizhi capped the ink. *"That is question one. You are not obliged to answer it and you have anyway. Go and sit down."*

## NOT ON THE LIST — A POST OF GRANITE

*1102.*

There is a bollard at the foot of the second terrace where the road becomes a stair.

The *Lucky Bird* discovered it at eleven o'clock in front of about four hundred people.

Yangyang, who had been ordered to sit down and had complied for eleven minutes, watched the whole thing from the rail at the top of the flight with her hat pushed back, and afterward gave the following account to nobody:

<font color=yellowgreen>"They stopped. There was a dispute in the cab about a gap of about four centimetres. The one with the scissors put the handbrake on and folded her arms, which settled it inside four seconds with nobody spending a word, and which was instructive. Then Chixia arrived and authorised something she has no authority whatever to authorise, extremely loudly, in front of witnesses, and told them to ignore a sign, and led them round the harbor road at a walking pace with her arms full of other people's fruit, and went the whole way without looking back once to check that they were following."</font>

Then Yangyang went and sat back down, as ordered.

She lasted nine minutes.

## ITEM 12 — THE HOIST

*1240.*

The goods hoist at the third terrace is a timber-and-iron cage on a counterweight that runs up the rock face in forty-one seconds, and it takes eleven people or two tonnes, and it is worked by a man in a booth who has been working it since he was nineteen and who charges nothing because the ward pays him.

It is the only thing in Jinzhou that is better than Mingting's.

Yangyang had put it at number twelve on a list at a night window with a particular quiet ferocity, and had never mentioned it to Chixia or to Baizhi or to anybody, because it is a goods hoist and she is a serving officer of the Mingting registry and there are limits.

Cartethyia got in first and put both hands flat on the cage wall.

<font color=skyblue>"It's a *room* that *goes up.*"</font>

<font color=teal>"It's a lift."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It's a room that goes UP, Cia—"</font>

<font color=teal>"They have them in Ragunna. There's one in the opera house. You've *been* in it."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"That one had *carpet.* This one's got the whole *rock* going past—"</font>

The cage went up.

Forty-one seconds of wet black rock sliding down past the bars, and then the wall of it dropped away all at once and the whole bowl opened underneath — forty kilometres of water with the plum-rain cloud lying along the far shore in a grey band, the willows going round in two shades, the dam above with its crest wall and its towers, the terraces stepping down in nine flights of grey tile and yellow lamp-iron, and the entire lakefront doing its ordinary Tuesday underneath.

Nobody said anything for the last eleven seconds.

Then the cage stopped and clanged, and the man in the booth said *"Mind the lip,"* and ten people got out onto the fourth terrace, and Cartethyia turned round immediately and asked whether they could do it again.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yes."</font>

<font color=teal>"Absolutely not."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"…It is free."</font>

<font color=teal>"Outrider, I have known this girl for a year and a half and I am telling you, in confidence, as a professional: do not give her a *free* one."</font>

They did it four times.

## ITEM 9 — ICE

*1305.*

Here is what a city does to six people and a wolf who have spent twenty days in a truck.

**Lupa** stopped inside the first eleven metres of the fourth terrace market and stood there with her head back and her eyes shut and her ears going round, one at a time, and did not move for ninety seconds while four hundred people went past her.

<font color=indianred>"Right,"</font> she said at last. <font color=indianred>"Nine hundred out on the road. There's four thousand in here."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Is that good?"</font>

<font color=indianred>"There's a thing under the fish that isn't fish."</font> Her ears pinned and came back. <font color=indianred>"It's *vinegar* and it's *old* and it's in a jar behind that counter and it's been there since before anybody standing here was born, and I want the word for it, and then I want to buy some."</font>

**Ciaccona** listened to the market for four minutes with her head on one side and then made a small sound of pure professional satisfaction.

<font color=teal>"D minor."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"The *market's* in D minor?"</font>

<font color=teal>"The city is. The tone's coming off the dam — there's a note under everything here, it's the water going through the works, and it's flat and it's about a D, and every single person in this valley has been tuning to it since they were born and none of them know."</font> She started walking again. <font color=teal>"The street-criers are all sitting on the fifth. It's why they sound good. They're not good."</font>

**Chisa** stopped at the mouth of the covered lane and stood with one hand on the post.

She has not been in a city of a hundred and ten thousand since Honami, and she reads people by voice, gait, breath, and the weight of a footstep, and the covered lane on the fourth terrace at one in the afternoon delivers about nine hundred of those a minute.

<font color=coral>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Yeah?"</font>

<font color=coral>"Hand."</font>

Amy gave her a hand, and did not make a single joke about it, then or afterward, which is one of about four things Chisa has ever asked anybody for.

<font color=coral>"It's not bad,"</font> Chisa said, after a while, walking. <font color=coral>"It's a lot. It's like standing in a river." </font> A pause. <font color=coral>"On the truck I had six. I knew all six by the third day. I could tell you who was awake from the far end of the box." </font> Another pause. <font color=coral>"I'd got used to knowing."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You want to go back and sit in the truck?"</font>

<font color=coral>"In about an hour."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Okay."</font>

**Iuno** stopped in front of the public clock on the fourth terrace and looked at it for rather a long time.

It is a plain iron-cased clock on a bracket over the ward office door, and it is wound on Mondays, and it agrees with the clock on the second terrace and the one at the yamen and the one in the works office and the one on the wall of the muster yard, because a man goes round with a watch, and this is his entire job, and he has done it for eleven years.

*Darling,* said the High Priestess, after some time, *it is a clock.*

*I have run a schedule across two hundred and eleven kilometres for nine days in a country where every village keeps its own noon, and every single figure I wrote down was a lie by up to forty minutes, and I made my peace with that, and I stopped writing times in the log after Shimen.*

*Yes.*

*Every clock in this city says the same thing at the same moment,* said Iuno, *because a man walks round with a watch.*

*…You are going to cry about a clock.*

*I am going to buy that man a drink,* said Iuno, *and I am going to find out his name, and I am going to write it in the log, and then I am going to have a holiday, which I am told is a thing that people do.*

**Amy** found a shop that sold radio parts and stood in the doorway of it in the manner of a woman entering a temple, and came out eleven minutes later with a coil of wire, four crystal diodes, a soldering iron with a frayed cord, and the name and shift pattern of the man who runs the relay on the dam face.

**Mei** bought a hat, wore it for nine minutes, gave it to a child, and could not afterward explain any part of this sequence.

---

**Cartethyia** went into a shop with printed prices on the goods and had a crisis.

It was a dry-goods shop on the fourth terrace with a glass front and electric light and small paper tags tied to everything, and the tags had numbers on them, and the numbers were the price.

She picked up a jar of preserved plums. She looked at the tag. She put the jar down. She picked it up again.

<font color=skyblue>"There's a *number* on it."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"That is the price."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Yes, but what's the—"</font> She turned the tag over. The back of the tag was blank. <font color=skyblue>"What's the *actual*—"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"That is the actual price."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"For everyone?"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"For everyone."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"For *me?*"</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"Yes."</font>

Cartethyia stood in the middle of a dry-goods shop in the city of Jinzhou holding a jar of preserved plums with both hands.

She has been overcharged in nine languages across four seas. She has been cheated by fruit vendors on two continents with total consistency and considerable good humour and has come to regard the transaction as a kind of tax on being small and delighted. She has, on this crossing alone, paid twelve for lemon cakes worth twelve, and fourteen for a chicken worth fourteen, and recorded both in a ledger with a straight face.

<font color=skyblue>"…I don't know who I am here,"</font> she said.

<font color=yellowgreen>"You are a customer."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I've *never* been a customer."</font>

<font color=teal>"Buy the plums."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"But what do I *do?*"</font>

<font color=teal>"You take them to the counter and you give the woman that number and she gives you the plums."</font> Ciaccona was leaning on a shelf with her arms folded and the specific expression of a person watching a religious conversion. <font color=teal>"It's going to feel awful. Do it anyway."</font>

Cartethyia took the plums to the counter and gave the woman the number.

The woman gave her the plums.

She came out into the lane and stood in the rain and looked at the jar.

<font color=skyblue>"That took four seconds."</font>

<font color=teal>"It did."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I once spent forty minutes on a *melon.*"</font>

<font color=teal>"I was there. It was one of the great afternoons of my life."</font>

---

At half past one Chixia detoured the entire party six streets out of their way to the harbor ward tax office in order to show them her nemesis.

They arrived. Ten of them stood in the courtyard entrance in a semicircle, in the rain, in silence.

The goose was asleep.

*"…It's asleep."*

<font color=skyblue>"It's asleep!"</font>

*"It is* never *asleep. In six years I have never—"* Chixia advanced two paces. The goose slept. She advanced two more. *"It's* doing *this."*

<font color=indianred>"It's a bird on a warm stone in the rain."</font>

*"It is doing this ON PURPOSE."*

<font color=coral>"She's right."</font>

Chixia told the story of the goose at ten past ten, standing up, in the lane, with her hands wide, to nine people and three neighbours and a passing dog.

It ran six minutes. It had four acts. It ended on the east approach and the sesame bun, which Chixia had once told nobody for two days and told a lane full of foreigners inside sixteen hours, and Baizhi turned her head very slightly and said nothing whatsoever.

The lane went up.

And Yangyang, standing at the back with her shoulder on the doorpost, watched her friend of six years perform for a crowd that had known her for one day and was giving her, without reservation, the entire room — and felt something she had a lot of trouble naming and eventually settled, privately, on *gladness*, and left it at that.

Chixia caught her eye across the lane, mid-sentence, mid-gesture, without breaking the story.

She raised her cup about four centimetres.

That was the whole of it, and it was said, and Yangyang raised hers back.

Yangyang went home at half past ten.

She let herself into her own room in the lane above the second bridge, put the lamp on, hung her coat, and stood there.

The shelf by the door was clear.

It had been clear for a year. She had cleared it in the fourth month of the previous year in about ninety seconds, without deciding to, on an evening when she had come in and looked at the room and found it wrong, and had taken the four things off that shelf and put them somewhere else and stood back and looked at the empty shelf and gone to bed.

On the shelf now: nothing.

Under the bed, in a paper wrapper, tied: one guest quilt, cotton, good, bought new, aired at the start of every season and folded back the same evening, four times, never once used.

Yangyang sat down on the edge of her bed with her boots still on.

She had been awake for thirty-five hours. She had met a person at a shore. She had bought that person breakfast at the wrong stall. She had been in a lift four times, lost an argument about willows, been photographed by a clerk, been given a callsign on a bench in a bathhouse, and been asked, by her oldest friend at a hatch, in front of everybody, whether she was going to be like this all week.

Her room was extremely quiet.

She lay down on top of the covers, fully dressed, and shut her eyes, and did not sleep at all.

At ten past two in the morning she got up, took the quilt out from under the bed, and left the house.
