# The Room Upstairs

Mei opened the door before Iuno reached it.

“I heard the stair. Come in; I've managed to clear somewhere for you to sit.”

She had moved everything from the chair onto the bed. A book lay open beneath a folded towel. Beside it, two socks of different lengths rested on a map of the south road. Iuno looked at the chair, then at the space Mei had occupied on the coverlet.

Mei took the cup from her. With that hand free, Iuno caught the edge of her coat and began working it off her shoulder. Mei helped it over the dressing, hung it behind the door, and came back close enough to put a hand against her cheek.

Iuno made a small sound.

Mei's thumb stopped beside her mouth. Then her other arm came round Iuno's waist, and Iuno bent into her, pressing her face into the loose hair above her ear.

For several breaths, getting up the stairs had been worth it.

Mei eased them onto the bed. The book slid against her hip; she pushed it toward the pillows and kept her arm where it was. Iuno could feel the heel of Mei's hand through her shirt. It lay a little above the torn buttonhole.

“Baizhi changed the dressing,” Mei said.

“The lower layer had caught. She freed it.”

“You look sore. Lean forward a little and I'll move this.”

Mei pulled the folded towel out from under Iuno's thigh, dropped it onto the chair, and settled beside her again. The room smelled of tea and the soap Mrs Ren bought in long bars. From below came Chixia's voice, raised over a passing cart.

Iuno closed her eyes.

“I told them about this morning.”

Mei waited with her hand at Iuno's waist.

“Your hand in my shirt. How you kept catching it when I tried to get up. I told her I wanted more mornings like that.”

Her voice had begun to steady. She disliked how quickly it happened here.

“She pulled the cloth. Here.” Iuno touched the fold beneath Mei's hand. “I had to brace myself against the table. Yangyang reached into the current, and it caught her wrist.”

Mei lifted her head.

“Baizhi was looking at it when I left. She was able to move her fingers.”

Mei's eyes went to Iuno's shirt. She found the broken stitch and followed it to the reddened skin where the hem had pulled aside.

“Let me see your hand as well.”

Iuno gave it to her. Mei turned it palm upward, pressing lightly along the fingers, then turned it back. The pale lines over the joints were fading.

“That was earlier,” Iuno said. “I reached for her bowl.”

Mei held the hand between both of hers. Her thumbs moved over the knuckles with the same care she used when somebody came back from a patrol with a story they were still trying to tell.

“I can understand why she wanted to hurt you,” she said.

The thumbs kept moving.

Iuno watched them.

“So can I. I held on to my shirt and moved the chair back. Yangyang had asked me to move if it happened again.”

Mei looked at her face then. Iuno's eyes had filled, and a tear had reached the corner of her mouth before she noticed it.

Mei wiped it away.

“What was she meant to do when you told her about us waking up?”

“I wanted her to understand what I was asking to keep.”

The answer sounded worse beside the bed. Iuno heard every comfortable object around it: the kettle within reach, the folded bedding, Mei taking another breath before she spoke.

“She already knows,” Mei said. “She brought us here. She watches us eat.”

Iuno bent her fingers against Mei's palm.

“Yangyang heard you laughing. She told me that. She was trying to take her shirt off while the Wind rested in it. She had put the chair close to the stove.”

Mei remembered the laugh. Iuno had finally persuaded her to surrender the shirt, and Mei had caught the loose hem between two toes. A ridiculous little victory. Iuno had called her a menace to civilisation and sat back down on the bed.

“She put the sleeve against her face,” Iuno said. “It was still cold.”

Mei's hands stopped.

Below them, somebody pushed a chair across the boards. The sound travelled through the bed frame.

“I should go down,” Mei said. “Yangyang has been sitting there all day. I could bring her something, or stay while she gets dressed properly. She might want to go out for a little while.”

She shifted toward the edge of the bed. Iuno's hand closed around her wrist.

Mei looked down at it.

The grip opened. Iuno laid her hand flat on the coverlet.

“I would like you to stay with me for a few minutes.”

Mei sat where she was, one foot already beneath her.

“I came upstairs because I wanted you to hold me,” Iuno said. “I knew you would see my face and reach for me. I thought about it while Yangyang was trying to tie the cloth around their shells.”

She swallowed. The room had become difficult to look at.

“I wanted to be up here so badly that I began watching the door.”

Mei drew her foot back onto the floor. Her knee stayed turned toward the doorway.

“I can sit for a while. Let me be worried about her, Iuiu. Give me room to say it.”

Iuno looked at the place her hand had held.

“I caught you before I asked. I'm sorry.”

The cup stood on the windowsill where Mei had put it. A thread of steam still rose from it. Iuno followed that small movement until her breathing had slowed enough to continue.

“I want to be able to tell you that something hurt me. Today, the person who hurt me is someone I have harmed so badly that I can spend the rest of my life learning the size of it. I wanted your hand on my face anyway.”

Mei rubbed her own wrist once, where the grip had been.

“I wanted to put it there.”

Iuno's chin moved. She managed to keep her mouth steady.

“Then I heard what happened downstairs,” Mei continued, “and I started wondering what it would feel like to hear us through the ceiling.”

They both looked toward the floor.

***

Mei got up to fetch the tea.

She carried it back by the handle, then remained standing beside the bed with the cup in her hand. Iuno watched her turn it slowly, looking for something in the pattern along its rim.

“At the meal,” Mei said, “when I asked about tomorrow, I knew how tired she was. I thought I was giving her something to look forward to.”

“Yangyang had asked her to keep the word for today,” Iuno said. “She told me downstairs. Then you asked, and the Wind wanted to answer you.”

Mei looked toward the door.

“I could ask Baizhi what warmth actually does for her. There might be something we could change in the room. The stove is awkwardly placed. And when we get to Mengzhou, whoever took the words might know how to—”

Mei stopped. Her thumb had reached the handle again.

At Norfall, Yangyang had gathered the Wind to her chest with both arms. Mei remembered the care of it, the way her hands had stayed cupped around the place a body should have been.

She sat down and gave Iuno the cup.

“I keep thinking of things I can put my hands on.”

Iuno held the tea against her thigh.

“Yangyang spent twenty minutes getting her arm out of a sleeve. She told us. Then she told the Wind how angry she gets with her, because she thinks about her every time she gets dressed.”

Mei looked at her own cuff. There was a little dried soap at the edge; she had washed at the basin after breakfast and pulled it down too soon.

“She must have been frightened to say that.”

“Her hand shook while she put the shells away. The Wind went to help her elbow, and she stopped moving.”

Iuno drank. The tea had cooled enough for a full mouthful.

Mei knew that pause at an elbow. She had watched it hundreds of times before she understood whose attention it was. Yangyang correcting a strap, reaching for a high shelf, raising her sword. A small adjustment arriving where it was needed.

She pressed her thumb into the dried soap until it crumbled.

“She should be able to be angry in her own room.”

“I agree. I would also like to keep a room with you.”

Mei turned toward her.

“Why are you saying it as if they're trying to take that from us?”

Iuno's fingers tightened on the cup.

*Careful, darling. You have begun assigning places again.*

Mei rose before Iuno could answer. She took the socks off the map and put them on the towel. Then she moved the map too, clearing another patch of bed that they could already have sat on.

“I have watched her follow Chixia across that room four times today,” Mei said. “Every time the crutch squeaks, she goes. Chixia has Baizhi beside her. She has all of us. And the Wind still has to go and see.”

Iuno set the cup on the floor between her feet.

“I was frightened when you stood up. I started arguing for the bed before you had said anything about leaving it.”

“I wanted to take Yangyang downstairs for something to eat.”

Mei held the folded map against her stomach. The edge bent beneath her fingers.

“I can be angry with you and still be coming back upstairs, Iuiu. You keep making me tell you where I'm going while I'm trying to say what happened to her.”

Iuno bowed her head.

Mei put the map down. She leaned both hands on the chair back and looked toward the window, where a strip of laundry had caught on the sill below theirs.

“In her city, the streets flooded up to the windows. She took Chixia's cap out of that water and carried it for a year. And I was somewhere else, eating eggs with you.”

“You were where I had put you.”

“I could have heard something. A letter could have reached me. Somebody must have travelled west.”

“The letter that reached you came tied to a kite. She carried it across a sealed sky.”

Mei pushed her weight into her hands. The chair gave a small complaint at one joint.

“I was happy there.”

“I know. I was with you.”

“That's the part I keep coming back to. I remember wanting breakfast. I remember making plans for the afternoon.”

Iuno looked at the back of her head, the loose hair caught under the collar. She had put her fingers there that morning to free it. Mei had leaned back into her hand.

“When I reached into the basin, you were lying on wet stones,” Iuno said. “You had yet to open your eyes. I chose where you would wake. Later, when you began making plans, I knew which ones would bring you toward the place I needed you to stand.”

Mei let go of the chair.

“If I had woken on the other shore, I would have wanted to help her.”

“She was trying to get you there.”

The cloth on the sill lifted and dropped. Mei watched it settle against the stone.

“I keep wanting to find the moment I should have done something.”

Iuno's hand moved toward her, then came to rest on the bed.

“My hand was in the water, Mei. I had the information. I used it.”

Mei turned around. Her eyes were wet, and her face had the bewildered, open look Iuno remembered from the first days, when a familiar word could make her stop in the middle of a street because she knew its meaning and had lost the place where she learned it.

“I still have to live in the morning that came after.”

Iuno made room beside her.

Mei sat.

***

They listened to Mrs Ren receiving another delivery. The porter had found the household that wanted the onions. Apparently there had been a dispute about the number of sacks.

Mei wiped her face on the heel of her hand. Iuno reached for the towel, found it under the socks, and gave it to her. Mei looked at what had come with it.

“Those are both mine,” she said. “I thought I'd solved something when I found a pair.”

Iuno took the socks and put them on the chair.

Mei laughed once into the towel. It caught painfully in her throat. She held the cloth against her mouth until the urge passed.

“I want to help everyone,” she said. “I came home thinking I could spend a few days doing the little things. Bring Chixia her lunch. Take the heavy bucket away from Mrs Ren before she hurts her back. Sit with you while the dressing dries.”

She lowered the towel.

“Yangyang has her whole arm through that woman's life every morning. I keep turning up with a bowl.”

Iuno looked down at her cup between her feet.

“She may want the bowl. You can ask her.”

“And then I'll come back here.”

Mei spread the towel over her knees. One corner had gone dark where she had pressed it to her eyes.

“At Norfall I could stand in the road. I could see the next man coming and hold the light where he needed it. I counted them with you. When the last one passed, there was a place to put my feet.”

Her heel moved against the floorboard.

“She's downstairs. She came all the way home.”

Iuno had counted one thousand nine hundred and seven. She could still feel where the numbers had worn at her voice, and the touch of Mei's hand when the counting was done. On the road south she had fallen asleep against Mei's shoulder, waking whenever the wagon found a rut.

She had wanted the ruts farther apart.

“I've been wondering how much of our life she can bear to be near,” Iuno said. “And whether I will start listening at doors before I laugh.”

Mei's fingers closed on the towel.

“I want to hear you laugh.”

“I want you to have reasons to. I want to wake you badly because the tea is ready, and have you steal my shirt, and quarrel about where you left the book I was reading. I want years of being inconvenienced by you.”

Her mouth shook at the last sentence. She pressed her hand against the mattress until it passed.

“I am frightened of how quickly you begin giving your part away. You were standing here a moment ago, trying to become responsible for a choice I made while you slept.”

Mei looked at the torn buttonhole.

“You keep asking me to want this while I'm looking at what it cost her.”

Iuno could feel the loose thread against her stomach. She had become aware of it with every breath.

“I wanted it downstairs,” she said. “She was right in front of me, and I wanted to come up here and find you. I held on when she pulled. I am going to remember holding on.”

Mei put the towel aside. Her hand rose toward Iuno's face, then stopped at the height of her shoulder.

Iuno watched it stay there.

“On the cliff,” Mei said, “I told you I would have helped if you had asked.”

The hand came down onto the bed between them.

“I've said it so many times in my head. Usually when I can see you're hurting.”

Iuno felt the urge to take that hand. She knew the next part by heart: Mei coming closer, the old sentence opening a way through what she had done. She could already feel the relief of being understood.

Mei looked at her.

“You taught me where to put my feet. When we landed, I'd turn around to see if you'd watched. I liked it when you were pleased with me.”

Iuno's throat tightened.

“At the balcony, sometimes I stayed after the tea was cold because I thought you might come back. I was learning which sounds were yours.”

Her gaze moved toward the door. Iuno had made those sounds just minutes earlier: the fourth stair, a heel on the landing, the small movement of a cup against its handle.

“I was happy then too,” Mei said. “You were making me ready to die for you, and I was happy to see you.”

Iuno sat very still.

“I'm still trying to forgive you for that.”

The towel slid off Mei's knee. It lay partly on Iuno's foot, and for a while they both watched it.

“I remember when you began looking back,” Iuno said. “It made teaching you easier. You wanted another try whenever I stayed to watch.”

Mei's face changed. Iuno had seen her brace for pain before, the small settling of her jaw as the body prepared to let something through.

*Let her hear it, darling. You came all this way to stop keeping it in your mouth.*

“I used how much you trusted me,” Iuno said. “I was fond of you. I enjoyed the afternoons. And I kept bringing you closer to the day I had chosen.”

Mei drew her hand back across the coverlet. The knuckles brushed Iuno's leg as they passed.

“Now there are afternoons I can remember with you that she spent trying to reach me.”

Iuno's eyes closed.

The room remained around her. Warm air at her face, the bed yielding beneath her weight, Mei close enough to touch with the hand she still possessed.

“When you told her about my hand in your shirt,” Mei said, “you gave her one more.”

Iuno bent forward. Her breath caught at the dressing, and she held herself there, working carefully around the pain until she could straighten.

Mei's hand went to her back.

Iuno began crying against that hand.

***

Mei stayed beside her while the sounds came unevenly, sometimes so small that Iuno could hear Mrs Ren's voice through them. Once she tried to speak, swallowed air, and had to start breathing again. Mei moved the hair away from her mouth.

“Take your time. I'm sitting here.”

Iuno rested her forehead against Mei's shoulder. Her living hand lay open on her own thigh. Mei kept rubbing her back, above the place where the bandage crossed it.

It helped. Iuno could feel it helping, and the shame of that went through her so sharply that her fingers curled against her leg.

She reached up and caught Mei's hand gently, bringing it down between them.

“I told Yangyang I kept wanting you to make this easier for me.”

Mei looked at their hands.

“I was trying.”

“I could feel myself waiting for you to say something kind about what I told you. Even after the balcony. Even just now.”

Mei drew a long breath through her nose. Her eyes were swollen at the edges.

“I was about to tell you that I know you love me.”

Iuno's fingers tightened. Then she let them loosen again.

Mei looked at the shirt. At the place her sleeping hand had held, and the place another woman had pulled hard enough to tear it.

“I want you close,” she said. “I'm angry when I touch you. Then you cry and I want to help, and while I'm doing it I remember something else that makes me angry.”

She shifted on the mattress. Her shoulder had begun to ache from holding it high enough to spare Iuno's dressing.

“And now my arm has gone to sleep.”

Iuno moved at once. Mei shook her hand out, wincing as the feeling returned, and rubbed the inside of her elbow.

They watched her fingers open and close.

Mei's hand slowed.

After a while she laid it on her knee. Iuno could see the pulse at her wrist.

“I'd like to go downstairs before supper,” Mei said. “I'll ask Yangyang if she wants company. I can carry the bowls up if she'd rather eat in her room.”

Iuno nodded, then bent to retrieve the towel from her foot. It took a little manoeuvring with the bandage. Mei waited until she had it.

“I want to come back here afterward,” Mei continued. “I expect I'll still be upset with you.”

Iuno folded the towel along its damp edge.

“I would like you to come back. You can tell me how it was.”

Mei looked toward the door again.

“She might be pleased to see me. I've been thinking about that too. She might come right up to my face, the way she does when I've been away.”

Her mouth trembled.

“I keep hoping she will.”

Iuno held the folded towel on her lap. Mei wiped beneath one eye with her thumb, and Iuno offered her the dry corner.

Downstairs, Chixia called to Baizhi. A moment later her voice came again, closer to the passage, explaining something about the cap. Mei listened until she could hear Baizhi answering.

She stayed on the bed.

Iuno reached for the cup and took a drink. There was very little tea left. She held it out, and Mei finished it, turning the handle toward Iuno before giving it back.

The light had reached the far bedpost. Soon it would leave the room. There was a lamp on the chair beneath the map, and oil for it in Mrs Ren's cupboard; Iuno knew which shelf. She had asked that morning because Mei liked to read after supper, sprawling across both pillows until Iuno wanted one.

Mei leaned against her living shoulder. Iuno could feel her working out where to settle, how much weight the sore side would take. At last she drew her feet up onto the bed and let her cheek rest against Iuno's collar.

“I'd like another few minutes,” she said.

Iuno put the cup on the floor. Her hand came up into Mei's hair, and Mei's breath shook against her neck.

“Stay here with me, then.”

They sat while the light moved off the bedpost. Twice Mei lifted her head at a sound from below. The second time she stayed upright for several breaths, listening, before lowering it again.

Her hand had settled at Iuno's waist. A fingertip caught the broken stitch, and she lifted it free with care. Then her palm lay over the gathered cloth.

Under it, Iuno's breathing began to ease.
