# Xuanfang Arc — Yangyang Part

## Narrative Synopsis

### The Terrace Goes West

Xuanfang Hold has survived, but survival has not restored the old order. Its stone terraces can move through the mountains; its rebuilt channels carry boats between new walls; its ministries, markets, and academy courts have reopened beneath a sky still burdened by old machinery. Yet Sentinel Hsin has withdrawn behind the sealed doors of Fogveil Pagoda, the Astrolabe Core turns above an empty office, and the Hold’s defenders keep watch over a peace whose foundations they no longer trust. The displaced and wounded crowd its storerooms. Roofs wait under new tarpaulins, the gorge road remains uncertain, and every household keeps a place ready for someone who may arrive late.

Yangyang enters this uneasy peace changed in body and in place. She is no longer only the quiet Jinzhou Outrider who habitually makes herself useful before anyone can ask whether she is hurt. She has crossed the dark as a Xuanling bird, carried her shijie home through the space between worlds, and returned with blue-white hair, a living collar of cerulean plumage, and three cracked ribs. Her shijie is another Yangyang from a drowned, unrealized Jinzhou: a woman who surrendered flesh and most of her name to become the Guest Wind, crossed realities carrying her mother’s shell and an address for Mei, and spent years arriving too early or too late to complete the simple errand that had consumed her existence. Once Mei finally woke on the right shore and gave the Wind a name that could hold, the living Yangyang inherited not merely her memory, but a sisterly lineage of watchfulness, lateness, and impossible return. Now that shijie lives beyond the winter dark with an older counterpart of Suisui, Yangyang keeps a list of ordinary things to tell her at the solstice: what food was served, who complained about the weather, which roof leaked, and how the people who survived used the morning she bought them.

The household around Yangyang is equally battered and equally determined to behave as if living together is work worth doing. Mei, the golden-eyed Arbiter who once wandered through centuries and now remembers little before her latest awakening, is healing from a wound through her abdomen. Iuno has lost her left arm and much of the prophetic certainty that defined her thirty years as Septimont’s High Priestess, but carries a velvet ring box cut from her own shoulder crystal and waits for a Tuesday on which to ask Mei to marry her. Amy, Mei’s daughter, alternates between reckless mechanical brilliance and a child’s vigilance for the punishment she expects to follow every good day. Lupa feeds everyone, threatens anyone who ignores a bandage, and hides the old arena hunger that can turn care into fury. Chisa, still wearing the suppression collar that marks what was done to her, answers noise with plain observation and remains the one voice capable of calling Lupa back from bloodlust. Cartethyia and Ciaccona carry their own recovered selves—the small girl and towering Fleurdelys, the bard’s wound and the paladin’s borrowed grandeur—without yet believing every part of them belongs to the same person.

In the first days after the battle, the Hold lets these survivors practice staying. They tour its newly opened streets and watercourses. Yangyang and Chisa take the green boat together while Amy’s vessel promptly drains itself. Suisui buys opera tickets instead of auditing the theatre. Mei, Lupa, Cartethyia, Iuno, and Ciaccona argue over mechanisms, food, and geese. At tea, an entire terrace travels west while they remain seated upon it, proving that the ground can move without scattering everyone who stands on it. Amy arranges a group photograph in which Suisui’s scarf is wrapped around Yangyang’s shoulders and every living member of the household fits inside one frame. That evening Yangyang sets a little blue turbine in the east window, where it will turn in the mountain wind, and records the day for shijie: the terrace went west; they stayed on it.

When the long-distance lines reopen, private survival joins a larger map of voices. Calls go out to Lahai-Roi, Ragunna, Septimont, Jinzhou, and the far houses where friends have been counting names. Chisa hears her mother. Ciaccona reaches the old networks that still remember her music. Augusta and Galbrena answer from a domestic life that once seemed impossible. Herta, Black Swan, and Phrolova keep their own tally of seven names until confirmation comes. Each conversation begins differently and reaches the same urgent facts: whose hands still work, who is eating, which bed is occupied, and whether a chair must remain empty.

Yangyang’s Jinzhou line releases sixty-one messages that Chixia sent into eleven days of silence. Wounded and confined to Mrs. Ren’s front room with fourteen stitches, a bad leg, and a crutch, Chixia had continued writing as though persistence itself could keep Yangyang alive: reports about soup, a broken window latch, the lantern kept burning, shijie’s blanket, and the exact place by the stove where Yangyang ought to be. When they finally speak, Yangyang refuses to turn the reunion into a clean recital over a wire. She tells Chixia that she became the bird, crossed the dark, carried shijie to an older Suisui, and came back. She also admits that she holds a physical letter addressed to “the harbour ward, the red cap, the inside of the lining.” She will not read it remotely. Shijie wrote it for the room, and Yangyang will bring it to that room herself. She offers only the first word—“Chixia”—and Chixia laughs and cries because the voice on the line is alive enough to make promises.

Baizhi’s call is quieter and no less intimate. She asks clinical questions about Yangyang’s changed resonance and new feathers, but beneath her precision lies the truth that she carried a replacement battery to the gate morning after morning and kept Chixia alive while waiting for news. When Chixia’s temperature fell, Baizhi committed what she calls an error of procedure: she spoke her wish aloud again, and the wish was plural. She wanted both Chixia and Yangyang to wake. Yangyang adds “Plural” to the patrol log beside the practical entries. Baizhi opens a narrow column in her incident ledger for shijie’s letter, to be read when all of them share the room.

The household’s first victories are deliberately small. Amy asks Chisa to go shopping without Mei supervising them. They buy matching summer uniforms, a viciously green star-shaped hair clip, and iced taro milk, reclaiming an adolescence neither girl was allowed to keep. Iuno persuades Mei that letting Amy leave without her is not abandonment: Amy is finally safe enough to spend a day being ridiculous. Elsewhere, Iuno finds an old arena wrapper whose image preserves Lupa while leaving the High Priestess’s chair empty. Lupa insists that Iuno sign it too, restoring in ink the woman history cropped away. Chisa ties her pale blue school tie around Lupa’s scarred throat and promises to make one that properly fits; Lupa claims she will never wear it, then sleeps with it knotted over her vest.

Mei’s own habits are harder to retrain. When Sir Honk the goose steals a small crown onto a roof, her first instinct is to climb after it despite her stitches. Instead, remembering every recent argument about disappearing under beams alone, she walks into the household and says that there is a goose and she needs a ladder. The request brings everyone running. The same ladder then exposes a relief contractor who stole twelve tarps and roofing slates; by evening three vulnerable roofs are covered, the goose is knighted, and Mei has remained on the ground serving as Clerk of Works rather than bleeding unseen on a ridge.

At night, Iuno finds Mei grieving the childhood of Amy she cannot remember. Mei fears that the empty half of her memory makes her a counterfeit mother. Iuno refuses the grand penance Mei wants to impose on herself. Amy does not need to be interrogated for twelve years of missing history; she needs an ordinary mother now. Mei therefore asks one foolish question about whether Amy ever had bangs. Amy answers with the story of shears, birch sap, a winter hat, and two Februaries created because Mei once drew a winch diagram on the kitchen calendar. The memory arrives not as evidence in a trial, but as family banter. Later, in bed between Mei and Iuno, Amy supplies still more details while pretending to be asleep. Mei learns to receive them without turning each one into a wound.

The same room teaches everyone how to be held. Ciaccona rubs wintergreen salve into Cartethyia’s aching arms after Lupa’s brutal sword drills and answers the knight’s fear that Fleurdelys is only a costume copied from chapel saints. A singer may learn her voice from someone large enough to fill a hall, Ciaccona tells her, but the voice becomes her own when she must use it alone. Yangyang returns late from the parapet to find two buns and hot ginger broth kept under a bowl on the warm hearthstone. She eats both because Lupa, apparently asleep, counts every bite. Then she slips beside Suisui, plants ice-cold feet against her sister’s calves as she did in childhood, and lets Suisui ask whether her new sword arm feels alien. Yangyang explains that the sword once felt like borrowed weight she had to carry correctly until supper; now the bone of her shoulder seems to continue all the way to the point. She no longer grips so hard to remain herself.

Mei and Amy reach the same lesson through anger. At the Skyworks, Amy finally tells Mei what it cost when Mei once called her grown and then vanished into danger: the praise became an order to handle frightening things alone. Amy does not want another apology polished into self-punishment. She wants the right to remain furious without Mei treating that fury as proof she should leave. Mei drops the grin she uses to soften every room and tells Amy to be mad. She promises to remain where Amy can be mad at her for a hundred years. Later, Amy pilots Nyx home while her injured body rides in a cart with a damaged wheel. The household walks beside it chanting that nobody is angry at the wheel, and Amy adds, quietly, “Nobody’s mad at you, Ma.”

An uneventful day frightens Amy almost as much as battle. She waits for the hidden bill behind hot chocolate, stories, and a bed full of people who came home. Mei admits that she too searches every good moment for the knife that must follow it. Neither can promise the world has stopped charging costs. They can promise that no one will meet the bill alone. The next time Mei disappears for forty-eight hours on a two-hour errand—rescuing a mule and propping three strangers’ roofs—the household feeds and warms her before Amy confronts her. Mei no longer lives in a cabin beneath four kilometres of ice, where only one pair of shoulders can reach the beam. There is not only one of her now. She must ask for the ladder.

At Yuan’s Flavors, the price of free food is a name in the proprietor’s ledger: one contact for where a person comes from and another for where she should be returned if she is late. Suisui claims the first line for Yangyang. Yangyang slides the ledger to Mei for the second, and Mei writes that she will be saving a plate. The household fills the margins with signatures while Suisui adds an instruction to keep it warm. Yangyang does not choose between the sister who formed her and the wandering family that waits for her; the ledger requires both columns.

### The Empty Shrine

Suisui cannot remain at the Hold. A river-lock crisis in Mengzhou threatens the winter grain of sixteen thousand people, and duty calls her back onto the road. Yangyang responds with a rare, childish sulk: she turns away on the bench, answers in clipped syllables, and eats the largest honeyed osmanthus cake from the middle of the plate. Suisui is secretly delighted. Her younger sister has spent years making every emotion convenient for others; petty resentment means she trusts Suisui enough to be difficult.

The separation still terrifies both of them. Liangyu reports that Hsin’s office remains empty, the shrines are cold, Qingxiao has vanished into the upper mist, and the Wardens are badly undermanned. Suisui has the authority to order Yangyang into the safety of her coach, but refuses to disguise fear as command. Instead, the household converts her requested quiet departure into a predawn procession. Lupa hides smoked meat and an axle-pin among Suisui’s silk clothes. Cartethyia recruits Sir Honk as vanguard. Ciaccona composes an unauthorized ballad. Iuno dresses Suisui’s hair while Amy heats her cloak and Yangyang serves her shelled egg and porridge. For once, the woman who habitually notices every cold shoulder and empty cup is pinned to a stool and cared for until she laughs through tears.

At the coach, Suisui gives Hsin’s amber Prism of Fate to Yangyang. She no longer treats it as a sacred hostage or the proof of a contest with the Sentinel. The stone was only the receipt for winning her sister back. Dumpling, the clockwork Rafter Kite, abandons Suisui’s cloak for Yangyang’s feathered collar. Suisui promises to expect her at the Mingting gate on the twenty-eighth day of the twelfth month before the dumpling water boils. After the coach rolls away, she discovers two extra walnut cakes and a folded note in her tin: Yangyang stole the large cake only to give her a reason to scold.

The Prism remains warm in Yangyang’s hands, but the Sentinel who owns it remains silent. At breakfast, the household tells Yangyang what Sentinels are really like. Jué, grand and ancient, could still be trapped by duty and require Jinhsi to force a transfer of mandate. Rinascita’s Imperator concluded an age of divine conflict by driving away in an Old Federation automobile with Leviathan. Baldur, the subterranean Exostrider, had to be operated from inside like a six-person construction crane. Divinity has never guaranteed wisdom, certainty, or grace.

They infer that Hsin is not dead and has not wholly abandoned Xuanfang. The Astrolabe still turns and the terraces still balance. She is hiding because Muyu, the master artisan she trusted, used that trust to butcher her people, and because Suisui’s refusal to sacrifice the Guest Wind exposed the cruelty of Hsin’s own fatal arithmetic. Hsin is trying to become the floor: useful, load-bearing, and unavailable for forgiveness. Yangyang recognizes the impulse because Mei has done the same thing with collapsing roofs and because she herself once tried to become weather. She decides to return the Prism directly. In Mingting, one returns a game stone to the owner’s hand, not to an empty bowl.

The household climbs through snow to Fogveil Pagoda, where the ceremonial doors refuse to open and a pantry entrance stands propped with a kiln brick. Inside a folding maze of cedar galleries, they meet Qingxiao. The celebrated Sword Transcendent has not ascended or vanished; she went upstairs to kill Discords in the wainscoting, and a confused clerk turned routine extermination into legend. She recognizes the household from Ministry files, notices Yangyang’s changed plumage, and has an unspoken moment of recognition with Mei, whose face belongs to a war two centuries old even though Mei herself remembers none of it.

At the inner sanctuary, Hsin opens only a six-inch deposit hatch with a velvet cradle for the Prism. Yangyang refuses to slide it through. Liangyu is still sweeping the front steps and the Hold is still waiting. Hsin must open the door and receive her stone in person.

An alarm interrupts the standoff. Discords have breached the fog line above Xianling Village. Qingxiao descends on a blade of Sword Intent and destroys the attackers without cutting a persimmon branch. The villagers immediately smother their immortal protector in buns, pickles, prayers, and a garlic wreath; when Yangyang arrives, her Xuanling plumage makes her a second object of veneration, allowing the mortified Qingxiao to escape the crowd. The laughter ends when a subterranean tremor shakes the terraces. The Celestial Rings grind to a halt. Every Autopuppet collapses, every surveillance ring goes dark, containment cages burst, and the Mingshen array that holds back the Disorienting Fog fails at once.

Yangyang, Liangyu, and the household evacuate Xianling’s people up the switchbacks while Discords spill out behind them. By the time the last cart passes the manually cranked portcullis, the Hold is blind, overcrowded, and nearly cut off. The Prism has turned cold in Yangyang’s hand. Qingxiao reveals that the public Pagoda is only an outer chamber; Hsin’s true Sanctum hangs inside the stalled Astrolabe Core. The emergency louver will admit only three before its counterweights fall. Qingxiao must go because she knows the old ways, Iuno because she understands the machinery and deceptions of sacred institutions, and Yangyang because she carries Hsin’s stone and can hear breath beneath noise.

Before they leave, Iuno finds Qingxiao on the watch gallery weaving Sword Intent from a qin into the cloaks of every defender below. Qingxiao confesses the choice that has tormented her since Muyu’s betrayal. He sabotaged the Hold while she spent three hours bracing a rotten spillway beam over twenty trapped workers. If she had let the beam fall and pursued him, perhaps the fortress would still be whole. Iuno answers with the ugliest fact of her own life: she once used prophecy and arithmetic to send one hundred and seventy-three gladiators into the salt marshes, and she later designed a chain of events meant to place Mei on an altar. Counting lives made her efficient, not righteous. Numbers are a coward’s way to love a city. The twenty people under Qingxiao’s beam are alive; that is the answer. Qingxiao closes the qin and rises.

### The Three Realms and the Siege

The three women enter the Astrolabe through dead gearwells, corrupted jade, and a funerary trigram laid backward across the Sanctum. The rot belongs to a Nethermancer, not a Sentinel. Hsin’s folded authority tears open around them, dropping them into a Mirage of Yuan Fortress as it might have existed had the disaster two centuries ago never occurred. Its red banners remain whole. Cloud Shuttles float in sunlight. Autopuppets and self-replicating mechanisms are credited with defeating the Threnodian while Hsin and the golden-eyed Arbiter have been erased from the victory.

Qingxiao teaches Yangyang and Iuno to ride blades of condensed thought between the ships. Yangyang’s first blade responds to the thought of falling by pitching toward the abyss; Iuno’s responds to both her conscious command and the High Priestess in her head by spinning like a music-box dancer. They master the crossing only when Yangyang stops clamping herself to the blade and lets her own wind meet it, and when Iuno stops trying to silence her second voice and instead shares the work with it.

The immaculate fleet is powered by a concealed atrocity. Its crews are Reverberations of artisans who died when Yuan Fortress burned. Muyu has severed grief from their minds so they can work forever without hesitation. Jianghang learns that his sister has died and continues turning the same bolt because mourning no longer reaches him. Lianqing watches a sunrise, remembers that beauty ought to exist, and considers jumping because she can no longer feel why she should stay. Qingxiao’s fury freezes the mist into blades. Iuno names the truth: a city that cannot mourn its dead has not been saved; it has been embalmed.

At the flagship helm, Muyu appears as an amber phantom. Once Hsin’s trusted artisan, he believes mortal feeling is the structural flaw that made Yuan Fortress fall. He accuses Hsin of lacking the will to use her power and Qingxiao of casting aside her own heart in pursuit of cultivation. He scorns the heroic cycle that demands golden-eyed women, sacrificed armies, and girls who become wind. His accusation strikes every bruise in the three intruders, but Yangyang sees the fraud beneath it. Hsin’s refusal to hollow her citizens was not weakness. Muyu has made obedient corpses and named them immortal.

When Qingxiao destroys the projection, they understand that it existed to delay them while Muyu attacks the real Hold. Panic drives Yangyang’s attention toward escape; she begins measuring whether she can become wind and slip back alone. Iuno stops her with a single command. She has watched one Yangyang unmake herself to finish an errand and will not watch another. Qingxiao, who once trusted no one enough to leave a wall, tells them to trust the defenders. They will hold.

Above them, those defenders face Muyu’s Censure Courts without the Core. Magistrate-shaped puppets and chained Discords climb through the Disorienting Fog, reading sentences against the living: found wanting, struck from the roll, demolition. Ciaccona holds an acoustic grid over the ramp so allies can hear true direction inside the false echoes. Amy leaves her body in Mei’s lap and pilots Nyx Ravenhollow through sleet, trusting Mei to hold her chin and not abandon the rug. Cartethyia becomes Fleurdelys beneath the portcullis and cuts a thirty-foot tribunal colossus in half—along with a forty-foot diagonal section of Xuanfang’s ancient wall. Bai Tieshan catches the severed masonry with earth-softening hands while Chisa stitches the breach with iron cable.

Lupa leaps from the parapet into the fog because battle offers a simplicity care never does. Her fire takes on the shapes of dead companions, and her joy becomes terrible as she begins pursuing the enemy beyond Ciaccona’s sound. Chisa says only her name. Lupa hears the breakfast-table voice inside the roar, comes back through the gate, and drinks water.

Mei keeps both knees on the felt blanket until winged Discords dive at Amy’s helpless body. She fights one-handed without releasing her daughter, tears her own stitches, and discovers a separate thread of Qingxiao’s Sword Intent tied secretly around her ribs. Later, when burrowing Discords erupt from the drain and she cannot make the second stroke, Abby explodes from her body, is torn apart, reconstitutes himself in furious gold, and destroys them because Mei is injured, Amy is his, and the hall behind them is full of children. Mei, who has spent lives acting as the only shield in reach, is forced to let even the smallest member of her household stand between her and harm.

In the Mirage’s buried Di Realm, Yangyang, Iuno, and Qingxiao find the real Yuan Fortress drowned in ash. Pale roots strip memories and grief from the dead, pumping blank frequency into the shining fleet. Muyu has not eliminated sorrow; he has buried it in the foundation and burned it for fuel. Qingxiao, meanwhile, is freezing from the wrists inward. Across the sealed realms she has kept every protective thread around the Hold taut, accepting each blow against its defenders into her own body. Iuno demands that she thin the wards and preserve strength for Muyu. Qingxiao answers with Iuno’s own lesson: if no life is reduced to arithmetic, then Iuno must name which twelve people should lose protection.

Iuno cannot. Instead, she admits that she is frightened. Mei is fighting with five-day-old stitches, her Terminal is dead, the sun outside the cabin does not move, and Qingxiao may burn herself to ash before they reach the real enemy. The confession does what command cannot. Yangyang divides one crushed turnip cake into three pieces, moves the lamp away from Qingxiao’s frost, and says aloud when her own ribs hurt. Care enters the room as practical inventory rather than surrender.

They speak about the voices within them. Iuno’s High Priestess is vain, theatrical, and often truthful before Iuno is ready. Yangyang’s shijie remained inside her as guidance rather than possession; another inner voice once urged her to abandon everything and live, and Yangyang survived by refusing to obey it without pretending it had never spoken. Qingxiao was taught that any inner voice is a demon to be cut down. Yet after hearing the other two women describe coexistence, she allows Yangyang to cover her with a wool blanket and lies down.

While Qingxiao sleeps, a second breath moves beneath her own. The untouched qin sounds. Frost points inward toward the bunk. A jade bead cracks. Her defensive threads briefly go slack in the waking world, then return darker and crueler, shearing attackers in half rather than merely turning blades. Qingxiao has entered the final duel her teacher Jingwei taught her to expect.

### The Heart Under the Mountain

Yangyang and Iuno follow Qingxiao into a lake beneath a tree whose branches are made of every defensive thread tied to Xuanfang. The lakebed is paved with sealed scrolls, and its shallows hold the cloven bodies of all the selves Qingxiao has killed to preserve perfect stillness. Jingwei’s lingering frequency tells them that if Qingxiao’s inner demon wins, they must strike her down before the backlash travels through the tree and kills everyone she protects.

Iuno briefly accepts the grim logic. Yangyang refuses. Qingxiao did not open her mind to women who would repeat Jingwei’s mistake. She chose Iuno, who stopped waging war against the High Priestess, and Yangyang, who carries shijie without allowing the dead to erase the living.

At the tree, a violet-eyed Qingxiao stands over the exhausted woman kneeling beneath the weight of the Hold. The reflection is not a foreign invader. She is the hard, feelingless armor an eight-year-old girl built after receiving one bowl of millet and going out with a hazel stick to defend the people who fed her. She unseals the scrolls Qingxiao buried: Lingfang leaving for war after singing the first half of a marching call no one answered; Yuan Fortress burning while Qingxiao fought elsewhere in the Gorges; Qingxiao carving Ruoyun’s name into stone with a broken blade; Hsin receiving Qingxiao’s fury behind a bolted door; two hundred invitations from Hsin left unopened on a snowbound peak. Qingxiao despises Hsin for becoming unreachable, yet has spent two centuries doing the same.

The reflection places her sword against the solitary thread tied around Mei. Mei does not remember the ancient manual, the rain, or Qingxiao’s face. Qingxiao walked the continent for four years seeking the golden-eyed swordswoman who saved the Gorges, only for that woman to erase her past and begin again. Why should Qingxiao bleed for someone who did not deem her worth remembering?

Before despair can break the tree, Ruoyun walks out of the mist. Qingxiao believed her dead at Yuan Fortress and carved her memorial. Ruoyun instead surrendered a body pinned under a gate beam and enlisted as a Celestial on the Hold’s own registers. For two centuries she has cleared pine needles from conduits, nudged lost Rafter Kites toward the right pass, and kept the third watch while Qingxiao grieved her as absent. She rebukes both halves of her friend: keeping one’s flesh frozen on a peak is not nobler than relinquishing a ruined body to remain among the people one loves.

The High Priestess recognizes Qingxiao’s reflection as kin. She tells the armor that it is not an abyssal terror, only a child’s protector furious at being locked in the cellar whenever Qingxiao descends toward life. Qingxiao finally answers the question of why she involves herself in mortal affairs. She does not invoke Hsin’s title or a transcendent duty. Xianling gave her garlic and buns. Yangyang divided a turnip cake into thirds. Mei is keeping rice crust warm for someone still below. A single plum branch over shallow water is enough reason to love a garden. Her hands have hurt for two hundred years.

Yangyang opens her Xuanling wings and sings Lingfang’s unanswered call. Qingxiao answers with the old cadence, and the violet-eyed reflection sings from the same breath. Every slain self in the lake becomes a bird of warm light and returns to Qingxiao’s breast. Qingxiao does not kill the reflection; she embraces her until only one shadow remains.

Jingwei warns that without frozen stillness anchoring her marrow, Qingxiao will not live long. Qingxiao accepts mortality. Her Way is to walk down the mountain while the soup is still warm. The cracked jade bead drops from her wrist. Plum blossoms bloom along the threads throughout the Hold, and the wards stop retaliating with cruelty; they turn each blow aside gently, leaving winter fragrance in the mud.

When Qingxiao wakes in the cabin, the room has thawed. She accepts Yangyang’s shoulder to sit up and admits that she is hungry. She also tells Iuno and Yangyang why Mei mattered to her. Two hundred years ago, Mei’s earlier self came through the rain beside Sentinel Jué and turned the Gorges battle with a blade broad enough to save a province, while Yuan Fortress burned elsewhere. Qingxiao searched for four years to ask how such a person chooses where to stand when every unattended roof falls. She never asked the present Mei because Mei had chosen to leave the old folders in a drawer. Iuno supplies the answer anyway: Mei does not choose by worth. She props the beam nearest someone’s head, shouts for a ladder too late, and comes home covered in plaster. Yangyang adds that house rules now require her to ask for the ladder first.

The trio breaks through the Mirage’s Tian Realm, where Muyu has inverted Hsin’s Sanctum into a storm of shattered courts and corrupt machinery. Yangyang hears the hitch where Muyu’s stolen frequency falls behind Hsin’s original four-beat ward. Iuno holds the collapsing barrier above them on her moonwheel while Yangyang steadies Qingxiao’s arm and calls the beat; Qingxiao cuts the seam open.

Inside, they find Muyu’s mortal body dead at a drafting table, his callused artisan’s hands discarded with his aging flesh. His consciousness inhabits a ten-foot, four-armed guardian body of nephrite, bronze, black lacquer, and porcelain. He attacks through the very systems Hsin entrusted to him and demands that Qingxiao cut her threads to gain the power to defeat him. She refuses. His attempt to draw ten thousand captive Reverberations from the deep reservoirs produces only empty air: the dead went home when Yangyang and Qingxiao completed the song.

Muyu’s construct cracks Iuno’s moonwheel, chips her crystal shoulder, and batters Yangyang’s ribs. Qingxiao cannot pierce his kernel while holding the Hold. Then Jingran appears behind the construct and drives a blade through its regulator. He had seemed part of Muyu’s Nethermantic machinery, but he used his access to smash all seven backup vessels containing Muyu’s soul. A Nethermancer may uncover what the dead leave behind; Jingran will not skin them for boiler fuel.

Suoming, the small woman in the faded plum coat whom everyone mistook for a child, leads the rest of the household through the sealed doors. The final fight becomes the opposite of Muyu’s solitary immortality. Ciaccona gives the room a true beat. Fleurdelys takes the giant scythes. Chisa cuts the conduits. Lupa burns open the magnetic ring. Amy grounds the lightning beside Mei. Iuno pins a joint. Yangyang clears the smoke with her wings. Mei stands at Qingxiao’s left and shares the weight of a descending blade, greeting her in the midst of destruction by asking whether she ate her third of the turnip cake.

With Muyu’s kernel exposed, Qingxiao draws the bloodstained plum petal from her cuff. It becomes a plain moon-white sword carrying the whole weight of a living heart. She drives it through the kernel, and the artificial immortal collapses into scrap.

Muyu’s last revelation prevents triumph from becoming safety. His cages were not merely prisons; they were plugs in a provincial barrier that has decayed for fifty years. With them gone, the deep seams are collapsing from below. Hsin is not in the Sanctum, and the cold Prism offers no answer. Qingxiao executes the final remnant of Muyu, but the Hold remains beneath a broken sky.

### The Debts That Remain

The first command after the battle comes from Suoming: eat. In the branch hall, the Fillet Fairmaiden feeds defenders and refugees from sentient cauldrons. Qingxiao sits shoulder to shoulder with Yangyang at a table meant for half their number. When the reserved pan of rice crust arrives, she passes it first to Liangyu and the Wardens who held the gate. After everyone else has eaten, Mei uncovers a bowl kept warm for the exhausted cook and tells her that it is her turn.

Yangyang then sends Mei to walk Qingxiao home. Mei thanks Qingxiao for the secret thread around her ribs. Qingxiao calls it spare; Mei knows it was not. On a quiet upper lane, Qingxiao finally asks the question she carried for two centuries: when two roofs fall at once, how does Mei choose? Mei answers that she runs toward whichever beam is nearest, then yells until someone brings a ladder. She is supposed to ask first now. Qingxiao calls it a good rule.

At an old cedar house, Qingxiao retrieves *The Swordcraft of the Golden-Eyed Stranger*, copied long ago from Mei’s forgotten forms and annotated in the cramped hand of Qingxiao as a child. Mei does not recover the buried life. She sees the ash, the small handwriting, and the turtle doodled beside a grounded heel, then asks only whether the manual helped. When Qingxiao says yes, Mei takes the book home to keep safe. Later Yangyang reads it beside Mei and recognizes the stance Qingxiao now makes her practice at the cistern every morning. Knowledge moves forward even when memory does not.

Qingxiao’s acceptance of life has a price. Ruoyun meets her on the third watch, solid and warm after two centuries of concealment. She is furious to discover that Qingxiao’s immortal frost is gone and that Jingwei measured her remaining life as “not long.” Ruoyun has watched generations age and die while tending the same Hold; she refuses to walk behind Qingxiao’s coffin. Qingxiao answers that the third watch will still need keeping. They admit that both the snowpeak and the long garrison years were lonely, retune an old camp song to Ruoyun’s imperfect pitch, share an oilcloth against the cold, and write their names side by side on the watch-board.

Buling also returns, the former disciple who left for cinnabar and wandered for more than a century as a geomancer, artifact maker, and notorious forum user. Ruoyun chases her through the market, catches her by the ear at a culvert, and drags her before Qingxiao to answer for the rounds she abandoned. Qingxiao laughs—openly, unexpectedly—when the two begin threatening each other with beheading and pickle jars. Then she sentences Buling not to exile, but to work beside Ruoyun on the eastern breach until the thaw. The lost members of the old Hold resume their quarrel over lime, crowbars, soup, and shoes.

Jingran and Suoming settle darker accounts. At two empty graves Jingran dug as a child, one for himself and one for the teacher who later vanished into a Sonoro seam, he buries the old man’s brass pipe tamper and knocks both nameless markers flat before runoff crews can reuse them. He then follows Suoming down from the cemetery rather than remaining with the graves. At a river drying shed, Yinlin intercepts artisans trying to smuggle Muyu’s deep-manifold charts south. She returns the fissure soundings to Suoming while taking the rest to Jinzhou. The conspiracy beneath the Hold is not wholly dead, and the people cleaning it up do so without public ceremony.

Supplies finally climb the reopened gorge road: rice, millet, coal, lime, cable, Huaxu equipment, a Jinzhou pike company, and Ministry surveyors who already know the Hold’s floor plan. Four mail sacks arrive slit and resealed by Mingting’s Internal Security Agency. Chixia sends eleven letters written on whatever paper she could reach, a hand-lined red winter cap for the Wind, and a tiny midnight note confessing that Mrs. Ren’s front room becomes too quiet before dawn. Baizhi reports both patients’ healing in clinical language, then admits Jinzhou is quieter than she prefers while Yangyang is away. Yangyang writes in her patrol log that the road is open and the hat fits the pocket. She and Mei agree that the first thing they will do upon returning is walk with Chixia along the canal before supper.

Yet the road also brings occupation disguised as relief. Southern soldiers fill the barracks. Ministry men measure cistern piers while rumors spread that Xuanfang will be dismantled after the gorge is cleared. Jinhsi’s censored letter hides a warning in four deliberate falsehoods: the moon is setting. That night, two days before the Waking Moon should be full, the moon crosses the sky too early and disappears behind the ninth spire. Hsin’s Core remains dark.

Iuno feels the loss of prophecy most sharply. She can list the rotting barrier, the missing Sentinel, the freed Celestials, Jingran’s soot, Suoming’s calm, Buling’s return, Mengzhou’s silence, and the Core that has grown too quiet for Chisa to hear. She cannot see five minutes ahead. Two promised Tuesdays have passed without her proposal—one while she was trapped inside the painted afternoon, another because she waited for Mei’s stitches, Augusta’s papers, and a safe sky. Mei refuses to let Tuesday become another postponed life. She retires her morning question about the day. Tuesday will be whenever Iuno takes out the box, even if roofs are falling. Iuno still does not propose before the festival, but the box remains in her coat because the future has become an act she must choose, not an hour prophecy grants.

### Reunion Under the Wrong Moon

On the first day of the Waking Moon, every neighboring household closes its shutters and cooks a reunion dinner. Yangyang pretends the custom does not matter because no one else is from Huanglong. She oils a hinge, polishes her sword twice, rewinds her bandages, and begins a letter to Suisui that cannot leave while the holiday gates are barred. Mei recognizes the discipline of someone making loneliness unobtrusive. She announces that their storehouse will hold its own *tuán yuán fàn*: one handmade dish from every member of the household, all at the same table by dusk.

Yangyang makes the clear chicken, yam, angelica, date, and goji soup her mother serves in Mingting. Lupa crams nine ingredients into one brass hotpot and argues that one rim means one dish. Iuno fails repeatedly before producing a mirror-smooth steamed egg with one hand. Cartethyia bakes a crooked Egla ring-loaf. Amy uses a brass reed to hear the exact instant sugar reaches the right temperature for candied hawthorns. Chisa cuts flawless cistern ice for osmanthus water. Ciaccona, deprived of tomatoes and proper flour, creates persimmon-garlic millet noodles that nearly defeat everyone but Cartethyia and Buling. Mei makes sizzling rice and keeps one dry golden corner under an upturned cup.

At dusk, Yangyang lays every bowl and every pair of chopsticks in the Mingting manner. Abby appears and demands an extra setting for the person behind, so Chisa adds a ninth bowl by the door. Yangyang serves Mei first, then goes around the whole table and fills the empty bowl last. Their reunion is imperfect, crowded, and loud. The bread resembles a badger, the noodles resemble axle paste, Mei freezes her teeth on Chisa’s drink, and the hotpot violates the spirit of every rule. It is nevertheless the first Waking Moon table Yangyang has not had to leave in order to keep someone else safe.

Visitors bring the rest of Xuanfang into the meal. Liangyu and the young Wardens arrive with preserved mustard greens and leave carrying mutton skewers. Buling contributes century eggs of uncertain age and eats a second dinner. Ruoyun sends bitter melon and egg while insisting it merely remained in the pan. Qingxiao pauses outside in the cold, reluctant to enter a crowded room. Mei uncovers the rice crust she saved and carries it to the threshold. Qingxiao eats it there, warm-handed and mortal, then follows Ruoyun and Buling toward the viewing terraces.

The household carries its benches and leftovers into the yard instead of climbing the official steps. All across the Hold, families gather beneath red lanterns and set paper boats on the cistern. The moon rises enormous and bright above the eastern pass, and the battered fortress cheers as if the old order has returned. Yangyang sits between Amy, Cartethyia, and the rest of the household, drinking Mingting soup beneath a borrowed scarf. When she first returned to Xuanfang, she was the late-arriving Watcher with cracked ribs, half-belonging to a sister across the dark and half-prepared to become wind again whenever someone needed saving. Now she is seated inside the life that sacrifice made possible. Her shijie remains part of her without consuming her; Suisui has gone to save another city without taking Yangyang’s home with her; Chixia and Baizhi wait down the reopened road; and Xuanfang has given Yangyang a disciple’s place, a family’s bowl, and people who will notice if she misses lunch.

The world around that table is not healed. As the cheers fade, the full moon darkens to copper-red and races westward too early. Ministry troops occupy the terraces, surveyors measure the foundations, the deep barrier continues to rot, and the Astrolabe Core hangs crooked and silent. Iuno holds Mei’s hand with the ring still unoffered in her coat. Qingxiao has exchanged ageless isolation for a shorter mortal life among Ruoyun, Liangyu, and the people whose soup she can now reach while it is hot. Ruoyun and Buling walk publicly through streets they once served from hiding. Jingran has abandoned his empty grave, Suoming holds the fissure charts, and the Wardens remain at gates the state may intend to close.

At the third watch, Qingxiao and Ruoyun reach the high gallery. Their weiqi board still bears nine black stones and the white stone Qingxiao placed the night before. Beneath that white stone, on the shoulder point nearer the edge, a tenth black stone has appeared, though the only stair has remained guarded. Hsin still does not open her jade door or restore the sky, but she has answered the board.

Xuanfang now stands in a condition opposite to the lonely, suspended survival in which Yangyang first found it. Its divine machinery is broken, its political future threatened, and its moon displaced; yet its human and Celestial inhabitants are no longer waiting in separate rooms for someone else to decide what their losses require. The absent have begun returning to their watches. The immortal has come down for soup. The martyr asks for a ladder. The bird remains at the table. And beneath the wrong moon, while the mountain’s oldest defenses fail, an unseen hand places one black stone beside another and makes the next move.
