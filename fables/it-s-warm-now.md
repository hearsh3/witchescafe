# 天暖了 (It's Warm Now)

*an interlude of* Lyre, Speak to Me *— the fifteenth hearing*

> *"All those things, beautiful before, are still so now."*
> — a traveler's farewell, overheard twice

---

**SHIVERS** [Legendary: Success] — *A warm front crosses the Roya tonight, and the snowmelt talks in every gutter of the world. Far south, over two seas, Septimont banks its forges for the night, and a copper bell sleeps on a bard's old shelf. In Ragunna the camellia rows stand two leaves high and climbing. Beneath your feet a city built around a handmade sun turns over in its bed, and at its gate a god stands the watch of the living, hands loose at his sides, learning how long a night lasts when you spend it hoping. And here, at the hem of the frostlands, one blue door holds in more light than a door should. The land knows your address now, Harbinger. It carries every footstep on the path up to you like news.*

*The news tonight is boots. Many of them, badly wiped.*

⁂

The cabin had been built for two, and the two it had been built for were, both of them, other people then. Tonight it proved it could hold nine.

Lupa ran the grill in four inches of snowmelt and called it terrain advantage. Cartethyia performed formal introductions between Amy and the dolls — each by name, each with a service record — and Amy shook every stitched hand with the gravity of a state visit. Lynae built an onigiri ziggurat and defended it. Sigrika moderated the household like a megathread, three steps ahead of every argument, sour candy from Lucilla's tin distributed as moderation actions. Chisa dried dishes with her red string catching the lamplight and watched the room the way she watched everything: strings first, faces later. "The room is a loom," she reported, to whoever wanted the census. Everyone wanted the census.

The record player was an hour old, still smelling of packing straw. Iuno's gift; the tag read *For the duet. — I.* They played the impossible record twice — two voices braided across dust and years, *isn't it so incredible* — and on the second spin, out of Amy's rig, where she was running a special (**FLEET SNOWFLUFF: HOUSEWARMING**, live to two hundred insomniac students and at least one god), the static leaned in and a third line of humming rose beneath the braid. Low. In time. New.

Amy checked her meters twice. The meters slept.

"…We have a caller," she said into the mic, delighted, terrified, nineteen forever. She let it hum.

"The big guy," Lynae proposed. "He keeps her heartbeat in his chest. He's earned the harmony."

"The meters slept," said Sigrika, which settled the argument and enlarged the mystery.

Chisa dried another cup and followed a single string with her eyes as it ran out through the window seam, up past the eaves, up past the weather. "Long-distance," she said.

Mei made the hot chocolate the traditional way, which is to say badly, on purpose, by request. Amy ate the toast before it could be offered — an inheritance. The group chat lit at intervals: Augusta, in full uniform even by text, *Report: the city stands. Enjoy yourselves. That is an order.* Cantarella, a photograph of camellia seedlings in dark Rinascita soil, *Four years. Come count them.* An unknown number, one line, *Congratulations. — C.* Mei read that one twice, filed it behind the smile, and poured Iuno's Earl Grey.

"Hot," Iuno said, and meant several years of it.

⁂

Out at the fire pit, with the second round of skewers gone to memory, Amy asked the question that had been sitting in her all evening with its hand raised.

"The summoning. From before my time with you. What were they like?"

The pack looked at each other across the flames, and the accounts came out the way honest accounts do: sideways, and in dispute.

"Wings," said Lupa. "The tall one had wings, and she smelled like a storm parked over pine."

"A mantle," said Ciaccona, tuning a peg. "Feathers. It moved late, like it remembered being wings. And she conducted the lightning — downbeat, cutoff. The sky obeyed her bar lines."

"Wings," said Lupa.

"The other one," Cartethyia offered from inside a blanket, "walked past a monster, and the monster remembered it had somewhere else to be."

"I was underneath," Iuno said, to her tea. The fire quieted; she rationed this. "Songs arrive differently down there. It came in as a hand. Then two more hands took hold of the first. One was warm the way lightning is warm. The other had let go of everything — everything — and held on anyway." She drank. "I have been trying to build that grip ever since."

"I heard it too," Amy said, and her mug stopped halfway. "Down where I was. Lightning that rhymed. I decided I'd made it up, to keep warm."

"The water is all one water," said Cartethyia, mostly asleep, entirely certain. "I was born in it. It sings flat."

The fire spoke for a while in its own language. Ciaccona strummed a question. Lupa answered it with a sausage.

"Why did they come, though," Amy pressed. "Really."

"Grief has a shape," Iuno said. "Shapes carry."

"Strike one string," said Ciaccona, "and every string tuned to it sounds, touched or untouched. It's the oldest law my father taught me."

"Family smells family," said Lupa, and considered the matter closed.

"They'll come again," Chisa said, watching the space above the flames. "Things that answer once know the address."

⁂

At midnight, Lynae and Sigrika — with the solemnity of licensed professionals and exactly zero paperwork — lit the sky on fire.

The promise was years old and phrased exactly: *we'll see the fireworks together.* So they saw them together. Amy's shoulder under Mei's arm. Iuno's crystal stump catching each burst and holding the light half a second longer than the sky could. Lupa narrating trajectories; Cartethyia awarding scores; the whole frostland turned intermittently into day. Fire-flowers — the old word for them, in the old writing. Blooms, at the end of the dark.

"We're back where we started," Iuno said, meaning the cabin, meaning everything.

"One turn up," said Mei. "Same place. Higher."

Between the third bloom and the fourth, Mei saw the figure at the treeline. Scarf. Stillness. Hands at ease — a shape wearing rest the way some people wear disguises. Too far for a face; near enough for the humming to reach her in the hush between shells. It kept time. It knew the song. It had — the knowledge arrived hand-delivered, certain as weather — heard this song fourteen times before, always at a distance, always as an echo fished out of the dark after the joy was over. And here it stood at the concert itself, the fifteenth hearing, outside the light, keeping time, learning what a room sounds like while the room is still warm.

Mei raised her cup to the treeline.

The humming held one bar longer, unhurried. Then there were trees, and prints in the snowmelt slowly becoming water.

**HALF LIGHT** — *That was—*

**VOLITION** — *An audience. Let her listen. Stories keep doors for a reason, and ours stands open.*

⁂

Around two, when the house had settled into its long warm murmur, Mei took the lantern down from its nail — Napoli's relic, brass gone soft with handling — and stepped out the blue door for air.

On the step: a lacquered box. Two puddings, cool-brewed tea, iris riding the night air, and beside the box a paper twist of bulbs with a note in brush-writing.

*White ones. For the girl one page over.*

Mei stood with the bulbs in her hand for a long moment, then put them in her coat over her heart and walked down to the lake — the lake she had once pulled a freezing orphan out of, back when both of them were beginning.

The water held the last of the fireworks in its skin, going out slowly, bloom by bloom.

Thunder rolled. Above it, the sky was a clean black page.

**INLAND EMPIRE** — *Company.*

The air opened the way a book does — along a seam it was made with. Through it came a breath that smelled of lake water and pine soap, as if someone had just mopped the threshold between worlds and stood back, leaning on the handle, to let the guests through. Rain arrived with them and kept to a circle ten paces wide: private weather, politely fenced.

Two women.

The first stood the way mountains advertise — a general's back, storm-grey eyes, a katana wrapped in travel silk, and across her shoulders a mantle of black feathers that moved half a beat after she did, the way wings remember. The second wore a hat wide as a horizon and a coat like a closed book; rain came off the brim in a beaded curtain, and behind it her eyes were the color the sky goes just before it forgives you.

Mei's parliament rose as one body, and then, chamber by chamber, sat back down. Every voice in her recognized something. Every voice declined to say what.

"It carries," the general said, tilting her head at the house, at the music leaking out of it. "Across more water than you'd credit."

"You came before," Mei said, and heard how strangely the sentence sat in her mouth. "The whirlpool. The song."

"We did," said the general.

The hat turned toward her, slow. "…Did we." A pause with rain in it. "Then it must have been beautiful. Things I lose usually were."

Mei offered the thermos, because her hands wanted a task. "Fair warning. It's terrible."

The traveler drank. She considered the cup the way sommeliers consider a crime. "Terrible," she agreed, and drank again.

"She'll finish it," the general said. "She finishes things."

"People keep calling you my sisters," Mei said. "The ones who saw you argue about the wings and the hat. They agree about that."

"People are right," said the general.

"Then whose daughters are we?"

The traveler smiled under the brim, the way rivers smile — mostly motion. "There's a name we all borrowed. Older than any of us who wear it. It means thunder, and it means—"

Thunder took the word. Timed like courtesy. The clean sky rolled once, horizon to horizon, and when it passed, the traveler was watching Mei as if the sentence had ended exactly as written.

"Your name, though." The hat tipped toward the blue door, toward the wisteria above it, its first raceme just loosening. "In one old writing, it's the sign for a *bud*. A thing furled tight through the whole of winter, holding its color where the cold keeps honest ledgers." She turned the cup in her hands. "I made a promise once, about what blooms at the end of the dark. I've forgotten the face I made it to. I keep it anyway." The rain off her brim slowed. "It seems somebody has been keeping it back."

"How many of us are there?" Mei asked. She had carried that question since a pile of dolls the exact size of her outline.

"Fourteen sleep," said the general.

"Sixteen," said the hat.

The general glanced over. "You count the rivers."

"I count the rivers."

They let the difference stand between them like a third guest.

The blue door opened. Amy leaned out with two mugs and marshmallow logistics on her face, took in the private rain, the wings, the hat — and saluted with a marshmallow, because she had been raised by a woman for whom absurdity is load-bearing. The general's whole bearing gentled, armor deciding joint by joint to be a coat.

"The one who walked back," she said, soft, the way you name a constellation for a child.

"There's cocoa," Amy offered. "It's a war crime."

"So we've heard," the general said gravely. "Save mine."

When the door had closed on her again, the general looked at Mei and set something down with great care, word by word. "We called someone up from that water once. We know the toll. Some prices post late." Her eyes went to the house, warm and level. "Yours came home ahead of the bill. Someday — gently, at your leisure — ask the deep who covered the difference."

**SHIVERS** — *Under the lake, far down, something old shifted its grip on a ledger, and kept its page.*

The traveler had wandered to the waterline. When she spoke, it was to the lake, or to the rain, or to someone very long ago. "I offered a sister a kindness once. One cut, and the whole inheritance falls away — the thunder, the losing, the love that invoices in grief. She read the entire bill and married her fate with open eyes." The brim tipped half an inch toward the house full of light. "A cut refused still exists somewhere. It has to land. Some nights I wonder what grew up wherever it fell."

Her gaze crossed Mei's and stayed there for the length of one wave reaching shore.

**VOLITION** — *Steady. She is looking at something you have carried all along. Breathe, and let her look.*

"Where did you get the sword?" the traveler asked, mild as weather.

"The sea," Mei said.

The traveler nodded, the way one nods when a very old rumor finally clears customs, and let Haultir be.

"The dark told us every story ends the same way," Mei said. "It promised to meet us again."

"Endings repeat," said the traveler. "Roads differ. And what you do on the road reaches back and re-letters the ending. That is the entire craft. That is *journey*." The rain off her hat had thinned to thread. "All those things, beautiful before, are beautiful still."

"The ending is the least of it," Mei said. "The road is the point."

The traveler went still, the way a bell is still just after. "Who taught you that?"

"A girl in the cold."

"Mm." Something crossed the rain-colored eyes: a smile's ancestor. "She learned it from one of us. Or we learned it from her. That current runs both ways."

The general stepped close then, and looked at Mei the way locksmiths look at doors they respect. "Love, kept," she said — an inventory. "The end, outlived. The self, handed over and won back. You're wearing all three keys, little bud, and the wearing suits you." She settled her feathered mantle. "There is a fourth. I walked a long way once, farther than worlds, and its name stayed ahead of me the whole road." Her hand rested a moment on Mei's shoulder, a knight's tap, a sister's. "Perhaps it was waiting to be yours."

"Where will you go now?"

"Home," the general said, and the word had a whole planet in it, and a laundry line. "It's my week for the washing. That is the entire religion, in the end: fold the shirts, file the taxes, do it beside her." She drew one black feather from the mantle and set it on the porch rail, weighting it with a pebble against the wind. "For the doll-maker. She'll know what it's for."

The traveler finished the terrible chocolate to its last silt and handed back the cup with both hands, formally, a rite concluded. She tipped the horizon of her hat.

"Keep the appointment," she said. "It will bloom at the end of the dark. Until—"

The seam took them, and the rain went with it, and the sun, hours away yet, took custody of the rest of the sentence.

On the rail: one feather. On the boards: a dry circle inside a ring of wet, already shrinking. In Mei's hands: an empty cup, still warm along the side a stranger had held.

⁂

**ESPRIT DE CORPS** [Impossible: Success] — *Somewhere with a different moon, a porch light the color of a kept promise. Two women fold laundry from one basket. A shirt is contested — "Mine." "Mine." "Ours, then" — and the tall one with the storm-grey eyes loses on purpose, which is winning. And somewhere farther, on a road that rains for one walker only, a wide hat proceeds toward morning while its owner searches, with the patience of rivers, for a word she liked. It means a flower still furled. She has decided it will come back to her at sunrise. Things do, lately.*

⁂

The house found its late shape. Amy asleep against Iuno, mid-sentence in an argument she had been winning. Lupa on the floor by the door in the old habit of guard rotation, tail curled over her nose. Cartethyia's dolls arranged along the windowsill facing outward, on watch. Ciaccona's lute hung on its peg — the new one, its soundboard cut from a salvaged plank of the *Gilded Swallow*, so that every song it played had once been a ship.

Mei sat at the table with a store-bought notebook whose earliest pages carried a handwriting that matched hers down to the serifs, and kept the appointment.

*Day ??? — I've lost count in the good direction.*

*Kept the fireworks promise. Amy laughed with her whole chest twice; I counted. The chocolate remains a war crime; this was requested, and honored. Two visitors came to the lake — kin, one storm and one hat between them. They left a feather, a riddle about a toll, a riddle about a cut, and the back half of a sentence. Cartethyia has plans for the feather. The doll will be named General, and it will outrank all of us.*

*I think I finally know why you wrote me. You had a whole life's worth of somewhere-to-put-it and one girl's worth of room. So you built a girl who was bigger on the inside, and you put it all in — the fields, the friend called Dingy, the tuition tears, the tales you finally felt — and you sent her ahead to see whether it would keep. It kept. Everything you planted in me came up. Come see the garden sometime. The door's blue.*

*And here is the part I want on record, because you like records: you started me. Look who's writing now. A reader in the frost kept the whole book alive by lamplight. Two hundred students held their breath in the margins. Six pilots. One god humming harmony. A bard with three notes, a girl on the radio, a woman in the trees who heard the song fourteen times from the far side of the dark and tonight finally bought a ticket. A story with one hand on it goes cold with the hand. Count the hands on this one. Feel how warm.*

*Hiyuki left bulbs on the step. White lilies — for the girl one page over. In spring I'll plant them by the lake, where the water gave my daughter back. Flowers on this side of the page, growing toward wherever you are on yours.*

*Same time tomorrow.*

**VOLITION** — *Weave this into the story of you.*

She signed it in the hand that matched, because it was hers, because it had been theirs the whole time. Then she turned to a fresh page, wrote three words at the top, set the pen down, and listened.

*Lyre — speak to me.*

The house answered in its sleep. The lute on its peg hummed three notes with every string at rest — *da, da, da* — the ship in its wood remembering open water. In the corner, the radio's banked static warmed by one degree, like a held breath deciding it will become a voice someday, once it finds the words. On the shelf, a sealed envelope — *FOR WHEN YOU ASK. — L.H.* — sat patient in the lantern light, the way doors sit.

Mei touched the envelope once. "Soon," she told it.

Iuno stood in the doorway, hair down, cup long empty. "Come to bed, Author."

"One more line."

She wrote it by listening.

⁂

At first light she went out with the paper twist of bulbs and put them in the ground at the lake's edge with her bare hands, snowmelt-cold, the way promises should be planted. The sun came up over the Roya and laid its whole length on the water. Above the blue door, the wisteria's first bud stood open — and while she watched, under the light's full attention, a second one let go, and unfurled.

"…Until we meet again," Mei said to the morning, finishing a stranger's sentence, a sister's. "Beneath the sun's rays."

Far off, in a sky wiped clean, thunder rolled once. The friendly kind. The kind that answers.

Behind her the house was waking — toast, a kettle, an argument resuming exactly where sleep had paused it. Lupa's voice found her through the door, the whole law of the pack in one word.

"Eat."

Mei went in. The blue door closed on the warmth, and kept it.
