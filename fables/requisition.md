# Requisition
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

---

## I.

Startorch ran the pack through Medical over two days and by the end of it three separate physicians had asked to be reassigned.

The intake nurse gave up on the standard form at nine forty on the first morning, having attempted to log nine patients whose combined answer to *previous surgeries* was a small book, and switched to a blank sheet.

"Occupation."

"Student," said Amy.

"Student," said Lynae.

"Rope," said Mei.

The nurse looked up.

"Ignore her," said Iuno.

Luuk Herssen came in at ten with a stack of imaging and an expression of complete serenity, sat down on a rolling stool, and rolled himself into the middle of nine people without appearing to move his feet.

"Right," he said. "Worst first. Lupa."

"I'm fine."

"You're extraordinary," said Luuk, "and you're also carrying four healed fractures that set themselves, two of which set *wrong*, and there's a piece of something in your left shoulder that has been there long enough to have its own blood supply."

"That's from the pens."

"How old were you?"

"Nine? Ten."

A junior physician made a sound at the back of the room and left it.

"I want it out," said Luuk.

"It works."

"It works *around*. There's a difference and your shoulder knows it." He turned a scan toward her. "You've been compensating with your back for eighteen years and one day your back is going to submit its resignation. Two hours. Local. You'll be lifting inside a week."

Lupa's ears went flat.

"Fine."

"I'll book it."

"Fine."

"Say it in a normal voice."

"*Fine.*"

---

Cartethyia's hip was a bad joint and an old fracture that had healed at nineteen without anyone looking at it, and Luuk described three options with total neutrality and then said, "You'll want the third."

"Which is the third?"

"Nothing at all, for now. A stick when the weather turns, and physio twice a week, and I see you in eighteen months."

"That's an option?"

"Most people refuse it because it looks like losing."

Cartethyia thought about it and took it, and then asked whether the stick could be a specific kind, and Luuk said any kind she liked, and by the end of the week she had made one with a carved finch on the handle and had begun to enjoy it enormously.

---

Rebecca's appointment lasted four hours and required two engineers.

"Your coolant's synthetic and it doesn't exist here."

"So make it."

"We'll make it." Luuk turned a page. "Your left arm has an actuator running eleven degrees hot and it's been running eleven degrees hot for at least a year, and I would like to know whether you were aware of that."

"It's *fine*."

"Everyone in this family says that word and every single one of you is lying."

"It's a *known issue*—"

"It's a *seized bearing*," said the older engineer, from under the arm, without looking up, "and if you'd left it another six months you'd have lost the elbow, and whoever built this did beautiful work and terrible maintenance."

Rebecca went quiet.

"Maine did the maintenance," she said.

The engineer put her tools down.

"He did good work," she said. "He just stopped, at some point."

"Yeah."

"We'll take it from here."

---

Mei's readings came back as noise.

They ran her three times. The third time Luuk looked at the output, and then at Mei, and then set the sheet down and said, "I'm going to write *unremarkable*."

"Is it?"

"No. But there's a box, and it has to have a word in it, and every honest word I could put there would put a Collective inspector in this building inside a fortnight." He picked up his pen. "*Unremarkable.*"

Mei looked at him for a while.

"Thanks, Luuk."

"There's a dish on the shelf," he said. "It's Tuesday."

---

## II.

Ciaccona was in and out in six minutes and was walking to the door when Luuk said, "Left hand."

She kept walking.

"Ciaccona."

"It's a bard thing. Everybody's got it."

"Come back and sit down."

She came back and sat down. Iuno, in the corner with a manifest, turned a page and did not look up in a way that was audible across the room.

Luuk took the hand, moved two fingers, and stopped when Ciaccona's face did something.

"How long?"

"Couple of years."

"How long."

"...Four."

"Does it wake you?"

"Sometimes."

"Do you play through it?"

"That's the *job*—"

"Do you play through it."

Ciaccona pulled her hand back and put it in her lap.

"Yes."

"Right." Luuk rolled to a cabinet and came back with a brace and set it on the table between them. "This is a tendon, and it's inflamed, and it has been inflamed for four years, and it will keep being inflamed until something changes. Ninety minutes a day maximum for six weeks. Brace at night. Physio Tuesdays."

"Ninety minutes."

"Per day. Total."

"I play *four hours*—"

"And in three years you'll be playing none at all," said Luuk. "I'd like to keep you in the profession. So: ninety."

The room had gone quiet.

"Who's enforcing it?" said Chisa.

"That's between—"

"Who's enforcing it," said Chisa.

"I'll do it," said Cartethyia.

Ciaccona turned around.

"You'll do *what*—"

"I'll do it," said Cartethyia. "You've walked into that lute in the hallway nine times' worth of my life and you carried me down a bridge and I'll take the brace off you at ninety minutes if I have to sit on you."

"That's an *overreach*—"

"It's a Tuesday and a timer," said Cartethyia. "I'm very good at both."

Ciaccona looked around the room for support and found, in order: Lupa with her arms crossed, Lynae examining the ceiling, Amy grinning, Mei entirely neutral in a way that fooled nobody, and Iuno turning another page of a manifest she had stopped reading eleven minutes ago.

"...Fine."

"Say it in a normal voice," said Lupa.

"I will end you."

---

## III.

Mornye's lab took Amy on the second afternoon and asked to run one more scan, and then another, and then asked Amy, with the careful lightness of a woman who had been rehearsing the question since breakfast, whether she'd be willing to demonstrate.

"Demonstrate what?"

"The thing you did in the Arasaka subnet."

"I bridged for Lucy."

"You bridged for a netrunner across an architecture you'd never seen, from a body, without a deck." Mornye's chair hummed a half-meter closer. "There are four people on this campus who could tell you how that's impossible and I'm three of them."

Amy sat on the bench swinging her heels.

"It's not a big—"

"Amy."

"It's like — " She looked for it. "You know when you walk into a room and you can tell if there's someone else in it? Before you look?"

"Yes."

"It's that, except everything's a room." She shrugged. "Machines are just rooms with the lights on. I can go in."

Mornye's hands went very still on the armrests.

"Go in."

"Yeah."

"Define *go in*."

"I go in," said Amy.

---

The demonstration happened at half four in the diagnostic bay with eight people watching because the pack had heard the word *demonstration* and had arrived as a body.

Mornye had brought a maintenance drone, a small one, four limbs and a lamp, the kind that ran the ventilation crawls.

"Something simple," she said. "Nothing weight-bearing."

"Sure."

"And you'll tell me before you—"

"I'll tell you." Amy sat down on the bench and put her hands in her lap. "Ready?"

"Go ahead."

"Going in."

And the lights in Amy went out.

---

It took about a second.

Her face stayed exactly where it was. Her eyes stayed open. She kept breathing, evenly, on schedule, and she sat upright on the bench with her hands in her lap and her back straight and she was gone.

The drone stood up and turned its lamp on and said, in a flat synthesized register, "*Oh, that's WEIRD—*"

Nobody was watching the drone.

Ciaccona had gone completely white. Cartethyia had both hands over her mouth. Lynae, who had never seen the shell and had only heard about it, was watching *the others* with a slow dawning horror. Mei had taken one step forward and stopped, with her hand halfway out, and stayed like that.

Iuno was looking at Amy's hands.

Open. Palms up. In her lap.

And Lupa walked out of the room.

---

The drone got about four steps before it worked out that something was wrong.

"*—okay, why is nobody—*" It turned its lamp around the bay. "*Where'd Lupa go?*"

"Come back," said Chisa.

"*I've only been—*"

"Amy. Come back."

The drone folded down onto the floor and went dark, and one second later Amy's chest moved differently and her eyes did something and her hands closed and she said, "Ow. Head rush," and then looked up and saw the room.

Nobody had moved.

"...Oh," she said.

"Mm," said Chisa.

"Oh, *no*. Oh, I didn't — " Amy was on her feet. "I didn't think about — Ma. Ma, I'm here, I'm *here*—"

"I know," said Mei, and her voice was fine and her hand was still halfway out.

"Where's Lupa?"

"Outside."

"I'll—"

"Sit down," said Iuno. "Somebody else will go."

Rebecca was already going.

---

She found Lupa on the loading dock behind the building with both hands on the rail and her head down.

Rebecca leaned on the rail beside her and said nothing for about ninety seconds, which for Rebecca constituted an act of extraordinary discipline.

"That's the thing, right," she said eventually. "The eleven weeks."

"Nine."

"You said eleven."

"Cartethyia said that. She had the mornings." Lupa's grip on the rail changed. "I was doing nights. I did nine."

"Right."

"She'd be — " Lupa stopped and started again, flatter. "Her hands were like that. Open. Every night I went in there and her hands were open like that and I'd sit down and I'd—"

She stopped.

"Yeah," said Rebecca.

"It's *her*. It's her, she's fine, she's nineteen and she's an idiot and she's fine, and I *know* that, and I walked out of the room like a—"

"Yeah."

"You're not helping."

"I'm not *trying* to help, I'm standing here." Rebecca kicked the rail. "You want help, go back in and say the thing out loud so the kid knows, because she's currently upstairs deciding she's the worst person alive, and she's *nineteen*, so she'll be very good at deciding that."

Lupa turned her head.

"When did you get like this?"

"I've *always* been like this, you people just decided I was the loud one." Rebecca pushed off the rail. "Also I'm freezing and there's no wall out here and I hate it. Come on."

---

## IV.

They solved it that evening at the kitchen table in the dorm, over four hours, the way this family solves everything, which is with a list.

"Right," said Amy. "Rules. I'm making rules, and then I'm following them, and you can all hold me to them."

"We'll hold you to them," said Iuno.

"You'll *enjoy* holding me to them—"

"Immensely. Continue."

Amy wrote on the back of a lab printout.

"One. I say *going in* before, and I say something stupid when I come back, so there's a bracket on both ends and nobody's guessing."

"What counts as stupid," said Lynae.

"Anything. It's a *word*. It's proof of life."

"Say a number," said Chisa. "Different one each time. Then we know it's current."

Amy pointed at her. "Better. Yes. Numbers."

"Two," said Lupa.

Everyone looked at her. She had come in at some point and had been standing behind Amy's chair for a while.

"Two," said Lupa. "Somebody's got a hand on you. The whole time. That's not — " Her jaw worked. "That's not for you. That's for me. I'll take the shift."

"Okay," said Amy.

"You don't have to—"

"I said okay." Amy wrote it down. "Two. Somebody's got a hand on me. Lupa's got first refusal on the shift and everybody else rotates. Three?"

"Three," said Chisa. "I listen. There's a frequency and it goes with you, and I can hear the difference between *out* and *gone*, and if it goes wrong I'll know before any of the equipment does."

"Can you actually?"

"I've been doing it since the diagnostic bay," said Chisa. "It's about four notes down and to the left."

"Four," said Iuno. "There's a clock, and it's on the table, and it's running, and if you pass eleven minutes I'm coming to get you and I'll have Chisa's frequency and Mornye's hardware and a very poor attitude."

"Eleven's arbitrary—"

"Eleven is the number I can tolerate," said Iuno, "and this list is for us, and I'm on it."

Amy wrote *11 (Iuno's number)*.

And then Cartethyia got up and left the room and came back with the felt Mei from the shelf and put it in Amy's hands.

The kitchen went quiet.

"Five," said Cartethyia.

Amy looked down at nine inches of felt, black yarn hair, a coat from a year she'd missed, two small gold buttons.

"You put her in my hands."

"I did it for eleven weeks and it did nothing at all," said Cartethyia, "and now you're in there and you can *hold* things, so it'll do something. Fold your fingers before you go. Then they're closed, and nobody in this room has to look at them open."

Amy folded her fingers.

"Five," she said. "Ma goes in the hands."

"That's *horrifying*," said Rebecca, delighted, from the counter.

"It's *practical*."

"Horrifying and practical, that's the whole family, I've been saying this."

Mei, at the end of the table, had her chin in her hand and had not said anything for about twenty minutes.

"Ma?"

"Six," said Mei.

"Go."

"You do it in front of us." Mei sat up. "Every time. No sneaking off to a maintenance crawl at two in the morning because you didn't want to make anyone sit through it. If it's worth doing it's worth doing where we can see you."

Amy wrote it down.

Then she said, "That's a rule for you, not me."

"It's a rule for the *house*," said Mei. "I've made every mistake on this list personally and I'd like at least one generation to skip a step."

---

## V.

The yard was on the north edge of campus and held eleven decommissioned units under tarpaulin, and Lynae found the right one in under a minute because she had shot it.

"That's it."

"You're sure?"

"Hyvatia-class, Unit Six." Lynae took a corner of the tarp. "There's a hole in the left sensor housing about the size of my thumb, and I put it there."

She pulled.

The pack stood in the cold and looked at it for a while.

Startorch had built the Hyvatia line to be looked at. That was half the design brief and everybody on campus knew it — a guard platform for an institution that put its endowment on its walls, so: white ceramic composite, gold at every seam, a slim armored figure with a high crowned helm and a visor of blue glass, and eleven feather-shaped flight blades folded down her back and flanks in two descending ranks like a bird at rest. Each blade had a jet housed along its spine. Each housing was full of a year of grit.

She was on her knees where the recovery crew had parked her. The white had gone the grey of old snow. There was moss in the left knee joint. And where her left eye should have sat there was a socket burned black through three layers of housing, with a puncture at the center of it about the size of a thumb.

Across her lap, held in both gauntlets the way you hold a thing you have been told to hold, lay the lance.

It was longer than she was tall. Sectioned white and gold, tapering to a bare emitter throat, dead and dark and still faintly ringing when the wind came off the ridge and touched it.

"Okay," said Rebecca, into the silence. "*Okay.*"

"Mm," said Chisa.

"No, I need somebody to say it out loud, because I've been in this world for a month and everybody keeps acting like this is *normal*." Rebecca pointed with her whole arm. "That's an *angel*. Your school has a *dead angel* in a *car park*."

"It's a security platform," said Lynae.

"It's got *WINGS*—"

"Eleven flight blades on a vectored mount," said Mornye's voice out of the handheld, "and if any of them still turn over I'll want to know about it before anyone does anything enthusiastic."

Cartethyia had gone very quiet, which from Cartethyia was a siren.

Mei stood looking up at it with her hands in her pockets.

"Ma."

"Mm."

"You okay?"

Mei considered the question with real seriousness, which was what she did with questions worth the delay.

"It's shorter than I remember," she said.

"It's *five meters*—"

"I was underneath it." She walked forward and put her hand flat on the shin plating and left it there, and then knocked on it twice, the way you knock on a table. "Yeah. Okay. It's a machine."

"You okay if I go in it?"

"Kill it or use it," said Mei. "It's had a year of the third option and look what that's done for anybody. Go on."

---

The rules were run in full. Clock on a crate. Chisa on a crate beside it with her eyes shut. Lupa sitting on the ground behind Amy with a hand flat between her shoulder blades. Felt Mei in the folded hands.

"Going in. Number's *forty-one*."

"Forty-one," said Iuno, and started the clock.

Amy went out.

The mech did nothing for six seconds.

Then a hydraulic somewhere in the spine let go with a bang that put four birds off the roof of the machine shop, and Unit Six came up off her knees in a rising groan of a year's settled weight, and stood, and swayed, and put one gauntlet down on the yard fence and flattened forty meters of it.

"*SORRY—*"

"THAT'S NEW," yelled Rebecca, delighted, from behind a container.

"*IT'S GOT — okay, she's got a REALLY BAD SHOULDER, hang on—*" The mech straightened. The remaining sensor came around and found them and dipped. "*Also she's blind on the left, obviously, so anything I hit on the left is canonically Lynae's fault.*"

"It was a *good shot*—"

"*It was a great shot, I've been in here nine seconds and I want to shake your hand.*"

"Six minutes," said Iuno.

"*I'm FINE—*"

"Six minutes and I'm reading it out at every one."

The first four steps were terrible. She put a foot through a drainage grating and had to be talked out of it by Mornye over the handheld, and by the sixth minute she had gotten as far as walking a straight line with her arms out like a toddler and the lance dragging a furrow behind her through the gravel.

"*Wings are seized,*" Amy reported. "*All eleven. Two of them won't even acknowledge me, they're just — they're a wall.*"

"Leave them," said Mornye.

"*I wasn't going to—*"

"You were absolutely going to."

"*...I was thinking about it.*"

"Nine minutes," said Iuno.

"*Coming out. Coming out.*"

Unit Six knelt — badly, with a crunch that made two engineers wince — and went dark.

Amy's fingers moved on the felt.

"Ninety-six," she said, out loud, to the yard, "which is a lie, the number was forty-one, and I said ninety-six on purpose to see if any of you were paying attention."

"Eleven of us were paying attention," said Iuno.

"There's nine of us."

"I was paying attention three times."

---

And then Cartethyia said, "How tall is she?"

Four people turned around at the tone.

"Cartethyia."

"I'm asking a *question*—"

"You've got a *stick*," said Ciaccona. "You've got a stick and a physiotherapy appointment on Tuesday and Luuk said—"

"He said *walking*."

"He did not mean—"

Cartethyia was already moving.

What Cartethyia does, when Cartethyia decides, takes about four seconds and involves a great deal of light. There is a sound to it that Chisa describes as *a chord resolving upward* and that Rebecca, on first exposure, had described in language that got her banned from the infirmary. Three blades come up out of nothing and sing. The yard's floodlights browned out. Something went through everyone's back teeth.

And Fleurdelys stood in a decommissioning yard on the north edge of a university campus in her full height and her full armor with her hair going in a wind that had no business existing indoors, radiant, enormous, holy, and absolutely delighted.

**"BACK TO BACK,"** she said.

"Absolutely not—"

**"GET HER UP. BACK TO BACK. I HAVE TO KNOW."**

"That's an *institutional asset*—"

**"SO AM I."**

Amy was already sitting down on the crate. "Hand."

"Amy—"

"*Hand*, Lupa, I'm going in, this is the best thing that's ever happened to me. Going in. Number's *twelve*."

"Twelve," said Iuno, in the voice of a woman who has decided to survive the next eleven minutes and file about it afterward.

---

It took three attempts, because Unit Six's knees were a year past their service interval and Fleurdelys kept laughing, which moved her.

They got it on the third. Two engineers with a laser level, one goddess, one guard mech, and seven people yelling contradictory instructions in a car park at half four in the afternoon.

"HOLD STILL—"

"*I AM HOLDING—*"

**"SHE'S LEANING."**

"She's *blind on one side*, she leans, that's a *known issue*—"

"Level's reading," said the senior engineer, and everything stopped.

She walked around them once with the instrument up.

"Guard platform," she said. "By fourteen centimeters."

The yard came apart.

Rebecca went face-down in the gravel. Lynae, who never made noise, made a noise. Ciaccona started composing out loud, immediately, badly. Amy took Unit Six's remaining arm straight up in the air in a gesture of such total unearned triumph that the shoulder actuator failed audibly and the arm came back down on its own about four seconds later, which made everything worse.

**"FOURTEEN CENTIMETERS,"** said Fleurdelys.

"*FOURTEEN—*"

**"THAT'S NOTHING. THAT'S A HELMET. I'LL TAKE THE HELMET OFF—"**

"*THE MEASUREMENT STANDS—*"

**"I HAVE NEVER LOST A HEIGHT—"**

"You've never *contested* a height, there's nobody to contest, you're the only—" Ciaccona stopped. Looked up. Looked at the two of them, back to back, gold and white and grey, one of them radiant and one of them filthy, both of them enormous, both of them arguing at a volume that was flattening the grass. "...Oh no."

"What?"

"We've got two of them," said Ciaccona. "That's two now. Two giant flying lunatic women. In one house."

Chisa opened her eyes.

"Only one of them flies," she said.

"Yet," said Amy, out of the mech, with the tone of a girl making an appointment.

## VI.

The acquisition took Iuno four days and a single form.

She started from the position that the unit was scheduled for materials recovery, that materials recovery was contracted out, that the contract had a clause about units carrying an active research designation, and that a research designation required a supervising faculty member and a named operator.

Mornye supplied the faculty member.

"What am I supervising?"

"A study."

"Of what?"

"Of *direct neural interface with legacy security platforms*," said Iuno, writing, "which is a real field, which you are qualified in, and which describes what happened in that yard with more accuracy than anything else on this campus could manage."

"That's actually true."

"Everything on this form is true. That's what makes it work." Iuno turned it around. "Sign there."

The named operator field caused an eleven-minute argument.

"Put my name," said Amy.

"Your name goes on the *operator* line. The line above it is the unit designation, and it will appear on every manifest, requisition, maintenance docket and insurance schedule this institution files for the next forty years." Iuno held the pen out. "Choose carefully."

Amy took the pen.

"Nyx Ravenhollow."

The room made a sound.

"*No*—"

"You cannot put that on a—"

"It's *my mech*—"

"It's an *institutional asset*—"

"It's *my mech*," said Amy, "and I have carried that name since I was fourteen and put it on five character sheets and every single one of you has been *horrible* to me about it, and now there's forty tons of it and I'm going to write it down in *ink*."

"Lupa," said Ciaccona. "Say something."

Lupa looked at the form for a while.

"It's a different Nyx Ravenhollow," she said.

"THAT'S NOT — you're *encouraging* her—"

"There's a precedent," said Lupa. "I established it. I'm bound by it, same as Iuno was."

Amy wrote it in the box.

Iuno read it, exhaled through her nose in a way that took about four seconds, and filed it.

---

## VII.

Restoration took five weeks and most of a workshop.

The wings came first, because Amy would talk about nothing else. Eleven flight blades, each one a vectored mount with a jet housed along the spine, each one full of a year of grit and two of them cracked through the root and requiring a fabrication order that Iuno got signed by describing it, accurately, as *structural*.

Lupa did the heavy pulls with her shoulder eleven days out of surgery and was caught at it twice.

Rebecca lived under the left arm for three weeks with the older engineer and came out of it able to name every actuator in the shoulder assembly and with strong views about four of them, and at some point in the second week stopped saying *the mech* and started saying *her*, and nobody mentioned it, and Cartethyia wrote the date down.

Lynae rebuilt the sensor housing herself. She did it alone, on a Sunday, without announcing it, and when Amy came in and found the socket cleaned out and shimmed and a replacement lens seated and the burn scoring polished back to bare composite, Lynae was already at the door with her coat on.

"You didn't have to—"

"I made the hole," said Lynae. "I'm not sentimental about it. It was a bad piece of work and it's been sitting in the rain for a year."

"Lynae."

"Mm."

"Thanks."

"Don't put my name on anything," said Lynae, and left.

---

The lance took the longest, because the lance was a weapon and weapons required a committee.

Iuno went to the committee.

The committee produced a permit inside nine days that authorized a *research emitter, tethered, low-yield, supervised*, and every one of those four words was doing work that a lawyer would have admired and none of them were technically false.

"Low-yield," said Amy, reading it.

"At the settings I entered."

"And who sets the settings?"

"The operator," said Iuno, and put the cap on her pen.

---

They flew her on a Thursday in the eleventh week of term, off the north pad, at six in the morning, with Mornye's whole department watching from the observation deck and about two hundred students watching from windows they had no business being at.

The rules were run in full. Clock. Hand. Frequency. Felt Mei in the folded hands.

"Going in. Number's *seven*."

"Seven."

Amy went out.

Unit Six stood up clean for the first time — quiet all the way, the knees taking it in one smooth rising motion — and white ceramic came up off the pad into six in the morning with the light going pink and level across the ridge behind her, and she stood there in it, gold at every seam, one blue eye and one brand new one, holding six meters of dead lance in both gauntlets.

Then the wings came out.

Eleven flight blades unfolded off her back and flanks in two descending ranks, one after another, in sequence, with a sound like a deck of cards being fanned by something enormous, and locked, and lit — and the jets came up along eleven spines at once in a rising blue that went through the frost on the pad in a ring.

The observation deck stopped talking.

She went up.

She went up slowly, which was the correct call and which Amy had been drilled on by four separate people, and she held at forty meters and turned once on her axis with the whole ridge behind her, and the light caught her, and she brought the lance up and around into a low guard and the emitter throat took the charge and eight meters of blue came out of the end of it and kept going.

Somebody on the observation deck said a word that carried.

And Ciaccona, on the pad, with her braced hand over her mouth, said, into the noise, "...Okay, the name's *fine*."

"WHAT?"

"THE NAME'S FINE," Ciaccona shouted. "I'M SAYING IT ONCE. I'M SAYING IT NOW BECAUSE IN ELEVEN MINUTES I'M GOING TO BE UNBEARABLE ABOUT HAVING SAID IT—"

"SAY IT LOUDER—"

"*THE NAME IS CORRECT AND I WITHDRAW FOUR WEEKS OF—*"

"I CAN'T HEAR YOU OVER MY EXTREMELY COOL MECH," said Nyx Ravenhollow, from forty meters up, on external speakers, at a volume that set off two car alarms in the faculty lot.

---

They painted it on the shoulder plate that afternoon.

Rebecca did the stencil, because Rebecca had the markers, and the only color available in the quantity required was the acid green off a Night City billboard at three in the morning, and by then nobody suggested another one.

It came out four feet high on the left shoulder, above the new lens, in a font Amy had designed at fourteen and reproduced from memory with total accuracy.

**NYX RAVENHOLLOW.**

"Somebody in a Collective office is going to open a manifest in eleven months," said Iuno, "and read that, and have an entire afternoon about it, and I would like it noted that I raised this concern in writing and was overruled by a *show of hands*."

"You voted for it," said Chisa.

"I abstained."

"You raised your hand."

"To *speak*."

"You raised it and then you said 'fine' in the voice," said Cartethyia, from a folding chair, with her stick across her knees and her ankles crossed and the enormous serenity of a woman who had lost a height contest by fourteen centimeters and had already scheduled a rematch.

Amy sat on the section of yard fence still standing, with a mug in both hands, looking up at five meters of restored guard platform with her fourteen-year-old handwriting on its shoulder and a brand-new eye where Lynae had put a hole.

Her mother sat beside her doing the same thing.

"Ma."
