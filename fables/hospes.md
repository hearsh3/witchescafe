Rome kept small gods for the parts of a door. Forculus had the leaf. Limentinus had the threshold, which is the piece that does nothing and settles everything. Cardea had the hinge, and her office survives in one line that has embarrassed its commentators ever since: *she opens what is shut, and shuts what is open.* Scholars have spent centuries deciding whether that describes a guardian or a burglar. The argument runs on because the line is accurate in both readings.

A theologian counted the three of them and laughed in his book. One household, one door, three deities. He is still quoted. Nobody bothered to answer him at the time, so the answer can be entered now: a door is a machine with several opinions, and it looks simple from the side that is already inside.

Those same people carried a *tessera hospitalis*. Two households would take a small thing — clay, bone, a coin worn past its portrait — and break it between them, and each kept a half, and the break was the entire instrument. No two breaks are alike. A break can be matched and cannot be forged. Sixty years on, a stranger could walk into a city his grandfather never saw, put a piece of rubbish on a table, and watch the household go to a box and produce its own piece of rubbish; and if the edges agreed, the man ate at that table and slept behind that bolt, and the gods of the door considered the matter closed.

The word for the guest was *hospes*. The word for the host was *hospes*. So was the word for the stranger, because at the threshold the question is still open, and the language had the manners to leave it open. One consonant away stood *hostis*, which began as the word for the foreigner and finished as the word for the enemy. The drift took about two hundred years, which is fast for a language and slow for a night.

Under a glacier, in a city that built its own sun and hung it on a schedule, there is a dormitory kitchen with eleven cups on a rack and twelve hooks to hang them from. Somebody who is dead now built that rack. The spare hook has been empty since the carpentry.

At three in the morning, in the hour after the mint has shut and before it opens, that kitchen belongs to the kettle and to the woman who counts.

## I. Limentinus

She came down the east stairwell with the pot in her hand and her whole morning rewritten.

An hour ago the steam on the balcony had leaned against a vent drawing the other way, and a dead woman made of weather had spent nearly the last of her name to say two things: *Ovathrax*, and *the youngest gate*. Iuno had stood at the rail afterward and let the arithmetic run, and the arithmetic had produced a war with a date on it and a house full of people eleven days out of surgery, and then it had produced, quietly, the only order she was still qualified to give herself.

Breakfast. All of it. First.

*You are going to cook,* the High Priestess had observed on the landing, *at a war.*

*I am going to cook at a Tuesday. The war can wait until people have eaten.*

The kitchen light was on.

Iuno stopped one step short of the doorway and did what she did, which was count. The vent cycle: correct. The kettle: warm, past its click, cooling at the rate of a kettle boiled about six minutes ago. The magnolia outside the window shedding on schedule. Two heartbeats three floors up, reading through the crystal into the bone of her shoulder, steady as surf, the meridian she navigates by.

And in the room, one chair pulled out and occupied, and a third heartbeat that the crystal declined to find.

The woman at the table had a face Iuno could not have drawn.

She knew the shape of the coveralls — Riseway transit, night shift, the grey ones with the reflective band at the cuff. She knew the knee brace. She knew, without effort, the name that went with the badge, because Iuno keeps a ledger of every person in this city who has ever done her a courtesy: *Tesch, O., night desk, second gate, sixty-one good evenings, one loaned pen.* She knew all of that, and she could not have described the face in a court of law, and she understood in the same half-second that this had always been true and had never once mattered until now.

The teapot on the table was warmed. Not filled and standing; *warmed first*, the way you warm it, swilled and emptied and then charged.

Two cups. The near one turned handle-out, guest-side.

Iuno stood in her own doorway and was made a guest in four seconds by a woman with a watering-can's patience and no heartbeat.

*Well,* said the High Priestess, and stopped there, which she almost never did.

Iuno crossed the room. She set her own pot down on the counter, because a pot on a counter is a pot that has not been surrendered. Then she reached across the table and took the cup out of the stranger's hands.

It came away without resistance. It was the one with the chip on the rim, the one that lives on the third hook, the one Amy steals at four minutes every morning with the ceremonial slowness of a spy conceding a square.

<font color=royalblue>"That's my niece's."</font>

The word came out of her mouth flat and unrehearsed and entirely load-bearing, and she heard it land, and the woman at the table heard it land, and the woman at the table put her hands in her lap like someone who has just been handed the whole household on a saucer.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Is it."</font>

Iuno washed it. Hot water, the pad, thirty seconds, the whole liturgy, in full view, at the sink with her back turned on four hundred years of appetite. Then she dried it and hung it on the third hook, and took another cup down, and set it on the table.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You washed it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I've been drinking out of it for twenty minutes. If I'd left anything in it, thirty seconds and a sponge would be a very optimistic answer."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The sponge wasn't for you."</font>

The stranger considered that. Then her mouth did something small and pleased at one corner, and she poured, and slid the fresh cup across, handle out, guest-side, into Iuno's own kitchen.

Iuno sat down in the guest chair.

She drank. It was very good. She held it in her mouth the length of a breath and read it the way she reads everything.

<font color=royalblue>"Four minutes."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"To the second."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mine never are."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I know. I checked. Six seconds of spread across two hundred and eleven mornings, and it tracks the vent cycle, and on the days you've slept badly it runs long."</font> The stranger folded her hands. <font color="#fdb4fe">"I did wonder whether to build the error in. I decided that copying a woman's mistakes is a kind of familiarity, and we haven't been introduced."</font>

*Ah,* the High Priestess said, from the far side of the sternum, where she had gone very quiet and very upright. *There she is.*

Iuno put the cup down and set her crystal hand flat on the table.

The stump takes the world in as bearings now. Wood, at that distance, reports as a low grain. Two floors up, the meridian: one deep and even, one lighter with a snag in it where a girl grips a sleeve in her sleep. Across the table, at eleven inches, through four legs of pine and a woman's forearms —

— her own pulse. Arriving back at her a half-second late, like a voice returned off a wall.

She left her hand where it was for three full beats, which was longer than she needed and exactly as long as she intended, and then she took it back.

<font color=royalblue>"The chair has a short leg."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Does it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Front left. Everyone in this house compensates without knowing they do it. Cartethyia sits on one foot. Lupa braces the table."</font> Iuno looked at the chair, and at the woman in it, and at the four points of contact that had not moved in the entire conversation. <font color=royalblue>"You've been perfectly level since I came in."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I'm very poised."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Are you in my kitchen?"</font>

The stranger smiled. It was warm and it was slightly sheepish and it had more architecture in it than the east wing.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I'm in *a* kitchen."</font>

The magnolia outside dropped a petal into the dark. The kettle ticked as it cooled. Somewhere three floors up the lighter heartbeat snagged and resettled.

<font color=royalblue>"Then I'll set the terms for this one,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"You stay off the stairs. You leave nothing behind that I haven't handled. You're gone before Helios, because there are ten people asleep in this building who have earned a boring morning and I intend to give them one."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"And in exchange?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You get the tea. It's already made. You made it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That's a very poor bargain, Doorkeeper."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It's the whole of my remaining leverage and I've decided to look confident about it."</font>

The Grand Architect of the Fractsidus laughed out loud in a dormitory kitchen at three in the morning, and it was a real laugh, and it came from a real place, and that was the first genuinely frightening thing that happened that night.

## II. Cardea

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Odile Tesch,"</font> the stranger said, tapping the badge on her breast. <font color="#fdb4fe">"You had it before I sat down. Sixty-one good evenings. She loaned you a pen last winter and you gave it back with a new cartridge in it, which she noticed, and mentioned to her sister, and has told approximately nobody else."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Where is she."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Asleep in a flat on the ninth terrace with the window open, which I disapprove of. Nothing has happened to Odile Tesch. <font color=royalblue>"Is that the pitch?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"It's an observation. I don't pitch to the retired; it's rude and the returns are terrible."</font> She turned her cup a quarter-turn on the table. *She was on the stairwell,* the High Priestess said.

*Or she has better sources than the wind. Or she is telling me a true thing sideways so that I'll spend the next four hours checking the wrong wall.*

*All three,* said the High Priestess. *That's how it's done. I used to do it.*

<font color=royalblue>"You built me a room,"</font> she said. <font color=royalblue>"Last winter. In the simulation."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That's good. That's — genuinely good, for a first exposure. What broke it?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You've cut yourself."</font>

The word went down on the table like a coin landing on its edge.

The Architect looked at it. She had spent an evening, some months ago, in a private theatre with a shrine maiden, running seven reels to prove one thesis — that love is a thing which ends and vanishes, that the ending erases the having, that a story concludes and the concluding is the whole of it. She had built the argument out of a hundred years of somebody else's grief and it had been, by any professional standard, airtight.

A woman with forty years in her purse had answered it in one syllable and gone back to her tea.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I've read you,"</font> the Architect said. It came out lighter than she had loaded it. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Fourteen printings. It's a hobby. <font color=royalblue>"And the fourteenth?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Somebody left a light on."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

Iuno refilled both cups. She made certain of her hand first.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Thirty years,"</font> the Architect said. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Boards and threads and a hired corpse and a girl pulled out of the water with a name written over the top of her. I have the file. It runs to four volumes and the last one is mostly weeping. Why?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The world was going to end."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Your turn,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"Denia. The collar. Two years of study sessions and a girl in a chair at the end of them. Why?"</font>

## III. Forculus

The old temple took its fee at the door and never once made an exception, and the reason had nothing to do with money. A reading given free is a favour, and a favour is a debt, and a debt sits in the room and reads over your shoulder while you work. So the girls on the Capitoline learned it before they learned the sky: *fee first, then the water.* The client pays and the client owes nothing, and both parties can afford to hear what comes.

<font color=royalblue>"I'm retired,"</font> Iuno said.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You resigned. In public. At cost. I have the minutes."</font> A shrug. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Resignation is an arrangement with an institution. I'm not an institution. I'm a woman at a table at half past three."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I don't have a basin."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You have attention. It was always the working part."</font>

*She's right,* said the High Priestess. *It infuriates me and she's right.*

Iuno sat with it. Then she reached past the teapot, took down the wooden salt box, and set it in the middle of the table between them, lid off, which was the closest thing that kitchen had to an altar.

<font color=royalblue>"Fee first."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Ah."</font> The Architect brightened, genuinely, the way she did at any properly kept rule. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Coin, or the older way?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I keep the mint. Try me."</font>

The stranger reached into the breast pocket of a set of Riseway coveralls, and what came out of it had no business in this city.

Five pieces of type. Lead and antimony, hand-cut, each one a small heavy rectangle with a nick down one side so a compositor's thumb can find the foot in the dark. They went onto the table in a row, face up, with the small deliberate clicks of somebody setting chess pieces.

Iuno looked at them for a while.

<font color=royalblue>"Bookbinders' Row."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Mengzhou. Yes."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You paid in very old coin, and you wore a different face every visit, and the founders talk about you at dinner."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"The founders talk about everything at dinner. It's a very small street."</font>

Iuno picked up the first sort and weighed it on her palm, then on the crystal, which is more honest. Then she took a spoon out of the drawer, turned it over, and stood the type up in a row along the back of it, and read them in the bowl of the spoon, in the mirror, the way type is meant to be read.

Four faces and one blank.

The characters were Huanglong hand, cut deep and clean, and they were not new. She had enough of the language to take two of them whole and one of them by its bones. The second sort had a fracture running up through the shoulder of it, arrested with a shim of softer metal by somebody who had wanted very badly to keep a broken thing usable.

She read the row twice. It gave her the tail of a phrase, an infinitive that meant *to come to nothing*, and, at the end, half of a character that wanted to be a name and had been snapped off at the radical.

<font color=royalblue>"This is somebody's inscription."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"It's a paid bill."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Whose?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Mine, now. That's the custom on the Row and I keep the customs of every street I work. She spent it; the founders lifted each sort out with tongs and read it aloud so she could hear what it cost; the tray clicked shut and went into the case, and I bought the case."</font> The Architect looked at the row of little metal blocks the way a collector looks at a thing already owned. <font color="#fdb4fe">"I've been buying type on that street for a season."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"What are you printing?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Nothing at all."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Then why buy it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"So that it can't be set."</font>

The magnolia let go of another petal. Iuno kept her hands exactly where they were and let one whole second go past her.

She picked up the fifth piece. Same lead, same nick, same weight in the hand. She turned it twice under the light and found the printing face smooth all the way across, clean and flat and finished, cast on purpose to leave the paper alone.

<font color=royalblue>"This one's blank."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That's a quad. It's the most expensive thing on this table."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It has nothing on it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"It holds the space. You lock up a page and every word in it leans on its neighbours, and the only reason there's room for a word you haven't set yet is that somebody put a piece of metal there to keep the others off."</font> A beat. <font color="#fdb4fe">"It holds. That's the entire job. Ask any compositor which sort they'd rather lose."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Fee accepted,"</font> she said. <font color=royalblue>"Sit back. Hands on the table. Tell it in your own order and don't tidy it."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Somebody told me about a counterfeit that smelled of iris. Correct tea, correct blanket, correct chipped cup, and iris all through it, because the whole of it was built out of what a mourner had kept."</font> Iuno turned the salt box a half-turn, squaring it. <font color=royalblue>"That's the same flaw. You've built one perfect four-minute cup and one perfect face and both of them are copies of a remembering. It's the only mistake you make. You make it every time."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Is that the reading?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That's the diagnosis. You had one of those in the rain a few months ago and I'm told you found it irritating."</font> Iuno put both hands flat on the table, the flesh one and the glass one. <font color=royalblue>"Go back to the afternoon. How was the grass cut?"</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"…Badly. I said."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"By whom?"</font>

The pause that followed had a floor and a ceiling and four walls, and the Architect stood in the middle of it.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"It's grass."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It's *maintained*,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"Grass that height means a schedule and a blade and somebody who came round on a day and did it wrong, and either they were in a hurry or the machine was old or they were doing it for the first time and nobody had shown them. There was a person. That person had the job. Which means there was a house, and the house had someone in it who kept it, and you have been standing in that afternoon for four hundred years staring at a hand."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I have examined that dream — "</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You've examined the two of you. You've never once turned round."</font>

The Architect of the Fractsidus sat in a dormitory kitchen with her hands flat on the table where a retired priestess had put them, and did not speak.

⁂

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That's a very small reading,"</font> she said at last.

<font color=royalblue>"It's the whole of it. You can have it in writing if you want, and I won't charge for the ink."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You've told me to look at a lawn."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I've told you that somebody was keeping the place where the good thing happened to you. You've spent four centuries auditing the good thing and you have never once costed the upkeep."</font> Iuno picked up the teapot and found it empty and set it down. <font color=royalblue>"Everybody does it. It's the commonest error in the trade. I did it for a month in that bed upstairs, running the bottom line of my own accounts every night at two in the morning and never looking at the days."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"And what fixed you?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Somebody kept a room swept."</font>

The Architect went still. It was the stillness a president had watched happen once on a terrace bench beside a row of drowning marigolds: a mind arriving at data it has no shelf for, holding position while the shelving is built.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"How many are you?"</font> she said.

<font color=royalblue>"Two."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"And she stays."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"She has a chair."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I've had thousands,"</font> the Architect said. <font color="#fdb4fe">"I keep the good habits. I keep the way one of them turned a cup and the way another one held a pen, and the whole of it is in me somewhere, filed, and none of it *answers*. It's a wardrobe. You've got a wardrobe that talks back and I would very much like to know the mechanism."</font>

She turned her head slightly, then, and addressed the air over Iuno's left shoulder with perfect courtesy.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Priestess."</font>

Iuno felt the whole country under her collarbone come to its feet.

*Let me.*

*No.*

*Darling. She said your name in a corridor once and the walls came down. Let me say hers.*

Iuno set her jaw and, in full view, so that it cost her, opened the door.

What came out of her mouth was her own voice pitched somewhere it does not go, older and grander and about four hundred percent more theatrical, and the ceiling light leaned by a degree.

<font color=royalblue>"You believe every story finishes the same way."</font>

The Architect's eyes narrowed with something close to joy.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"They do. I've read all of them."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You've read the last page of a great many. You've never been to one."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"…Meaning?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I have been subtracted,"</font> said the High Priestess of Septimont, through the throat of a woman she has occupied for fifty years. <font color=royalblue>"Nine and a half minutes, in the Nihility, entire. The word *nothing* is a wild overstatement; nothing ran over the void's budget. Then somebody used one word with my name nowhere in it, the sentence required me, and the universe worked backwards from the grammar."</font> A pause. <font color=royalblue>"That is a primary source, and it is the only one you will ever be handed, because you cannot go and you cannot send. So attend. I came back into a shape that had been kept. Swept. Dusted. Furnished, by a woman who declined to redecorate for the length of a war."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That's a housekeeping arrangement."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

The Architect opened her mouth and closed it.

Twice in one hour, in one kitchen, from two different women in one body, she had been handed the same answer, and it was about grass and it was about sweeping, and she had four hundred years of correspondence and a network across two continents and a private theatre with velvet seats, and the counter-argument declined to arrive.

Iuno came back into her own voice the way you come up a step you'd forgotten was there.

<font color=royalblue>"She'll be insufferable about that for a month,"</font> she said.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I should think so."</font> The Architect picked up her cup, found it empty, and set it down. <font color="#fdb4fe">"You have a *mother* in there."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I have a colleague."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Mm."</font>

Something went across the borrowed face then that had no business on it, and it lasted about a second, and Iuno — who has spent her life reading faces at range and has just spent an hour failing to read this one — read it clean.

The Architect hummed.

Four bars. Under her breath, absent, the way a person hums while filing. It was an entr'acte, and it came from a piece of foreign matter that had been the rage of the Mengzhou season, three acts, a god who dies at the end of the second, an opera about a woman with two names.

Behind Iuno's sternum, in a velvet lounge with a new window and an open door, a woman who hums that exact tune badly, on purpose, over the shuffling of cards, went absolutely silent.

<font color=royalblue>"Stop."</font>

The humming stopped.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Ah."</font> The Architect's eyes were bright. <font color="#fdb4fe">"That's a real one. I've had four hours of your professional face and there's a real one."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Where did you learn it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"A teahouse off the Row. Third act. Everybody weeps into their cups on schedule and the god dies before the interval, which is a structural error and the season adored it."</font> She tilted her head. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Why?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"No reason."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Mm,"</font> the Architect said, and filed the whole reaction whole, and let it lie, because a woman who has been alive that long knows when to leave money on the table.

## IV. Clusivius

She stood up at four.

<font color=royalblue>"Wait."</font>

Iuno went to the rack.

There are eleven cups in that kitchen and twelve hooks, because a dead carpenter cut the rail to a standard length and had the good sense to leave the spare unexplained. Ten of those cups have hung there a year. The eleventh has hung there three weeks and gets stolen every morning at four minutes. Iuno had counted the hooks in her first week in the building and had been walking past the empty one ever since.

She took a plain white cup down from the cupboard, checked it for chips, and hung it on the twelfth hook.

Then she went back to the sink and started on the eggs.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"What is that."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"A cup."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Yes, thank you, I can see that it's a — "</font> The Architect stopped. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Doorkeeper."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"There's a hook. There's been a hook since the building went up. It's been bothering me since spring."</font> Iuno cracked an egg one-handed on the rim of the bowl, which is a thing she taught herself over nine months and has never once mentioned to anybody. <font color=royalblue>"Now there's a cup on it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"With a condition attached."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"No."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"There's always a condition. I've read your file. Thirty years of conditions, magnificently drafted."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That was the old firm."</font> Second egg. <font color=royalblue>"We don't plan the people any more. We plan the luggage. A gift with a rider on it is an order in a nice hat, and I've been retired from orders since a purple evening. So: there's a cup. Use it, break it, take it with you, ignore it for a hundred years. Nobody will ask you anything and nobody will thank you, which I'm told is the important part."</font>

The Architect stood in the middle of a kitchen with her hand on the back of a chair with a short leg.

She stayed there a while.

Iuno kept her back to the room and did the third egg and the fourth and heard, in the specific quality of the silence, an entire negotiation being conducted by somebody who had not moved a muscle. A woman had offered her the same thing on a terrace, months ago, holding a watering can over drowning marigolds — *sit in the study room, borrow her pens, be ordinary* — and she had put down one flat syllable and left. Kindness has a shape and the shape is a hand out, and a hand out has to be taken, and taking is a thing done with the whole body while somebody watches.

This was inventory. This was a hook that had been empty since the carpentry and a woman who counted things and had finally got round to it.

It was very nearly small enough.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Ask me in Jinzhou,"</font> she said.

The whisk stopped.

Iuno turned around. Her face did nothing at all, which took everything she had.

<font color=royalblue>"Say that again."</font>

The Architect picked her hat up off the table and put it on with the practical efficiency of a woman finishing a shift.

<font color=royalblue>"Say it again."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"The willows on the west bank come out three days ahead of the ones on the east,"</font> the Architect said pleasantly. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Nobody has ever explained it. The whole city argues about it every spring as though the argument were new. It's the single most charming municipal fact I've collected in two hundred years and I recommend it to you sincerely."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Where."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Mm."</font>

She went to the door. At the door she stopped, one hand on the frame, and turned her head just enough.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"There's a man who reads the sluice gauges at the third watch,"</font> she said. <font color="#fdb4fe">"Above a city in a bowl of water. Very ordinary. Wife, two daughters, a bad back, thirty-one years in the same post. His name is — "</font>

<font color=royalblue>"No."</font>

The Architect's eyebrows went up, honestly surprised, which was worth watching.

<font color=royalblue>"I know what that is,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"You hand me a name to protect and it goes in my chest and it steers me for the rest of my life, and it costs you one breath. I drafted a woman into a death with an instrument that size. I'm not taking delivery of one at four in the morning in my own kitchen."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You've already got most of it. Third watch. Bad back. Two daughters."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Get out of my house."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Doorkeeper."</font> Said gently. <font color="#fdb4fe">"It'll keep."</font>

And then, because she is what she is:

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You never once used my name tonight."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I don't know it."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"You've had eight to choose from and you declined all eight, which is either very good manners or very good tradecraft, and with you it's generally both."</font> The borrowed face under the hat brim did something at one corner. <font color="#fdb4fe">"You did call me one thing all night, though. Four times. You didn't notice."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"What."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Guest."</font>

She went out through the kitchen door into a corridor she had no reason to be in, and by the time she reached the stair she was nobody, and nobody went down the stair, and nobody was gone.

⁂

The chair with the short leg was still perfectly level. Iuno put her hand on the seat. It was cold.

On the table, in a row: four sorts of Huanglong type and one blank quad standing in the salt.

She sat down in her own chair and she did the thing she had been doing since she was eleven years old, which is that she filed.

*Willows, west bank, three days ahead of east. Municipal, unremarkable, offered as small talk by a professional at the exact moment she wanted my attention on the door.* Her hand went to the drawer where the household notepad lives, and she had the pencil out and the heading half-written before she saw what the heading was.

*Weather.*

She sat looking at the word for a long moment.

Then she put a line through it, and turned the page, and took the little blue ledger out from under the bread tin where it lives now, and opened it to the second page, under an entry in a hand that trembled and stood anyway, and wrote:

*Willows. West bank three days ahead. She wanted me at the door.*
*A man at the sluice gauges, third watch. Bad back. Two daughters. Name withheld — by me.*
*Type bought so it cannot be set. Ask what she is unprinting.*
*Quad: it holds.*

*Filed under: people.*

She gathered the four faced sorts into an envelope and wrote the date on it. The fifth she turned over once more, the smooth one, the one whose entire trade is standing in a locked page keeping a space open for a word that has yet to be cut — and then she put it in the inside pocket of the coat on the back of the door, the good one, the one with the reinforced seams, which is the coat she wears when there is somewhere to go.

In the old country, the one Septimont copied its offices from and its manners out of, two households used to do this with a piece of broken crockery. Half each. A break that can be matched and can never be cut twice the same. Sixty years on, a grandchild who arrived at the right door with the right edge in his pocket got the table, the bolt, and the household's word, and the gods of the door filed the matter closed.

She had a cup on a hook and a blank in a coat, and she could not have said, sitting there in her own kitchen at ten past four, which of them was her half.

*You are going to have to tell Mei about this one too,* the High Priestess said.

*I know.*

*Tonight. Not in a week when you've built the sentence properly.*

*I know.* Iuno closed the ledger and put it back under the bread tin. *After breakfast.*

*After breakfast,* the High Priestess agreed. Then, with the composure of a woman calculating blast radius: *She hummed our entr'acte.*

*Yes.*

*Darling, I have hummed that thing badly on purpose for two years and I could not tell you where I got it.*

Iuno sat with her hand flat on the table.

*She asked for a reading,* the High Priestess said, in the register she keeps for load-bearing items. *And she got one, and it landed, and she went out of that door carrying it. I watched it land. I would testify.*

*Yes.*

*And she also walked in here with five pieces of Mengzhou lead in her pocket and a line about willows already loaded, and left with our furniture rearranged and a war in the room, and I cannot tell you which of those two things was the errand.*

*No,* Iuno said. *Neither can she.*

She stood, and went back to the bowl, and beat eight eggs into submission with a fork.

⁂

She did all of it. Eggs, the good bread, tomatoes cut and salted and left to sit the way Lupa likes them, the porridge for Cartethyia's stomach, cardamom set out beside the tall cup out of pure premeditated devotion. The false sky beyond the window began to run its slow dawn program, engineers' rose-gold coming up the eastern rim, because in this city even the light keeps appointments.

At three minutes past the kettle, the door made the sound of a nineteen-year-old attempting stealth in fuzzy socks.

Amy manifested at the counter with her hair in a state of civil war, executed the theft of the chipped cup from the third hook at glacial and fully-witnessed speed, and stopped.

<font color=mistyrose>"There's a new one."</font>

Iuno did not look up from the pan.

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Twelve hooks. There's always been twelve hooks. I counted them in week one."</font> A pause, and the flat morning assessment, cataloguing exits, or perhaps just the pastry situation. <font color=mistyrose>"Whose is it?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Nobody's yet."</font>

Amy looked at the cup on the twelfth hook for slightly too long, and Iuno let her, and neither of them entered anything into evidence.

<font color=mistyrose>"One more minute,"</font> Amy said, to the middle distance, in the tone of somebody returning a borrowed thing with the cartridge replaced.

<font color=royalblue>"It matters."</font>

Down the hall a wolf's door went, and Ciaccona's first three notes of the day found their tuning, and a woman's voice called a question into the world sleep-rough and entirely confident that the world would answer.

Iuno poured the tall cup and set it on the tray.

Behind the bread tin, in a blue ledger, four lines stood on the second page under a heading that said *people*; and out past the walls of that kitchen, coming down the long air on a schedule nobody in the building had been told about, a red kite crossed the Riseway passage with a shell bound to its spine and a letter folded small beneath it — carrying, in honest type, pressed deep, a line about willows that had already been said aloud once that night at a kitchen table by somebody with no business knowing it.

## Coda

On the eleventh terrace, where the bioluminescent grass runs down in steps toward the Phosphor District, the maintenance crews cut at four-thirty because the light is even and the tourists are asleep.

The man on the cutter had been at it eleven years and had never once been spoken to by a member of the public at that hour, so when the woman in transit coveralls stopped at the verge he cut the engine out of pure surprise.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"How often do you do this?"</font>

"Every nine days. Ten in winter — the sun runs shorter." He wiped his hands. "You lose it if you leave it. Comes up rank and then it comes up dead."

<font color="#fdb4fe">"And if you set the blade wrong?"</font>

"Then it's yellow underneath and everybody sees it for a fortnight." He laughed. "Happened to me my first summer. Foreman never let it go."

The woman stood at the edge of the cut and looked at it for what he later described, to nobody in particular, as an oddly long time.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Thank you,"</font> she said.

He said it was nothing. He restarted the engine. When he looked up again the verge was empty and the terrace was going gold at the rim and he thought no more about it for the rest of his life.

⁂

Far above, in a bowl of water and mountain, the third watch came on at the youngest gate of Huanglong.

The lamp-grid lit from the north side first and the gold ran down the terraces the way something poured runs down a step. On the crest wall a lamp guttered and was relit by a woman whose name had been on the garrison roster six weeks and on another list rather longer. Down at the sluice house a man with a bad back and two daughters wrote a gauge reading into his ledger and underlined it, because the number had moved, and because he was the kind of man who underlined things.

East of there, in a Mingting garrison office, a berth held warm all season by a Marshal for a party out of Lahai-Roi came up for review and was reassigned overnight — in the correct hand, under the correct seal, by somebody entitled to do it. The Marshal would find out on the Thursday and take it as weather.

Nobody was watching.

It happened anyway.
