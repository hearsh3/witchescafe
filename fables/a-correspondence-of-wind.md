# A Correspondence of Wind
### being the letters delivered to Jinzhou at the winter solstice, tied with a boot-lace, and entered into the register entire
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

烽火連三月，家書抵萬金。
*Beacon fires three months running; a letter from home is worth ten thousand in gold.*
— Du Fu, "Spring View"

君自故鄉來，應知故鄉事。
*You come from my hometown. You must know how things stand there.*
— Wang Wei

---

> **PRINTER'S NOTE, BOOKBINDERS' ROW.** *The courier who delivered the return edition at the solstice delivered, with it, a bundle of fifteen letters on Mingting paper, folded small, tied with a boot-lace of the kind an Outrider uses to tie the ones she has kept. The knot was a bird's knot. The founders untied it with tongs.*
>
> *The letters are in one hand. The founders set the first of them beside a page of the forty-one that a girl in this city once wrote at a night window, under the glass, and looked for some time, and then asked to sit down. The hand steadies through the bundle: the first letters are written by somebody learning the weight of a brush, and the last by somebody who has remembered it.*
>
> *Where the writer reached for a word she had paid to this street and found her hand stop, she left a blank the width of the word, as the copy of her crossing did. The founders have set the blanks. They are the same blanks. One of them occurs in three letters to three people, and is one compartment, and the court declines to rule on what stood in it.*
>
> *The letter to the girl was delivered before the rest, directly, and came to this office already going soft at the folds.*

## To the corner table at Panhua's, the seat nearest the window, the bowl

寒露 — Cold Dew

Auntie.

I have hands. I am using them to write to you first because you fed me first, and you put the bowl out on your own say-so.

A year of the third bowl. You put it out before anybody sat down. You moved it out of the door's draught and into mine. You took it down off the shelf and put food in it on the night the column came in and kept that to yourself. I was there for that. I was there for the shelf going up and the stitched bird going onto it and the bowl going beside the bird. I was there when you told her *when you can hear the oil* and I was there when the wolf heard it.

Take the bowl down.

Put it in front of somebody hungry. There is always somebody. The steam will rise straight and that is correct; the air over your tables is yours again. Let it rise. That is what I would want to see if I could see your back room, and I can: I am picturing it, the lamps leaning, the good table. My sister here taught me that a letter is for picturing a room. I have your room exactly.

The crispy thing. It resists every explanation I have tried on Suisui. She says she will attempt it when the oil is fresh. I will report.

— Yangyang, of Mingting

## To the lamp that lights the stair, the crest wall, at the trimming

寒露

Keeper Pei.

You asked it once. Everyone told it and nobody had asked it, and you asked, at the fourth watch, with the flame leaning: *and you. Who does the wind tell?*

The lake. You watched the grass lie down. You know.

This is the rest of the answer, since I have a hand to give it with now. I told the lake your brother. The whole man, the way you gave him to me: the debts, the snoring, the puppet show and the dust. I carried him three watches and I carried him down the bank and I gave him to the only thing in Huanglong bigger than what I was holding, and it took him, with great courtesy, the way it takes everything, and it has him. He is in good water. I checked the weight on the way down. It was all there.

The boy under the third tower. Off the brow, back. You called it the one handhold a wind has and you were right, and I found it the first night it was all I had, and I used it on you all, on the wall, for a year, and you kept that to yourself for the whole of it. You trimmed lamps for a guest. I have sat in the guest's chair now. It is a real chair. The cushion is thin.

Trim the stair lamp anyway. Somebody will come up it.

— Yangyang

## To the window that is usually shut, the yamen, the lake side

寒露

Excellency.

Your lake dreamed a girl at a post who never landed the cut. I was the one dreaming it into your lake, at the fourth watch, for a year, and you woke every time before the stroke because I woke every time before the stroke. I was the girl. I had left the measure unmet and I kept putting it in front of you.

You said, half to the window: *I find I want very much to see her land it.*

The post is in a courtyard in Mingting, in a printing your lake has been remembering all this time, unlabelled. I have my hand on it. The measure is still unmet. I have decided to leave it that way for a while and go inside to eat, which is the step the girl at the post skipped in every one of your dreams, because I made her skip it.

Enter it however you like. You have a column for me now. I am told I will hate it. I find I am pleased to have taken up a whole column in a book kept by a woman who wanted a stranger's stroke to land.

You had eight beats rung for a column of one. I heard them from the kite. I have been counted returning twice now, by two cities, and this one used a registry clerk and a pencil, and I prefer yours.

— Outrider Yangyang, Mingting registry, formerly of your weather

## To the shrine terrace above the frontier court, the warm stones, the hawthorn

霜降 — Frost Descends

Sentinel.

You said: *bring back many hands, little gust. Bring back the kind that catch.*

I brought back seven and a wolf and a girl with a radio and I stood in your Sphere and watched them catch. Then one of them grew wings and put me in the torn one and carried me, and here I am, and I have hands of my own, two, and the first thing they did was hold a bird too hard.

You asked at the terrace which I was. A visitor out of another book, or your own last chapter blown loose and come back round to the front. I have the answer now and it is the one the court dislikes most: I was the visitor, and I have gone back to being the last chapter, and it is still going.

You offered to print it for me. Yesterday's moon. I took one evening and broke the plate. I would like you to know that I have been given the same evening again, unprinted, and that the tea has a smell, and Chixia is dead here, and Baizhi is at Huaxu and is still to be told I am back and I am still deciding how, and the stitched bird on the hat is a real bird now and sits on a post, and the whole of it is off-register at the edges in the way real things are, and I will take it. Every night. At interest.

You held Suisui at a board and offered me across it. She refused you. I am writing to say she was right and you knew it and you let her win. I have watched you let a merchant win and I know what it looks like from the terrace. Thank you for the hawthorn.

— the little gust, sat down

## To the roof rail above the east market, the kite, the crew list

霜降

Amy.

You filed the kite as crew. I was on the rack of your truck the whole road east, folded, the way soldiers sleep, and you let me ride free.

You said *it's humming* when your mother held the shell. It was. It was me, saying the only thing I could say to a girl I had watched hold a door shut for ten years from the far side of a sealed sky, which was: here is somebody who came across a dark for her. You had already done that. You knew what it cost. You said *I like her* to the sky.

I called you the Night Window in my head before she called it out loud. You were the one who kept a light on.

Tell your mother the kite goes up against the grain of the wind. That is what it means in Mengzhou, and now in Jinzhou, and in Mingting it is waiting on me to say what it means. When you next fly it, it will climb on its own past the dam face. Let it. That is me, in the other printing, remembering.

— Y.

## To the windowsill, the paper people, the fifth one

霜降

Cartethyia.

On the ninth tower, two weeks after the shore, you asked her who the third seat was for and she told you, and you have refused to repeat it since, to Amy, to anyone, on the grounds that it belonged to somebody else.

It was mine. You held it for me the way you held your own for a year before it was true. You held it through a Sphere that tried to name you six ways and through a mud floor where you said your own name into a god's theatre and it held.

I have it now. The border gave it back with the rest of me. You can say it. It is true.

You made paper people so the dark would have to keep the fact that somebody remained inside. Make one for the seat. Three inches. Say the name when you fold it. Then burn it at Old Feng's, because I am out of the dark, and the paper is for people who might disappear, and I have a body now that a threshold stone can catch.

— the third seat

## To the lute, the fourth string

霜降

Ciaccona.

You put your ear to jade and heard a woman singing a song built for two, alone, running out of breath at every line. You said *oh, you stubborn thing, that's a duet*, and you played the answer down through two hundred years of mud, and a dead gorge sang it back. You said somebody had already finished one song alone in there that day, and that this one would go differently. It did.

You held a rest open for hours in a valley and wrote its value under it so it would stay open. I owe you one of those. Here is its value: one bird, one winter, held.

Play the fourth note when she lands.

— Y.

## To the string that goes up out the door seam

霜降

Chisa.

You said the corner had two strings on it, and one went up, past the eaves, past the weather, and drank the tea. You knew where the far end of it was every minute from the twenty-seventh and you kept it to yourself, because it belonged to somebody else to say.

The far end of that string has a body on it now. Two hands, one chin, a scar across a knuckle. It is in a courtyard in Mingting, in a printing on the far side of a fold, and it is heavier than it was.

Pull. It will hold. I have been holding it from this side since a river.

— the far end

## To the stairhead

霜降

Lupa.

You threw food at anybody who started a Moment. Underhand, a metre, never a miss. You called me your aunt and meant it as a warning to the household.

I have started fifteen Moments in one bundle and I am aware of it.

Throw.

— your aunt

## To the harbour ward, the red cap, the inside of the lining

霜降

Chixia.

You said hello to me eight degrees wrong. Every time. Over a month. You turned your face to where you thought I was and you were always eight degrees off, and I went and stood in the eight degrees so that you would be right.

The Sleeve. You had the cap open on your knee and you said you kept getting as far as how long I carried it, and then you started thinking about where I put it when I needed both hands.

Here is where.

A branch. A nail. The projecting end of a roof beam. Against my stomach, under my coat, when a hook was missing and I needed the hands anyway. It dried wherever I slept and I took it down every morning before I fastened the pack. The red came off on my hands from washing it. I washed it because of the river mud. The stitching along the crown had torn and stood up in a pale thread, and you had complained about that thread over lunch, on the last day, and asked for somebody's scissors, and the scissors were in your room, and you said you would do it after the shift.

It is on a nail at a shrine on the post road where the road leaves the water. It has dried. It is the colour of brick. Somebody has mended the stitching with a different thread, and done it badly, and I stood under it with a hand and held the hand under it to see if it dripped, and it was dry.

Sew your own lining. Badly. Use the blunt scissors. Baizhi will say something and you will say something back, and I want that to happen in a room I can picture, and I can: the bench, the crutch with the new rubber foot, the cap on your knee.

You gave me the lining of yours, in a gorge, warmed by a whole afternoon, and said Yangyang had told you I liked hers after she had been in the sun. I lay in it for three breaths. It is the warmest place I have ever lain. I have a bed now and it is colder than your hat.

You said you were frightened and your side hurt like hell and you could use some help getting through the night. That is the only useful thing anybody has ever said to me about the cap.

— shijie

## To the incident ledger, the cause column, the sixth entry

霜降

Baizhi.

I have a temperature. Suisui says I run warm. She distrusts every thermometer in Mingting and has tried two. Enter it as *warm, unquantified* and underline it, and then strike the underline, because the pattern has introduced itself and the study is closed.

You underlined me for a year before anybody said my name. You moved a bowl three finger-widths out of one draught and into the draught I was, and said *caloric intent noted*, and wrote in your private book. You plotted saves against shifts and gave her the folder in a doorway with one line on the last page. *Underweight, your weather. Continue.*

I continued.

You stood in the rain with a thermometer for ninety seconds and then went back through every entry since the second month and wrote a name in six empty cause columns and said it was the most satisfying afternoon of your professional life and that you would prefer nobody comment. I am commenting. It is the most anyone has ever done for weather. You made a column and put a person in it and it is the correct filing and you did it before the Bureau, before the Magistrate, before Chixia said it out loud.

The Baizhi here is alive. She is at Huaxu. She is still to be told I am back. I know how she will take it because I know how you took it: she will measure something and write a number and prefer nobody comment. I am going to go and let her.

You told me, at the door of the treatment room, that someone would remain by the bed. Someone did. You were the someone. I would like that entered.

— the four-degree gradient, resolved

## To the scarf, the loose end, the fold under the chin

立冬 — Winter Begins

Suisui.

You said that when there were some words for me you intended to become a very demanding correspondent. Here are the words. Demand.

You stood in City Hall with a travelling bag and a strand of hair you kept missing and asked whether I was the friend she calls shijie. I went at you so fast the papers came off the table. You set the bag down. You said thank you for bringing her back, to the cold at your neck, and I came apart, because you were thanking me in her voice for a thing I had done in a gorge to a girl with a red cap who was alive.

Then you lifted the scarf and put me in it.

I have been held by a great many people in the air. Held is the wrong size for what you did. You turned. Every time I lost my hold you turned a little so the cold could find another warm place, and you said *come closer, I keep trying to find your shoulders*, and I had left mine at the rim of the world, and you went on looking. My sister here does the same thing. Exactly the same. The small continuous adjustments. I had forgotten them until you did them, and then I knew them from the inside, and then I came here and she did them with a body under her hands, and I understood I had been given the rehearsal so I would know the performance when it came.

You said: *your sister was making bread.*

She still is. There is a board with room left on the far side of it, for a second batch, floured fresh every night, for years. I put my thumb in it. She has flour on her thumb. She had it the last time I saw her and she had it when I came through the gate and she put it on my cheek and it is, in a way that refuses to be made smaller, the same flour.

You sent me tea. You said you would have sent something warmer. You sent her. She was still looking for a way to reach me and you were the one who found the words for it, in an apartment, with a lute going, with the shells in your lap, and you said *I'm glad I get to know you*, and I have carried that across a dark and it arrived whole.

You said you wished you could tell her where I was. Tell her. She is you. That is the only thing about two printings that comes out even.

The osmanthus cakes. I ate the larger ones first. She left you two. She has told me so herself, in this kitchen, at some length, and I have confirmed the number and she is unbearable about it.

— your sister's sister, in the other scarf

## To the front room at Mrs Ren's, the hook by the door where the coat lives

小雪 — Light Snow

Iuno.

You set out a cup on a balcony in a city whose weather is ducts, and turned the handle outward, and said *it's Earl Grey, there's a history, drink it anyway*, and I drank it. You were the first person who ever set a cup out for me. You did it in full view so that it would cost you. I saw what it cost.

You asked why I died. I said same answer. Here is the whole answer, since I have a hand.

I died because I arrived after the beginning. I was an Outrider in a city that was under water and I could reach every drowning person after they were drowned and I could reach a shore after somebody else had knelt on it, and I did the arithmetic my teacher started and I went through the dark to be on time once. That was all. To be on time once. You had already done the same thing, for the same reason, from the other side, with a basin and a plan and a woman's life, and the two of us met at the rim of one shore and pushed, and you were the one who won.

You kept her west. You said it in a valley in the rain with the shore hanging in the air over everyone and I spent a word on it. *Late.* I would like you to know that the word was aimed at me.

I sat in your window gap for a month and read your correspondence, rudely. I read the four-character paper twice from the wrong side. I know what it says and I know what the seal space is for. I will say one thing about it, since you have been told to do it on a Tuesday and I am the only person in two worlds who has read the paper and is now permitted an opinion: the chop it wants is the one on the return you filed at the freight apron. Her name is on that return, in a column, in a foreign clerk's careful hand, and that is the only chop she has. Use it.

The Sleeve. You said you wanted to live and that if I raised a blade against you again you would act on it. Good. I have hands now and I will knock. I hurt her wrist. I hurt it because I could see your thumb on the creases at your waist and I could see the morning you wanted more of, and I had watched you have that morning, from a window, from a roof rail, from a stove, for a year, while my own printing stood behind me with a red cap in it. Some nights I hated you. Then the wind changed. It has changed for good now; there is a body in the way of it.

I ■■ you for it. All of it. The shore and the west and the cup with its handle turned out. I have gone through this sentence four times with the brush and the hand stops in the same place each time, because I paid the word away in a printing quarter for one evening of a table that reprinted itself, and the founders will set a blank there and you will look at the blank for as long as you looked at four marks on a magistrate's palm, and I am content to let you.

You told me *later, then. We'll find the rest of it.* You were talking about my name. A woman found it in the sand with her hand. You were an hour late up the path, on purpose, with your whole attention, and I want you to know that I felt you being late. It was the first thing anyone had ever done for me at the correct speed.

Turn the cup a quarter-turn. She does it. You watch her do it. Do it once for me, in the front room, with the window that will not latch, and I will be in the gap in the other printing, at the same hour, out of words on purpose.

— the aunt

## To the bed with four feet, the side nearest the window, the hand in the shirt

小雪

Mei.

You said *hi. You're right on time.*

It was the first time anyone had said that to me. Everyone had said held. I had a needle under my sternum for a country and a dark and two seas and it swung at you across an opera house in a printing quarter, and you walked past a kneeling prophet on a stage of water with your eyes ahead, and I stood at the back and three lanterns went out. That was the first time I saw you. You were already loved. The second act ran on it.

I said, to a wall in Xuanfang, to a post that keeps what it is told: *some nights I hate her. Then the wind changes.* I want you to have that from me directly, since you were the one it was about and the one it was kept from.

The rail. Your coffee, criminal strength, and a tea for me, and it's not Earl Grey, you're welcome. You told the sky your family's day and you turned the cup a quarter-turn and you said: *the shell gives me a word a night. I'm up to eleven. I do the punctuation myself.* I was giving you one a night because that was all the purse would stand. You made whole sentences out of them. You did the punctuation. That is the best description of you I have and I have spent a year looking for a better.

You made the deal. Jinzhou, the shore I meant, wash up properly, and that's the once, and it'll be worth my fare.

It was worth the fare.

You came out of the lake with a shell on your sternum and my sentence set down at last at its own address, and you put your hand up beside your cheek and held the air, and you said the thank-you in the wrong tone at the wrong speed like wildflowers, and I laughed the size of a moth, and then your hand went down into the sand and finished my name. I felt it finish. Half of me had stopped on a teacup a year before, apologising, at the edge of what was left, and the other half came out of your hand at the speed of a signature. You said *huh*, to your hand. It is the most completely anyone has ever made me, and you said *huh.*

You gave the shell back. You said it was cargo and its address was elsewhere and you were the only person on that shelf who had ever been cargo. You were right. It came with me. It is on a shelf in my mother's kitchen beside a case that has been shut for a long time, and it holds two seas, and my sister has held it to her ear and said nothing, in the way she says it.

You said *same time tomorrow* and I spent the last word I had on the answer, at a table, in front of people eating, and it cost the girl her questions. I would spend it again. I am sorry it was hers to pay.

She will hold your hand on a canal. Let her. You have already told her which vinegar to get wrong. I was in the hat for the pear cakes and I know exactly what you look like when you turn toward her too quickly and your knees knock, and I know what Iuno looks like at the field kitchen when you take her hand and she is very tired, and I have decided, from the far side of a border that admits one of me at a time, that the two of you look at each other correctly and I am done hating anybody about it.

I ■■ you. The founders will set that as a blank and you will guess the word and you will be right, and you will also be right if you guess the other one. I have found that the hand stops in the same place for both.

Every wind is met once. It was a good wind.

— the one you were looking for, found, and gone back to the front of her own book

## To the window. The desk. Under the inkstone

大雪 — Great Snow

Shimei.

You wrote forty-one to somebody you hadn't met. I read every one over your shoulder. This is the forty-second, the other way, and it has taken me since the frost month, because a brush weighs more than I remembered and a hand has to be taught.

The elbow.

You asked once why I kept the explanation to myself. You said it in the Sleeve, to a sleeve, that the explanation lives in the muscle. It does. Here it is anyway, since you were owed it in words and the words were the thing I had least of.

I have it too. When the sword gets heavy the arm goes out looking for a wall to lean on. It finds air. Bring it in. That is the whole correction, and it took her two fingers and a sigh, and it took me a year of pressure at your elbow every morning at first light, and it took you one afternoon in the rain to give it back to me at the moment it counted, on a blade I was swinging, at a throat. You caught the line. You brought it in half an inch. It is the best teaching I have had in either life.

You said manners. On the wall, at dawn, when I tugged your hat. You said it to the open air with your ears going pink and you went back to your room to sleep. I have been called a great many things by a great many walls. That was the first thing anyone said to me as if I were a person with a habit that could be corrected.

You asked, on festival night, whether spring would still come by there when I found her. I lingered on your face longer than an answer. The answer was yes. It comes by everyone's. I stayed past it because the answer was yes and I wanted one more moment of your face before I went and made it true.

You said: *together doesn't count. When I can do it alone, I'll look.*

The post here has twelve years of me on it in tenths of inches and one stroke above them that I have aimed at for everything I ever did. I put my palm on the old cuts on the first morning and I left the measure where it was. It will keep. Cut yours when you can do it alone. Look. Then write and tell me the sound it made. I have only ever heard it from inside the swing.

You put me in the wing.

You opened your arms in a gorge with a hole in your side and you said *you carry me*, and then you went up, and you laid me along the torn side and made me the part that holds still. I had been the part that spends. I had been that for a country and a shore and a valley and a road. You took the spending away from me on a burning slope by giving me somewhere I had to be, and the somewhere was a wing, and one scrap of myself thrown at anything and we would both have gone down, and I knew it, and I held. It was the first time in either life I had been the part that holds still. It is harder than the other thing. You knew that. You learned it on a bench above a drainage grate from a woman who declined a grate as a goodbye, and you did it to me, and it worked.

You sang us across. Both halves. The dark took feathers off you one at a time and you kept singing, and where your chest ran out I poured, and where I thinned you carried, and that is the flute on the wall with the directions reversed and reversed again until the breath stopped belonging to either of us. We were a third thing, wearing both of us, for the whole of the dark. I would like that entered somewhere. You will know where.

Then the border read us and gave me the name and gave you the wings, and you have been a bird in my mother's courtyard for two months, eating properly, which is more than I can say for myself.

Here is the itinerary. You made it. I am reporting.

Suisui burned the first tray because she was talking. She set two pairs and a dish and pretended the count had escaped her. I sat in the chair, in it, with the weight going down, and the steam rose because the air over it was empty, and I leaned toward it myself. You said *you can't get spent before dinner, it's on the itinerary.* I was at dinner. I ate the browned part from the bottom of the pot with my hands and my sister let me. Enter it: kept.

You did the talking for both of us. You said you would until Mengzhou and you did it past Mengzhou and through a Sphere and across a dark and up a beacon stair with my chin bleeding on it. I do the writing now. The founders will set these beside your forty-one and see that the hand is the same hand and they will ask to sit down. Let them.

You said, in the Sleeve, that you get angry with me for being in everything, and then you remember how long I kept coming to your window and you sit down again.

Put your arm in your own sleeve. Both arms. Get dressed because you are hungry and go downstairs. Pick up the sword and feel where you meant it to land, and mean it there. I am in a coat now, my sister's, it is the wrong size, and I have my own sleeves, and I put them on in the morning, empty, and I think about you every time, and I go downstairs anyway. That is the arrangement. It is the same one from both sides.

Out, one. Back, two.

I read it over your shoulder at the back of your log, in the first ink, the count you have kept since you were nineteen and kept to yourself. It came out. Wrong printing. That is how counts come out in this family: one sister leaves and two come back through the other sister's gate, and a registry clerk in a city you will only ever hear about writes *family* in a column after considering *livestock* for some time.

The border keeps you on your own shelf. It lets a bird through and it keeps a woman where her book is; I asked it, at the threshold, with everything I had, and it read me and told me the rule. It has only ever had one of us. So it will be the bird. Every winter. When it is cold. Suisui has already started calling her Dumpling and you may take that up with her when you have words, which you will, because I will send them.

Come ■ when it's cold.

You know the word. I spent it. You have it. Say it for me at your own gate, to your own Suisui, in the printing where she is still young, and eat the cakes she puts in front of you, and leave her two.

I have your shell and mine on the shelf beside Ma's flute case. The case is still shut. I am waiting until I can play it alone. Then I will look.

I ■■ you. Same blank. Same compartment. You will have guessed the word by now, because you are the only person in two printings who has been on both sides of that hand.

— 師姐, with hands

## Colophon

*entered at Jinzhou at the winter solstice; the court keeps the register open*

**I.** The letters were delivered in the order tied and are set in the order tied. The founders observe that the first was to a cook and the last was to the girl, and that the writer knew what she was doing with the boot-lace.

**II.** The recipients in this city have been given their originals. The register holds fair copies. The cook took the bowl down the same evening and put it in front of a porter, and the porter ate, and the steam rose straight, and the cook watched it rise and then went back to the oil.

**III.** The keeper of the ninth tower has asked whether a letter addressed *to the window that is usually shut* is a document of the Magistrate's office or of the Bureau's. The Magistrate has ruled that it is hers, and has had the window opened, and has not said what she read.

**IV.** The founders have set three blanks in three letters from one compartment. Readers who have seen the copies have proposed, in writing, three different words for it, and each reader was certain, and each of the three words fits every one of the three letters, and the court has entered all three proposals and struck none of them and declines to rule.

**V.** The courier, asked by this office whether there would be more, stood on the counter for the length of time it takes a bird to consider a question, and then went to the window and put her head against the glass, on the inside, facing east, and stayed there until the founders opened it.

The court keeps the register open.
