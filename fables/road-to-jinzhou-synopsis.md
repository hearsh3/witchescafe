# The Road to Jinzhou
### *A narrative synopsis of* Hospes, Antiphon, Quorum, Next of Kin, Bailment, Hapax, Muster, Viaticum, Itinerarium, Mansio, Visa, Mora, An Impression of Wind, *and* Turba.

*Internal chronological order, from the night a kite came down through a sealed sky to a staircase in Jinzhou on the thirty-first of the fifth month. Told in narration; where wording is load-bearing to the corpus it is carried in reported speech.*

---

## I. THE HOUSE UNDER THE GLACIER

Lahai-Roi is a city in a hole under the Dimmr Plains, lit by an artificial sun that rises on a schedule because a committee agreed it should, and gets its blues wrong after four o'clock. In the Startorch Institute's east wing, nine people live in a dormitory built for students and colonised by a family, having arrived there the way survivors arrive anywhere, which is by being unable to leave one another.

Mei woke on a beach some years ago with a hole where her first forty years should be, and has spent every day since catching what was falling: golden-eyed, loud, indestructible in a way no physician has managed to write down. Her daughter **Amy Aemeath**, who broadcasts as Fleet Snowfluff, spent ten years inside the Drive holding a door for a world that was busy having meetings about her, and came home three weeks before this account opens — solid, nineteen, permanently allergic to being decided about. **Iuno Caecilia Severa**, formerly High Priestess of Septimont, keeps the household's records with one hand of flesh and one of crystal, and carries a second woman behind her sternum: the High Priestess herself, subtracted entire for nine and a half minutes in the Nihility and returned into a shape somebody had kept swept for her.

Around those three: **Lupa**, a Septimontian wolf out of the fighting pens who has buried more people than she will name; **Ciaccona Toccata**, a Ragunnan bard of four hundred years, struck off her country's record, who plays badly on purpose in strange rooms because what a place believes gets told to a bad musician; **Cartethyia Fleurdelys**, the Blessed Maiden, who spent twenty years sealed alone in the dark with four hundred and eleven cloth dolls she made and named so the dark would have to go round them; **Chisa Kuchiba**, who cannot see faces, wears a suppression collar that chimes at six every morning, and navigates by gait and counted steps; **Lynae**, ex-Fractsidus, now a student with one identification card and her own face in the photograph; and **Rebecca**, out of a dead city in another world, who draws her dead in acid green on a stairwell wall.

There are eleven cups on the kitchen rack and twelve hooks, because a dead carpenter cut the rail to a standard length and left the spare unexplained. It has been a good year: Mei has slept through the night four times in her recorded life and three of them were this month. Iuno's boots stand by her door, cleaned and laced to the second eyelet, in a room with three weeks of dust on everything else.

## II. THE GUEST IN THE KITCHEN

Past midnight, on a balcony where the steam had been leaning east for weeks against a vent that draws west, Iuno had been setting out a second cup of tea, handle turned guest-side, for weather. That night the weather spoke through a door open a finger's width, at the rate of a purse going to zero: it named **Ovathrax**, a Threnodian of War, and **the youngest gate**, which is Jinzhou, a garrison city in a bowl of water with a dam above it. Then it asked for Mei — and, told that the woman had only just begun sleeping through the night, dropped fifteen degrees, skinned the tea with frost, and spent nearly the last of what she had rewriting her own sentence into three words: *her to choose.* Iuno went inside, looked at her boots, put them by the door, and stood next to that hypocrisy every morning afterward.

She also pulled a reading she had performed at twenty-two in the east tower on the Capitoline: clear sky in every direction, wind on the water for one hour, leaning east. The instrument had a column; the column had a word; she wrote it correctly, per the instrument, in a careful young hand. **Weather.** A woman had spent thirty years crossing to a shore that was already used, and Iuno was the one who used it.

At three in the morning she came down to cook, having decided the war could wait until people had eaten, and found the **Grand Architect of the Fractsidus** at her kitchen table in a transit clerk's borrowed face, with the teapot warmed first and two cups out, the near one turned guest-side. Iuno crossed her own kitchen and was made a guest in it in four seconds. She took back the chipped cup Amy steals every morning at four minutes, named it as her niece's in a voice that surprised her by being load-bearing, washed it at the sink with her back turned on four hundred years of appetite, hung it on its hook, took a fresh one, and sat down in the guest chair.

What followed was two professionals doing the same job. The Architect's tea was perfect at four minutes, she having measured Iuno's own across two hundred and eleven mornings and declined to copy its six-second spread, on the grounds that copying a woman's mistakes is a kind of familiarity and they had not been introduced. She asked for a review of the Orrery — a room built last winter in which nine people tended a Mei-doll on a plinth in shift rota while Iuno was told she was the parasite that had fed on the woman for thirty years, and which had held nine minutes. Iuno's note was that it proves the wrong thing: every room the Architect builds for her contains somebody who does not leave, and she cannot construct an empty one. Then the Architect found the ring unprompted — a facet cut off the inside of the crystal forearm along the grain, so the loss is structural, taken anyway — and named the transaction as handing a woman who cannot die a widowhood in a box and calling it a proposal. Iuno's answer was the single word *good*, and the most experienced liar alive said nothing for nine seconds.

She asked for a reading and paid first, because Iuno keeps the old temple rule. The fee was **five pieces of hand-cut Mengzhou type**: four faced sorts off a paid bill on Bookbinders' Row, where the Architect has spent a season buying type in very old coin under a different face each visit, printing nothing whatever, **buying the sorts so they cannot be set** — and a fifth, a **blank quad**, cast smooth to leave the paper alone, which holds the space in a locked page so the words leaning on each other cannot close over a word that has yet to be cut. It holds; that is the entire job.

The reading is four hundred years old and identical every time: an afternoon; grass cut badly, all the way down, gone yellow underneath; a warm hand smaller than hers; a child's finger-hooked promise in a voice she insists is hers and never has been; a bar of low light from the west; and a face the whole dream is built to arrive at, with nothing on it. Iuno's diagnosis took one move — how was the grass cut, and by whom? — because grass at that height means a schedule and a blade and a person who came round on a day and did it wrong. There was a house; somebody kept it; and the Architect has spent four centuries auditing the good thing without once costing the upkeep, and has never turned round. Invited, the High Priestess added the only primary source in existence: she has been subtracted, entire, and came back into a shape swept and furnished by a woman who declined to redecorate for the length of a war. Twice in one hour the Architect got the same answer from two women in one body, and it was about grass and sweeping, and no counter-argument arrived.

At four Iuno hung a plain white cup on the twelfth hook, with no rider and no condition, because the hook had been bothering her since spring. The Architect stood with her hand on a chair for some time and told her to **ask her in Jinzhou** — leaving behind a municipal fact offered as small talk at the exact moment she wanted Iuno's attention on the door (the willows on Jinzhou's west bank come out three days ahead of the east, and nobody has ever explained it), and a name to protect, a man who reads the sluice gauges at the third watch, which Iuno refused to take delivery of, having drafted a woman into a death with an instrument that size before. On her way out she observed that Iuno had used none of her eight names all night and had called her *guest* four times.

And she hummed **four bars of an entr'acte** while filing — a Mengzhou opera, three acts, a god who dies before the interval, a woman with two names — which are the same four bars a woman in blue silk has hummed badly on purpose over cards for two years and cannot say where she got. Iuno's hand had the heading half-written, *Weather*, before she saw it, struck it, and wrote in the blue ledger instead: **filed under people.** The faced sorts went into a dated envelope; the quad went into the inside pocket of the good coat, the one she wears when there is somewhere to go. And out past the walls, a red kite was coming down through a sealed sky with a shell bound to its spine and a letter beneath it — carrying, in honest type, a line about willows that had already been said aloud once that night by somebody with no business knowing it.

## III. TWO HOUSES, ONE OFFICE

Iuno went up at ten to five with tea at four minutes, because Mei will not ask for a thing twice and has therefore stopped asking for anything, and Iuno answers structural problems by building institutions. Mei's sleeping hand closed round the crystal wrist, the bearing pulled, and Iuno shut her eyes, which in that house is how you open the door.

In the lounge behind her sternum — candles, a stolen moon, a courtyard fire, and a framed strip of prop-paper off the floor of the Nihility reading **CHARACTER ARC: LOVES MEI. CONCLUDED** — there was now a door in the wall behind the piano, with a cold nine-second pulse of draught under it. The High Priestess had known a week and spent it drinking, on the grounds that every door they ever opened in the line of duty was somebody else's. Iuno pushed, and it went.

Beyond it stood a chapel she had built out of a temple she resigned from and had never entered, its east wall lit from a source outside the boundary she has spent her life calling the edge of the world. **Fourteen pictures**: in nine, a woman with grey-blue eyes lies dead on nine grounds under nine skies while a golden-eyed woman walks out of the frame into an empty margin; in two they go down together; in one there is no beach at all, only a woman at sixty in a temple office, signing something, well-fed and unaccompanied, which is the most peaceful window in the room; and the fourteenth is a house at night with one square of yellow in it. The **fifteenth light** stands clear at the north end, plain glass held in came cut and soldered with exactly as much care as the fourteen — somebody spent a day on the lead alone — and it is hinged, and has a **felt wedge** driven flush into the top corner where nobody looks. In front of it stands a temple-issue **harp of twenty-nine strings, every one tuned to the same note**: at eleven years old a girl with a professional turn of mind went up alone to find out whether the god was silent or absent, found the wedge, went back down the stairs, told nobody, and grew up to keep doors and accounts.

Past the harp, through a low silvered door of the kind you find at the sea end of anything, is **Mei's country**. There is no wind; the air stands, and has stood so long that the draught went out into it in a single visible sheet, low along the ground, like something poured. Dark rock runs flat in every direction under still water holding a sky of faint coloured stars, with one directly overhead, gold, larger than all of them, with real weight in it. Along the water's edge stands **an arc of named stones** — Sumika, Mya, Avidius, Phrolova, The Shaper — cut from the ground they stand in and set upright with the honest slight wrongness of things put up by one person working alone. About forty are in scripts Mei cannot read, belonging to people who were there before her, and she washes them anyway, because they are on the round. Between the stones and the water the rock has a groove worn a centimetre and a half deep, wider at the turns where a person shifts weight.

She washes them nightly, with no pause anywhere for feeling; she stopped once for a couple of years and came back to find she could read about half, and getting them back took longer than the stopping. One stone, third from the water, she walks around with no visible adjustment, the path simply going out and coming back, and the groove proving it. It says **AEMEATH**, and it is dry, and Amy is three floors up eating a pastry she believes she stole. Told to take it down, Mei said she would get to it; Iuno washed it three times instead, and Mei let her. Above them there is **one more star than there are stones**: Mei gets the same gap every time, and the extra one holds still only at the corner of the eye, and it has been there since she arrived, and she cannot say when that was. She asked for the count of the stones and was refused, because she has never said that number aloud in her life, and Iuno saying it would put it in that room forever in Iuno's voice, handed over unasked and called help.

At the far end lies **a square of grass ten feet by twelve**, cut recently and cut badly, all the way down, so the whole square has gone yellow underneath and shows it. Mei cuts it; the thing is old, the blade is off, she has never been good at it, and there is no light of any kind. Iuno — who ninety minutes earlier had told a four-hundred-year-old woman exactly what grass at that height means — said nothing at all. Further out stands a **doorframe with a door in it and snow on the ground for one metre around it and nowhere else**, which Mei says is hers and does not open. The hinges are on her side, and Mei knows.

Then Iuno invited her through, and Mei asked whether she was allowed, and Iuno read that face and declined to itemise the cost. Mei's thumb found the ring on the crystal hand, stopped for one breath, moved on, and closed. She walked the chapel backwards from the north end, which produced the correct order for the first time, stopped a long time at the ninth from the end where Iuno is on the floor and the golden-eyed woman is simply walking, and turned round with the look that comes out about twice a year. Iuno could not say who hung the glass — the void, the woman who reads her as a hobby, or her own hands at two in the morning — and, asked which she thought, said her own hands. Mei told her she has a row too. Then she reached the fifteenth light and arrived at the true thing by the shortest available road: there are fifteen in that room and this is one of them, and it has lead in it, and you do not do corners like that for a gap.

In the lounge she tracked salt on the velvet, ate cake standing up, and said the framed joke was wrong and then that the other half was wrong too, because *arc* is a place and it has a fire in it — which sat the High Priestess down rather suddenly. The room declined to make a third chair; all three noticed; Mei sat on the floor against Iuno's knees and appeared entirely content. Asked what happens if she wins — a question held for two years — her face did something and stopped, and the warm one arrived instead, and she admitted she has no answer: everybody keeps asking her things that have one, and she keeps agreeing warmly, and then it is four in the morning. Her turn produced six names from the High Priestess, every one of them a job: Regina, abdicated with paperwork; Moneta, who warns, office closed; Sospita, reassigned to a committee; Covella, retained in perpetuity; Lucina, who brings the child into the light, open, with years of work in it; and Pronuba. Mei sat with it, squared the deck to the table edge, and then asked Iuno by name, in passing, in the middle of doing something else, whether she was going to eat the rest of that cake. A woman addressed by an office for fifty years had never once been called by her own name by somebody clear about which of them she meant; Iuno watched it happen three feet away, made no move to clarify anything, and paid for that in one instalment.

At the end Iuno got her thumbnail under the wedge, dropped four hundred years of institutional caution on the flagstones, and pushed the fifteenth light open a finger's width. Three rooms at three pressures with two doors open, and the air went — down the chapel and out under the north door into a country that has never had wind in it, crossing twenty-nine strings tuned to one note on the way. The harp filled the chapel with a chord nobody had played, and then played **four bars**, clean and in time, which a harp tuned to unison cannot do, and which Iuno knew before the second bar finished, and denied knowing when asked. They went out and the wind went with them, and every sheet of water began to move, and the coloured stars broke up and scattered and came back together and broke again, and Mei stood in the middle of it with her arms slightly out from her sides while Iuno — twenty-two years old in an east tower with wind on the water for one hour — declined, in full, to write anything down.

Then she left the low door standing wide, and the light came out low and lay down long across the rock, past the stones and the groove and the arc and the water, and reached the badly cut grass and lay over that too, gold, at an angle, so every blade stood up with its own shadow and the yellow underneath showed. **It made a west out of the doorway.** It will stay on, Iuno said, for as long as there is a fire in the other room; and if there is not, somebody will have let it go out, and there will be a conversation about it. She surfaced at dawn, put a cup at exactly the right temperature into a hand that came up before its owner did, could not tell which of the two women had just been thanked, and let it stand. Downstairs she got four lines into the ledger and the pen stopped, because three floors up a nineteen-year-old had frozen with a stolen cup halfway to her mouth, and a wolf's door had gone all at once the way it goes when the nose gets there first, and something red was coming down through a sealed sky.

## IV. THE LETTER, AND FIVE DAYS OF HONEST

The letter was set in honest Mengzhou type with clean rectangles where two words should have been — sorts gone from every case on the Row. An **Outrider** in Jinzhou writes the incident ledger for her patrol and has written the word *weather* nineteen times this season: a Discord's claw turned aside by a gust, indoors, in a stone room with the door shut; a rope bridge held through a squall, with a porter swearing the wind leaned on the knot; a beam coming down onto a man who was suddenly four feet to the left and could not say how; and, at her old training ground, a post she had cut at for two years without marking it, found after wind with one new stroke across the old scars, three inches deep, the grain parted like water, and a wooden sword standing at its foot with the edge to the east. And the **Magistrate** of Jinzhou kneels at the water and asks it about the year ahead and is shown the year behind — a library burning that stands whole across the square from her own window, floodwater at second-floor shutters on streets dry for a decade, a red cap turning in a current — which the Ministry files under her grief, in a very tidy file. The water is showing them a year that belongs to somebody else; the patrols write *weather* in the cause column; and weather is not a cause but a warning, and warnings are for the people who can still act.

Lupa's nose found Iuno in the doorway and named what she had been smelling for three weeks: two tea services every night, one cup coming back full and cold and washed standing up, which she had assumed was grief, because people do stupid things with cups. So Iuno told it standing. Then Mei crossed the roof, closed her fist round the bike keys, stood eleven seconds with her back to the roof while everyone watched her hand — and hung them up, and braced both palms on the doorframe the way you brace a thing that wants to move, and Iuno saw what it cost and will be thinking about it for years. Honest takes four days done properly. **Take five.**

The first rule landed badly and Iuno had budgeted for it: nobody speaks about it tonight, because Mei arrives at decisions in the gap between two heartbeats and is nearly always right, and the table would agree inside four seconds and be unable to say when. The house stayed up and stayed quiet, and those turned out to be two separate skills — and at three in the morning Amy came down for water and found her mother at the sink washing one clean cup, and setting it in the drainer, and taking it out and washing it again, and squaring it so the handles all pointed the same way. She went back upstairs without her water, and kept the rule, and it cost her more than it cost anybody in that building.

They built the instrument at the kitchen table over four hours: **eight rules, owned, each named for whose limit it is.** Mei speaks last, because otherwise seven people ride to a war they never chose and she will have done to them what got done to her. Nobody says their answer in the room first — Lupa's, because she goes in four seconds and the room goes with her, which is weather with a name that sounds like a vote — amended by Iuno so that a keeper reads each sealed answer aloud in their own voice, and nobody gets to *perform* the most important sentence of their life. Chisa listens, because voices do a thing at the third syllable when the reason underneath differs from the reason on top; she may call anyone once, and a refusal goes in the record; and Amy calls her, because you cannot verify yourself from inside. Anybody may change their answer up to the moment the wheels turn, and the rest accept it and never raise it again. You say what your reason costs the person on your left. The people staying get a vote of equal weight — Amy's, because for ten years she was the thing everybody was deciding about, and nine people who all loved her decided about a girl who was not in the room, and it was still the worst thing that has ever happened to her and she includes the void. Numbers before feelings. And one true line about every other person, kept by Rebecca, who drew her dead off memory and got some of it wrong, and the wrong parts are the ones she thinks about.

Lynae's estimate took fourteen minutes and came with a professional disclosure: the number stands and the number is garbage, because she has priced that table four times and been correct once and wrong three times in the same direction — so plan on the ugly one, then go and be unmodelable, and she will do the paperwork afterward and complain. Rebecca's estimate was the room, afterward: the plates your hands keep setting for a number that has changed, months later, every time like brand new information. Her ask was an hour with each of them, drawn alive, sitting in a chair being annoying, so she is working off a chair and a Tuesday forever instead of off memory.

The private reasons came out in assigned pairs. Iuno's, to Cartethyia, was that **she is going to be very good at it** — eighteen months of learning to ask before she moves, and a war is a room where asking gets people killed; the four seconds under a falling wall will be hers, and she will be magnificent, and it will work, and they will thank her, and when they come home she will have to put it down again and can already make an extremely persuasive case for keeping it. Cartethyia told her to fail out loud where somebody can grab her arm: she was very good at hers too, and they put her in a window, four metres of coloured glass with the sword up, and every single person who knelt under it thought they were being kind, and nobody ever grabbed her arm. Mei's, to Rebecca, was that if she stays she has to find out who she is when nobody needs saving — three weeks of a daughter, three of four lifetime nights of unbroken sleep, nothing to walk toward, all of it called peace out loud since spring — and then a kite came down and her hands were on the keys before the second line and what she felt was **relief**. Lupa's was Amy, both halves: wanting her home, and wanting to be there when she is not. And Chisa's fear, extracted under rule three, was that she goes and it takes three months and she comes back and her mother has died of being sixty-three — ordinary, in a chair, on a Tuesday, with the kettle on and nobody in the room — while she was off holding a gate.

Lupa let that stand where it landed and then gave the instruction that changed the rest of it: **record her.** When Lupa went into the pens at seven, her mother was at a window with her back to the room, and that is the whole file, and she has been filling in the voice herself for twenty-nine years, and the guesses have got worse. So Chisa went, which meant staying the night, and explained the errand badly three times, and had it called the first sensible thing anybody in that building had said since **Kuchiba Chiyo** arrived, because everyone else does the face. Chiyo read for fifty minutes into a handheld — the rice recipe, seven names practised in a borrowed alphabet, every street between the tram stop and the building, and then a children's story about a crane that neither of them had said aloud in thirty-one years, read without stopping in the wrong places, which meant she had been keeping it somewhere. At six the collar chimed and the bedroom door was already open, because she had been standing in it since five fifty-five; she took down the brass clock that runs fast, moved the hand two minutes, and said, with her back to the room, that it was a decent sound and she had imagined something with more metal in it. Her bag was already packed by the door, because Rebecca had knocked on the Tuesday, said the house has nine bedrooms and two of them would be rattling around in it, and asked whether she played cards.

At the table on the fifth day the sealed papers were read out by other people's mouths. Chisa's ran to one sentence: she is going, her mother is in the building, and she would like everyone to know she knows exactly what that is worth. Lynae's ran to eleven words about staying for a girl who does not know she needs her. Rebecca's ran to four — *this is my house* — read twice, because Lynae's voice did something on the third syllable the first time and Chisa's head came round. And Amy's begins with **never once having seen her mother work**: everyone in that house has watched Mei do the thing she does, and two hundred students have, and a woman in another country wrote a song about it, and Amy has a poster. On the back: if they make her stay she will be a person who got left behind, and she has already been that, and she would rather be dead than do it twice, and she knows exactly how that sentence sounds and wants it read out anyway. Under rule five she costed it at twenty-two days — the whole time Mei has had her — which Mei will spend and then add three months to, cheerfully, and everybody will believe her. She counted the days, wanted that in, and is going anyway.

Then Lupa used her one permitted asking and laid out the picture: a wall, a good reason, *going in*, and her own hand between the girl's shoulder blades in the dirt of a country she has never been to, holding a body with its eyes open — after nine weeks of a room and a chair and two open hands she has never once described to anybody. Chisa called her on the third syllable; Lupa refused; the refusal went in the record; and Chisa said the true thing herself, because the rule binds the speaker and leaves the room free. **She is the only one who ever came back.** Mya did not, Avidius did not, Sumika did not, nor six from the pens, nor a boy whose name Lupa says at four in the morning when she thinks the corridor is empty — and the one person who went into the dark and walked back out of it is nineteen and eating a pastry, and Lupa has been treating that like a debt that could be called. Lupa sat down the way a wall sits down, and Amy handed her the pen and took the terms: a hand on her every time, Lupa's shift with first refusal, permanent; a clock on the ground; Chisa on the frequency; and if Lupa calls her out, she argues for three seconds and comes out.

Ciaccona then asked that Amy never be the last resort, because in nine hundred years of her family's archive she has never found a war story without the same bar in it — somewhere in the third act the room spends the youngest person in it, and there is always a reason, and the song gets measurably better when they do it, and she has played those songs for money in rooms full of weeping people and taken their coin. Amy refused the protection in her own name and made it universal: nobody at that table is the last resort, seven go and seven come back, and if the arithmetic says otherwise the arithmetic can have the city. It passed eight to one, Lynae against on the grounds that a rule like that kills the two people standing next to the protected one in the half-second where everybody checks a rule instead of moving — and then undertaking to enforce it anyway, from here, in writing, with a stamp if she could find one. And Iuno put her pen down and disclosed, in daylight, with everyone present, that **she will break it**: there will be four seconds in which the rule and one of them are on opposite sides, and she will do the arithmetic, and she will be correct, and she will break it. Saying so in advance is the entire improvement, and the only one she has, and she has been working on it for eighteen months.

Rebecca read Mei's paper last and then turned it over and read her own question underneath — **what happens if you win?** — which Mei had not answered. Mei stood, confirmed the reason was true and bad and hers, and then gave the table the fear nobody had. Everyone has the same picture, her daughter in the dirt somewhere she cannot reach, and Mei has had that one ten years and has furniture in it. The other one gets her up at four: **Amy will go in.** There will be a wall and a clock and a good reason and eleven people who need something, and she will fold her fingers and say a number and go in, and she will be *right*, and Mei taught her that at nine, in a kitchen, cheerfully, with the radio on. Telling her to stay home would be telling her the lesson was wrong — and that lesson is the only thing Mei ever managed to give her, and she survived ten years of nothing on it. So Mei's own rule was that **one person gets to tell her no**, binding, on the spot, and the thing where she agrees warmly and goes anyway at three in the morning with the kitchen clean and a note on the table ends on Thursday. She picked Amy, the only person who has ever caught her at it. Amy took it and made it a trade: both ways or neither. Nine hands went up.

Two nights later Mei handed Lynae a folded printout on a loading dock: the Architect has been on campus the whole time, at the funeral and at the gate, and has taken an interest in **Sigrika**, and has a name for her. Mei had carried it since before the kite, through five days of honest and eight rules, because on that table it would have kept Chisa and Lupa home and made Amy volunteer to stay, and all three would have been wrong. She offered the confession without dressing: she built a machine for five days to keep herself honest and then went round the back of it. Lynae had already chosen to stay for that girl days before the table — the first thing in eleven years she picked out on her own for a person instead of a fee — and Mei had just turned it into a job, so that every time she sits on that floor and makes her eat something she will be on post. She took the file anyway, and cannot tell Sigrika, because a girl who knows she is being watched changes how she walks and the thing watching her reads walks. Her price was a real date, written down: **the day they come back through that gate**, whoever is still standing walks straight past the soup and tells her the whole thing, including that Lynae knew.

Later, in a kitchen with one lamp on, Iuno admitted she would probably have gone anyway if nine hands had stayed down, being about sixty per cent of the way to the woman who would have stayed. And Mei admitted worse: she would have left alone on the Thursday, early, on the bike, with the kitchen clean and the blanket folded and a note on the table saying something warm and completely useless. That is what the five days were for — she wanted it to be theirs so badly that she built a machine to make it theirs, and she built it because she already knew what she would do if it was not. The vote was taken again on the last morning, formally, in the cold of a Thursday: seven going, two staying, nine in favour.

## V. FIELD NINE

The Institute's forms came down on the Monday. **Field 9 wants one name, the relation, and an address reachable inside ninety days; the nominee must remain outside the deploying party; blanks are disallowed** — under a line of small type saying the field exists so that a letter has somewhere to go, and that the Institute regrets its necessity. Iuno read it twice, sat with her hand over her mouth for eleven minutes, then made tea and got everyone up.

The kitchen did the arithmetic in thirty seconds in seven private orders and arrived at the same door. Lynae took it first and out loud, with the lights on, because what the box means is that some morning in nine weeks a woman with a formal face comes up her stairs, and she would rather agree to that at a table than find out on a landing: seven mornings signed for, the invoice clearing when all seven come back and take them off her. Rebecca split it four and three, having had it twice and knowing exactly what the door sounds like — two knocks, then a pause that is too long, because they are deciding whether to do the third one — and asked for the odds on her side in writing, knowing that is not how it works. Amy wrote two letters of a name and stopped, and every person at that table saw Iuno see it: everybody she has is on the bus. She named Rebecca, because Rebecca draws people, and if it goes wrong she wants to go on the wall. Lupa's form came back with the box empty; she wrote the *blanks are disallowed* clause herself once, in Septimont, for people who had somebody.

Mei went to a noodle stand in the Third Ward, where **Chiyo** announces her own name into a room before entering it, pitched for the middle distance, so a girl who cannot hold faces always knows who has come in. On the counter lay a sheet with seven names on it, eleven times each, in a borrowed alphabet, in a hand that stayed careful to the bottom of the page; an eighth is pending, because she puts a name down when it comes up twice, the first time being anybody. Beside her elbow sat a second bowl, steaming and untouched, because it is Thursday and Chisa comes most Thursdays, and when she does not it goes cold and Chiyo pays either way. Four people have been very moved about this and every one of them got it wrong: **it is a seat, and a person needs somewhere she is expected**, and expecting that starts when she walks in is a thing called luck, and luck charges differently.

Told that Mei was taking her daughter into a war, Chiyo corrected the sentence — Mei is going to a war and Chisa is going with her, and the difference between those two sentences is the only thing she has left that anybody can give her. She has signed a paper about her daughter once already, in a room where a man buckled a clasp onto Chisa's throat while a printed line called the signature a formality; the entire difference is that Mei came to her table. When Chisa arrived, Chiyo read the whole form at the speed of a woman burned by fine print, wrote her name and address, and wrote the relation last, taking her time — **mother** — the whole word the same weight all the way through. Then she dictated the letter she wants if it goes wrong, having had one before from an office that ran to four lines with two of them about entitlements: write the day, and the hour if you have it, and if you do not have it write that you do not; write what she was doing with her hands; write the name of whoever was holding her and spell it out, because she intends to say it aloud for a long time and would like to get it right; sign it yourself, in an envelope you have touched, carried to the post yourself. **Then come to this table on a Thursday, and tell her before the paper does.**

The same instrument produced stranger answers elsewhere. Cartethyia had **seven dolls** finished by two in the morning, one for each traveller, made for the people staying, on the procedure she ran for twenty years and came out of with her own name still on her: you make the thing and you say the name and then the dark has to go round it. There was an eighth in the basket — a girl in a green coat with a bird stitched on the hat brim the size of a grain of rice, made off the letter, so the writer would be in the house before they got there — and a ninth, paper-pale cloth, cut and pinned and left blank at the head, unfinishable, because she has not got the name, and you need a whole name to sew.

Amy brought her mother six written rules and shut the door with her heel, which is a tell she learned by having it done to her. Say a number coming back, and a different one every time, so the thing returning cannot counterfeit the thing that went. A hand or a voice on her at all times. Chisa listens. A clock, and the number is eleven. Fifth was a doll on the blanket — a school jacket in the wrong blue, a radio the size of a thumbnail in one hand, **NYX** in acid green on the shoulder — to be held before anything, so the fingers are closed and whoever has the shift does not have to look at them open; Amy watched them do exactly that for her for eleven weeks at the foot of a bed, and has had three weeks to work out where she picked up the habit that put her there. And sixth, underlined twice, was Mei's own rule handed back — **you do it in front of us** — because she made it a rule for the house and exempted herself in her own head four seconds later, and Amy knows the exact face. Then the tell that closed it: before Mei goes somewhere she is not planning to come back from, she does the dishes, including the clean ones, and folds the blanket with the seam turned in, and puts the shoes toes-level under the bench. She did it the morning she left the cabin; Amy came down at six and the kitchen was perfect; she was nine and thought it meant they were having company. The house has had this for three weeks. Lupa has Tuesdays: the sink.

At the kitchen table at one in the morning Iuno confessed she had carried the letter inside her coat since the roof and could not read one character, and asked Mei to read the third paragraph aloud — which she did easily, thumb moving down the column, stopping where the sentence ended, which her mouth knew a beat before she did. Then Iuno asked her to write the word for *lake*, and got one clean horizontal stroke, and four more words produced four more single strokes, each first and correct and alone. The Row's phrase for it, out of Mengzhou, is that **the crossing keeps what is made of sound**: a tide is already a kind of wind, the sound comes through, and the rest gets left at the gate. Iuno's second confession was the song Mei sang on the roof, written down phonetically at four in the morning from two flights below and carried three weeks in secret; Ciaccona cannot place the mode, and Chisa says the letter and the song are the same chord four notes apart and that she has heard it before and cannot say where. Iuno's reason for going is Mei; her reason for packing three weeks early is a page she cannot read, in a city where several thousand people can.

Mei took it plainly: leaving is the one fact anybody has ever established about her, and it is in every file — she leaves, and does something enormous, and somebody has to go and get her. Having sat that Thursday with a woman who taught herself a whole alphabet in case a list ever went up, she could see the shape of what might be on the other end: a house, a room, kept. **She would rather find out with somebody standing next to her.** Iuno's estate in return is a temple, a set of robes cut for a larger girl, and one sentence carried thirty years at full weight — *a Priestess must never fall in love* — which is going catastrophically, and which she intends to keep up.

Iuno filed her own form at four in the morning, struck out Mei and Amy because both are deploying, and entered **Lilith, correspondent, Penrose-512, last recorded heading the Etheric Sea**, with a note that correspondence is received and a reply has been had — which the registry would reject, and which she entered anyway, being the registry, that being the single convenient feature of the entire arrangement. Mei filed on the Wednesday, put *rope* in the occupation box, and then at Field 9 her hand wrote **three characters** in one unbroken movement at the speed of a signature, in the hand of the letter, with a small trailing hook. She looked at it, and then turned her hand over and looked at that, and asked what she had written. Iuno had no idea, beyond that it has the shape of a name.

## VI. THREE GOODBYES AND A STRUCK-OUT FOURTH

Two days out, a list of three goodbyes came out of Iuno's coat and went under the pot, which is where the house keeps its business — and under the third name stood a fourth line in a hand nobody in that house writes, small and upright and spaced as though set rather than written, **struck through once, cleanly, with a rule**. Held to the lamp, the characters under the strike were the hand from the letter and the hand out of Mei's own fingers. Somebody had written a fourth person onto her goodbyes and then crossed them off, which is worse.

**Professor Mornye** hears a chair before a door and has kept the same office thirteen years, declining one refurbishment in writing. She gave four honest minutes rather than nine polite ones, and a typed manifest, because she writes badly by hand and the recipient was going somewhere with weather: ninety salt tablets; hydration salts; a laminated Discord-frequency reference sorted by what each class does to a resonator's inner ear rather than by taxonomy, because taxonomy is for people with a laboratory; nine pairs of socks held since the autumn, because they were correct in the autumn and have remained correct; eleven years of river level against the dam's stated discharge; and marshmallows entered as *Item six. Morale, calorific*, on which she holds data. She has thirteen years of data on Amy, a sentence she delivered while reaching past her for the kettle, and which went and stood in the middle of the room.

Asked to come, she produced three pages dated March with a cost column, written before the kite on the general principle that this household leaves — transport solved, supervision load routable by courier, and page three, where it stops. Then Amy said that was not the real reason and got it: **forty-one**, being applications to the Synchronist track since the President finished with the investor board, against eleven before the sealing and six in Amy's own year. By autumn there will be children in Rabelle who grew up hearing that a girl held a door for ten years and came home to a party, and Mornye writes the load curves that decide which of them sits in a chair. She intends to stay, and would like it recorded that she wants to go — having spent her working life in a seat doing sums while other people walked into things, and having once said a thing in a field, in flowers, undignified, about walking together this time, which she has had eleven months of being pleased with and now gets to revise a second time.

Then she showed Amy the cabinet: steel, four drawers, off-system, a disciplinary matter on the strictest reading of her terms, kept thirteen years in a room with her name on the door, and nobody has ever asked. Postcard cards — weekly for four years, fortnightly when the subject acquired friends and the data got noisy, weekly again from the fifth year because she got good at hiding things and Mornye raised the sampling rate. Every card in the first two years carries **a single brushed character** in the corner, the way an address sits on an envelope, got on the corner of a requisition slip the night Mei asked to be deleted and pushed it across a bench, forty minutes before she left the building and the record and the city. Mornye has been writing to a character she cannot read, at no address, about a child who was not hers, inside a building where she had personally erased the only person who might have wanted them; her preferred word for it is **filing**.

The mark stops clean, and the nine weeks between are filled by two sheets of letterhead: a request for release from an informal supervisory arrangement with the reasons box blank, and its withdrawal eleven days later, both kept in the same folder for eleven years, in order, because a result you dislike is still a result. She stopped putting the heading on them because the addressee was doing nothing with them. Amy kept to herself the thing she knows: that she owns a diary that opens *Dear Ma* and runs that way a long time, and that at some point the salutation comes off and the entries continue for years addressed to nobody at all, in a smaller hand, about work.

Past a divider the cards stop, and the last carries two words and a figure in a hand that went wrong halfway through and continued anyway: **99.6. Held.** Mornye said it with a subject and a verb, having spent ten years not writing it down properly — the abort in her hand, thumb on it, four and a half seconds, and a nineteen-year-old asking her to leave it, and she left it. Amy told her, in a room with the paperwork open, that the nine people who call it her fault are wrong: somebody in that room did what she asked when everybody else in her life had done what they thought was best for her, and it was the worst outcome available, and it was **hers**. The fourth drawer holds three cards since the gate, no heading on any, the last recording a deliberately wrong cocoa recipe offered as the year's single measurement — which Amy clocked in the shop and let go, because Mornye looked like she needed it, and has therefore spent a year thinking she got away with it.

**Lucilla**, President of the Institute, had countersigned Iuno's Field 9 three times against a clerk's technically flawless appeals, disliking an open line in a file belonging to somebody who is leaving. She has costed her own won argument to one child since February, most evenings, twice running it a third time hoping for a different figure; and she used Amy twice, for the funding and for the amnesty, and asks to be held to the whole of it in the same breath, because the second use is why that household has legal enrolments and a birth record. Then she asked Iuno to write an abort protocol for the Synchronist chair, the current instrument being one line long and reading *supervising faculty shall exercise judgment*. Iuno refused in a way that took thirty years, and gave the basin and the column and the word and the continent it cost. Lucilla's counter was that somebody writes that rule this year regardless, and if Iuno declines it goes to a careful man in compliance who has never signed a form that ended in a funeral, and his rule will be excellent and built of thresholds, and the first time a threshold and a person disagree the threshold will win and he will be commended.

Iuno's condition was that **every limit be stated as somebody's tolerance, carry that person's name, and be signed by that person**, in the copy the candidate reads. Lucilla produced a draft already saying exactly that, dated four days earlier, written at two in the morning after reading a five-day protocol built at a kitchen table by nine people, in which eight rules were owned by name and six of the eight were the owner's own limit. She had the clause and lacked an author who could survive the committee — a rule you are argued into is a rule you defend for a season, and a rule you arrive at is a rule you defend from a war. Iuno's price was one line in Lucilla's own hand, filed with the draft, describing how the author came to write it, because in twenty years somebody will die inside it and there will be an inquiry, and she would like the file to say what actually happened in that room instead of the tidy thing.

And then the other business. Lucilla had had the three characters read twice, by two readers who do not know each other, paid separately, because one reading of three characters is an opinion. The first, Academy staff, calls it a personal name in a provincial hand thirty years out of date in its stroke order. The second — **a letter-writer at a gate in Jinzhou**, forty years in the trade, charging by the character — agrees about the first character and declines to call the other two a name, because they are a formula, cut on lintels and painted on gates all over the province in exactly that pairing, and on grave-tablets too. Together they mean **there is someone**: over a door, the house is occupied, don't pass by; on a stone, what you would expect; and as a given name, what a family calls the next child after a bad year, which looks ridiculous on paper and does not to anybody who was there. Both readers agreed independently on the first character, a real surname held by real households in a specific prefecture, which Lucilla wrote on a slip and folded rather than saying aloud, because Iuno will have to say it to Mei in her own house with her own mouth.

**Luuk Herssen** ran Mei's panel three times and got what he always gets: clean, structureless, cheerful noise where a person's numbers should be. He wrote *unremarkable*, as he has for nine years, because every honest word puts a Collective inspector in that building inside a fortnight and he knows which of his patients they would ask about first. Then he told her to ask the question she had brought to the door on nine of eleven visits, and gave her the true answer first because it is the smallest: he does not know what she was planning, and in four years of two-in-the-morning confidences she never told him, and he has concluded she was doing it on purpose.

Three things, in order. She kept a list of city names, which he knows because she asked him about climate and water and pressure sickness in a rhythm, one city at a time, for a year, and then stopped and started again from the top — and twenty-four years ago she asked him about a river bowl with a dam above it, in a country that writes downward, while stealing from his sweet dish. On a roof at an indefensible hour she asked what happens to a thing when you take away the door holding it shut, and where it goes through *to*, and then said she would need somewhere for it to go; he has had three decades to find a benign reading and has produced four and believes none of them, and she was cheerful when she said it, which is the part he would have left out if he were being kind. And third, an envelope she left with him eleven days before she went, with an instruction in the common script: if she comes back and does not know him, leave it and let her be new; and if she goes again, give her this, and do not ask what it says, because she will not know either. She knew she would arrive not knowing and that her own hand would be shut to her, and wrote a contingency for both, in advance, on a Tuesday, while eating his candy, and then asked what was for lunch. He opened it in the second year, has never pretended otherwise, and could not read a stroke, which is the whole of his crime and cost him nothing, which is the humiliating part; he twice tried to have it read and twice stopped himself, burning the reply slip the second time, because if it said something he could act on he would act on it, and his instruction said let her be new.

He came to that city twenty-seven years ago for a man who had said he would come and was never within four seas of it, and got a post, and a second post, and then a life; and after about the eighth year you stop being able to tell whether you are waiting or whether you simply live here. He writes every month — three hundred and twenty-odd letters, verified as leaving the building four times — and has declined to investigate whether they arrive, because he knows what he would find and he likes the ritual. Everybody in that building writes to somebody who does not answer; it is the local industry. He refused to let Mei carry one, having read the file on the gate where ten minutes became fourteen, and gave her instead an errand that costs nothing: in a garrison city, standing next to a physician, off duty, ask lightly whether they have ever treated a patient **who bled gold** — and then watch the hands rather than the face.

At the sweet dish by the door his pen stopped: **she took the sour ones.** Every Tuesday for four years, thirteen years before her daughter did the same thing at the same dish, with the same absolute conviction that he had failed to notice. He bought extra for her, and stopped, and started again when a child arrived with a fifty-credit haircut and no manners, and it took him until the second year to hear what he was doing. Mei reached in without looking, the way you reach into a drawer in your own house, and her hand came out with a sour one, and the wrapper was the colour it had always been.

That night Iuno laid out the photograph, the folded slip, and the page from Luuk's cabinet, and Mei read the surname and put it down, because it does nothing and she keeps waiting for it to do something — Chisa's mother heard her heel on a floor and knew her, and this is a shape. Then she pulled the pad across, drew one stroke, stopped — and then picked the pen up again and wrote the thing from the form, and her hand kept going. **Four characters.** Six times at a signature's speed; on the seventh her hand stopped after three and sat there like a limb gone to sleep, and then produced the fourth anyway, slower, with a wobble the others lacked. There is one more than there was, which Iuno declined to call good and called **information**.

## VII. THE NEAR STONE

Running beside all of this, in the same city, is the account of a girl who was already gone.

**Sigrika** is nineteen, from a valley in the Roya, and reads Old Royan — an alphabet cut before it was written, which is why it has no curves in it. Boundary stones come in pairs: a field has a near stone and a far stone, the formula runs across both, and the reader is expected to walk. At the break the cutter leaves **a tick**, two shallow strokes angled down, which the grammars gloss *continuation* and the road crews call the go-on; it carries no sound and no meaning of its own, and dropping it ruins a translation, because it is the whole difference between a sentence that has finished and a sentence that is somewhere else. Where a stone is broken you set a bracket and leave the bracket standing, because an emendation that is loved becomes a reading, and a reading loved long enough becomes the stone, and then the field has a new edge that nobody ever cut.

**Denia** went into the black water at the bottom of a borehole under the Dimmr Plains — a hole a clerk with a Friday deadline named *Lahai-Roi* out of a book he read on Sundays, after a well in a desert where a woman nobody was keeping a record of found out she had been seen, and said so, and gave the place its name. Sigrika came up out of it with half her ink gone, and the water came up behind her and made a lake, and nobody has been down since, because there is no down. Denia's enrolment record has a free-text field for a person of contact that most students fill at induction and never touch, and most of them write *mother*; hers was amended in second year, in a hand that slants backward, and it has said Sigrika's name for two years and eight months.

At the Registry, deputy registrar **Fenn** copied the object Sigrika had brought — a facility duty list headed **Subject D**, ink thirteen months dry, which means somebody wrote it and then went home and had dinner — and Sigrika watched the machine make its long grey noise and understood for the first time that a true thing can be photographed into meaninglessness. Then Fenn produced the disposition form: withdrawn, awarded, deceased, each needing a signature or a certificate or a coroner's order, and six ruled lines under one word, REMARKS. There is no certificate, and no order, and there is a lake — and if nothing comes in by end of term the system closes it itself, and its default is **administrative withdrawal, on the ground of non-attendance**. Sigrika made her write that down, with the date, and Fenn did, on a compliment slip, in a bureaucrat's small immaculate hand. At the door Sigrika came back to ask one thing: was she polite? Fenn considered it with real care, and said she was terribly polite, and that it was very rude.

The season that followed is a girl running a discipline on her own life. She booked two quarter-hours on the long-distance line, which she has never done, and told her mother the ink was gone from the wrists to mid-forearm and had stayed stopped eleven days. Her grandmother, who will not take a telephone, sent four words through the kitchen — *Húðin man þik. Kom.* — which Sigrika made her mother repeat three times without tidying it, and wrote in blue, the ink for cross-references. *Man*: the skin remembers you, present indicative, the same verb that stood on the erased mark at her wrist. And the vowel was long, which belongs to the **stefnu**, the meeting-mood, attested on grave-markers and boundary stones and nowhere else, deprecated by the year the corpus closed: *the skin is going to remember you; come.* Both readings sat on the page and neither would move over, and a philologist told since the age of nine that every question has one correct answer stood in a booth and could not conjugate her own grandmother. The grammar says the mood is not to be constructed; its compiler has been dead sixty years and cannot be asked; her grandmother is seventy-nine and constructed it on a Tuesday, in a kitchen. Sigrika wrote *§414 — see also: Amma* underneath and put the book back face out, spine to the wall, which is what you do when you intend to come back. Also: her grandmother has put the soot jar by the door.

She collected testimony from people who were offering none. The fry-station cook said *double* before her mouth opened, and that in four years she never saw the girl eat anything else, and that she said *ta* every time, which students who have been there three years do not; Sigrika let two orders go cold in front of her over forty minutes, wrote it in red — the ink for data — and ordered double again the next day. She read four hundred and six entries on a memorial thread and left every one where it was, because copying them out would have been a way of holding them at arm's length; and when a report came in that no student of that name appears in the directory, she resolved it with one line, that **the directory is a copy, and copies go stale**. Then a reply arrived at 02:41 from an account eleven minutes old, saying the girl used to say the sky gets its blues wrong after four o'clock. Denia never once remarked on the sky. The person who said that said it in a study room with three colours of pen and a hum going under her breath, four bars, absent, the way a person hums while filing. Sigrika typed three different replies and deleted all of them, pinned the thread where the emergency notices go, and wrote in her journal: **wrong friend.**

A shopkeeper soldered the record switch shut on the toy she carries — a bubble wand holding a recording — so it will play and never record again, took no payment, and gave her one instruction for nothing: run it now and then, because the membranes go stiff if they are never worked, and the safest thing in the world is a drawer, and it ruins everything you put in it. A conductor with shaking hands read the recording by ear and gave her a heartbeat at fifty-eight that slows at the end of a phrase and picks up again — an organ with a person over the top of it, breathing, taking their time about it, doing it for somebody — and then refused to be a witness to whose it was, because Sigrika would file it, and in six months the document would be doing her believing for her, and then somebody would find a fault in the document. Instead she drew a fermata and wrote **lunga** under it: a hold, the only mark in four hundred years of the trade that refuses to say how long, because the instant you write a number under it you have written a different mark.

At the shrine above the glacier Sigrika swept a step that had needled since the day it opened, and asked **Hiyuki** to take the reach into the lattice. The cold went in, found what it was looking for, and came back with its hands empty: the lattice takes what stayed on the near shore, and hers **went across**. And then, at once, before a cathedral could be built on it, Hiyuki said out loud that the girl's whereabouts are exactly as unknown tonight as this morning, and that the shrine's opinion is worth very little — because Sigrika would have gone down the ridge with it and read it eleven times before sleeping, and in three months, on a bad Tuesday, decided it was a trick and lost it, and lost the evening as well, and a mercy like that spends at a very poor exchange rate. So Sigrika asked instead for it in writing, which cost ninety seconds and good paper and was correct and complete and did nothing whatsoever — because she already saw it, and there is no instrument in the world that will tell you a thing you already know; every one of them goes silent about the thing you want, which is different, and further away, and has no form. She may have a hundred of them; Hiyuki will write one a week for the rest of her life and never once ask why.

Afterward Hiyuki asked to be taught the threshold stone she has stood on four thousand times. It is a re-used field stone laid face up because somebody wanted a flat rock, carrying the standard perimeter formula, the first thing they teach you to read: **halt, this far.** A shrine with no lock and no hours and a broom for whoever is nearest, and the thing everybody walks over says *stop*. And at the worn edge, half eaten by a century of boots, two shallow strokes angled down. It is **a near stone**: the sentence continues on the far stone, and the far stone is wherever it is, and you are supposed to walk to it. Sigrika heard herself say that they came in pairs, and stopped. The lamp guttered and stood, and Hiyuki made a sound that was a different sound from her others, and both of them heard it be different.

She did the transcription on a Sunday night with a shaving mirror propped against a stack of books, properly, the way she has done it forty times for other people's stones. Her forearms are a text; she has known that the way you know a thing you have never said aloud, and has gone twenty years without reading them. The right arm reads *the road ahead* above the loss and clean skin below it. The left parses as a tail with no head — a verb inflected to agree with a subject no longer anywhere on her body. And at the break, set into the very bottom of the surviving spiral, so small she has run her thumb across it a thousand times without once looking at it, **two shallow strokes, angled down**. Her grandmother finished the first spiral at her wrist when she was six, as every grandmother in the Roya finishes it, with the go-on. She has been carrying a continuation mark since the age of six; the void ate everything below it and stopped at the join; and what is left of her begins with an instruction to go and find the rest. She wrote it up in full and left the bracket unemended: **near stone; far stone not located; formula printed as it stands, per convention.**

Then she came to the twelfth word — the one nobody in the Roya can read and every one of them carries, laid at the wrist by a grandmother in a kitchen, which the standard grammar prints as a numbered blank. It is gone, and she has no image of it, because nobody photographs their own arms. Exactly one image of that mark existed anywhere in the world, inside four years of photographs taken from under tables and across rooms by a girl who was never meant to have preferences — and Sigrika held that journal in both hands, in the water, and opened her fingers. There is a lake over it now. She put her head down on her folded arms for a while, and then sat up and finished the apparatus, because the apparatus is indifferent to how you feel, which is the single kindest thing about it.

Two results came in that week and she kept both against instruction. The Heliogyre residual, returned marked as instrument drift to be smoothed and resubmitted, is **eleven counts and a rest**, present in every set since autumn, and it moves — each repetition sourced fractionally further east along the root network, like a word walking, and drift keeps no beat. And underneath it, at the floor of the instrument's range where the manual says to disregard, there is something else: **one beat, slow, alone**, holding no relation to the eleven, staying exactly where it is, on a bearing running south and slightly down, out under the plains, toward a basin full of black glass. She retained both appendices under her own authority, left the source bracket empty, and posted them at eleven at night when there was nobody to ask what she was doing.

Then she filed the disposition form with all three boxes empty and the remarks field full: *status, absent, field; duration, until the weather turns.* Told the system would kick it back every term, she said she would file it every term; asked for how long, she thought about a fermata and a word in pencil and a woman standing in front of forty children with her arm in the air, and said **there is no number, and that is the mark.** Fenn took off her glasses, got up, and put it in the physical cabinet nobody has used since the migration, which the Registry keeps because a building that throws out its old cabinet is a building with no memory: the system does what the system does, and the drawer is hers.

That week Sigrika heard the household's five-day argument through a floor, and learned to reach past a plain white cup on a twelfth hook for the one she wanted, the same as everybody else. Lynae wrote a four-figure number on the kitchen whiteboard with no box and no word beside it, and nobody rubbed it out, and it came to nothing against days, kilometres, rosters, serials or four calendars, and went into the journal in blue with the other things that were true and would not sit down. On the Friday her own lost pen was in her pigeonhole with a new cartridge in it, and no note, and no record at the porter's desk, and nothing in the book. That night she took a white marker to the black endpaper at the back of her journal and set the bracket, and wrote inside it what convention requires and nothing else — **[1 word, illegible]** — and after it, at the edge, two shallow strokes, angled down. Then she put the recording beside her ear where a heartbeat goes and ran it, because the membranes go stiff if they are never worked, and slept six hours, a record for the term, and woke without once checking the time.

## VIII. THE LIST ON THE KITCHEN WALL

The rules went up at six on yellow paper taped between the spice rack and the calendar: one bag each, instruments count as persons; snack stops every four hours, mandatory; driver rotation, everybody takes a turn; the group chat name is final; first to complain about the route is on dinner duty; and sixth, *we are having fun, this is a rule, see above re: mandatory.* Lupa added a seventh in red forbidding Mei from naming anything else until they cross the city line. Cartethyia won an eighth: **we stop for birds.**

While Mei took the market party out, Lupa and Iuno chose the northern variant through the Reaches, which adds two days and avoids the choke at the gorge — because two extra days is two more nights around a fire and two more mornings of Mei waking with her daughter real and the war in front of them and both of those true at once. Lupa had already marked it, wanting the extra campfires, which she builds deliberately too large so everyone has to sit close and calls heat distribution. Iuno had filed their passage on the Tuesday, four days before Mei announced the trip, having been planning since the boots.

That evening every person in the house cooked one dish from wherever they are from, on four burners, in a kitchen exceeding its designed occupancy by a factor of two: Septimontian bread with anchovies and rosemary; miso from scratch with tofu cut so even it looked printed; tteokbokki from a recipe memorised at twelve from a woman who is dead, in a kitchen that is gone, in a city Lynae was trained to deny she was from; lemon cakes that against prediction became lemon cakes; toast assembled with the grim efficiency of a combat loadout, which everyone reached for first; hot chocolate with the cinnamon that lives behind the flour when the flour exists; and zongzi studied out of the Academy archive under *Regional Cuisine, Eastern Nations, Huanglong, Municipal District: Jinzhou*, with the string wound three times too many until Amy said it goes diagonal, having memorised a photograph behind a physician's candy dish. In the centre, alone, stood Iuno's teapot, placed with the expression of a woman who had contributed what she had and dared anyone to mention it.

They toasted the road, the birds, the playlist, the food, getting there, and the toast; and Iuno said **to coming back** into her cup, quiet enough that the table moved on over it. Late, after the dishes, Mei added a rule beneath Lupa's red one and smoothed the peeling tape down: **8. We come back.**

## IX. THE LOADING DOCK

The vehicle came off the Collective's disposal desk as **Lot 411, sold as seen**: a Mingting Heavy six-wheel survey platform whose habitat body had spent eleven years as a mobile dental surgery, with a floor-bolted patient chair whose removal was quoted above the value of the truck and a six-foot laminated molar adhered to the wall. It had been listed four times and withdrawn three, each withdrawal signed by a different officer, none of whom minuted a reason; and field 4, vessel name, was left blank by the purchaser, who was advised twice that the field is mandatory and produced on the second occasion a printed copy of the regulation demonstrating that it is not. On the sample bed behind it knelt **NYX RAVENHOLLOW**, four metres of white ceramic angel under a tarp, her lance in two pieces in a padded case.

Mornye came down at one in the morning and found the rear axle carrying four point one tonnes against a plate rating of four and a half — correctly calculated, and calculated on a level floor, with eleven kilometres of four per cent grade ahead. The fix was to move the machine eleven centimetres forward: it is usually eleven centimetres, and it is almost never a new truck, and people want it to be a new truck. She left an envelope in the driver's door pocket, where a person sitting down finds a thing and a person searching never looks, and taped the corrected figure to the dash in a hand that had been set rather than written. Thanked, she said *noted*, which is what she has instead, and which she is told banks.

At 03:04 Amy did a last broadcast off the books on an unlicensed band, where a child of about eight asked whether taking the mech was allowed. She answered him straight, on an open band, on purpose: it is four metres of somebody else's property with a weapon on it and a permit that stops at their own front door, and taking it over a border makes her **a thing arriving instead of a girl on a road trip** — and she knows what that looks like from underneath, having been the city watching something come over the ridge, with nobody asking whether she wanted it. She would rather arrive heavy than arrive late, which is the reason everybody uses, and which she likes a great deal less in her own mouth than she did in other people's. She signed off in the form a certain registrar likes: **Aemeath, of the cabin with the blue door, Roya Frostlands, Ward 734; back when she is back; keep the radios on.**

At 04:14 Mei painted the characters on the cab door and got four down at the speed of a signature — and then the brush went back to the tin without being sent, and loaded, and came up, and put **a fifth character** beside them with the same confidence and the same trailing hook, and her arm sat down on her knee like a dog told to wait. She had decided to do four. Iuno photographed it from three angles and gave the worst of her three readings, being the one she believes: **something is getting closer to being able to finish a sentence.**

The rest of the night was people handing things over. Ciaccona gave Rebecca her spare lute and made her play four notes eleven times, telling her to play them badly out loud where the vents can get at it, because a song that belongs to everybody cannot be confiscated — and came back four steps down the stairs to add that every hand she has ever taught changes at twelve weeks. Iuno weighed every bag with the face of a woman conducting a customs inspection on people she loves, and entered the lute under **persons**, because rule one went on the wall and nobody struck it. Cartethyia left her camellia with Lynae, took cuttings, and handed over the box of seven dolls. Luuk arrived pushing the Institute's rolling stool four hundred metres down a corridor, made Lupa sit on it, gave Chisa the field kit by its order rather than its labels because at some point she will open it in the dark, and doubled items four and seven — sleep and water — knowing they are the two every person there will ignore. And the dock's night supervisor, twenty-two years in post, knotted a strip of yellow tape round the base of the wing mirror and cut the tail off with the blade on her pen: forty times in twenty-two years, thirty-eight of them came back.

At two minutes to six Chisa stopped four conversations by standing in the middle of them. The collar has gone off at six every morning for sixty-two days and she has always arranged to be somewhere else for it, and it took her two months to work out she was doing that for herself and calling it manners — so she would like them to have heard it before they go, it being four seconds. It chimed, and Ciaccona wrote it down as a descending minor third, a hair flat on the second note and flat for twenty years in a building full of engineers, because it is a note somebody chose and Chisa has been carrying it alone since before they met. Lupa crossed the dock in four strides, put a hand on the top of her head for exactly as long as she holds it on anybody, and went back to her straps.

In the cab Iuno found the grab handle moved to the left, a machined spinner knob at ten o'clock, and every control inside an arc her left hand can walk without her shoulder leaving the seat — three weeks of nights, and four collars made because the standard part is four millimetres off on that column, the first three thrown away. So Iuno told her the thing she had been awake about for nine nights and told nobody: rule three says everybody drives, and every version of the arithmetic ended with her standing in a road in the second week explaining to six people who love her that she could not take her turn. Then she put Lynae's name in the log on the modification line, in ink, with a date, because Lynae has spent her entire life making herself unfindable and it has cost her everything she ever had, and Iuno is a registrar, and this is the only weapon she carries. Lynae's own reason came out under pressure: she wanted one thing on that truck that she made, so that when it is in a mountain pass at night there is a piece of her in the cab doing something. Iuno named it **a bailment** — a thing deposited to be kept and given back, on a term this house knows, which is *when I come back*, and which no instrument in the world closes.

At half past six **Sigrika** came down the ramp with a folder against her chest: rule eight, discharged in advance. Nineteen species for the eastern corridor and four they almost certainly will not see, drawn from forty-year-old plates in green ink, each with a wingspan, a call in her own private phonetics, and **a box for the date** — just the date, because people write things clever and then stop doing it by day nine. Cartethyia took it in both hands and declared it, at a volume that made a man on the gantry drop a spanner, **a commission, hereby accepted, to be discharged in full and returned to the hand that issued it with the boxes filled.** Then Sigrika came down the dock with her chin up and said she was not asking, having stopped asking, and that everybody gets to keep whatever they are keeping — but would like it on the record that she is not stupid, and that when somebody finally sits her down and tells her she is going to be very good about it, and would like *that* on the record too, in advance, so nobody spends three months being frightened of the conversation. Told there is a date, written down and signed, and that it is the day they come back through that gate, she called it a promise about a day nobody can guarantee, and said she had better see them at the gate. Lynae finished coiling a hand line and stood with both palms flat on the rear quarter panel with her head down for four seconds.

At 06:44 Rebecca shut the habitat door with her hip while carrying a crate — correctly, considerately — and eleven seconds later Cartethyia came out and said it in one go, at normal volume, to the dock in general: **she cannot have that shut.** It is a box with a light in it and a door that goes *clunk*, and she lived in one of those for twenty years, and she had been telling herself for nine days that she would be fine because the truck moves, and has just found out, at a quarter to seven, in front of everybody, that the moving is not the part that matters. And to Rebecca, who had started to apologise: if she apologises about a door, Cartethyia will genuinely never recover. Lupa was already walking. She came back with a lump hammer and a punch, drove both hinge pins up, took the door off by herself and leaned it against the bay wall in ninety seconds, and then held out two oiled steel pins, so that nobody else can put it back. Iuno logged it as a modification with the owner of the limit named and signed in the copy that goes to the border post — a clause she had written for somebody else on the Tuesday and had been dying to use on themselves.

**Lucilla** came down a ramp that requires a permit with two documents. The first was a letter of accreditation naming seven persons as a field party conducting a hydrological and resonance survey at their own risk and expense, with one legacy security platform under permit for a tethered low-yield emitter: it will carry them past two posts and fail at the third, and when it fails it will fail as a document rather than as a lie, which buys a conversation instead of a barrier. Every word in it is true and the whole of it is a costume, and both women know that is a real difference. The second was the abort-authority draft with four signatures in nine days, the first being Mornye's, who entered her own tolerance in the box in her own hand and then wrote a second line underneath that the form has no field for, which Lucilla told Iuno to read in a mountain pass. Then she went down the line with the candy tin, and Cartethyia stopped moving for the better part of a minute and reported that **there are two sours in it** — the sharp one everybody gets, and a second underneath that arrives when you have stopped expecting anything. Lucilla has been handing those out of that drawer for eleven years, watching every face and waiting for that sentence; she put the tin into Cartethyia's hands and closed both of Cartethyia's hands round it with both of her own, to be rationed and brought back empty.

At seven Chiyo went first, because she had decided to, and put a flask into her daughter's hands the same way. Rebecca crossed the dock and threw her canvas bag at Lupa's chest from four feet — jacket, twelve markers, everything she owns from the world she came from — because it goes on the truck and Lupa holds it and it comes back to that dock, in her hands, that being the only address it has and the only thing that counts as delivered. Where she is from, you leave your stuff with somebody and they have to come back; it is stupid and it does not work and she watched it not work six times and is doing it anyway. Lupa, who treats a thank-you like an ambush, put the strap across her body the way you carry a thing you intend to keep on you, unbuckled the front pocket, took out the green nub, and threw it to Amy, who caught it left-handed without looking up.

They got in, Mei last, because the arrangement now is that she goes last. Iuno found Mornye's envelope at 07:02: **forty-two blank river-level sheets**, pre-addressed and pre-stamped, in four bundles with a date on each band, and a typed card revising the sentence said once in a field about walking together — together being a scheduling problem, and she has solved worse, and the dates are on the bands. And as the convoy cleared bay three, four metres of ceramic angel came alive with her wings still folded and put Amy's voice through speakers rated for a parade ground — **keep the radios on** — over four notes played badly on a bad instrument by somebody who had learned them the day before.

At the top of the outlet road there is a strip of brass with LAHAI-ROI stamped along it in letters worn shallow by every vehicle that has ever left. They stopped on it, rule seven expired, and Mei named the truck **Lucky Bird**, after a fried chicken establishment on the ninth terrace. Chisa settled the objections: two syllables, both hard at the front, shoutable across a road with weather on it, which is the only test that matters and the one nobody ran. Iuno filled in field four, having waited eleven days for one box, because a girl in an underground city argued with a registrar to keep it empty for nine days so that a woman could make a bad joke on a ramp. And Amy knelt on cold brass and wrote **LUCKY BIRD** under the five black characters in a dead girl's acid green, four inches high, in a font she designed at fourteen; the nub gave out on the D and she finished it by holding the marker sideways and scrubbing.

Nineteen minutes earlier and eleven kilometres down, Rebecca had got five plates out of the cupboard before she noticed and stood at the counter holding five, and Chiyo told her to put back two and sit down. Afterward Chiyo read the yellow paper all the way down and asked for a pen and wrote a ninth rule at the bottom in a careful borrowed alphabet, one letter at a time: **9. There is someone in the house.** It is what you put over a door; it means somebody is in, and a traveller should not pass by. Told it was a nice thing to put on a rules list, she said it is not nice at all — it is an instruction to strangers, and nice is what people call it afterward. Rebecca went to the drawer, came back with a black marker, and went over Chiyo's letters one at a time, following the shape exactly and improving nothing, so that when the tape gives out and the ink fades the ninth rule will still be there in the hand of a woman who waited twenty years and knows how it is done.

## X. THE ROAD

*(The crossing ran twenty days: twelve across the frostlands and the Disputed Reaches to the mountain pass, then nine more from the border post to the lake — a great deal of which was weather, food, birds, argument and small kindness among strangers, and only some of which bears on what follows.)*

House rules went up inside a doorframe that has no door, amendable by any hand at any hour, contested amendments struck through and left legible — among them that you **say your own name before you come through the curtain**, and that **nobody discusses the tooth**, which stood unamended all the way to Jinzhou. Chisa found the truck's honest speed inside twenty minutes: above fifty-eight the kneeling mech takes the crosswind like a sail with a person-shaped hole in it and pulls a hand's width left every eleven seconds, and correcting that is what she would be doing for fourteen days if they ran at sixty-two because it looks better on a chart.

Then the truck taught them something Iuno needed. Her fuel chart was beautiful and rigorous and built on a number she had never questioned, because it came off a manufacturer's plate — and on the third evening Chisa found the fault by ear in four seconds, the burner cycling short in an ambient four degrees colder than the night before. The thermostat's sensor sits eleven centimetres above the burner casing, where it was put for a vehicle holding one dentist and a great deal of daylight, and it **runs four degrees high**, and has since the brass strip. The dial had said fifteen and the room had been at eleven; everybody who put it up was right; and the woman who kept putting it back down was working from an instrument that lies. The High Priestess identified it at once as **the column with *weather* in it — the correct procedure and the wrong instrument, beautifully enforced for three days.** Iuno said it out loud at her own expense, taped a real thermometer to the far wall away from every heat source, and wrote *instrument error, unverified by me, three days* into the log against her own name.

The language tape produced the crossing's other discovery. In the four-second pause after the first phrase, from the fourth bunk, at conversational volume and entirely without self-consciousness, **Cartethyia reproduced all four tones** — which Ciaccona, four hundred years a professional musician with twelve dialects transcribed, could not do, and knew exactly which ones she was missing and by how much. By the third day the tape had installed itself below the level at which a person consents to anything: Amy said *my friend is unwell* to a raven, and Mei said it in her sleep in an accent that made Chisa sit up in the dark.

Ciaccona took her first bike shift under protest and rode badly for four kilometres, and then stopped riding badly, and then stopped thinking about riding at all — and the realisation arrived complete in the fifth kilometre. She has been alone for four hundred years, professionally, on principle: arrive in a town, buy a round, play badly until somebody demands better, learn what a place believes, leave before the second market day. She had now spent seventy-nine consecutive hours in a steel box a metre wide at the galley with six other people, and her last hour alone had ended on a strip of brass. It was **homesickness, running backwards**. Cartethyia, who did twenty years in a room with eleven dolls, diagnosed it in one line: she has known them in houses, and houses have doors and streets and other rooms, and this has a curtain — and it goes away in about nine days and comes back the day you get off the truck, and that one is much worse and nobody warns you about it. Ciaccona also read eleven stanzas of an epic she had been writing since the brass strip and had waited four days for somebody to ask about; the eleventh had no joke in it and **ended on a half-line**, which everybody noticed and one person said so.

At the Collective relay on the plateau, the warden read the accreditation letter entire, including the annexe, standing at minus nineteen, and handed it back: it will do here, and it will not do at Kelvet, which is Ministry-staffed since the spring and run by a man who reads everything twice. Their paper is honest, and that is the trouble with it, because he will believe every word and then ask the question the paper does not answer — **does a survey need a lance?** And, outside his duties: eastbound traffic is up about a third, westbound up more, and westbound is families, and four of them in one fortnight said *the water's wrong* and none could say what they meant, and one got angry at being asked, which people do when they have said a true thing they cannot stand behind. His board has no cause column; it has *purpose of transit*, a line and a half of space, and what he writes in it is **personal**. Iuno, warned off by the High Priestess from telling a good man on an apron what a wrong box costs thirty years too late, said only that if he is ever instructed to add a column with one word in it, and the word is a kind of weather, he should object in writing, on the day, and keep his copy. He wrote something on the corner of his board and went in.

The tenth evening produced the confrontation the crossing had been building toward. Ciaccona brought Mei a drink on the tailgate, put the credit on the table first, and then delivered the thing she had sat on for three days: **Mei is running.** Up first on nine days of ten; the noise in every silence over four seconds long; a morale speech at twenty past six unprompted; and watching the whole table eat before she starts, which she does and thinks nobody sees. There is a specific tempo to it — you get up first and make the day happen, and by the time it is dark you have done fourteen small kindnesses and have not had one thought all the way through. Mei had known since about day six. On the road she is the one taking everybody somewhere, and anybody can do that, and there is a new hundred kilometres of it every day; **in Jinzhou she is the one who has to be right.** Ten days of not-yet, with everybody in one box where she can count them — and Iuno has clocked it and Lupa has clocked it and neither has said anything, and she has been enjoying that, which is also running.

Ciaccona withdrew half of what she came with and kept the rest: the arriving has been dreaded in the abstract for ten days, and the abstract is where a fear gets to be any size it likes, so make it small and put a date on it. What is the actual first decision? Whether to go to the cliff at dawn or the Ministry first — two options, one morning, and she already knows which one, and it is obviously the cliff. **So the thing she had been carrying for ten days was a decision she made before the city line.** Offered a question back, Ciaccona said the eleventh stanza has been on a half-line since the fourth night, that she knows exactly what goes there, that she has written it out nine times, and that every time she does she puts the notebook down and goes to check where Cartethyia is — and then declined to answer, and asked for credit for having let the question be asked at all.

At the top of the last rise before the mountains, Chisa asked for the frame she had reserved: all seven, in front of the truck, with the name on the door in it, taken by somebody else so nobody is missing. It is for her mother, who has had one photograph of her since she was fourteen and it is a school one and she is not really in it — and who will want to count them and see that it is seven, and will hold it up close and go through every single face in it, one at a time, out loud, saying the names. **And Chisa will never see it, and would like it taken properly anyway, and asked that somebody make sure Lupa's ears are in.**

---

Past the pass, the nine days from the border post to the lake ran against a Superintendent's written estimate of **two**, and three things in them bear on the rest.

At a bridge post, a man who has painted and read the same staff gauge for nineteen years produced, when asked — and nobody had ever asked; the Ministry publishes a figure, which is a different sentence — **forty-one years of river levels in four hands**, kept in school exercise books in a box under his counter, the oldest recording levels in *chi* with somebody converting them in pencil down the margin in a fifth hand he has never identified. Iuno sat at that counter two hours and ten minutes with Mornye's eleven-year chart and copied. The published figure runs **eight and a half per cent optimistic**, consistently, which makes it a method rather than an error — against Mornye's estimate of eight, made at four in the morning eleven thousand kilometres away, which is half a point out, and which Iuno will have to write and tell her, and which she will read in a corridor and say nothing about and put in a drawer.

At a school up a spur, Mei discharged Luuk's errand word for word, with Cartethyia translating and then stopping talking, asking a doctor cleaning a child's ear whether she had ever treated anybody **who bled gold**. The answer was no; brass once, off a man who worked with it; and if they mean jaundice the word is different. **The hands went on at exactly the rate they had been going before the question**, and Mei watched them all the way through, because she had been told to, and wrote *asked; no* on the back of a river-level sheet with a small four beside it — the fourth time she has asked a doctor something on somebody else's behalf and got a straight answer she will have to carry back a continent.

And at a standpipe up an unnamed spur, a man in a good shirt came out of the second house, watched them get the drums down, walked the length of his own lane, stood in front of the cab, and **read the door out loud — all five characters, slowly, at the pace of a man reading a lintel** — and then turned round and told them no. He named eleven times the meter rate in the voice of a man reading a bus timetable; Iuno paid it; and he opened the tap a fifth of a turn so it ran in a finger-thick rope with air in it, and told them one at a time, and stood there for ninety-four minutes. Lupa argued for leaving; Iuno held, being a party under bond with a named responsible person and four inspections ahead, and would spend ninety minutes standing in a lane before she would spend one paragraph in a file; and Mei added the other half, that the next foreign truck up that hill will be two people with a phrasebook, and whatever happens here is going to be waiting for them. Amy started to say a great deal at about minute twelve and Chisa put two fingers on her wrist and she stopped. Cartethyia held her own hand, and nobody had told her to. At six o'clock **the collar rang** — one clear note off a pattern issued for four hundred people before the seal, with no function whatsoever any more — and the man with the key looked up, and looked at Chisa for about two seconds, and looked back at the tap. At minute seventy a woman came out of the third house with a wooden stool, carried it down the lane, set it beside the smallest person present, and went back in and shut the door, keeping her eyes on the ground the whole way down and the whole way back, and so did he, and both of them were doing it on purpose.

The debrief went badly for forty minutes while four people produced four explanations, until Chisa stopped it: **they want a reason so it stops being about them.** Sit in it; it is five minutes; she has done it for twenty years and is still here. He heard the collar at six; he looked up; he knew what it was; he looked back at the tap. That is the whole file, and everything else is them doing his paperwork for him. And Cartethyia gave up the last piece at eleven o'clock in the dark, at the water drums, having translated nine hours of a country that day and gone quiet exactly once, in front of the cab door: **he read** *don't pass by* **off the side of that truck, out loud, in his own language, and then turned round and made them stand in a lane for an hour and a half, and she could not think of one single reason to tell Mei that.** Told not to be sorry — she held it for six hours to see whether it needed carrying, and then gave it to her at eleven at night by a water drum where nobody could see her face, which is a skill Mei knows exactly who she got off — she asked whether it is a bad one, and was told it is a good one used at the wrong distance, and they will work on the distance.

Ciaccona wrote the missing half-line in a tobacco shed on the nineteenth day, in the rain, in ink, with hands stained purple to the second knuckle, and when asked what she was writing said **the weather** — and Cartethyia said it was very good weather and went back to a card game, having accepted it in about a second and a half, at least half a second faster than a person accepts a true statement, in the opinion of the two people at the other end of the shed who happened to be looking; and Chisa turned her head very slightly and Mei kept stirring, and the two of them left it exactly where it was.

Iuno did the arithmetic that afternoon, having put it off for two days by doing four other pieces of work first with great efficiency: twenty-three halts between the border pole and a tobacco shed, listed by place, time, duration, cause — and the cause column had gone wrong, because she had been writing it in the vocabulary of a woman running a schedule, and reading all twenty-three together the vocabulary came apart in her hands. **In nineteen of the twenty-three, the truck had stopped and then somebody had come to it.** She went through again and wrote down, for each, where that person had been standing when they started walking, and in every single case the answer was: somewhere they could see the door of the cab.

She sat with the log for four minutes, then walked round to the front of the truck in the rain and stood in front of five black characters that had come out of a woman's hand over four seasons, one at a time, in a script she cannot read, in a stroke order out of the schools for thirty-one years. She had been keeping the distance column for seven days as an accusation — sixty-one, forty-four, twenty-nine, thirty-one, eleven, fourteen, four — and carrying that number around like a bill. **They have been driving a lintel across a country that reads lintels. There is a blessing painted on the door of that vehicle in a hand nobody can source, and it says** *don't pass by***, and for seven days this country has been doing what it says.** Mei took a minute with that, having looked at the door every morning for nineteen days and thought about a village with eleven families on it and whether anybody there is alive, and having just been told it has been working the whole time.

Iuno gave her the minute, and then named the fault: **the man with the key read it too.** He read all five characters at the pace of a man reading a lintel and then charged eleven times the rate and stood over them for ninety-four minutes — and he goes in the same column as the other nineteen, in the same hand, with no annotation, **because a door that only ever produces welcomes is a lie about doors**, and she has written four hundred instruments in her life that were lies about doors and has decided to stop. Mei asked that the woman with the stool go in too, and was told she was already in it.

The rain stopped at four and the whole valley steamed off the terraces until the bowl filled with white and the road ran through it at head height. They drove eleven minutes. Then Chisa took her foot off, found Iuno in the mirror by ear, and asked the registrar to stop the truck — and Iuno stood on a hard shoulder above a steaming valley and watched the light do something for twenty-five minutes, and then went back and wrote **4 km** in the distance column with no annotation of any kind. Amy noticed that she had written a four and said nothing about it and asked whether she was ill; Lupa said she was on holiday, her first, and to give her a minute, because she has never done it before. The log gives the cause of that halt as **the light**, and closes with a substitution: nineteen of the twenty-three entries replaced by a single heading, **people came over**, with the four remaining standing as written, including the one at the spur, which stands as written on purpose.

The road came over the last shoulder at half past five on the twentieth day and the valley opened all at once — forty kilometres in a bowl, the water taking the whole sky, the willows going round in two shades — and the lamp-grid came on while they were standing there, from the north side first, because the switching is done by hand and has been for eighty years. They camped nine kilometres out. At half past eleven the tape ran for the last time and Mei said her four sentences to the dark — and then added a fifth she had never been taught, picked up the way you pick a stone off a beach, because it had been said to her by nineteen people in eight days, every single time she left. **慢走.** *Go slowly.* Out past the eaves, at about the height of a person sitting down, the air did something for about a second. Ciaccona objected that the tone was badly wrong and possibly a different word entirely, and was told that nineteen people standing in a road watching her leave had all meant *take your time*, and she is keeping it: **it is the whole country in two syllables, and they have been telling them to go slowly since the border stone, and they have been obeying at a rate of twenty-six kilometres a day** — twenty-six point four, said Iuno, from the dark.

At midnight Iuno sat with the map for eleven minutes and wrote nothing, **working out how late to be.** She arrived at a shore early once, and it cost a courier her errand, and she has been paying that account for two years, and it takes exactly one coin. Nine kilometres: **first light plus one hour, on purpose, with her entire attention** — and she will need all six of them to help her do it, and one of them is going to argue, which the High Priestess corrected to two.

When the seal came off whole and uncut at the Lower Sluice on the ninth day, the closure notice went back to the border post, and the Superintendent read it twice — once with his eyes and once with his lips — and then took the schedule card out of the drawer with the forty-one tabs in it, and struck out the figure, and wrote a new one, and initialled it, and put it back, and said nothing whatever about it to his office. The card now reads **allow nine.** Every party through that post since has taken nine. The Superintendent regards this as a coincidence; the clerk does not, and has been told, and has entered the disagreement, and the Superintendent has initialled that too.

## XI. KELVET

*Visa* is the neuter plural of the past participle of *videre*: **things which have been seen.** It certifies nothing whatever about the person carrying it, and one fact only, about somebody else — that an official, on a date, at a post, looked.

The queue was forty-one vehicles and moved at six an hour, and Mei got out at minute six to talk to some goats and came back at minute one hundred and four with sesame cakes, the name of a guesthouse in Jinzhou, the intelligence that the Superintendent is regarded as fair and slow, and a nine-year-old's crayon drawing of the truck, which she put in the vehicle log and which was entered as an annexe. She has four sentences of the language, a face that people confess to, and a total absence of embarrassment — Iuno having watched her walk a hooded fugitive through two armed checkpoints by explaining that the woman was growing mushrooms, twice, both times successfully.

At seven in the evening Iuno rehearsed the border on the tailgate in the dark, so that when it happens under a light in front of a man with a stamp it has already happened once: he will ask who you are, why you are here, and how long; you answer those three and stop; **helping is what gets people held.** Ciaccona produced, four months early, a Ragunnan document with her surname printed in a government's typeface — the first her family has had on that country's record in four hundred years — having handed over a musician's transit permit at nine borders, which is the document they issue to people whose surname is a problem. Chisa had a twenty-one-year-old municipal card from an authority that spent twenty of those years sealed inside itself, and a collar certificate signed eleven days before departure; warned that the card may go into a back office and that the correct response is to sit down and let him, she said she knows how to sit in a room while somebody decides about her — and then immediately said that came out flat and she would like to redo it: **she has a certificate this time**, signed by a man who wrote *unremarkable* in a box for nine years to keep an inspector out of a building; she has a mother with an address; and she has six people on the other side of a barrier who will still be standing there in four hours. So he can take the card into a back office, because it is a completely different room from the last one.

Amy had a one-year-old birth record for a nineteen-year-old with a margin note and a President's countersignature, and four drilled words for when he does the maths and looks up: **my registration was late.** That is the entire truth stated in four words in the register's own vocabulary — she has spent a year learning to say the enormous true thing at full length to people who love her, and at a border you say the small true thing and stop, and both are honesty and only one is procedure. Cartethyia had a residency card and a problem — field four, height, one hundred and fifty-four centimetres, which is right *now* — solved by a small physical instruction that occupies the part of her that acts without asking: **hold your own hand**, from the tailgate to the desk and back. Every priestess Iuno trained held her own hands during the long standing; half of them thought it was piety and it was ergonomics.

And Mei had a document reading NOT ESTABLISHED, SEE ANNEXE under place of origin. Iuno asked her for something and told her the whole of why before asking, preferring to be transparent about using her rather than good at it: the Ministry's entry form has a **field eleven, name in local script**, which ninety-nine people in a hundred leave blank, and the man behind that desk reads that script the way Mei reads a menu. Two paid readers, a paleography faculty and a President had produced two readings and no glance. It costs him about four seconds; it costs them five characters in an official Huanglong registry in her handwriting, permanently, in a file anybody with authority can pull. Mei agreed fast, having looked at that door every morning for twelve days: **somebody is going to read them eventually, and she would rather it was a bored man in a booth than somebody who came looking.**

**Superintendent Ruan Zhi** keeps a treaty schedule in his drawer with forty-one tabs cut by hand out of a cigarette carton, and reads every document twice — once with his eyes, and once with his eyes and his lips, silently, at about half speed — and his clerk learned inside a fortnight that the second reading is the one that matters. Seven people took two hours and eleven minutes. Ciaccona took ninety seconds and turned round to find every single one of them looking at her. Chisa took thirty-one and the card did go into a back office, and a second man asked to see the collar, and she unfastened her scarf and turned her head without being asked twice — and Cartethyia caught the fast Huanglong that nobody else did: it is the old pattern. Ruan looked at her for the first time and then asked, at speed, whether the collar **rings**, at six. Told yes, he wrote one line on the certificate in his own hand, initialled it, and said, in the common tongue, that his wife's brother had the same one, that they stopped making them in the ninth year, and that **if it fails there is a man in the third ward at Jinzhou who still has the parts and will do it for nothing, and his name is now on the back of that certificate.** Amy took eleven seconds longer than she should have because he did the maths and looked up, and got her four words, and got a stamp and a turned page — because he has seen four hundred of them, and every one was a bad year in somebody's family, and he has learned not to make people say them out loud at a counter at nine at night. And when a raven landed on the sill and Cartethyia went to declare it, having noticed a livestock truck outside with forty goats on a manifest and wanting to know where the line is, Ruan set his pen down with great care and ruled that it has no owner, no certificate and no container, and **is therefore a wild animal moving under his own power across a line that he cannot see, and the Ministry does not regulate the sky.**

Mei's four seconds happened at twenty past eleven at night. She wrote the five characters the way they come out of her, fast and whole. Ruan turned the form round and read it once, and then read it again silently at half speed with his lips moving, and then did the thing nobody had seen him do in two hours, which was to stop. Asked where she learned to write it, she said she did not know. He looked a third time, outside his own procedure: **she writes it like his grandmother.** The stroke order is out — that fourth one is how they taught it in the provincial schools before the reform, and the reform was thirty-one years ago, and he has not seen a hand do that since he was a boy watching a woman write letters for the neighbours. Then he turned the form back round: **it is also the wrong box. Field eleven is for a name. That is an address.**

The first character is a surname, real, held by real households in the southern prefectures, and he could take them to nine of them. The second and third are the formula you see on lintels — *the house is occupied, somebody's in, don't pass by.* And the last two are **a place**: a village, a hamlet, a bend in a river with eleven families on it, of which there are about forty thousand, and the ones that got a road got put on a map and the ones that did not got remembered by the people who left. What they want is a letter-writer at a gate, that being who reads names for a living — so they are four days from an answer and have been however long they have been. He pushed a new form across and told her to write it in field two, and to **write it the way she writes it, with the stroke order wrong**, because whoever reads it at that gate will be about seventy and the wrong stroke order will tell her more than the characters do.

The vehicle came last. Ruan walked it with a lamp, walked round the mech twice, and read the accreditation letter once with his eyes and once with his lips: it is an honest document, and that is the difficulty, because a dishonest one he could reject in ninety seconds. Then, from the fourth tab: **does a survey need a lance?** Iuno put her hand flat on the desk and said no — a survey does not need a lance, and she would not stand at his counter at eleven at night and tell him it does, because he would know inside four seconds and everything else she said that night would cost double. And then the whole of it: seven people going to a city a woman wrote to them about, in the ordinary post, describing four incidents her patrol logged under *weather* — a word Iuno has read in a cause column before, in her own handwriting, at twenty-two, and which cost a continent thirty years.

Schedule 4, Part C is closed: a note between governments lodged forty days in advance, which they have not got; Ministry escort, which he cannot provide with four staff, one working vehicle and a queue of thirty-one; or leave of the Imperator's office, at nine weeks. There is no other route, and the Superintendent of a land post has no discretion under that Part. And then Iuno asked him to read out his own pencil annotation beside C.3, which she had read upside down at four minutes past nine and had been sitting on for two hours — four words and a question mark and his initials, written in the fourth month, and the only sentence in that building either of them actually cared about. **Then what am I for.**

They drafted it between midnight and four on two spoiled forms and then a clean one. The emitter comes off the platform and is cased and sealed with a numbered seal entered in three places, inspected at four posts on the way; and the platform itself crosses **as equipment, under specification**, because a machine has a specification rather than a file — a distinction the Ministry has held for eleven years and had never before found useful to anybody. Iuno named herself the responsible person and told him to add *formerly High Priestess of Septimont, retired*, because it will be pulled by somebody in nine years and save them a fortnight: it is the last piece of that woman she has, and she has spent every other piece of her deliberately on tables in other people's cities, so spend it, because it is worth more in his register than in her mouth.

At half past two they reached the part with no form. What keeps him awake is that in eleven days there is a bad afternoon in a valley and somebody in that party takes a knife to the seal, and it will be the right thing to do, and he will be in a room in the capital in the ninth month with a transcript in front of him, having to say he did not foresee it — which will be a lie, and he has gone twenty-two years at that desk without telling one and would rather not begin at fifty. So Iuno took out the four stapled pages that had been against her ribs since a loading dock, read the lines a woman in a wheelchair had written at four in the morning in a box the form has no field for, read them twice, put the pages face down, and asked for a clean sheet to write something worth nothing at law and everything at an inquiry.

She wrote down, in advance, in her own hand, **the exact circumstance in which she will break his seal**: if in her own judgment a person in front of them dies otherwise; by her own hand and no other, so that there is one name against it; reported in person at the nearest post inside twenty-four hours with the fragments and the sheet — told to him in advance, on the day, in writing, so that when it is read out in a room in the ninth month he can say that he knew and be telling the truth. Called a confession to a future offence, she named it instead **a statement of a limit, attributed by name to the person whose limit it is, and signed by that person** — the other kind of instrument from the four hundred she wrote in thirty years, every one of which hung an unnamed threshold over somebody's head. Ruan read it once with his eyes and once with his lips, which took a very long time, and then took the pen out of her hand and wrote four lines of his own on the same sheet: his name, his post, the date, and the circumstance in which he would decline to prosecute. They had made a document with no legal existence whatsoever, at three in the morning, in triplicate; and he wanted his copy in the drawer with the schedule.

The seal number was **40114**, which Amy, waking against a radiator for the sealing, approved of on the grounds that it has an eleven in the middle. They crossed at twenty past six under a red-and-white pole on a counterweight raised by hand — nine kilos of pole against fourteen hours, a seal, a bond, a personally liable officer, two spoiled forms and four pots of a young man's tea. Ruan Zhi came out onto the step with a cup in the cold without a coat on, which the clerk noticed and wrote down nowhere, and watched it go under the pole, and then said something to nobody, in his own language, at conversational volume — which **Cartethyia heard through an open window at eleven metres** and kept entirely to herself, then and afterward, on the grounds that it had been said to the air. She wrote it phonetically inside the back cover of the bird folder, and asked a letter-writer at a gate in Jinzhou what it meant eleven days later, and paid her by the character, and cried in the street about it for approximately forty seconds, and then went and bought lemon cakes.

The road came down for two hours through cloud and out of it at nine hundred metres into a morning with the sun on it — and forty kilometres out and three thousand metres down, in a bowl of water with a dam above it, there was a city. The willows on the west bank were three days ahead of the ones on the east. The letter had said so, and they could see it from there.

## XII. THE SHORE

Three editions of that morning circulate on Bookbinders' Row and they disagree, and where they disagree all three are given, and the court has ruled the blanks admissible.

The night before, Amy held the protocol up at the tailgate — nobody sleeps alone in the dark where they cannot reach, her rule, countersigned — and Mei answered that somebody has a hand on her the whole time, that being the whole appointment. Ten going down; come back different; she always does. Chisa's chin had been up for a minute already, because the valley was strung that night and something was holding the long note under it — so Ciaccona played three notes out over the terraces, small, once, the way you set a lamp in a window, on the principle that any three notes will do if there is someone to play them for. The note stayed exactly where it was sung. And the wind moved — and this time, for the first time in the whole quarrelling history of the record, **both of them pointed at the same shore.**

Lupa walked her down and stopped where the beacon path gives out, because from there the night had a hand on Mei and everyone on that hillside knew whose. Mei went down through the grass with a shell against her sternum and a piece of Mengzhou lead in her pocket, talked to the waterline for a while the way she talks to weather, and then lay down where the water could touch her hem and told the lake it was its move. She sleeps anywhere now that the house is full, trusting the building; that far from any roof the trust had to go somewhere, and she gave it to the weather. Far down, the lake turned over slowly and with great courtesy, and the lamps along the crest wall guttered once, all together, and steadied; and on the public stair a keeper felt it through the rail and answered the runner's question before it was asked, logging wind all night, and trimmed a lamp whose oil was fresh, because you trim lamps for guests.

**Yangyang** came off shift at the grey hour. Twice on patrol she had stopped mid-street for a pressure that crossed the road and went on, businesslike, freighted; once the willows had leaned all together toward the lake, held it, and straightened, like a crowd craning at a gate. Her window stood as she had left it with the flute on the sill, and the note came to greet her as it has every evening for a year — and it came out **full**, round and warm with the whole breath under it, the way it sounded on festival night with two players on one instrument. A note with luggage; a note that had come home carrying something and wanted her to know it. She stood in the lane with her hand on the gate and heard it to the end. Then the wind crossed her cheek, took the brim of her hat, and tugged — twice, with intent, in the cadence of a sister's hand in a market crowd — and she decided the way she decides everything, all at once and permanently, and took the beacon stair at a run.

At the waterline one strand of hair had strayed across the sleeper's mouth. The wind moved it — **off the brow, back**, the licensed gesture, the one handhold a wind has — and then performed the second half of it, the half that had waited twenty years and two printings for its turn, the half mothers do when a fever breaks: she stayed. Palm to brow, a pressure like a hand that decided long ago to be gentle, and in it, set down at last at its own address, the sentence she had carried across the dark between stories: **let her wake here.**

Mei surfaced the way she always surfaces, all at once, from the bottom, with one hand already out — the Fool catches things; it is the first fact about her — and the hand rose slowly, and open, and stopped beside her own cheek, palm curved around the pressure there, catching nothing, holding it. She said hello, and that the wind was right on time. And the wind spent a word, at the old rate, the way words come through a door open a finger's width, while every lamp on the crest wall leaned toward the lake to hear it: **Found.** Mei sat up in the shallows with the water running off her sleeves and gave back the thank-you in the wrong tone at the wrong speed, the way you bring wildflowers — picked, crooked, hers — and got a laugh the size of a moth. Then she closed her hand round the shell and said the rest plainly, the way she makes all her deals: **every wind is met once, and it is a good wind.** She had asked the dark for exactly one thing, once, at a gate, to a garrison's back — *let it be a good wind* — and the record shows the request was filed, and carried, and answered, and witnessed by a lake, a lamp, and a keeper's habit.

Asked what to call her, the water at the sand's edge dimpled, and lines drew themselves where the last wave had left the shore smooth — a stroke, a second stroke, the start of a character, careful, like a student's first brushwork — and stopped, where it had stopped on a teacup a year ago and a hemisphere away. And Mei's own hand was already moving. It went down to the sand without consulting her — the hand that has been finishing a stranger's address one character a season on kitchen tables and border forms and the door of a truck — and supplied the rest of the strokes at the speed of a signature, and sat back on her knee. **The character stood whole in the wet sand between them. Her fingering; its breath. Where one gave out, the other had carried, and what stood there was a third thing, wearing both of them.** Half of that wind had gone into the first stroke on a teacup a year ago and stopped, apologising, at the edge of what remained; this morning the other half arrived from outside her and finished her. She had been unmade once at the rim of the world, gladly, with no road back; this was the other operation.

Then Mei took the Mengzhou quad out of her pocket — Iuno had put it there at the top of the path with the whole instruction, that it holds, and that is the entire job — and pressed it flat over the character the way you press a seal, and held it while the third wave came in and took the sand back to smoothness around her wrist. **The lead came up bearing what a blank is for.**

**And the wind sat down.** Everything settled at once, all around the two of them, into a stillness with a centre, and the centre was warm, and out along the bank the grass lay down in one long line, water to sleeper, like something walking in that weighed nothing. Yangyang reached the grass as the sun found the saddle and stopped, because ahead of her the air was standing up off a stranger the way something stands that has been kneeling — unhurried, finished, light — and went up the sky like a spark going home to its fire. **She kept the once one heartbeat past the handoff. Letting go was, this time as every time, the delivery.** It crossed Yangyang's cheek on the way, one touch at the old spring pressure, and then the brim of her hat, once, gently, and the morning was only morning, with a woman sitting in it, soaked to the shoulders, laughing at the sky with her whole chest.

The pull under Yangyang's sternum — the one she stopped explaining to herself years ago, the certainty, ridiculous and load-bearing, that somewhere a stranger was owed a seat at her table — swung once, all the way round, and settled, the way a needle settles when you finally stop walking. She went into the shallows, boots and all, and put her hand out, warning her that the stones roll. Mei takes hands. She took this one and came up out of the lake of Jinzhou into the first hour of the day, streaming, upright, **met** — and they held the grip a moment longer than the job required, which is a thing that happens on that shore, the record shows, in every printing.

Over fried dough at the stair foot Mei put the shell into Yangyang's hands, sideways, mid-sentence, and Yangyang put it to her ear because that is what you do, and some knowledge lives in the arm. A tide, long and patient. Under the tide, wind. And at the very bottom, faint as a lamp four rooms away, **a second tide, older, in a different mother's rhythm** — a sea from a printing where the same house had stood on the same lane, and a woman with unhurried exact hands had wrapped this shell the way you dress a wound. Yangyang lowered it, and her face performed the family craft, and the craft failed, and she wiped her eyes with her sleeve in the practical way, once each side: **her mother's work, both of them.** Keep it a while, Mei said; it knows the address.

Then Mei stood, faced the hillside, filled her lungs, and was loud: **fourteen.** Up at the bend, Amy lowered the field glasses and relayed it, and Lupa started the engine. Iuno came down the path at the head of the family one hour late, on purpose, with her whole attention, carrying the pot — having written the river level down for Mornye before she wrote anything else, because a promise outranks a sunrise, and then allowed herself the sunrise. The word *Jinzhou*, exchanged with the High Priestess, closed a question two years old, and both of them let it. The truck came down the dam road at a ceremonial pace with a raven riding the air over the cab at parade height on a current that kept its pace the whole descent; the tarp had worked a corner loose and a white ceramic wing showed under it, and by noon the lakefront had decided, city-wide and without a meeting, that the foreigners had brought a votive, and approved. A fisherman read the five characters twice, the second time with his lips, and told the truck it was **a good lintel.**

Then the kite. Amy checked the spars the way crew checks crew and let it up into the offshore air, and the string paid out, and at the height where the draft dies against the dam face **the kite kept climbing** — red, against the grain of the wind, up the whole blue page of the sky over Jinzhou. In Mengzhou everyone knows what that means; Jinzhou learned it that morning, shopfront by shopfront; a child on the shrine street said *look* to the sky, and the sky, for once, was already looking back; and at the yamen a window that is usually shut stood open. And the towers of the youngest gate — on an order signed that morning at the Magistrate's own desk, under the doctrine that the first census after any dark is of who came back — beat once each as the kite passed along the wall. Tower by tower. **Eight beats**, at full morning, for a column of one, entered in the garrison log as **returning; counted.** The ninth beat was left where it has always been left; and past the north face, where something has been counting down the frost months in tremors at eight days and then seven, the mountains that day kept a silence of their own, a full day's worth, which the war college has been arguing about since.

**Baizhi** had come down from the clinic and stood at the rail with the incident ledger under her arm, holding a year of it: a claw turned aside indoors; a bridge that held because the wind leaned on the knot; an Outrider shoved clear of a falling beam by air that arrived with intent; a cliff face that came down on Third Squad and opened a corridor in itself and held it, and held it, until the last man cleared it. Cause: *weather*, underlined in her own hand one hundred and four times. She opened the book, took out the second ink, **struck the word through, wrote one character above it, and closed the book on it**, and went down the steps to be introduced to seven foreigners and a wind. Two women, hemispheres apart, have now made that same correction in that same column, and the clerk sets it down without comment, twice, which is the clerk's way of commenting.

That evening the wind took the long way along the crest wall, because for the first time since the crossing everything she had was under one sky. An Outrider's window first: the flute on the sill, their low note, and the last of it split half and half without discussion. Yangyang read the reply at that window by lamplight, a much-folded page that had crossed a border in a coat pocket; the court has seen it and declines to copy it, because some pages belong to their holders; and the record admits only that the lamp leaned and forgot to wander for the whole reading, and that the wind read it over her shoulder, every stroke, twice. In the second ink her patrol log gained a marginal line: **log clear; wind home; guest one; seat filled.** And on a guesthouse roof, at the hour the lamp-grid goes gold from the north side first, Mei came up a ladder with two cups — coffee, cardamom, criminal strength, and a tea for the rail — and told the dark it was local leaf tonight; and the air at the eaves came down and settled over the cup the way a guest settles who has finally been given the good chair, and the steam leaned all through the telling, one way, steadily, like a flame with company. Mei turned her cup a quarter-turn on the tile. **Same time tomorrow**, she said. The steam off the second cup bent, once, the size of a nod, and stayed.

The Almanac Bureau closed out its forty-year blank — **客風, the Guest Wind** — with one stroke, and entered beneath it, new: **接風, the Welcoming Wind.** *Fifth month, at dawn, off the lake. Arrives with guests; met with a meal, as the word requires. Stays.* Marginalia in two hands: filed under weather, struck; filed under family, standing. It is the first wind on that list to have filed itself and the first whose entry required the kitchen's concurrence. At the Magistrate's office the runners' log reads *wind, all watches*, countersigned in her own hand — and on the following night **the lake dreamed forward**, the first forward dream of the tenure, the contents requested by the Ministry of Rites and refused. That office has since doubled the kitchen budget, ordered lamp-oil in war quantities, and instructed the towers that any column counted returning is to be counted *whole, however constituted*, with a note in her own pencil beside clause C: **rain before the fire, this time.** At the Plum Rain Troupe a lamp at the back left relit of itself before the overture, and the master, found afterward in the property room with his hat in his hand, answered from the doorway that **act three may begin** — and, asked when the doors close, answered from the stair that they do not, and that this is act three.

And on Bookbinders' Row the lead returned from the shore is entered as **quad, faced; matrix, probationary; do not distribute.** The founders cast nothing from it and take turns looking at it. On the strength of that morning one of the long-missing sorts was recut, and **held in the mould**, which no cutting of it has done in twenty years. The word was **hope**. They have let the sort for *fail* lie, on the grounds that nobody has asked for it. That same week a customer with an unfamiliar face bought all three editions in very old coin, read the Burned Edition twice at the counter, and left smiling — the founders agree on the smile and disagree about its temperature — leaving behind, in payment or in promise, **one sort, face down on the counter-cloth.** The house has not yet dared to turn it over.

## XIII. THE THIRTIETH

On a loose page at the back of a Jinzhou patrol log, between the ruled lines, in a second ink, over four seasons and in at least three moods, there is a list headed **客** — *guest.* Meet her at the gate; at the shore; *wherever she is.* Somewhere to put her bag, with the shelf clear and the quilt aired every season. The dough at the stair foot: the first stall opens at five, the second stall is better. The bathhouse, because she will have come a long way. **The lamps from the wall, at the hour — do not explain it; let her ask.** Panhua's, the third seat. *Tell Chixia*, added in the eleventh month and underlined twice. The willows, both banks. Ice, if it is summer. ~~Ask where she is from.~~ Ask what she takes in the morning. The hoist. A thirteenth item struck through hard enough to open the paper. And a fourteenth, blank. Beneath it, **nine prepared sentences, in case**, gone over the way you go over a route: two of them good, and one so good she had struck it out twice.

At the shore all nine arrived at the door at once and jammed there like a crowd at a gate, and what came out was an observation that Mei was soaked. Then she did a headcount out of pure trade reflex: **one.** She has done headcounts for six years, on entering and halting and leaving, at every gate and lane end and cellar mouth and bridge, and has never once done a headcount of one and had it come out feeling like a full column.

At the stair foot the fishermen made room the way that stair has always made room, which is by nobody moving and there suddenly being room. Asked which of the two dough stalls is better, Yangyang heard herself deploy sentence number five, in the wrong order, on the wrong day, at a stall she had never intended to be standing in front of — which produced, from Mei, the observation that they therefore have somewhere to be tomorrow, and, from Yangyang, an affirmative delivered in the report voice, in the manner of a woman reading out a mooring depth. And when Mei asked how she knew the border post is two hundred and eleven kilometres away, the answer arrived in stages: it is on the schedule card at the post; the post road runs a courier twice a week; the courier stops at the second landing; and she is generally at the second landing on those days, because of the patrol pattern. Mei called that very thorough, gravely, and let it lie, and Yangyang sat on a public stair with her ears going hot and discovered that she was having an extremely good time.

Item seven went unperformed, because the towers had beaten eight and a runner had come off the yamen stair and a keeper had logged wind on all watches, and somewhere in the middle of it a fisherman's boy told his aunt, who told the noodle window, which told the muster yard, which is faster than any official channel and has been for eighty years. So **Chixia** arrived mid-sentence off the night muster with a paper bag going translucent with oil, demanding that buying strangers breakfast at her age be minuted — and stopped, and took in the stair, and observed that there were seven of them, and was told that Yangyang was aware and had not said anything at all yet, it not being eight in the morning. The bag changed hands four times in the next minute and came back empty, and Chixia announced to the stair at large that she had extra, and that she is a generous person.

**Baizhi** got to her last, on purpose, with **sixteen questions** to be spaced across the day, several made to sound like small talk — which Yangyang will notice, and will let her, because she has owed Baizhi a set of answers since the equinox and they both know the sum. Also: Sergeant Mo struck her off the roster for two days at six twenty, unasked, writing *leave, compassionate* and then crossing out *compassionate* and leaving the box half empty, which is the most emotional document that man has produced in nineteen years, and the clerk has photographed it. That was question one; she was not obliged to answer it and had anyway; and she was told to go and sit down.

Twenty days of laundry went onto eleven metres of line in a bathhouse courtyard, in the plum rains, in the wettest fortnight of the Jinzhou year, and the landlady said what everybody says: four days, and it will come off the line smelling of the line. Then a wind came over the courtyard wall, low and businesslike, and went down the whole length of the line and up the other side, and eleven metres of foreign laundry stood straight out sideways like a signal flag and stayed out, and the water came off it in a flat sheet that hit the far wall and ran, and it went back down the line the other way and did it again, and again, with an enthusiasm that could not be described as anything other than showing off, **for thirty-eight minutes.** Amy put a sock against her cheek and reported it crunchy, which is dry, and she has had wet socks for three weeks. Mrs Ren looked at eleven metres of bone-dry laundry, and then at the sky, which was raining, and went back up to her guesthouse and thought about her rate for two hours and revised it at her own door at dusk, and had it revised back at her by a woman with one arm and a copy of the ward schedule. Baizhi wrote in the ledger under the eave with the book on her forearm and asked question four: how long can it keep that up? No idea. **Does it know?** Yangyang opened her mouth and shut it, and said that was a better question than hers has ever been — and was told yes, and that Baizhi has had a year and has been saving it.

In a bathhouse cooling room Amy conducted an interview with a rolled towel standing upright on the bench for a microphone. How many letters? **Forty-one** — a pause of about a second and a half, and two people who had been lying down stopped lying down. To a person she had never met, over a little more than a year; the first eleven were not addressed to anybody; after that they had a heading, and the heading was **to you, whom I haven't met.** Written at her window, at night, after shift, with the shutter open, because **it reads over her shoulder** — extremely rudely, and without pretending otherwise, and once it turned a page over to see the back. And what she told it about was the day, and specifically **the absurd ones**: a cloud shaped exactly like Sergeant Mo, a melon vendor with an opera voice, a goose. In the still weeks she had begun putting things aside during the day under a heading, and the heading was *to tell*, and there was nowhere to tell them, and she told the room out loud twice and heard how it sounded, and after that wrote them down instead, because it was quieter and it kept better. Nobody in the cooling room said anything. Then Amy put the towel down, declared it the best interview she had ever done and one she is never doing again, and issued a callsign that was not up for discussion: **Night Window.** Yangyang said nothing about it then or afterward, and used it on a handheld eleven days later at two in the morning without one instant of hesitation, which is how everybody found out.

Item five was performed exactly as written. At ten past seven Yangyang put them along the parapet at the place she had picked out for it in the eleventh month, and said nothing, and waited — and the pack ruined it immediately, until Chisa said *quiet* in the level voice, once, and six people shut up in the middle of six separate sentences, which Yangyang had never seen anybody do to anybody. The first lamp came on, and then the second eleven metres down, and the row went off in sequence, and the gold came down the rock face flight by flight and ran along the crest wall under their own boots and out onto the harbour road, and the water took all of it and gave it back doubled, and the far shore did the same thing four minutes late because the far shore has its own man. Nobody spoke until it finished. Four people, one to a district, every night for eighty years. They costed a switch in the year Yangyang was born and it came out cheaper, and the ward voted it down.

And Yangyang, who had written **do not explain it** in the second ink and underlined it, and had held that instruction for eleven months through four drafts of an evening she never expected to get, opened her mouth to explain it — and Mei said it first: **because you'd stop looking.** Yangyang shut her mouth, and confirmed that the minute says the switching is to remain manual so that the hour is kept by somebody. Four people whose whole job is to make sure the evening happens on purpose. Ciaccona reached for the tally book, declaring it hers now; Iuno numbered it twenty-six, being the count of things Mei has said in two years that Ciaccona has written down and pretended she thought of.

**Panhua's** was full, because it is Tuesday, and because Chixia had told her *about sixteen* at six twenty in the morning at a hatch at a shout while she was doing the first proving, and had received a sound, which is not a booking. The hatch opened. Panhua looked out at ten people standing in her lane in the rain; and then at Yangyang; and then at the woman standing next to Yangyang, who had a lake-water tidemark still faintly visible on the shoulder of a borrowed shirt; and then at the back corner table by the window, **where a third seat has been wiped down every week for a year.** She said one word into the room behind her, and the room did not visibly change. Then she said, out of the hatch, in the tone of a woman announcing weather: **step; relays; four at a time; she will send it out.**

Baizhi asked her question at the top of the ninth relay with the ledger shut on her knee, having spent the day asking about long journeys and about what is difficult to digest on the road and, at the bathhouse, in the manner of a woman making conversation, whether anybody had a condition a physician would want to know about before an emergency — and having received, in ninety seconds, without any of them appearing to notice they were being examined: one collar, one prosthesis of unknown material, one four-hundred-year working life, one transformation with a mass differential she immediately began doing arithmetic about, and one flat, sunny, entirely unbothered mention of a recovery time measured in seconds. She had written none of it down where they could see. Fourteen asked; two left.

Question fifteen was about the wind. **Does it eat?**

The step went quiet in the specific way a step goes quiet when a question has landed in the middle of it. Mei turned round and looked at the corner table through the window, at the back seat, under the shelf with the little stitched cloth bird on it, where a bowl had been sitting since the second relay with a piece of the crispy thing in it that nobody had put there and everybody had walked past. **The steam off it was leaning.** Yes, she said. It eats.

Baizhi opened the ledger, wrote two characters, and shut it: that is the answer to a year, and everything else follows from it, and none of the rest is her department. **A thing that eats has a body's economy, and a thing with a body's economy can be overdrawn** — and she has spent a year watching something spend itself on that city at a rate nobody was measuring, and until nine seconds earlier had no way to say that in a sentence a Bureau would accept. Then question sixteen, over the top of her glasses: does Yangyang know what she has done? She met somebody at a shore. **She has put a load-bearing element of the weather of that province on a household register in another country and given it a seat, and there is going to be paperwork about this for four hundred years.** Baizhi stood, brushed off her coat, and went to get another bowl, declaring herself extremely pleased and noting that this would have to be taken on trust, her face not doing it.

Yangyang asked her own question at half past nine, of Chisa, because Chisa had turned out to be the person you ask. She had been trying since six in the morning to establish who is in charge, running the standard indicators, and had come away at four separate points with four different names. It depends what is broken — which is what the wolf said, and what everyone will say, because you get seven answers and every one of them is true on a different day. **Mei decides. Iuno signs. Amy finds out first and leaks it. Lupa says the thing nobody wants said. Cartethyia goes through the wall. Ciaccona keeps the count. And Chisa drives.** That is not a chain of command. It works: nineteen days out of the border post, four seas, one god, one dark, seven out and seven back — and the honest answer is **whoever's nearest.** Yangyang called it a terrible way to run a column, and Chisa agreed, and the two of them sat on a wet step in complete professional agreement and enormous good humour and finished their bowls.

The guesthouse rooms were allocated by a process Amy had been building all evening. She offered to take the landing and was refused under her own rule, read back at her by Lupa, who has had it read at her four times this year. Chisa took the market room over objection, because **it is a map**: wake up, know where you are, no work involved, and it is the best room in the house and none of them want it. Lupa took the stairhead, refused a bed, and closed the matter with twelve years. Amy took the downstairs back room with a wall between her and the lane and the rig on the sill, because there is a relay on the dam face and she wants to know if it is line-of-sight. Which left the front downstairs room, with one bed, and two of them — an outcome Iuno correctly identified as having been built all evening by a girl who had allocated herself the room with the radio mast in it and arranged the order of speaking. A vote was moved, seconded twice, and carried before anybody had voted; **the registrar abstained, minuting that she abstained to keep her hands clean of it, that the vote was rigged, that she saw it being rigged, that she said nothing at the time, and that she was going to bed.** Six to nil, one abstention.

Going up the passage — calling them both appalling, and saying she is fond of them, and that those facts have coexisted for two years — Iuno got four paces before Mei caught her sleeve and said something nobody else on that landing heard. Whatever it was, Iuno stopped, and **put her hand flat against the front of her coat for about a second, over the pocket where she has been carrying things since the frostlands**, and then took it away again. Amy, halfway through a door, saw the gesture and filed it and said nothing, then or since: she is nineteen and has been listening to rooms for a decade, and she knows exactly what a hand does when it checks that a thing is still there, and she has decided to let her aunt have it.

Yangyang went home at half past ten and stood in her own room. **The shelf by the door was clear** — cleared a year ago in about ninety seconds, without deciding to, on an evening when she came in and looked at the room and found it wrong. Under the bed, in a paper wrapper, tied: **one guest quilt**, cotton, good, bought new, aired at the start of every season and folded back the same evening, four times, never once used. She had been awake thirty-five hours. She lay down on top of the covers fully dressed and did not sleep at all.

At ten past two she got up, took the quilt, and left the house. What she had worked out, lying in the dark for three and a half hours, was that it is the plum rains, and the guesthouse backs onto a north-facing courtyard, and Mrs Ren's spare bedding lives in a chest nine steps from a wall that has been wet since the twenty-fourth. It was a good chain of reasoning, correct in every particular, and — as she was entirely aware — insane, and she carried it across four terraces of a sleeping city in the rain anyway, at a fast walk, with her patrol coat buttoned to the collar. The lamp over the door was on. She worked out what she was going to say and had four options ready by the time she reached the step. The door was on the latch. She went in.

**There were five of them on the stairs.** Lupa across the stairhead, on her side, in her clothes, one arm out along the top step. Cartethyia three steps down with her head on a folded coat and her feet against the wall. Ciaccona below her, asleep sitting up, chin on her chest, lute across her knees. Chisa on the landing with her back to the newel post and her blade laid flat along the boards beside her. And on the bottom four steps, a girl of nineteen lying full length down the flight with her boots on the banister and her head in her mother's lap, out cold — and her mother sitting on the step with her back against the wall and one hand in the girl's hair, awake. There was a bowl of something on the third step, half finished, going cold. **Every one of the four bedroom doors stood open, and every one of the four rooms was empty.**

Invited to sit down, she sat two steps up with the quilt across her knees, and did not ask, and was told anyway. **Twenty days in a box; twelve before that at minus twenty-six; everybody breathing the same air, everybody in arm's reach, all night, every night.** Iuno put them in four rooms with four doors and everyone said thank you and went in and shut them, and by half eleven there were three of them out there, and by one there were five, and nobody has said one word about it and nobody is going to. The registrar is asleep, in an actual bed, in an actual room — **having two years of not sleeping to make up, and finally doing it, and every single person on that staircase came out at least partly so she would have a quiet house, and if any one of them were awake they would deny it.** Asked why she herself was awake, Mei looked down at her daughter, who is nineteen, and has been nineteen for a decade, and came back, and was drooling faintly on her coat, and said: **no reason at all.**

Yangyang got up quietly, shook the quilt out of its paper, and put it over the bard, who was nearest and asleep sitting up in her shirtsleeves. It went over her to the shoulder; Ciaccona surfaced about four centimetres, registered a warm weight, said something in Ragunnan that was probably a name, and went back down. Asked whether the quilt had been for her, Yangyang said it was for a guest — and then, hearing how that sounded, let it stand, which she has been learning to do all year and had not previously managed on the same day as anything else. Mei laughed silently, with her whole chest and no sound at all, which is a thing you learn to do around a sleeping person. Then she looked at her properly: thirty-nine hours, coat buttoned to the collar, boots wet, sitting bolt upright on a staircase at twenty past two in a boarding house belonging to a woman she had argued a tax down for that afternoon.

**You must be tired**, Mei said.

Sentence number one. Prepared over a year, rehearsed at a window, struck once and re-entered — said to her, by the guest, on the guest's first night, on a foreign staircase, in the wrong language. Yangyang agreed that she was.

**There's a seat**, said Mei — sentence number four — and shifted about eleven centimetres along the step, and put her free hand flat on the boards beside her.

Down at the landing window the shutter stood a finger's width open, and the lamp on the newel post leaned all through it and forgot to wander. At about three the rain slackened, and by four it had gone off entirely, and the air came in over the sill and went round the house once, unhurried — the stairhead, the three steps down, the bard and her borrowed quilt, the woman with the blade at the newel post, the girl and her mother at the bottom of the flight, the shut door of a room where a woman with one arm was asleep for the first time in a fortnight — and settled at the top of the stairs, at about the height of a person sitting down, and stayed there until it got light. Mrs Ren came down at half past five with a bad hip, found her staircase occupied along its whole length, stopped on the third step from the bottom, and looked at it for a while. Then she went round through the courtyard, which cost her ninety seconds and a great deal of pain, and put a pot of something on the kitchen table, and opened the kitchen shutter, and said nothing whatsoever about any of it, then or afterward, in the fluent way.

## XIV. WHAT STANDS AT THE END OF IT

On the thirty-first the list was closed out on the same page in a third ink. Meet her wherever she is: **done; item was unnecessary; she was already there.** Somewhere to put her bag: nine days out of date, there being seven of them and a truck and a machine four metres tall; quilt deployed to the wrong person; correct outcome. The dough: first stall, wrong stall, and they are going to the second one tomorrow. The lamps: she did not explain it. Panhua's: full; ate on the step; **the seat was used.** Tell Chixia: the city told Chixia, who has forgiven her on condition it is minuted, and this is the minute. The willows: argued about at length in front of her, and she was on the ground's side. Ice: forgotten entirely; the ice is still there; the ice keeps. Where she is from: struck last spring and staying struck, because **she is from here now, which is a different question and one Yangyang already knows the answer to.** Of the nine prepared sentences, two were used **on her**, and one was pre-empted because Mei handed over the shell before she could ask; it is in the orders pocket and has not come out of the orders pocket. And number seven — *I have wanted to meet you for a long time* — struck, re-entered, struck again, has been **re-entered on the thirty-first**, with a note: she is going to say this one, and not this week. A second list has already begun beneath it, ending in a single word: *sleep.* Then the page runs out, and the log resumes on the ruled lines in the first ink, sixth month, first: **log rain; wind home; guest seven; seat filled.**

Set that against the house under the glacier where this began. A woman with no first forty years had been finding jobs for four days to avoid three goodbyes and could produce exactly one stroke of a language she speaks fluently. Her hand now writes **five characters** at the speed of a signature, read since by a Superintendent from the capital, an old woman in a shed on ground belonging to nobody, a man at a bridge, a village in mourning, and a fisherman on a public stair — and they are an address: a surname held by real households in the southern prefectures; the two characters cut on lintels meaning *the house is occupied, somebody's in, don't pass by*; and a place with about eleven families on it, on a bend in a river, that never got a road and so never got onto a map, and is remembered by the people who left. She has driven that lintel across a country that reads lintels, and nineteen halts out of twenty-three were caused by somebody walking over to it — and one man read *don't pass by* off the door and made them stand in a lane for ninety-four minutes, and Iuno has entered him in the same column as the other nineteen with no annotation, because a door that only ever produces welcomes is a lie about doors.

Iuno began with a chapel behind a wall she had never entered, fourteen deaths in glass and a fifteenth light standing clear, a harp gagged with felt for four hundred years, and one word in a thirty-year-old cause column that cost a continent. She has pulled the gag; left both doors open so that a country with no weather has a wind and a west; put a name-owned limits clause into a national instrument and a personally liable bond onto her own name at a border post; written down in advance the exact circumstance in which she will commit a future offence and had a Superintendent write beside it the circumstance in which he would decline to prosecute; corrected an eleven-year chart to eight and a half per cent off forty-one years of a painted board nobody had ever asked for; replaced nineteen entries in a cause column with the heading **people came over**; and, on a hard shoulder above a steaming valley, written **4 km** in a distance column and said nothing about it at all.

Amy began with a mother back three weeks and a poster. She has now seen her work; holds a countersigned right to stop her; has broadcast across four hundred kilometres of nothing and been received every time by a woman thirty metres away who listens whether or not she calls; has been bitten by an insect and kept the receipt; and has left one sentence on a cassette in a schoolyard that a man will play every year until the machine dies. Chisa began with a mother in the building and sixty-one mornings of a chime nobody had heard; her mother is now in that house with a flask and a packed bag, an hour of her voice rides in Chisa's inside pocket on the left, four officers at four posts have heard the collar and one wrote a repairer's name on the back of the certificate — and a man in a lane heard it at six and looked up and looked away. Cartethyia has reproduced four tones on one hearing, taught a four-hundred-year-old musician forty-one characters, been a customer for the first time in her life, held her own hand through a border, and been handed eleven years of a President's research for reporting a result — and she carries two hinge pins in a coat that hangs differently for them. Ciaccona has handed over a card with her surname printed in a government's typeface and had it handed back without a flicker, and finished the eleventh stanza in a tobacco shed and called it the weather and been accepted in about a second and a half. Lupa has cut bread badly for two thousand kilometres, carried the entire inventory of a dead world across her body, taken a door off its hinges in ninety seconds, and told the whole of the mule so that nobody has to ask her again.

Behind them, Lynae and Rebecca and Chiyo hold four mornings and three mornings and a rule in a borrowed alphabet, in a kitchen where a black marker has gone over the letters so they will outlast the ink; and a girl called Sigrika files a remark every term into a drawer that is not the system's, carrying a near stone with the go-on on it and a bracket she has left standing.

The invoices are open. Rebecca's bag comes back to that dock, in Lupa's hands, or it has not been delivered. Lupa's envelope goes into the post at eleven months, to a name she does not have. Sigrika gets the whole thing, start to finish, including that Lynae knew, on the day the party comes back through that gate — a promise about a day nobody can guarantee, signed on a rail in the dark, which is the point.

And what is open in Jinzhou is larger than any of it. The city has a wind in its Bureau's book under **family** — a wind that filed itself, that eats, and that can therefore be overdrawn, and that has spent its last word and has one seat, and whose weather-column entry has been struck by two women in two hemispheres. The Magistrate has begun dreaming forward, has doubled a kitchen budget and ordered lamp-oil in war quantities, and has written *rain before the fire, this time.* A war college is arguing about a day's silence from mountains that had been counting down in tremors at eight days, then seven. The ninth tower's beat is still where it has always been left. A blank quad now carries a face at striking depth and stands entered as a probationary matrix nobody has cast from; a sort for **hope** held in the mould for the first time in twenty years; a sort for *fail* lies where it lies because nobody has asked for it; and the customer who has spent a season buying a street's type so it cannot be set has begun selling it back, one sort at a time, face down — and has left one on a counter-cloth that the house has not yet dared to turn over.

Iuno's coat still holds a ring cut from her own arm, four stapled pages, a photograph, and a folded slip with a surname on it, and she has been picking locations and losing them across a continent. There is a woman at a stall with a cloth awning at the east gate, mornings, impossible after eleven, who charges by the character and will count yours, and who is four days from an answer that has been thirty years coming. Inside the back cover of a bird folder there is a sentence a Superintendent said to the air at a border, written down phonetically by the only person who heard it, waiting to be paid for by the character. And on a ward's shelves, in a physician's second ink, and under a lake beneath the Dimmr Plains, three separate slow beats go on being counted by nobody at all.

**Ovathrax is still coming.** That is what the letter said, and the letter was right about the willows.
