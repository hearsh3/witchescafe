# Housewarming
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

---

## I.

Rebecca gave up on sleep at ten past four and came down to find the kitchen lit and Iuno at the table with the binder and a pot.

"You're up."

"I'm always up." Iuno turned a page. "There's tea."

"I hate tea."

"There's tea."

Rebecca got a cup and filled it and sat down across the table and hated it in silence for a while.

"It's the quiet," she said eventually.

"Mm."

"I keep waking up because there's nothing. Back home if it goes quiet it means somebody's cut the power on your floor and you've got about ninety seconds." She turned the cup. "So I wake up and I do a sweep and there's a *tree*."

"There are quite a lot of trees."

"There's *so many trees*."

Iuno wrote something in the margin.

Rebecca watched her do it for a minute.

"You were gonna feed me to it," she said.

The pen kept moving for one more word and stopped.

"Yes."

"On the glacier. You wanted me and Lucy back through the hole with Smasher on the other side."

"I did."

"And nobody's said anything about it for like three weeks."

"No," said Iuno, and set the pen down, and looked at her properly. "Would you like me to apologize?"

Rebecca thought about it with real care.

"Nah," she said. "Then I'd have to figure out what to do with it."

"Good."

"So say the other thing."

Iuno turned her cup a quarter-turn.

"I would do it again," she said. "Under those conditions, with that information, with Mei nine feet from a tear that was eating the landscape — I'd spend you. I've kept a list my whole adult life of who I'd spend and in what order, and you'd been in my world for four hours, and you went on it at the bottom, and I slept."

"Yeah, obviously."

Iuno's eyebrow went up.

"That's how it *works*," said Rebecca. "That's not even — Maine did that. Maine would've done that. Everybody does that, the only difference is most people lie about it after and dress it up like it was for your own good." She drank the tea she hated. "You're the first person who ever just said the number out loud."

"You're off the list."

"Since when?"

"Since you moved thirty feet across an imaginary bridge for an imaginary lamplighter with four hit points left." Iuno picked up her pen again. "It was a *game*, and there were no stakes whatsoever, and you wrote the number on your hand beforehand so you'd have it ready."

Rebecca went a color.

"That's a *stupid* reason to take somebody off a list."

"It's the only reason worth anything. Everybody performs correctly when it counts." Iuno turned a page. "I've spent thirty years watching what people do when it doesn't."

"...Can I have coffee instead?"

"There's a jar in the second cupboard and Mei has done something to it."

---

## II.

The house evacuated at seven twenty.

It went like this: Mei lit the stove, the stove drew for about ninety seconds, and then the main room filled with smoke at a rate that suggested purpose. Nine people came out of the blue door into knee-deep snow in various states of sleepwear, coughing, in a rough order that told you a great deal about all of them — Lupa first with two people under her arms, Chisa second with the medical kit she had packed and everyone had mocked, Lynae third with a sidearm, and Ciaccona last, holding the lute.

"Was that necessary?"

"It's *irreplaceable*—"

"So's Cartethyia—"

"Cartethyia can *run*."

The smoke came out the door for four minutes and stopped.

Chisa went in with a lamp and came back out.

"Nest," she said. "In the flue. Big one."

"So we burn it out," said Rebecca.

"You'll crack the liner."

"So we — okay, what, we get a *stick*—"

"There are *birds in it*," said Cartethyia.

Everybody looked at her.

She had come out in a blanket and one boot and had her hair in a state that suggested she had fought it. She looked at nine people and folded her arms.

"There's a nest," she said, "and it's the wrong season for a nest, which means somebody's got it wrong and moved in early, and if we take it out in pieces they've got nothing." Her chin came up. "So we take it out whole."

"Cartethyia, it's a *chimney*—"

"We take it out *whole*."

---

It took two hours and every single person in the building.

Lupa and Rebecca went up on the roof, which took eleven minutes of argument about who was heavier and ended with both of them up there anyway. Lynae held the ladder and provided a running assessment of their technique that neither of them requested and both of them followed. Chisa stood at the base of the chimney inside and directed by sound, because she could hear the nest shift when they got near it and nobody else could.

Iuno directed from the ground with the total authority of a woman who had never in her life been on a roof.

"Left."

"Which left."

"*Your* left—"

"Whose left is *my* left when I'm facing—"

"North," said Chisa, from inside the house, at a volume that carried up the flue and out the top, which startled Rebecca so badly she nearly went off the ridge.

"WHY IS THE CHIMNEY TALKING—"

Amy sat on a cargo crate in her coat with a mug and heckled for the full two hours and was, by the general assessment afterward, the most valuable member of the operation.

They got it out whole at ten past nine.

It came up the flue in Rebecca's two hands, a soot-black cup of twigs and wire and one long strand of something pink and synthetic that had come off her own head three days ago and gone out a dorm window in Lahai-Roi and got here somehow, and there were four eggs in it.

Rebecca held it at arm's length like an unexploded device.

"Take it," she said. "Somebody take it."

"You're doing fine—"

"*Take it,* I've got — my hands aren't — " She was rigid. "These are *military-grade*, I have shot through a wall with these hands, take the *bird house*—"

"Bring it down the ladder," said Cartethyia, from the ground, calm as milk. "Both hands under it. Like a bowl. Don't tip it."

Rebecca came down a nine-rung ladder in about four minutes, holding a nest, with her tongue between her teeth.

They put it in the crook of the old wisteria on the south wall, wedged with a shim Lupa cut, and Cartethyia stood under it for a while looking up.

"They'll come back?"

"They will."

"You don't know that."

"I do," said Chisa. "The pink thing's in the weave and that means they got it after we arrived, which means they were working on it yesterday."

Cartethyia's whole face came apart into a grin.

Rebecca sat down in the snow, still holding both hands slightly out from her body, and said, "I need a minute."

---

## III.

The war started at noon and Rebecca started it.

She hit Lupa in the back of the head from eleven meters while Lupa was carrying firewood, and then stood there with her arms out and her chin up in an unmistakable invitation, and the resulting escalation consumed the entire afternoon and produced four casualties, one structural complaint, and a lasting change in the household's understanding of at least three people.

Teams formed badly and immediately.

"Me, Lynae, Amy."

"You get *Lynae*—"

"I called it—"

"Lynae's a *professional*—"

"So's Lupa!"

"Lupa's a *brawler*," said Lynae, from behind a snowbank she had already improved. "It's a different discipline."

Iuno declined to participate and took her tea to the porch.

---

Things learned that afternoon, in the order they were learned:

**Lynae was terrifying.** She went up the ridge inside four minutes, established what she referred to out loud as an enfilade, and pinned three people behind the woodpile for eleven minutes with a supply of pre-made ammunition and no apparent emotion. Ciaccona, from behind the woodpile, began negotiating terms.

**Cartethyia was a factory.** She stopped throwing anything within about ninety seconds and instead sat down behind the water butt and produced, over the course of an hour, upward of two hundred perfectly spherical snowballs at a rate that never varied, humming, entirely content, supplying both sides without discrimination and refusing on principle to take one.

"You're arming the *enemy*—"

"I'm arming *everyone*," said Cartethyia. "That's a different job."

**Chisa could not be flanked.** Fresh snow made every footstep loud and distinct, and Chisa stood in the open with her eyes closed and turned to face people before they came around the corner, and by the second hour both teams had independently agreed to leave her alone.

**Mei was a problem.** She got hit constantly. She got hit more than everybody else combined, because she had no tactical sense whatsoever and enormous enthusiasm, and she went down laughing every single time and was back on her feet in about nine seconds because that is how she is built.

"She's *out*—"

"She's *never* out—"

"That's the *fourth time*—"

"IT'S NOT MY FAULT," Mei yelled, from somewhere in a drift.

**Amy built a trebuchet.** Or the beginning of one. It took her forty minutes, it used a cargo strap and a sled and a sapling, it launched exactly once, and the payload went almost straight up and came down on her own team.

The cheer from the ridge was audible in the next valley.

**And Iuno was eliminated at two forty-one** by a shot from her own household that came in over the porch rail at a range Amy would later describe as "absolutely fair" and everyone else would describe as an assassination.

The teacup survived. The tea did not.

Iuno stood up.

She set the cup down on the rail with tremendous care. She removed her coat and folded it over the back of the chair. She walked down the three steps into the snow, and picked up one snowball from Cartethyia's stock, and weighed it, and Cartethyia said, quietly, "oh no," with total delight.

Iuno hit Amy in the sternum from twenty-two meters, one-handed, without a windup, and did not stop walking.

"She's got a *chakram* arm—"

"THAT'S ILLEGAL—"

"There are no rules," said Iuno, picking up a second one.

The Iuno phase of the war lasted nine minutes and is not fully documented, because most of the witnesses were prone.

---

The thing happened at about the four-hour mark, somewhere around the woodpile.

Rebecca had Lupa down. Actually down — she'd come off the roof of the woodshed onto her, which was a maneuver nobody had authorized, and they went into a drift together and came up wrestling, and Rebecca got an arm around her neck and locked it, and the whole field was yelling, and Rebecca had that arm at about forty percent and knew exactly where the other sixty was and was holding it back without thinking about it.

And then she stopped.

She stopped and let go and sat back in the snow.

Lupa came up spitting snow. "What."

"Nothing."

"You had it."

"Yeah."

"So finish it."

"It's — " Rebecca looked at her hands. Then at the field: Ciaccona surrendering to Lynae with enormous theatricality, Cartethyia's assembly line, Amy dragging the wreck of her trebuchet, Mei face-down and moving. "Nothing's gonna happen."

Lupa sat up.

"Say it properly."

"Nobody's gonna *die*." Rebecca was still looking at her hands. "I've been in like four hundred fights and every single one of them, somebody — and this is just — " She gestured at the entire afternoon. "This is *nothing*. This is four hours of nothing and everybody's still here and we're gonna eat dinner."

Lupa wiped her face.

"Yeah," she said.

"That's *messed up*."

"Yeah."

"How long does it take?"

"To what?"

"To stop checking."

Lupa thought about it honestly, which took a while.

"I'm twenty-eight," she said. "I'll let you know."

Then she hit Rebecca in the face with a handful of snow at point-blank range and the war resumed.

---

## IV.

Cartethyia went and got it out of the drawer at half past five, while the light was going and everyone else was arguing about the stove.

She found Amy in the main room, alone, drying her hair with a towel.

"I've got something and I'd like to do it fast," said Cartethyia.

"Okay."

Cartethyia held it out.

It was nine inches of felt. Black hair in three strands of yarn. A coat, dark, with a collar that had been got right by somebody working from memory and doing well. Two small gold buttons for eyes.

Amy's hands stopped in the towel.

"That's Ma."

"That's your mother."

"When did you—"

"Three years ago." Cartethyia's voice stayed completely level, which took visible work. "It's — you were in that bed and she was in the chair and she'd been in the chair for about nine weeks, and I couldn't do anything about *any* of it, and I'm not — " She stopped. Reset. "I make things. That's what I've got. So I made her, and I put her in your hands, because I thought if you were somewhere, wherever you were, and you had her, then you weren't on your own in it."

The stove argument went on in the kitchen. Somebody said *Ser Pancake* and somebody else laughed.

Amy took the doll.

She turned it over twice and looked at it very closely, and Cartethyia stood there and let her.

"The coat's wrong," said Amy.

Cartethyia blinked.

"It's the Ragunna one," Amy said. "She stopped wearing that one before I—" She turned it again. "And her hair's down. She's had it up since forever. Since before I went."

"I can fix it—"

"Don't."

"It's four minutes' work—"

"*Don't*," said Amy. "That's the one you made. That's what she looked like to you, that year, in that chair. Leave her like that."

Cartethyia's hands went flat against her sides.

"Did she work?" Amy said.

"...Which way do you mean?"

"Did I hold her?"

Cartethyia looked at the floor for a second.

"Your hands were open," she said. "The whole time. So I put her in them and I closed them, and every morning they were open again and she was on the blanket, and I put her back, and I did that for about eleven weeks."

Amy nodded slowly.

"That's a lot of mornings."

"It was a good job to have."

Amy stood there holding a felt woman in a coat from a year she'd missed, in the room where her body had spent it, with her hair wet and a towel around her neck.

"I'm going to put her on the shelf," she said. "Next to the planes."

"Okay."

"And I'm going to be *completely normal* about it for the rest of the night, and then at some point I'm going to go upstairs and have about four minutes, and if anybody follows me up I'll put them through the window."

"Understood."

"Thank you, Cartethyia."

"Mm."

Rebecca came through the door backwards carrying a chair and said, at volume, "Okay so the good news is the stove works, and the bad news is your mom's in there with a *jar*—"

---

## V.

The party was small and started at eight and had no structure whatsoever.

Cartethyia's cake came out of the oven at a slight angle and stayed at a slight angle and was, structurally, a compromise between a cake and a landslide. It was eaten entirely. Lynae had three pieces and said nothing about any of them, which from Lynae constituted a review.

Firearms were placed in a crate by decree. The decree was Chisa's and it was obeyed instantly by everyone including Lynae, who had two more than anyone had counted.

The record went on. It came off. Ciaccona played instead, at the window, with the lute that had lived on that stand for four months in a different year, and she played loud things, and nobody said a word about what she was doing and she did it for two hours.

Mei made hot chocolate. Nobody asked what she'd done to it. Four people drank it and three of them finished.

The wall got another drawing — Rebecca did the cabin, badly, with all nine of them out front and the chimney with a nest in it, and put it up next to the neon green cape.

At some point around eleven Rebecca stood up with a cup and said, "Okay, so where I'm from you do a thing."

The room settled.

"You do the names," she said. "At the end of a run, whoever didn't come back, you say them out loud so somebody's said them." She held the cup up. "Maine. Dorio. Pilar. Kiwi. Da—"

She stopped.

She stood there with the cup up and the whole room looking at her.

"That's — " She swallowed. "That's the toast. That's the whole toast, that's how it goes, and I've got nothing *after* it because there's never — " Her jaw worked. "Everybody I've ever raised a cup to is *dead*, that's the — that's the format, I don't know how you do the other one."

Lupa stood up.

She took the cup out of Rebecca's hand, held it up herself, and said:

"Mya. Avidius."

Then she handed it back.

"Now you go again," she said. "With the ones in the room."

Rebecca looked at the cup.

"...All of them?"

"All of them. It takes longer. That's the point of it."

So Rebecca did it. She got about four names in before her voice went, and Amy shouted "*LOUDER*" from the floor, and Ciaccona put a chord under it, and by the time she got to Cartethyia the whole room was saying them with her, badly, out of order, over the top of each other, nine names in a warm room in a wooden house in the middle of a frozen nothing.

She got to the end.

"That's *awful*," she said, wiping her face with the back of her wrist. "That takes way too long."

"You'll get faster," said Chisa.

"I don't want to get faster."

"Then it'll keep taking too long," said Iuno, "for a great many years, and you'll complain about it every time."

---

## VI.

Rebecca went up at half one and slept nine hours.

She would mention this at breakfast with genuine confusion — she had lain down expecting the usual, the ceiling, the four-hourly sweep, the silence with nothing in it, and instead there had been noise: Ciaccona still going downstairs, and Amy arguing with somebody about a dice ruling, and Lupa on the stairs twice, and the stove ticking as it cooled, and a house full of people being loud below her in the dark.

"It's the *noise*," she said, over eggs, to nobody. "I need it to be *loud* and then I'm fine. That's so stupid. That is the stupidest thing about me."

"Mm," said Chisa.

Out on the south wall, in the crook of the wisteria, wedged with a shim Lupa had cut to size, something small had come back in the night and was making a great deal of fuss about the arrangements.

— fin.
