# YOUNGEST GATE 19

## 解甲 — STAND DOWN

*(the ninth hour of the twenty-eighth, Norfall Valley and the road south)*

---

## I. THE INTERVAL WIDENS

The bell's eighth beat reached Norfall twice. It came through the open relay a half-second late and thinned by twelve kilometres of copper and weather, and it came up through the ground along the Tacet seam that runs south under the eastern road, which was faster, and which everybody on the shelf felt in the soles of their boots.

After it there was a space where the ninth beat would have been. The space stayed empty, and everyone standing in that valley heard the emptiness and understood that the bell had said what it came to say.

Then the drum.

One second. One second. One second.

Then two.

Qiuyuan had his palm flat on the stone with Baizhi's dressing packed between his fourth and fifth ribs and his coat open in the rain, and he was the first to have the number, because he was the only person in the valley whose whole body was an instrument for having numbers like that.

<font color="#c46f68">"Two,"</font> he said. <font color="#c46f68">"Now four. It is opening."</font>

Baizhi did not look up from his side. She had one hand under the dressing and two fingers on the pulse point above his wrist and a bad opinion of his coat.

<font color="#a171d1">"Continue reporting. Also stop moving your arm."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Eleven."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Your arm."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Twenty-two. Then forty. Then it broke off the count and went somewhere I would have to follow it to hear."</font> He let his hand come off the stone, slowly, because the resonance burn across the palm had opened again and the rain had got into it. <font color="#c46f68">"It has stopped asking."</font>

The ground had been folding upward all night in tiers, stone becoming more stone, the geological insistence of a thing that had been fed for three years by memorial and applause and a file closed with the word *acceptable* in it. Now the tiers settled. They came down the way a stack of wet paper comes down when the weight above it is taken off: slowly, in one piece, with a great deal of water running out of the joints. The violet along the Tacet scars pulled back into the seams. The suspended sea over the valley let go of its borrowed moon, and the moon went out, and what was left in the sky was ordinary cloud at the ordinary height, going east.

Bao sat down on her ammunition box with her bad leg straight out in front of her and her bound thumb held against her chest like something she had been asked to mind, and watched the north wall of the valley subside, and said, to nobody in particular, in the flat voice of a woman announcing the weather:

<font color="#609e47">"孤掌難鳴."</font>

Sergeant Du, going past with a coil of relief rope over his shoulder, stopped long enough to look where she was looking.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Say it again in a language the foreigners can put in a report."</font>

<font color="#609e47">"One hand makes no sound."</font> Bao rolled her shoulder, found it disagreed with her, and left it alone. <font color="#609e47">"It needs somebody to hit. We stopped holding still."</font>

That was the whole of the analysis, and it was correct, and it went into three separate written accounts over the following week in three separate hands, and every one of them attributed it to a gun captain sitting on a box with her thumb strapped, which is the only kind of attribution the corpus of Huanglong's military doctrine has ever really trusted.

---

The guardians stood down at a quarter past.

Shield locks came apart along the northern line with a sound like four doors being unbarred in four different streets, and the crews came down the ladders, and San Jie's left shield stayed where it was because the weld along its lower edge had opened during the last press and the shield was, in Hsin's phrasing, *promoted from a component to a decision.* Lao Er vented steam for eleven minutes and smelled like a workshop. Si Ge sat down on its haunches in the mud, which the crews had never seen a guardian do outside a maintenance cradle, and which the engineering log recorded without editorial as *unit assumed rest configuration, unprompted.*

Hsin walked the line herself with her paper umbrella open over her head and her sleeves pinned back, and she did not delegate the inspection, and she did not hurry it. She put her hand into the seam of Wu Mei's seventh motor group and drew it out black to the wrist and looked at what came away.

<font color="#c86596">"Fang Qiu."</font>

The engineer arrived at a run with her thigh strapped and her hair plastered flat, twenty-three years old, having climbed a ladder in the dark the previous morning to take up a post she had held for one day.

<font color="#88d891">"Sovereign."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Your unit walked on seven groups for four hours and then held a wall on six."</font> Hsin turned her wrist so the light caught the grease. <font color="#c86596">"Tell me what she did to keep the line and I will tell you whether it was your idea or hers, and then we shall argue about which of you is to be commended, and I intend to lose that argument, so prepare your case."</font>

Fang Qiu opened her mouth. Nothing came out for a moment. Then the technical vocabulary arrived, which is what technical vocabulary is for, and she talked for six minutes without stopping about load transfer and brace timing and a bad habit Wu Mei had of grounding through her right heel, and Hsin listened to all of it standing in the rain with her umbrella tipped so the runoff fell behind her and the girl stayed dry.

At the end of it she said, <font color="#c86596">"It was hers,"</font> and Fang Qiu's face did something complicated, and Hsin added, <font color="#c86596">"which she learned from a woman who has been climbing that ladder for one day. Machines are a slow argument, Engineer. You have already won part of it."</font>

The paper sign was still folded in Wu Mei's forward stowage where an engineer had put it three days earlier, before the column left Jinzhou, in a boy's handwriting.

**FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK.**

Nobody took it out. It stayed where it was and got carried home.

---

Jiyan read the field return standing on the same flat stone Jinhsi had used, with his right hand wrapped from the heel to the base of the fingers and the wrap already through with blood, and he read it the way the doctrine required, which was aloud, with the people in it present.

Confirmed dead: two. Engineer Deng Hao, of the works section, killed in the first ten minutes by a stone splinter through the throat while reaching for Master Gong Mi. Ranger Cao Yi, second section, twenty-six years old, killed at the turnover of the rain by a phantom arrow that had stopped being a phantom on the way down.

Wounded: forty-one, nine of them badly, three of them requiring the lower cradle road and a night's carry.

Prisoners: nineteen, of whom four had been carried in by the people who had shot them, because Jiyan's rule was that anyone who reached the recovery markers got treated before custody was recorded, and four of them had reached the recovery markers.

Missing: none. He said that part twice, and the second time he said it with his eyes on the western berm, where a section had spent forty minutes in the dark counting itself.

<font color="#b0c4de">"Deng Hao's works section will carry him. Cao Yi's section will carry him. Nobody's friend goes on a stranger's shoulder tonight."</font> He folded the return along its crease. <font color="#b0c4de">"Both names go south with the column and both names go on the ward boards after their families have been told by somebody who knew them, and not before. Sergeant Du has the second part of that."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Sir."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Not tonight. Tonight you are going to eat something and let the physician look at your forearm."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Sir."</font>

Chixia was sitting up against a medical crate with her leg out and the iron still in her calf and Jinhsi's scarf tied around it in a knot that Baizhi had already privately assessed as *sentimental but structurally adequate,* and she heard the name Cao Yi and turned her head.

<font color="#ff5757">"Second section — that's the one with the tall one? The one who kept — "</font> she stopped, and started again in a different register, quieter, which she almost never did in public. <font color="#ff5757">"On the walk up. There was a kid arguing with the whole line about the tax office goose. Saying she was protected, saying there's a statute."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Cao Yi,"</font> Du said.

<font color="#ff5757">"He was *right.*"</font> Chixia put her head back against the crate and looked at the underside of the tarpaulin, where the rain was pooling in the sag and had been for hours. <font color="#ff5757">"There isn't a statute. But he was right anyway. She's got a column in the census, and once you're in a census you're in it, and I have argued that exact thing with the ward office four times and lost four times, and that kid had it worked out on a road at night with a pack on."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Do not sit up further."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"I'm not sitting up, I'm *speaking* up, they're different."</font> She stayed where she was. <font color="#ff5757">"Somebody write it down. Cao Yi, second section, first person in the Jinzhou patrol service to get the goose question right, and I'll take it to Assessor Lü myself, and if Lü tells me it isn't a finding, I'll tell him it's a bequest."</font>

Iuno, four metres away with her crystal arm out of its sleeve and her living hand pressed over the fracture, said, in a voice that had spent the night getting worse, <font color="#5881fe">"Give me the spelling."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"You're bleeding out of your *arm.*"</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"I am bleeding out of my arm and holding a pencil. My range of simultaneous activity has always been remarked upon."</font> She got the pencil out of her coat with her teeth involved and set the paper on her knee. <font color="#5881fe">"Spelling, Captain."</font>

Chixia gave her the spelling.

*She is going to be insufferable about this for a year,* observed the High Priestess, from the small warm room behind Iuno's sternum, with her feet up. *She is going to file a bequest on behalf of a goose.*

*She is going to win,* Iuno answered, and wrote the name.

---

The rain went on hanging.

That was the thing everybody noticed at about half past, once the ringing had gone out of their ears and the count had been read and there was nothing immediately in front of them: the Retroact was still up there, still suspended, still holding the last twenty minutes of nineteen hundred and seven people in individual drops the size of a fingernail, and it was thinner than it had been at midnight.

Baizhi noticed it because she measures things. She had a glass slide out of her kit and a cloth and a watch, and she stood in the open with her arm up for ninety seconds and counted what settled, and then she did it again eleven minutes later, and then she went and found Iuno, because Iuno was the only other person in the valley who would understand the number without needing the number explained.

<font color="#a171d1">"Density is falling."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"At what rate."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Two samples. A rate from two samples is a guess with arithmetic on it."</font> Baizhi held up the slide anyway. <font color="#a171d1">"The guess is that it halves every four hours."</font>

Iuno was quiet for the length of time it takes to do a piece of division in your head that you would rather not have done.

<font color="#5881fe">"Twelve kilometres of road."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Nineteen hundred people walking at the pace of people who have been standing for three years."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"And the seam runs south under the road the whole way, which is the only reason any of this is possible, and the seam is where the density is highest, and the density is halving."</font> Iuno looked north up the valley, where the last of the column was still coming out of the folded ground in ones and twos, the way people come out of a building after the alarm has stopped. <font color="#5881fe">"Say the sentence, Doctor. I have found that saying it out loud stops one from arranging it."</font>

Baizhi put the slide back in its sleeve and closed the sleeve and put it in her coat.

<font color="#a171d1">"If they are still on the road when it goes, they will go on the road."</font>

## II. THE TERMS

The amber box sat at the valley's centre where the stage had been, no wider than a theatre's front rank, with two people inside it and the rain running off its outside in sheets that never touched the two of them.

Cristoforo had his cane across his knees and his punctured hand open on his thigh. He had stopped bleeding an hour ago. He had not once looked at the exit.

Nivora came down off the northern lip at a walking pace with her hands at her sides.

She had put on a face for it — an older face, a working woman's, the kind that gets you served quickly at a market and forgotten by dinner — and she stopped a courteous distance from the amber and waited to be acknowledged, which nobody who had watched her for the last four hundred years would have predicted, and which Hsin noted and filed.

<font color="#c86596">"Grand Architect."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Sentinel."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"You have been standing on my ridge for eleven hours."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I have been standing on Huanglong's ridge for eleven hours,"</font> Nivora said, <font color="#fdb4fe">"and I take the correction as offered. May I come nearer, or would you prefer to do this at the volume where the Magistrate can hear every word, which I assume is the reason you have not moved the box."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"You may come nearer. The Magistrate will hear every word regardless, because I intend her to, and because she has walked over here with a clerk's pad and a face I have seen on tax assessors."</font>

Jinhsi had. She had come across the churned ground in her field clothes with mud past her knees and the civil seal case under her arm and a folded pad in her hand, and she stopped at Hsin's shoulder and stood there with the pad flat against her thigh, waiting to be got to.

Behind her, at ten metres, in a loose and entirely deliberate arrangement that Lupa had put together in about nine seconds without saying anything to anybody: Mei on the left with her bandaged hand in her coat, Iuno on Mei's right, Qiuyuan standing with his weight on his back foot and his wrapped sword returned to him under field custody and his listening hand held palm-up against his chest, Amy at the relay with the channel open and recording, Jiyan at the front with his cut hand at his side, and Lupa herself at the outside edge of the whole arrangement with her spear butt in the mud, watching the approaches, because somebody always has to.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I would like him back,"</font> Nivora said.

<font color="#c86596">"Of course you would."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"He is a material party to a homicide committed inside Jinzhou's prefectural jurisdiction,"</font> Jinhsi said. Her voice came out flat and civil and carried further than it should have. <font color="#c8e4d7">"Engineer Deng Hao, of the works section, killed at approximately five minutes past the second hour by an explosive charge laid under a medical track by hands acting on that man's signal. I have nineteen prisoners who will each be asked who gave the order and when. I have the schedule of the ceremony, which reached the Fractsidus from inside my own administration and which is sealed as evidence with every office that held a copy named beside it. I have a valley full of witnesses. Article eleven of the prefectural code gives me the person of an accused within my boundary. I am telling you the article number, Sentinel, because I have found that people who intend to ignore one prefer to know which one they are ignoring."</font>

Hsin turned her head, and for a moment the amusement went out of her face entirely, and what was under it was four hundred years old and had spent most of them doing paperwork.

<font color="#c86596">"You are correct in every particular."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Then he stands."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"He stands inside a Sonoro Sphere in which I hold sovereign citation. Your article reaches the boundary of my writ and stops there, Magistrate, and mine reaches the boundary of yours and stops there, and the two of them have been touching without touching since the moment I put my foot on his floor."</font> She let that lie for exactly as long as it needed. <font color="#c86596">"I am going to release him."</font>

Qiuyuan's head came round.

<font color="#c46f68">"Sentinel."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Senior Operative."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Eleven years. Seven prefectures. Every production a rehearsal for this one."</font> He was very calm, which was the worst version of him and the only one he had. <font color="#c46f68">"The order that sent me to kill an innocent man carried a seal that a dead person's hand could not have pressed. The Grandmaster who was replaced is buried under a name that is not his any more. I have come across half a country with a Forte that charges me blood to use. Last night a man put a blade between my ribs and offered me a private verdict, in the clean shape, blade to blade, with a crowd, and I refused it out loud with the blade still in me and I would refuse it again this morning. I came to testify. Testimony requires a defendant."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"It requires a record,"</font> Hsin said. <font color="#c86596">"You will have the record, entire, with my chop on it and my crews' statements under it. That is a smaller object than the one you came four hundred kilometres for, and I am naming it at its own size so that you can keep the difference and hold it against me, which I expect you to do, and which is your right."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"Name your price, Sentinel."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"I have named it. You have not yet said no."</font>

Nivora was silent for four seconds.

Nobody standing at ten metres heard a price. There was no price said. Later, when Amy went back over the relay recording with the gain up and her headset on and a bad temper, there were four seconds of rain, and then Hsin's voice at ordinary volume saying something that the channel had received as a flat tone with no formants in it, the way a channel receives a sound that has been decided about somewhere upstream of the channel.

<font color="#fdb4fe">"That is expensive."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"It is."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"I accept it."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"You accepted it before I finished."</font> Hsin's tails moved once, all nine, and settled. <font color="#c86596">"Which tells me the thing I actually wanted, and which you have now given me for free. Thank you."</font>

<font color="#fdb4fe">"…Ah."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Mm."</font>

The whole negotiation had taken under a minute. Iuno, who had spent thirty years in rooms where people bought and sold the movement of armies, watched it from ten metres with the professional stillness of a woman recognising a technique she had never been good enough to use, and afterwards, when Mei asked her what had actually happened, she said only that two extremely old creatures had agreed on something with the speed of people who had rehearsed against each other for a very long time, and that the speed was the information.

The amber came apart along a line that had never been visible.

Cristoforo stood up, put his cane down onto real ground, and tested his weight on it. He was a man of no particular size in a coat that had been good three days ago. Rain got into his hair immediately and he let it.

<font color="#fafadb">"Magistrate."</font>

Jinhsi did not answer him.

<font color="#fafadb">"You read the direction,"</font> he said. <font color="#fafadb">"On the stone, in mud, in the flat register of a woman entering a civic order. Restored a removed stage direction over a Threnodian's cue and made it hold. It is the best piece of dramaturgy I have witnessed in forty years and I am aware that you will find that sentence obscene, and I say it anyway, because you are owed the professional assessment and there is nobody else within four hundred kilometres qualified to give it to you."</font>

He shifted his weight onto the cane.

<font color="#fafadb">"You read it,"</font> he said, and now the courtesy had something under it. <font color="#fafadb">"You have not written it."</font>

Jinhsi looked at him for a while.

Then she took the folded pad out from under her arm, and put it against the seal case, and got a pencil out of her collar, and wrote standing up in the rain with the paper going soft under the point, in the small square hand that eleven wards had learned to recognise on a notice board.

*Prefecture of Jinzhou. Casualty file, Norfall, third year. Correction entered at the ninth hour of the twenty-eighth day of the sixth month, on the ground, by the Magistrate, in the presence of the general commanding, the Grand Marshal's liaison, and the surviving company.*

*The finding of the third year, that the losses at Norfall were acceptable, is vacated.*

*The company of General Geshu Lin did not disappear. One thousand nine hundred and seven persons were present with him and remained present. Their names, personal details and true roads are entered in the two registers appended, which are to be filed as one document and are not to be summarised.*

*Signed at the place, at the hour, in the weather.*

She wrote her name. She sanded it with nothing, because there was nothing, so she held it out flat in the rain for four seconds and let the water do what sand does, badly, and then she folded it once and put it in the seal case.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"There. It is written."</font> She closed the case. <font color="#c8e4d7">"Get off my valley."</font>

Cristoforo bowed to her — a small one, done properly, with the cane held out of the way — and went.

He went north, which was the wrong way for a man who had just been released, and eleven people noticed it, and Iuno was the one who said it out loud on the walk back to the shelf.

<font color="#5881fe">"He is going over the ridge and down the far side. There is one road on the far side and it runs west and then north, and at the end of it there is a city that prints things."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Mengzhou,"</font> Ciaccona said, and adjusted the strap of a lute with two strings on it, and did not sound pleased about any part of the sentence.

Nivora walked with him to the lip and stopped there and did not follow him down. She turned back once, and found that the person she was looking for was already looking at her: a woman four hundred metres off with her hand bandaged and her coat wrong and her daughter's shoulder against her arm.

Neither of them raised a hand. It was not that kind of accounting.

Hsin folded her umbrella and tucked it under her arm and stood watching the ridge until both figures were off it.

<font color="#ffdead">"You paid for that."</font>

Mei had come over. She did that. She had been doing it for four hundred years and nobody had ever managed to install a warning system.

<font color="#c86596">"I bought something with it,"</font> Hsin said. <font color="#c86596">"Those are different transactions and I would like the difference minuted."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"What'd you get?"</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Ask me in Mengzhou."</font>

Mei laughed. It came out cracked, because her tongue had a piece missing out of the edge of it and her ribs had opinions, but it was a real one, and Hsin's ears came forward at the sound of it the way a fox's ears do when something unexpected happens in the grass.

<font color="#ffdead">"Somebody said that to me once. About type."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"In a city where things stand for other things,"</font> Hsin agreed, <font color="#c86596">"and you have been carrying a piece of my lead in your pocket since before you knew there was a Mengzhou to carry it from, and I have known that since the moment you walked onto my shelf, and I have said nothing about it for a day and a half, which for me is a form of religious observance."</font> She started back toward San Jie's foot, and then stopped, and looked back over her shoulder with all nine tails perfectly still, which is the fox equivalent of putting down a cup. <font color="#c86596">"When you come, bring the quad. Do not have it cast. Bring it as it is."</font>

## III. THE ROAD IS THE RAIN

Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying came up the gorge road at a quarter to ten with her theatre reserve behind her and her sword across her saddle, and she had the problem in front of her before anybody said it to her, because she had been reading battlefields by their negative space for thirty-two years and the negative space in this one was a road with a clock on it.

She was sixty-one. She got down off the horse without assistance and stood in the mud for a moment with one hand on the saddle, letting the leg decide, and then walked forward at the pace she intended to keep for the rest of the day.

Jinhsi met her at the shelf's edge with Iuno half a step behind, and Baizhi's slide in her hand, and the number.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"It halves every four hours. The estimate is from two samples and the physician has entered her own caveat in writing. Twelve kilometres. Nineteen hundred and seven. They walk at the pace of the slowest, because the register is one document and I will not have it summarised."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"How long does the slowest take."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Nine hours,"</font> Iuno said. <font color="#5881fe">"Ten with the ford. The lower cradle road adds two and the wounded need it, so the living column and the dead column separate at the third marker and rejoin at the north gate, which means two roads, two sets of relief, and two sets of everything else."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"And at nine hours the density is a quarter of what it is now."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"An eighth."</font>

The Grand Marshal looked south down the gorge for a while. Rain came off the brim of her hat in a line.

<font color="#cbd369">"Then the road is the weather,"</font> she said, <font color="#cbd369">"and my reserve has just become a convoy. Fetch me my clerk and somebody from Xuanfang who understands what those projectors actually do, and get the kitchen off its wheels and onto the ground, because whatever else happens in the next nine hours, four thousand people are going to want feeding twice."</font>

---

It took her forty minutes to write, and she wrote it standing at a folding table under a tarpaulin with the rain drumming eleven inches above her head, and she read every clause out loud to the officers standing around the table as she wrote it, which the Board of War had given up doing some years before she was born.

The problem was that there was no column for it.

The Board of War's forms had a column for the escort of prisoners, the escort of matériel, the escort of civil authority, the escort of the sick, the escort of the dead in the sense of coffins and cradles and the ninety-one pages of the Interment Regulations. It had no column at all for the escort of one thousand nine hundred and seven persons deceased three years previously and currently walking.

Li Zhaoying solved it the way lamp-keepers solve things, which is by writing the true thing in the space provided and letting the audit come.

*Order of the Grand Marshal, in the field, twenty-eighth day, sixth month.*

*The theatre reserve is dissolved as a reserve and constituted as an escort convoy, effective on reading.*

*Task: to conduct the Norfall column from the valley floor to the north gate of Jinzhou, by the eastern road, at the pace of its slowest member, in weather.*

*Composition: four shield sections forward and four in echelon, spaced at four hundred metres, projectors at the setting supplied by the Sentinel of Mengzhou's engineers and no other; two gun platforms at the ridge line, elevation locked, laid on the northern approaches and on nothing south of them; four medical wagons; the field kitchen, dismounted; the works section; every paired runner I have.*

*The column is under the civil authority of the Magistrate of Jinzhou for the entire distance. Military authority extends to the ground, the road, the water and the enemy. It does not extend to the column.*

*I have assumed the attached obligations under my seal. I request confirmation, amendment, or personal relief from office by a hand willing to assume them.*

She got to that last clause and stopped, and looked up, and found Jinhsi standing on the other side of the table with her arms folded and something on her face that had no business being there after the night she had had.

<font color="#cbd369">"You wrote that to me four days ago."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I did."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"It was insolent."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"It was."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"It was also correct, and I have thought about it for four days, and I have decided that I am going to use it for the rest of my career and never once credit you."</font> The Grand Marshal signed. <font color="#cbd369">"Read it back, Zhou Lian."</font>

Her driver read it back. He was a spare man in his fifties with a face like a closed shed, and he had carried Falling Rain for eleven years and disliked every road she had ever chosen, and he read the order in the level unimpressed voice of a man who has read four thousand of them and expects to read four thousand more.

At the end he said, <font color="#b9a882">"The projector setting. That is a foreign sovereign's number in a Huanglong order."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"It is."</font>

<font color="#b9a882">"The Board will ask."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"The Board will ask, and I will tell them that the alternative available to me was a Huanglong number that I invented on a wet morning, and that I preferred the one supplied by the woman who has been building the machines for four hundred years."</font> She capped her pen. <font color="#cbd369">"Put it in the case with the rest."</font>

---

The setting was the whole of it, and Fang Qiu explained it twice, once to the Grand Marshal's engineers and once, more slowly, to Amy, who had asked and who had a relay channel open to a city that was going to want to know.

The guardians' resonance projectors had spent the night scattering the war's compression into frequencies it could not use. Run at a lower output, with the phase inverted, the same emitters could do the opposite: they could hold a frequency in place. What the Retroact carried was a frequency. The rain was condensed Remnant Energy off the Etheric Sea and it was thinning because the source had spent itself, and nothing anybody had was going to make more of it. What four guardians spaced along a road could do was stop it dispersing sideways.

<font color="#88d891">"The total is fixed,"</font> Fang Qiu said. <font color="#88d891">"What we can do with a fixed total is move it sideways. Thin over the fields, thick over the road. It is the same water either way. We are putting it where the people are."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"An umbrella held upside down,"</font> Amy said.

<font color="#88d891">"…That is not the technical term."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"It's going out on the relay in about ninety seconds and a hundred and forty thousand people are going to understand it immediately, so it is now."</font> Amy was already turning away with the headset half on. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Ward Two, this is the field. I have an engineer from Xuanfang who is going to be very embarrassed about a metaphor in about a minute. Here is what is happening to the rain."</font>

Hsin, three metres up on San Jie's foot, put a hand over her own mouth, which fooled nobody.

<font color="#c86596">"Four hundred years,"</font> she said, to the valley, <font color="#c86596">"and a nineteen-year-old with a microphone has finally explained my life's work in six words. I am going to have it painted over the Academy door."</font>

---

They put Falling Rain back across the saddle when the horse came up, and Zhou Lian settled it in its open travelling cradle the way he had settled it every day for eleven years, and Cartethyia — who had been carrying a cracked terrarium around all morning like a woman carrying a bird — stopped in the middle of the track and looked at it and said the thing out loud, because she is the one who says things out loud.

<font color="#b8ffff">"Grand Marshal! Your sword is out."</font>

Every officer within hearing became extremely busy.

Li Zhaoying turned round.

<font color="#cbd369">"It is."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"We *won.*"</font> Cartethyia said it with total conviction and no swagger at all, which is the register she reserves for facts. <font color="#b8ffff">"The company is out. The play is over. The man is going home over a ridge with a hole in his hand. The drum has stopped counting. You can skip the speech, everybody has been making speeches at me since midnight and I would like some soup — but the sword goes away when it's finished, doesn't it? That's what a sheath is *for.*"</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"It goes away on my retirement or my death,"</font> the Grand Marshal said. <font color="#cbd369">"Those are the only two entries in the regulation. It came out of the sheath eleven years ago on the day I was appointed and the interval since is my watch. That is the tradition and I have been told several times that it is a metaphor."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Is it?"</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Today it is a forecast."</font>

She put her hand flat on the steel, briefly, the way you touch a rail on a stair you know.

<font color="#cbd369">"Ask the swordsman what the frequency did."</font>

Qiuyuan was sitting on an ammunition box eleven paces off with his coat closed and his hand wrapped and his head at the angle it goes to.

<font color="#c46f68">"It stopped asking,"</font> he said. <font color="#c46f68">"It is still in the ground, and silence in that frequency means a thing has run out of surfaces to press against. It will find one."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"There it is."</font> Li Zhaoying nodded at the north wall of the valley, where the tiers had come down and the seams had closed and there was nothing to look at but wet rock. <font color="#cbd369">"That thing eats a war. We took the war away from it. We *starved* it, and I would like everybody in earshot to hear me use that verb and put it in their letters home, because in about nine months somebody is going to write this up as a victory and put it in a school hall, and that is precisely how the last one got fed."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"…Oh,"</font> said Cartethyia, in a much smaller voice.

<font color="#cbd369">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"So it's hungry."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Permanently, in my professional opinion. A condition comes back the moment somebody feeds it." </font> The Grand Marshal got her foot into the stirrup, and got up, and took a moment about it that she would have taken privately ten years ago. <font color="#cbd369">"So the sword stays out, and I stay on the roster, and when a Threnodian of War is hungry the correct response of a professional army is to be extremely boring at it for twenty years. Get your soup, Knight. You have earned it and I am told the cook is competent."</font>

She rode south.

Cartethyia watched her go with the terrarium held against her chest and then turned round and reported the entire exchange to Ciaccona at high speed and at slightly wrong volume, ending with, <font color="#b8ffff">"— and she called me *Knight*, Cia, she used the *title* — "</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"I heard it. I was standing here."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"You were standing there and *not appreciating it.*"</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"I have two strings and a hand that will not close,"</font> Ciaccona said, <font color="#77f8c0">"and I have been standing here for four minutes working out how to make the sound of an old woman refusing to sheathe a sword, because that is a very good sound and I would like to own it. I am appreciating it at a technical level. It is the highest form of appreciation available to me and it comes with no facial expression whatsoever."</font>

Cartethyia thought about this.

<font color="#b8ffff">"…That's just how you seem to be."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Do not do the doctor's line at me."</font>

Lupa had been standing at the north end of the shelf for eleven minutes with her head up.

Anybody who has hunted with her can read that back off her at forty paces: ears forward, weight on the front foot, and the tail doing nothing whatsoever, which is the tail's loudest setting.

<font color="#ff9742">"Swordsman."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Gladiator."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"You just told the Grand Marshal the big one stopped asking."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"I did."</font> Qiuyuan turned his head a few degrees north, which for him amounts to standing up. <font color="#c46f68">"You have something else."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"There's a small one in the north cut. It has been sitting still since about the time the bell went, and it smells like the big one, and it also smells like about four hundred other things, and I cannot put those two halves of a sentence together, which is why I have been standing here for eleven minutes looking like an idiot in front of an army."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"My sweep went over the north cut twice,"</font> Jiyan said, from the command board. <font color="#b0c4de">"Both passes clean."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Your sweep teams have noses like bricks."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"They do."</font> He went on writing. <font color="#b0c4de">"Take somebody with you."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Everybody's got a job."</font> She had the spear up already. <font color="#ff9742">"Four hours. If it's five, it's still four. Nobody comes up after me, and I would like that heard specifically by the four people standing in this half of the shelf who are already working out who's coming up after me."</font>

The four people standing in that half of the shelf looked at the middle distance.

<font color="#ff9742">"Chisa."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Four hours."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Thank you."</font>

She went up the eastern cut on her own with her raw palms wrapped in a strip torn off a tarpaulin, in the rain, in a direction the doctrine specifically discourages, having announced it in front of a general, a physician and a woman with scissors, which is her entire method for getting away with things.

Chisa listened to her go for about a kilometre and then went back to her bowl.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"She took the spear and left her flask on the crate,"</font> she said, to nobody in particular. <font color="#ff8a8a">"So she thinks it is small."</font>

## IV. THE FOURTH RELIEF SECTION

The column formed by sections, because it had always been a column and sections were what it knew.

They came out of the valley floor in the order the register had them, which was the order the second forme on Bookbinders' Row had set at three in the morning by six rival printing houses working under one another's elbows, and Cartethyia stood at the head of the road with her paper figures in a satchel and read the section numbers off as they passed so that the living could hear who was going by.

Jinhsi walked back along the line against the direction of travel, which is the harder way to walk, with the seal case under her arm and one wooden chopstick held loose in her right hand.

She had carried it four hours north in a map case and through an ambush and a stage and a war, and it had a shine down one side where a thumb had gone for years, and she found the fourth relief section at the second marker, coming south at the pace of people who had been standing for three years.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Gao Shun."</font>

He stopped. He was a big man gone thin, in cloth that had been a uniform when the play was younger, with the ceremonial plate split off his right arm from the shoulder down and the arm underneath doing what it had done for three years without any use for it: the fingers going, very slightly, in a movement of about half an inch, over and over, in a rhythm.

<font color="#e0c9a6">"Ma'am."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Your father came to my table on the twenty-seventh, at the eleventh ward hour, in his good coat. He had six days of argument prepared and he did not use any of it. He put this down in front of me and asked me to carry the detail."</font>

She held the chopstick up where he could see it.

Gao Shun looked at it, and his hand came up out of habit, and his fingers closed on rain.

He tried it twice more, which was the part that was hard to watch, and then put the hand down and stood there with the whole of the fourth relief section going past behind him.

<font color="#e0c9a6">"I'll take the message,"</font> he said. <font color="#e0c9a6">"You can tell him I took it."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"He sent a correction."</font> Jinhsi turned the chopstick over once in her fingers. <font color="#c8e4d7">"He said you clicked it against the bowl when you wanted another serving, and that you believed for your whole life that your mother missed it."</font>

<font color="#e0c9a6">"She never said anything."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"She heard every time."</font>

Nothing happened for a moment, because there was no moment available; the column was moving and the rain was thinning and a man had been walking north inside a play for three years and had six hours in which to be a person.

Then he said, in the voice of somebody working out a piece of arithmetic that has been sitting in a drawer since he was nine:

<font color="#e0c9a6">"…She used to put the pot back on."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#e0c9a6">"Before I did it. She'd already have — "</font> He stopped. <font color="#e0c9a6">"She was ahead of me the whole time."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"By some minutes, I should think."</font>

The field kitchen was two hundred metres south of them, dismounted onto the ground the way the Grand Marshal had ordered it, with four fires under it and a queue of Rangers and engineers and prisoners' escorts stretching back past the medical wagons. Jinhsi walked Gao Shun down to it against the traffic, which took eleven minutes and required her to explain herself to three separate officers, and then she stood in front of the kitchen sergeant and asked for a bowl.

The kitchen sergeant was a man of about fifty with flour to the elbow who had been feeding a theatre reserve for four days on a ration schedule signed in Mingting.

He looked past her at the column.

<font color="#a8b8c8">"Ma'am, they don't eat."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"No."</font>

<font color="#a8b8c8">"Then it's grain out of the return with nothing against it."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"It is."</font> Jinhsi put the seal case down on the corner of his table, which is a thing magistrates do when they want the next sentence to have furniture under it. <font color="#c8e4d7">"Sergeant, in about two hours a great many people are going to walk past this kitchen who have been inside somebody else's ending for three years, and every one of them was pulled out of it by a stranger saying one small true thing about them at volume. I have one bowl's worth of small true thing in my hand and it belongs to a man in the fourth relief section. I am asking for the bowl. If you require it entered, it goes against my seal and the Grand Marshal will countersign, and if the audit comes I will attend it personally and read this conversation into the record."</font>

The kitchen sergeant considered the shape of his afternoon.

<font color="#a8b8c8">"How many in the column?"</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"One thousand nine hundred and seven."</font>

<font color="#a8b8c8">"…Right."</font> He wiped his hands down his apron. <font color="#a8b8c8">"Then I'll want the four big pots on and somebody to run me water, because if we're doing one we're doing all of them. I'm not having a column go past my kitchen where some of them got a bowl and the rest looked at it."</font>

He said it the way people in that trade say everything, which is as a complaint about logistics, and he was already turning round and shouting for his water detail before Jinhsi had finished writing him his authority.

They set a flat stone at the roadside and put the bowl on it.

Jinhsi took the chopstick and clicked it once against the rim.

It made the small domestic sound that that particular collision has made in every kitchen in Huanglong for four hundred years, and it went about nine feet in the rain, and Gao Shun stood over the bowl and looked down into it.

The steam came up and leaned.

It leaned toward him and stayed leaning, all the way through, the way a flame leans when it has company, and it went on leaning while the fourth relief section went past behind them and a hundred and ten people saw it happen and every one of them kept walking, because they had somewhere to be and because some things are best done in front of people who are busy.

<font color="#e0c9a6">"Tell him I heard her,"</font> Gao Shun said.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I will tell him tonight. He lives on the north terraces and I know the door."</font>

<font color="#e0c9a6">"Tell him the pot thing. He won't have worked out that I know about the pot thing."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I will tell him the pot thing."</font>

He went south with his section.

Behind him the kitchen sergeant got his four big pots on, and by noon there were eleven flat stones along the eastern road at intervals of a kilometre with bowls on them, refilled by a works detail that had invented the job that morning and had already developed strong professional opinions about it. The entry in the Grand Marshal's supply return for the twenty-eighth day of the sixth month reads, in a clerk's hand, under *issued against no requisition:*

*Grain, one hundred and forty measures. Salt, four. Fuel, as available.*

*Reason: the column.*

*Countersigned. See attached, which is not to be summarised.*

## V. TESTIMONY

Qiuyuan asked for the meeting himself, and he asked for it in the proper form, which took him some time, because the proper form required him to establish first that he was under two valid detentions and that his standing to ask for anything at all rested entirely on the courtesy of the person he was asking.

<font color="#c46f68">"I am the subject of an Internal Security detention order and a prefectural summons as a material witness. Both are valid inside their own chains. I am asking the holder of the second one for eleven minutes and a clerk."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"You may have the eleven minutes,"</font> Jinhsi said. <font color="#c8e4d7">"The clerk is in Jinzhou holding a key. Will a foreigner with no office and legible handwriting satisfy you?"</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"If she can be sworn."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"She can be sworn. She will complain about the wording."</font>

Iuno complained about the wording.

She complained about it for ninety seconds, standing under the tarpaulin at the end of the medical line with her crystal arm strapped across her chest and a pencil in her living hand and a magistrate's field oath being read at her off a card, and her objection was that the oath required her to affirm that she had no interest in the matter, and she had an interest in the matter, because the man giving the deposition had a hole in him that she had watched go in and the man taking it had been at her elbow all night, and she declined to begin a document with a false sentence.

Jinhsi struck the clause with a pencil and wrote four words above it and initialled the strike.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"*Interest declared and recorded.* Will that do?"</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"That will do very well."</font> Iuno sat down on an upturned crate and set the pad on her knee. <font color="#5881fe">"It is a better clause than the original and you should keep it."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I intend to."</font>

*She is going to steal it,* said the High Priestess, comfortably. *A magistrate has just stolen your clause in front of you and thanked you for it. Darling, I have not been this proud since the tariff schedule.*

*She asked in front of witnesses and I gave it to her.*

*That is how the good ones steal, and you know it, because it is how we used to.*

---

They put Geshu Lin at the end of the line under the same tarpaulin, sitting on the ground with his back against a wheel, which he did without being asked and which several people noticed. The ceremonial plate had gone off him piece by piece all morning as he walked. What was left was a young man of twenty-seven in field cloth with a burn scar across the knuckles of his right hand and a wound under the ribs that had been arranged three years ago by somebody with an artist's understanding and had spent the last hour becoming an ordinary bad injury.

Qiuyuan came in and stopped four feet away and stood still.

He was listening. Everyone present understood that he was listening, and nobody had watched him do it at close range in a quiet space before, and the effect was of a man reading a page very slowly with his whole body.

<font color="#d4b896">"You've been standing there a while."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"I am checking something."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Take your time. I've got about six hours."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Your breathing has a catch on the third beat. It has had it since you were eighteen, when somebody cracked two ribs on your left side and they set badly, and you took the air short to keep clear of the place that hurt. The ribs finished mending inside a season. The habit stayed and became the way you breathe."</font> Qiuyuan let his weight settle. <font color="#c46f68">"I heard that catch nine years ago in a village on the Wuting road, in the dark, standing behind you at about this distance."</font>

Geshu Lin held very still.

<font color="#d4b896">"Say the rest of it."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"I was sent to kill you. I carried an order bearing the seal of Grandmaster Liang Dongyuan of the Internal Security Agency, which named you as a minor official who had killed Tacet Discords outside sanction. I stood behind you for the length of four breaths, and what I heard was a man with cracked ribs standing between a blade and three farmers, telling the farmers to go up the bank. I declined the order. I withheld from you that I had been there, and I withheld from the Agency the reason, and by the time I understood what the order actually meant, a man was dead in Mingting and the seal on that page had become the only physical evidence I have ever held that the thing which killed him was inside the Agency nine years before it killed him."</font>

Iuno's pencil went across the pad without stopping.

Geshu Lin looked at the tarpaulin for a while.

<font color="#d4b896">"I knew there was somebody behind me."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"You turned your head eleven degrees and left it there."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"I had three farmers on a bank and a Discord in the ditch and I made a decision about which direction to be frightened in."</font> He put his head back against the wheel. <font color="#d4b896">"I thought about that for nine years. On bad nights. Somebody was behind me in the dark and did not do the thing, and I never found out whether that was mercy or bad footing, and I could not ask anyone, because the only way to ask is to say out loud that the Internal Security Agency sent a man to kill you, and the last official who said that out loud in Mingting spent four years being audited."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"It was a man hearing a thing that could not be true, who chose to be wrong about his orders rather than wrong about you."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"That's mercy with extra sentences on it."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Possibly."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"You've been carrying it nine years."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"I have been carrying the paper nine years. I burned my copy of the order in the second month, at a shrine, badly, and I have regretted it since with a specificity I could describe for an hour."</font> Qiuyuan crouched, which cost him, and put his wrapped hand flat on the ground for balance. <font color="#c46f68">"General. I came north to testify, and testimony requires the thing I lack, which is the document. You saw it."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"I saw it for about four seconds by a lamp in a magistrate's clerk-room in the ninth month, in the tray with the rest of the day's post, because the clerk left the room to get me tea and I read the top sheet upside down, which is the single most useful thing my mother ever taught me."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Describe the seal,"</font> Iuno said, without looking up.

<font color="#d4b896">"Who are you."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"A foreigner with no office and legible handwriting, sworn eleven minutes ago under a clause I wrote myself. Describe the seal."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"…Right."</font> Geshu Lin closed his eyes. <font color="#d4b896">"Vermilion. Square, four characters, the Agency's beast in the upper right. There was a nick out of the lower left corner of the frame, about the width of a thumbnail, and the ink had run into it, so the impression had a little wedge of solid red where the frame should have been open. I remember it because I thought at the time that somebody's chop wanted recutting and that the Agency had money."</font>

Qiuyuan's head came up.

<font color="#c46f68">"Say that again."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Lower left. A wedge."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"The Grandmaster's chop was cut in the fortieth year and it carries a flaw in the *upper* left, from a fall. He refused to have it recut on the grounds that a flawed seal is harder to forge than a perfect one, and he said that to me in a corridor while I was carrying his lunch."</font> Qiuyuan's voice went quieter, and everybody under the tarpaulin heard the temperature of it change. <font color="#c46f68">"The order that sent me to kill you carried a copy, cut by somebody working from an impression, who put the flaw on the wrong side. The Agency passed it, because nobody in the Agency looks at the corner of a seal."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Nine years,"</font> Iuno said.

<font color="#c46f68">"And a Grandmaster in the ground since the eleventh month of the year before last."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Then a second chop exists, or existed, and somebody had it cut, and cutting a chop requires a cutter, and cutters keep books."</font> She wrote three more lines and underlined one of them. <font color="#5881fe">"General. The dispatches you received at Norfall. Did any of them carry that same wedge?"</font>

Geshu Lin was quiet long enough that Qiuyuan turned his head.

<font color="#d4b896">"The sixth,"</font> he said.

Nobody spoke.

<font color="#d4b896">"The sixth dispatch came in on the second morning. Clean paper, correct initials, filed under the category that means *we know, we have calculated, the calculation is entered, hold another line.* I have had that page in front of me every hour for three years because the rain keeps handing it back to me."</font> He opened his eyes. <font color="#d4b896">"Lower left. A wedge. I have looked at it about four thousand times and never once looked at the corner, because you don't. Nobody in a command tent looks at the corner of a seal."</font>

Iuno stopped writing.

Jinhsi, standing at the tarpaulin's edge with the rain coming off it two inches from her shoulder, said, <font color="#c8e4d7">"Read it back."</font>

Iuno read it back. It took four minutes. She read it flat, with the corrections in, and she read out her own struck clause and the four words above it, and at the end she read the standard closing and stopped before the last line.

<font color="#5881fe">"There is a problem with the attestation."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Say it."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"The form requires the deponent to sign. He cannot hold a pen. I have watched a man in the fourth relief section spend eleven minutes failing to hold a chopstick."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Then we use the blind-deponent form, which exists, and which requires the deposition to be read back in full in the deponent's hearing and affirmed aloud before two witnesses of whom one must be an officer of the prefecture."</font> Jinhsi came in under the tarpaulin properly and stood at the end of the crate. <font color="#c8e4d7">"It has been used four times in my tenure. It was written for people who cannot write. I am going to use it for a person who cannot hold, and I am going to enter my reasoning, and the Decree Council will have an opinion about it, and I will hear the opinion in a room with the door open."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Admissible?"</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"That ruling belongs to another table and I am going to make it here, on the ground, in the weather, because the deponent will be unavailable by this evening and the alternative is that the corner of a seal goes into the seam with him."</font> She held out her hand for the pad. <font color="#c8e4d7">"Deposition of a witness deceased three years prior to the taking, made possible by an atmospheric condition of limited duration. Taken at the roadside. Objection to admissibility is anticipated and is entered in advance as reasonable."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Magistrate."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"General."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Is that going to stand up?"</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"On its own, it is a piece of paper about a corner."</font> Jinhsi wrote her name at the bottom and blew on it, uselessly, in the rain. <font color="#c8e4d7">"Beside a chop-cutter's book, if the Senior Operative finds one — beside four thousand pages of a printing house's account of what was staged in seven prefectures over eleven years — beside nineteen prisoners asked the same question separately by a person with a great deal of patience — it is where the rest starts. That is what evidence is. The only reason anybody believes otherwise is that the plays are written by people who have never sat through a hearing."</font>

Geshu Lin laughed.

It was a short and extremely surprised sound, and it hurt him, and it was the first one anybody in that valley had heard out of him.

<font color="#d4b896">"Affirmed,"</font> he said. <font color="#d4b896">"All of it. Say the form at me and I'll say the words."</font>

She said the form. He said the words.

Afterwards Qiuyuan took the black bamboo flask off Sergeant Du, who had been holding it since the departure under a written arrangement that put the flask, the authorization, the dressing and the medical return line in four separate hands, and Du handed it over and stood there, because the arrangement also said he stood there.

Qiuyuan uncapped it and poured a measure onto the ground.

<font color="#c46f68">"Liang Dongyuan. Grandmaster. Nine years."</font>

The rain took it sideways and the earth took the rest.

He tipped the flask a second time and stopped.

<font color="#d4b896">"Don't."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"You are entitled to it."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"I'm entitled to it at about eight o'clock tonight,"</font> Geshu Lin said. <font color="#d4b896">"I've got six hours and a road and there is apparently soup. Pour for me when I'm somewhere else, because right now it's a man wasting medicine on my boots, and I've had three years of people being solemn in my direction and I would like a break."</font>

Qiuyuan capped the flask.

<font color="#c46f68">"Very well."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Also — "</font> Geshu Lin got his good hand under him and got up off the wheel, and Du moved forward half a step and then stopped himself, which was correct and which cost him something. <font color="#d4b896">"Nine years ago. On the bank."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Thanks."</font>

<font color="#c46f68">"It arrives as a sentence nine years late."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"So did everything else,"</font> Geshu Lin said. <font color="#d4b896">"Turns out that's the weather round here."</font>

## VI. THE TIED BOWL

Sergeant Du had two halves of a rice bowl tied together with a bootlace at his belt, and he had been carrying them for a month, and everybody in the second section had stopped seeing them at about day four.

Chisa heard them on the first morning, because Chisa navigates by sound, and two pieces of glazed ceramic tied against a man's hip make a specific small click against a canteen every fourth step, and she had spent the walk north with that click in her map of the column the way another person would keep a landmark.

She found him at the medical line with his forearm being wrapped and asked for the bowl.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"It's broken."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"It's been broken a month."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Yes."</font> Chisa put her hand out and left it out. <font color="#ff8a8a">"My blade cuts, and it also sews. I have mended six hells and a door. A bowl is smaller than either of those, and I have had nothing small to do since the twenty-seventh, and I would like something small to do, and you have been clicking at me for four hours."</font>

Du looked at her hand.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"There's a piece missing. Out of the rim. About the size of — "</font> he made a shape with his thumb and finger. <font color="#a9d6d0">"It went into the mud at the highland camp and I looked for it for twenty minutes with a lamp and then somebody needed me."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Then it will have a hole in the rim."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"What good's a bowl with a hole in the rim?"</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"It holds soup up to the hole."</font>

Du untied the bootlace.

It took her about forty minutes and she did it sitting on a crate at the end of the medical wagons with the two halves on her knees, and the blade did the thing it does, which is that it went along the fracture without touching either edge of it, and something on the far side of the metal took hold of the fracture the way a needle takes hold of two selvedges. It is a repair nobody in Huanglong had seen before. It leaves a seam you can feel with a thumb and cannot see; the ceramic on either side goes on being ceramic; the joint ignores heat and has yet to fail.

The crescent stayed missing. She had no way to make a piece of glaze that had gone into the mud at a highland camp four hundred kilometres away, and she said so, and handed him a whole bowl with a bite out of the rim.

Du turned it over twice.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"That's — "</font> He stopped and started again. <font color="#a9d6d0">"That's his bowl."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"It is."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"I had it in two pieces for a month because I couldn't work out what you *do* with it. You can't bin it. You can't give it to the sister, she'd have to be told what it is and then she's got a broken bowl and a thing to feel. I've had it on my belt clicking and I have not once been able to say why."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You were keeping it in the shape it was in when he stopped."</font>

Du sat with that.

<font color="#a9d6d0">"…Yeah."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"That is a good reason and you can stop now."</font>

He took it up to the kitchen at noon and stood in the queue with it, and the kitchen sergeant filled it, and Du sat down on the wheel arch of a medical wagon and did the thing his hands do.

He went through the bowl with the chopsticks and picked out the pepper.

There was a fair amount of it, because the field kitchen's cook was off the south shelf garrison and had views. He picked it all out, one piece at a time, and put it in a neat pile on the flat of the wagon's step beside him, and then he ate the soup out of a bowl with a hole in the rim, tilting it so it went where he wanted.

Bao came past with her arm strapped and a mug, and stopped.

<font color="#609e47">"You like pepper."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Yeah."</font>

<font color="#609e47">"You've been doing that since the highland camp."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Yeah."</font>

Bao looked at the pile. Then she sat down on the step, which took her some doing, and got her own bowl balanced on her knee, and went through it with her one usable hand, and put four pieces of pepper onto the same pile.

Neither of them said anything else about it.

By the meal on the shelf there were nine people doing it, and by the following week it had reached the highland camp and become a thing the second section did without explaining it to new arrivals, who picked it up in about a day the way soldiers pick everything up, and who were told, when they asked, that it was for Rui, and who did not ask what that meant, because you don't.

## VII. WHAT THE COAT CARRIES

They took the pellet out of Chixia's calf at a quarter to twelve in the second medical cradle, and she talked the whole way through it.

She talked about the goose. She talked about a statute that does not exist. She told the orderly holding her ankle the entire history of the harbour ward's dispute with the tax office over the census column and got about two-thirds of it right, and when Baizhi asked her to hold still she said she was holding still, she was only *speaking* while still, which was a completely different activity, and Baizhi said, without any change in her voice, that a leg attached to a talking woman moves eleven per cent more than a leg attached to a quiet one and that she had measured it, and Chixia said that was a *lie*, and then said, <font color="#ff5757">"…Did you actually measure it,"</font> and Baizhi said <font color="#a171d1">"Yes,"</font> and Chixia went quiet for the exact duration of the extraction, which was the intended clinical effect.

The pellet went into a dish. It was the size of a plum stone and it had a flat on one side where it had come to rest against the bone.

<font color="#ff5757">"Can I keep it."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"It is a foreign body from your own leg and it belongs to the incident file for eleven days."</font> Baizhi wrote the eleven days down. <font color="#a171d1">"After that it belongs to you and I will bring it to you myself."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"…Thanks."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Do not put weight on it before the fourth hour. I have told the general and I have told the outrider and I have told a wolf, and one of those three will physically prevent you, and I have not said which."</font>

---

Two cradles down, Iuno had her coat off.

That was the part Mei found difficult to look at, and she looked at it anyway, because she had spent thirty years learning to look away and about two years unlearning it and the unlearning was not finished. The crystal stump lay bare across a folded blanket. The fracture ran from the shoulder line down and across, along a plane that had been under pressure since the day the arm ceased to exist, and it had stopped weeping at about the eighth hour and had left an amber deposit along both edges that had already begun to set.

Baizhi worked on it for a long time with a lens and a pair of very fine tools she had not opened in front of anyone before.

<font color="#a171d1">"It has feeling in it."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Since about the fourth hour. That is new."</font> Iuno turned her shoulder an inch so the lens could get at the plane. <font color="#5881fe">"Eleven years of ache in weather, and ache in weather is a fact about barometric pressure. This is a fact about a surface."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Describe the sensation."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"A hand on a scar."</font>

Baizhi wrote that down verbatim, which she does when a patient produces a phrase she believes she will want again.

<font color="#a171d1">"I have three columns for this and none of them takes it. It is a discharge, which my instruments will take. It is an emotional event, which sits outside them. It is a wound that everyone including the patient had entered as concluded, which makes it a filing error of eleven years' standing."</font> She set the lens down. <font color="#a171d1">"I am recording it under discharge and appending the other two in the remarks."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"That is exactly what I would have done."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"I know. I read your appointment paper. You put your spending limit in the margin."</font>

*She likes you,* the High Priestess observed, delighted. *That was the doctor's version of a bouquet.*

*It was the doctor's version of a citation.*

*Darling, in that woman's hands there is no difference and you know it perfectly well.*

Mei sat on the crate beside the cradle with her own hand freshly closed — four stitches through the web between the second and third fingers, which had taken Baizhi nine minutes and had made Mei say a word in a language nobody present could identify — and she watched Iuno argue about column headings with a physician while a coat hung over the end rail of the cradle, dripping.

She reached over and straightened the coat on the rail. That was all it looked like.

Her stitched hand went into the pocket with the ring closed in her palm, and her fingers found the lining and the shape of the other thing that lives in there: a folded paper, four characters, a seal space with a chop pressed into the fibre at the mill by somebody who had the paper made before they wrote on it and left the space empty anyway.

She left it where it was. She did not turn it over. She had carried that ring since a stage in the rain, in a bleeding fist, through a war and a dispersal and a deposition, and she had thought about it for nine hours in the way you think about a thing you are not going to do anything about yet.

The ring went down into the corner of the pocket where a small object settles.

Mei took her hand out and put it back in her own lap and said, <font color="#ffdead">"Ask her about the sample intervals."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"I was going to."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"I have them."</font> Baizhi produced the folder without being asked twice. <font color="#a171d1">"Rover. Bruising at Ragunna, four days. The ninth, thirty-one hours. The eleventh, nine hours. The fish bruise on the twenty-first, before the fifth hour. Today: a bitten tongue at the second hour, closed by the sixth; a scraped rib, closed; a torn hand which I have stitched because the tear is through the web and I decline to let a thing that will heal in six hours heal in the wrong shape."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"So it's the same as the twenty-first."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"To the hour, across eleven separate observations."</font> Baizhi turned the page. <font color="#a171d1">"That is the finding. It halved once, on a specific date, and it has held at the halved value through eleven separate observations. A rate that changes once and then holds is a rate that changed for a reason. I have no reason. I have a date."</font>

Nobody said the date out loud, and nobody said what had been filed at the ward office that day, and the folder went shut, and Iuno put her living hand out for it and Baizhi gave it to her.

<font color="#5881fe">"I keep the page."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"You keep the page,"</font> Mei said.

---

Nobody saw her do it.

She had chosen, out of four hours of standing about in a valley full of people who watch her for a living, the eleven minutes in which a physician had both hands in somebody's shoulder, a magistrate was arguing about grain, a bard was fighting a peg, a knight was counting leaves, a wolf was four kilometres up a scree slope on business of her own, and a girl of nineteen was reading a stores figure to eight wards.

It is the most competent thing she did all day and she will go to her grave without mentioning it.

## VIII. TWO SEAS

The Wind had been at Chixia's left shoulder since the turnover of the rain and had stayed there through the extraction, four degrees colder than the air, and Baizhi had entered the four degrees in the observation column at eleven-forty and had, for the sixth time in a month, left the cause column blank.

Mei came over at half past twelve with the shell in her good hand.

It had come down out of a false sky over Lahai-Roi on the spine of a red kite and put its string across her palm, and it had travelled since through a truck and four seas and a lake and a moon and a stage. Yangyang had held it once, on a public stair at breakfast, and had given it back eight days later at the guesthouse door in the flat voice, on the grounds that it knew its own address and the address was Mei.

It was pale and sea-worn and warm from a sun it had never stood under.

Mei sat down on the duckboards beside Yangyang, who was cross-legged with her sword across her knees and frost still coming out of her sleeve, and held it out.

<font color="#ffdead">"This is yours."</font>

Yangyang looked at it.

<font color="#667cf9">"You were told to keep it."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"A while. That was the whole clause,"</font> Mei said. <font color="#ffdead">"And a while's up. And I've had about four hundred years to work out the difference between a gift and a delivery, and I got it wrong for most of them, so I'd like credit for finally being on time about something."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"It was addressed to you."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"It was *carried* to me. There's a sentence that came with it and the sentence was an address and the address was for a shore I never got to, and the shell was cargo, and I'm the only person on this shelf who's ever been cargo, so I know one when I'm holding it." </font> Mei turned her hand over so the shell sat in the palm with the stitches around it. <font color="#ffdead">"Somebody's mother wrapped this. It didn't come four seas to end up in my coat."</font>

Yangyang took it.

She held it across both palms the way you hold something asleep, and put it to her ear, because that is what you do; some knowledge lives in the arm.

A tide. Long, patient, shore after shore of it. Under the tide, wind. And at the very bottom, faint as a lamp four rooms away, a second tide, older, in a different mother's rhythm.

Then she put it down on her knee and reached into her kit-roll.

She had to go past a spare shirt and a grammar book and forty-one letters bound with a boot-lace, and she came out with a small cloth bundle tied the way a person ties a thing they intend to untie once a year, and she unwrapped it on the duckboards in front of Chixia and Baizhi and a wind that was four degrees colder than the air.

A shell.

Pale. Sea-worn. The same size, the same curve, the same three ridges running back toward the lip and the same worn patch on the underside where a thumb had been for a long time before either of them was born.

Chixia stopped talking.

<font color="#ff5757">"Yangyang."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Coming-of-age gift,"</font> Yangyang said, in the report voice, which is the voice she uses when the other one has stopped being available. <font color="#667cf9">"From our mother. She wrapped it and left it with Suisui to hand over, because I had already gone to the registry office by then and she was too proud to post it. Suisui gave it to me at the checkpoint when I made Outrider. Nine years ago in the fourth month."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Put them side by side."</font>

Baizhi had come off her stool. She had her lens out. She did not ask anybody's permission, and nobody asked her to explain, and she got down on the duckboards in her good coat in the mud and put the lens over the two of them.

Same wrap-marks. Same four creases where the cloth had gone round. Same small careful abrasion at the lip where somebody with unhurried exact hands had taken the sharp edge off a shell before giving it to a child, the way you dress a wound.

And on the inner face of each, in a fine old cut, running with the curve, an inscription.

Mei's had blanks in it. The crossing had taken characters out of it the way the crossing takes everything, and what the court had recorded as legible afterwards was this:

> *"Like moths to a ■■, all who hear the ■■ walk the road of ■■■■. To the last ■■■■ of the ■■■ left in ■■ world: may a journey unhoped-for, and fated to ■■, find its ■■■■ in the presence of ■■■."*

Yangyang's had characters in every one of the blanks, and Yangyang had never been able to read a single one of them.

<font color="#667cf9">"I asked her what it said when I was eleven,"</font> she said. <font color="#667cf9">"She told me it was an old cut and I would get it when I was older. She died in the winter of the following year, and Suisui does not read the old forms either, and I have carried a sentence around for nine years with eleven characters in it that I cannot say."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Show me."</font>

Yangyang showed her.

Baizhi looked for a long time. Jinhsi, who had come across the duckboards for something else entirely and had stopped four feet away when she saw what was on the ground, looked for longer.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I can name three of the eleven and I would be guessing at two of those."</font> She crouched, seal case on her knees. <font color="#c8e4d7">"They are not Huanglong forms. They are the old Mengzhou cut, from the hollow art, and the person to ask is a founder on Bookbinders' Row, and the founders on Bookbinders' Row have eleven blank cases where those exact sorts should be, and every one of them cracks in the mould when it is cut fresh."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Don't say the one you think you've got,"</font> Ciaccona said, from behind them, without turning round from what she was doing to her lute.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"I had no intention of saying it."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Good. Because if it's the word you think it is, it'll still be that word on Thursday, and if it isn't, then you've put a word in somebody's mother's mouth and there's no getting it back out."</font> She tightened a peg. <font color="#77f8c0">"Ask me how I know."</font>

Nobody asked her how she knew.

Yangyang picked up both shells, one in each hand, and put one to each ear.

Hers held one sea.

Mei's held two.

<font color="#667cf9">"…Ah,"</font> she said.

It came out very small, and it was the whole of it, and everybody there understood that the flat voice had finally reached the end of what the flat voice can do.

---

The cold came down and settled against her cheek and did not move.

<font color="#ff5757">"Okay,"</font> Chixia said.

She was sitting up on the cradle with her leg wrapped and her red cap crooked on her head, and the Wind had been invisible to her from the first morning and had stayed that way, and she had spent a month arguing with an empty chair at a noodle house and a cold patch of air over her left shoulder and had been perfectly cheerful about it because she is a person who talks to things.

<font color="#ff5757">"Okay, I'm going to say it, because everybody here is doing the thing where they've worked it out and they're all being *dignified* at each other, and I'm the only one in this tent whose job description is saying things loudly to people who'd rather I didn't."</font> She pointed at the two shells. Then she pointed, roughly, at nothing, four degrees colder than the air, about ten inches off her own shoulder. <font color="#ff5757">"Same shell. Same mother. Same wrapping. And she's been fixing your *elbow.*"</font>

Yangyang's head came up.

<font color="#ff5757">"For a *year*, Yangyang. Every night. Same stroke, same fault, same half-inch, and you told me and Baizhi about it at the noodle place and you were so pleased and I said it was creepy and you said it was *pedagogy* — "</font> Chixia's voice cracked and she carried straight on through it, which is how she gets through things. <font color="#ff5757">"Nobody knows a fault like that except the person who's got it. A stranger would want twenty years to find your drift and another five to work out where to push. She had it on the first night, in the dark, without being told."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Chixia."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"And she cut the post."</font>

Yangyang stopped.

<font color="#ff5757">"You read me the letter. On the terrace stairs, in the frost month, with the noodles going cold, and you read it out in that voice you do where you pretend you're reading a duty roster." </font> Chixia had both hands flat on the cradle rail. <font color="#ff5757">"*Someone visited. Everything is still here. The guest left a mark your teacher would have liked.* Three inches deep. One stroke. The measure you left home without."</font>

The tent was very quiet, apart from the rain on the canvas and, somewhere down the line, a man being told firmly that he would not pass out.

<font color="#ff5757">"So."</font> Chixia turned her face toward the cold patch and got the angle about eight degrees wrong, as she always did, and did not care, as she always didn't. <font color="#ff5757">"Hello. I have been arguing with you for a month. I thought you were some — some *general-description guest*, some poor woman off a wall somewhere, and I have been extremely rude to you about the tax office goose on at least four occasions, and I want it on the record that I would have been *exactly* as rude if somebody had told me, so nobody's got anything on me."</font>

The cold moved. It came across from Chixia's shoulder to a point about a foot in front of her face and stayed there, and the frost on the cradle rail went out in a line, and Chixia's breath came out white.

<font color="#ff5757">"…Oh, that's *awful*, don't do that, I can't see you and now you're breathing on me — "</font>

Baizhi took her thermometer out.

She held it in the space in front of Chixia's face for ninety seconds by her watch, and read it, and wrote the number in the observation column, and then she did the other thing.

She turned back four pages, and five, and six, to the entries she had been making since the second month of the year, every one of them with a temperature in one column and a struck-out word and a blank beside it, and she went down them one at a time, and in each blank cause column, in her small exact hand, she wrote the same word.

The word was a name. It belonged to the woman sitting on the duckboards three feet away from her with a shell in each hand.

She wrote it six times and closed the book on it, and put the book in her coat, and picked her lens up off the duckboards, and said, in exactly the voice she uses for a splint and for a compliment:

<font color="#a171d1">"I have been carrying an unexplained four-degree gradient for four months and I have now explained it. Thank you. This is the most satisfying afternoon of my professional life and I would prefer that nobody comment on it."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"You're *crying.*"</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"I am standing in rain."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"You are standing *under a tarpaulin* — "</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Captain, your leg."</font>

Yangyang wrapped both shells in the one cloth and tied it the way her mother had tied it, four creases, and put the bundle in the inner pocket over her sternum, and then she sat there on the duckboards with her hands on her knees and the cold against her cheek and the whole of the last year arriving at once and jamming in the doorway of her mouth.

What came through was:

<font color="#667cf9">"You could have told me."</font>

The pressure at her cheek stayed exactly where it was.

<font color="#667cf9">"I know,"</font> Yangyang said. <font color="#667cf9">"I know what it costs. I know you have one left."</font>

She put her hand up into the air where the pressure was.

<font color="#667cf9">"New terms,"</font> she said. <font color="#667cf9">"Keep it. I'll do the talking for both of us until we get to Mengzhou and find somebody who can cut you a new one."</font>

## IX. LEFTOVERS

They heard him before they saw him.

That is worth putting down accurately, because it went out live on an open channel and eight wards of a city twelve kilometres south heard it too, and the harbour ward has been arguing about the exact wording ever since.

What came down the eastern cut at half past the second hour, ahead of Lupa, ahead of her spear, ahead of any warning whatsoever, was a small clear voice at a considerable volume saying:

<font color="#ffe3f1">"— and I want it *noted* that I said the ford was too deep, I said it at the ford, I said it *in* the ford, and did anybody listen? No. Absolutely no. And now we are both wet and one of us is *magnificent* and still wet, which is worse — is that a *kitchen?* Is that a kitchen. That is a kitchen. WOLF LADY. There is a kitchen."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"There's a kitchen."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"I *said* that. I said it first."</font>

Lupa came out of the rain with mud to the hip, her palms through the tarpaulin strip, her spear on her shoulder and her ears very slightly back, in the specific posture of a woman who has been losing an argument for three kilometres and has stopped expecting to win it.

Four metres above and behind her, flying, and complaining, was Abby.

---

The Bureau of Rites' hazard tables have four hundred years of entries, and every single one of them describes something you should back away from.

This one was the size of a well-fed cat.

White — properly white, a dense fur that the rain was landing on and running off, which is the first thing that ruled out half of Baizhi's shortlist, because the things the Retroact makes do not shed water; they *are* water. Long ears, held out sideways like a pair of narrow wings and doing a fair amount of the work of flying. Golden jewelry at the ears and the throat, in linked pieces, floating a finger's width clear of him and turning slowly on its own schedule, indifferent to wind, indifferent to the fact that its owner was upside down at the time.

And in the middle of the forehead, small and clean and unmistakable to every Resonator on that shelf: a Tacet Mark.

He came down in a long slow spiral over the medical line, made an unhurried inspection of forty-one wounded people, a magistrate, a fox, two hundred soldiers and a field kitchen, selected the highest available crate, landed on it, folded his ears, and sat.

<font color="#ffe3f1">"Right,"</font> he said. <font color="#ffe3f1">"Introductions. You may all sit down."</font>

Everybody was already sitting down.

<font color="#ffe3f1">"*Excellent* start."</font>

---

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Ward Two, hold — hold, hold, the field is fine, nobody is hurt."</font> Amy had one hand over her ear and the other flat on the crate and the register she runs cities in already engaged. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Our gladiator has come back off a sweep of the north cut and she has brought something with her, and I am going to describe it accurately and slowly, because if I describe it inaccurately Baizhi will take the microphone off me."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"I will."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"She will. It is about the size of a large cat. It flies. It is white, it has gold on it that does not touch it, it has a Tacet Mark on its forehead, and it has been talking without stopping since before it came round the corner."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"I have *not* — "</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"It is talking now."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"That was a *correction*. Corrections are a separate category. And I am a *he*, small pink one, kindly amend the record, and you are on the wrong side of that microphone."</font>

Amy looked at her mother.

<font color="#ffdead">"Don't,"</font> Mei said.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"I wasn't going to — "</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"You had your professional face on."</font>

---

The debrief took four minutes and was, in the professional opinion of every officer present, the worst debrief delivered on Huanglong soil that year, chiefly because there were two people giving it.

<font color="#ff9742">"North cut. Culvert under the old spoil road."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Doing what."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Eating a boot."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"I was *tasting* a boot."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Whose boot."</font>

A pause.

<font color="#ff9742">"Mine."</font>

<font color="#ff5757">"HOW,"</font> Chixia said, from a cradle eleven metres away, with her leg up and her voice at a volume Baizhi had ruled on twice already that afternoon.

<font color="#ff9742">"I took them off. There was a ford."</font> Lupa's ears went flat. <font color="#ff9742">"I put them on a rock. It came out of the culvert, took one, went back in with it, and I sat on that rock for a while thinking about my life, and then it brought the boot back."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"With a *stone in it*,"</font> said Abby, with enormous satisfaction. <font color="#ffe3f1">"A very good stone. A better stone than the boot, frankly, and I am *not* going to apologise for an upgrade."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Why a stone?"</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"Because you cannot give somebody an *empty* boot, small green one, what were you *raised* by — "</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"From the ford to the third marker,"</font> Chisa said, in the flat voice, to her bowl, <font color="#ff8a8a">"there was one set of footsteps."</font>

The crate went quiet.

<font color="#ffe3f1">"…I was flying escort."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You were asleep."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"ABSOLUTELY NO."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You were asleep inside her coat for two kilometres of scree and she put you down at the third marker so you could come in on your own, and you have been flying four metres behind her ever since because that is where she put you."</font> Chisa ate. <font color="#ff8a8a">"I do not have an opinion about this. I am reporting."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"That is *slander*, that is — WOLF LADY. Tell them."</font>

Lupa looked at the middle distance for four full seconds.

<font color="#ff9742">"It followed me,"</font> she said.

And that is the version that went into three separate written accounts by three separate hands over the following week, and it is the version Jinzhou tells to this day, and the only two people alive who know better have never contradicted it once, for entirely different reasons.

---

He ate for the next forty minutes.

He ate the way weather happens. He took a bowl off the trestle without asking, emptied it, put it down upside down on his own head for reasons he declined to explain, went along the queue at shoulder height accepting tribute from soldiers who had been under artillery nine hours earlier and were now feeding a flying cat by hand, and gave a running commentary throughout on the quality of the salt.

Somewhere in the middle of it he did the thing.

He crashed at twenty past.

<font color="#a171d1">"Leave him."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"He's — "</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"And the other thing?"</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Same curve."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"To within the error on my slide, which I have written down beside it."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Eight hours."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Eight hours to the value at which the column stops being able to stand up. He is smaller than they are and denser than they are and I have no idea what those two facts do to each other."</font> Baizhi closed the slide sleeve. <font color="#a171d1">"He goes when the rain goes."</font>

He had woken up. He had woken up all at once, the way he did everything, and he was sitting upright on the crate with his ears coming forward and the whole shelf watching him and every adult present arriving at the same problem in the same half-second, which was that somebody was going to have to say it to his face.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"You do,"</font> she said. <font color="#c8e4d7">"The rain that is holding you up is thinning at a measured rate and the physician has it at eight hours. Abby sat on the crate.

The gold went on turning. The rain came down. Two hundred soldiers and a fox and a household looked very hard at their own bowls, which is the kindest thing an audience can do.

<font color="#ffe3f1">"…Well,"</font> he said. <font color="#ffe3f1">"That is *extremely* inconvenient."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"I have plans."</font>

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Tell me one."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"Dinner,"</font> said Abby.

<font color="#c8e4d7">"Then I am going to try very hard to get you to dinner,"</font> Jinhsi said, <font color="#c8e4d7">"and I would like you to understand that I mean the whole sentence and that I have no idea yet how to do it."</font>

---

It was Hsin who said the thing everybody had been circling, and she said it the way a sovereign says a thing she intends to charge nothing for, which in her case is rarer than the other kind.

<font color="#c86596">"There is one container within four hundred kilometres rated for a resonance of that class, and it is standing over there with four stitches in it, pretending to look at the road."</font>

Everybody looked at Mei.

Mei had been on the far side of the crates for eleven minutes doing exactly that.

<font color="#ffdead">"Yeah,"</font> she said. <font color="#ffdead">"I know. I worked that out at about the second spoon."</font>

She came over and sat down on the duckboards, which put her at his height, which everybody noticed and nobody mentioned.

<font color="#ffdead">"So here's my problem, and I want it said out loud in front of all of you, and I want somebody to write it down."</font> She put her marked hand palm-up on her own knee, where it could be seen. <font color="#ffdead">"This hand has been reaching for things since yesterday morning. <font color="#5881fe">"Three times."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Three times, thank you, my partner keeps a ledger."</font> Mei turned the hand over and back. <font color="#ffdead">"So I don't trust the reach. I trust all of you. Lupa got there first, and she got there hard, and she came at it from the one direction nobody expected, which was against herself.

<font color="#ff9742">"I carried him four kilometres inside my coat."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You said he followed you."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"He followed me *and* I carried him, both of those are — "</font> The tail cut once. <font color="#ff9742">"Shut up, Chisa, I'm making a point."</font> She crouched, which put her spear across her knees and her raw palms on top of it. <font color="#ff9742">"I picked him up because he was small and he was going to die and I wanted him not to. <font color="#ffdead">"Lupa — "</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"There's a clause."</font>

Iuno's pencil stopped.

<font color="#ff9742">"There is a clause in a foreign country's permanent day-book with my name on it, and I paid for it with the only number that belongs to your body, and it says that nobody in household four-one-one gets committed without being told to her face, in a room, in advance, by a person who is actually present."</font> Lupa looked at the small white creature on the crate. <font color="#ff9742">"I wrote it for you. <font color="#ff5757">"THE GOOSE."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Ma."</font>

## X. THE SOUTH SHELF

They ate at the third hour, on the south shelf, in the rain, off the ground.

The Grand Marshal's kitchen had been dismounted for five hours by then and had achieved the specific condition of a field kitchen that has been overtaken by events: four pots on, two fires out and relit, a works detail who had discovered they were bakers, and a queue that had stopped being a queue and become a neighbourhood. Rangers ate with printers' apprentices who had come north with the second forme. Xuanfang crews ate with Mingting infantry and translated insults for one another in a spirit of professional courtesy. Nineteen prisoners ate under escort at the far end and were given the same food, which Jiyan had entered in the order, in writing, at the top.

Forty steamed fish had come up the eastern road at noon on a cart from Ward Two.

They arrived with a note tied to the handle in a shaky hand that said the ward had heard the name Li Guorong on the relay, and had heard what he did about fried fish, and that this was what the ward had to send, and that it was to be steamed, and that anybody who fried it would be answered to.

The kitchen sergeant read the note twice and pinned it above his pots and steamed the fish.

<font color="#ff9742">"Eat the fish,"</font> Lupa said, to the table at large, which was a tarpaulin over four ammunition crates and about eleven people at any given moment.

<font color="#b8ffff">"I *am* eating the fish."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"You are talking about the fish. Different activity."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"I am *describing* the fish, which is a service, because Chisa cannot see it and I have appointed myself — "</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"I can smell it."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"— I have appointed myself the *herald* of the fish — "</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Eat it."</font>

Cartethyia ate it. She eats everything slowly, including field rations, including things that are objectively bad, because taste came back after twenty years of nothing and she has never once got over it. She had the singed terrarium beside her knee with the lid off and the camellia inside it going through its own difficult afternoon: four leaves gone brown at the edge, one pale new tip still curled, and a smell of scorched glass that had not come off it since the eastern blind.

<font color="#b8ffff">"She's alive."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"You have said that eleven times."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"She's alive *eleven times.*"</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Eat your fish."</font>

---

Abby came out at four minutes past the third hour.

That was the first test of clause two, and it passed on its own. Mei put her hand flat on a crate and waited, and out he came, at volume, mid-sentence, having apparently been mid-sentence for the whole thirteen minutes; and Baizhi wrote the time in the margin of the entry and underlined it, because a clause that works on the first day is worth an underline.

<font color="#ffe3f1">"— which is *precisely* why I raised it in the drafting, and nobody listened, and now look: FISH."</font>

He went down the queue at head height, and the queue rearranged itself around him the way a queue does, and the kitchen sergeant — who had that morning issued a hundred and forty measures of grain against no requisition, to feed one thousand nine hundred and seven people who could not lift a bowl — leaned over his own trestle, looked at a small white flying creature holding a spoon in each front paw and announcing a clause number at him, and experienced a professional emotion he was unable to describe to his wife for some weeks afterwards.

<font color="#a8b8c8">"Right,"</font> he said. <font color="#a8b8c8">"*Right.*"</font>

He fed him four times.

The fourth time produced a formal discussion between a physician, a foreigner with a pad, and a gladiator who was standing eleven feet away pretending to be somewhere else, on the subject of whether a thing of that class can be said to have a stomach; and the discussion was concluded by observation.

The observation was that he went quiet — which by then everybody had learned to notice the way you notice a stopped clock — and drifted down onto the wheel arch of a medical wagon beside Sergeant Du, and looked at the small neat pile of pepper Du had picked out of his own bowl, and looked at Du, and asked nothing at all.

Then he reached over with both paws and added two pieces to it.

Du did not say anything.

He put his hand down flat on the wheel arch beside him, in the rain, and left it there, and finished his soup out of a bowl with a hole in the rim with the other one.

After about a minute Abby said, in a much smaller voice than his usual one, <font color="#ffe3f1">"Who is it for?"</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"Bloke called Rui."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"Is he coming?"</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"No."</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">"…Then he gets the pile,"</font> said Abby, as though settling a point of law, and put two more on it, and went back to shouting about the salt.

---

Amy came in at the third hour and eleven minutes with a headset round her neck and a bruise coming up under her ribs the colour of a bad sunset, and she sat down between Mei and Iuno without asking, which is how she sits down.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Right. Post-action on the callsigns, because I have to file this and I am filing it while I still remember."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Do we have to do the callsigns."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"We do the callsigns."</font> Amy put a bowl down in front of her mother without looking, the way a person puts down a thing they have been carrying for somebody for an hour. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Field call *Fool*, used four times, three by me, one by Sergeant Du, who used it correctly on the first attempt, which I want minuted because he had it explained to him once, in the rain, at speed, by a nineteen-year-old."</font>

<font color="#a9d6d0">"It's a good callsign,"</font> Du said, from the next crate. <font color="#a9d6d0">"It's short and it's got nothing else that sounds like it."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"*Thank you.* Return calls: *Ma* on my channel, *Mei* on Iuno's. Both used. Both worked."</font> Amy stopped, and turned her spoon over, and went on in a completely level voice, which is the one she ran a city on. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Iuno's went first. When the boards came down. She said *Mei* and it came through the whole stage and it landed."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"It was one syllable off the ground,"</font> Iuno said. <font color="#5881fe">"I had three minutes of enforced silence in me and a voice like a rasp and that was the entire performance."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"It worked."</font>

Iuno was quiet for a second.

<font color="#5881fe">"She turned her head on the first syllable,"</font> she said. <font color="#5881fe">"She has never once done that for a title."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"So the doctrine's good and I'm writing it up and I'm sending it to the war college, and they are going to hate it, because the whole doctrine is *have two people who are allowed to insult you.*"</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Send it to me first,"</font> Jiyan said. <font color="#b0c4de">"I will put my name under yours and they will hate it in a more useful direction."</font>

---

Ciaccona had the card out on the crate in front of her, face up, weighted at one corner with a mug.

It was a Fractsidus calling card of the ordinary kind, blank, which is what had made it a problem for a month: a bard cannot write nothing, because writing nothing is a rest, and rests are loud. It had three grey marks on it in a row, made without ink, at intervals — the Threnodian's drum, tracking itself down the card, second and third closing on one another as the day went.

The third mark had stopped moving at the ninth hour.

She looked at it for most of the meal with a hand that had gone the colour of a plum and would not close past halfway. Then she got the stub of a pencil off Iuno, and put the card flat, and drew one thing on it, and put the pencil down.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"What did you put."</font>

Chisa was sitting on her right with her bowl on her knee and her head at the angle it goes to, and she had heard the pencil go on and come off and had counted the strokes, and there had been two.

<font color="#77f8c0">"A rest."</font> Ciaccona turned the card round so it faced the wrong way for herself and the right way for a person who reads by pressure, which is a joke, and Chisa took it as one. <font color="#77f8c0">"Whole bar. With the value written under it, because if you leave a rest without a value some idiot fills it in."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"How long is the value."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Long."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Mm."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"He told me a rest is only silent if nobody puts anything in it. He was right, which I resent, and he used it to put nineteen hundred deaths in four seconds of my silence, which I resent more." </font> She weighted the card down again with the mug. <font color="#77f8c0">"So it's got a value on it now. That's the whole of my answer and it took me a month and it is two pencil strokes long, and it's going back to him one day, and when he reads it he'll know exactly how much I meant by it, because he's the only person alive who reads that fast."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You refused the fourth beat all night."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"I did."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Are you going to play it."</font>

Ciaccona looked down the shelf.

At the far end of it, past the fires, the last sections of the column were going by on the eastern road at the pace of people who have been standing for three years: a man carrying nothing with his hands open, a woman with a spear across her shoulders like a yoke, two brothers walking close enough to touch and declining to, four guardians spaced along the road at four hundred metres with their projectors on the setting Fang Qiu had given the Grand Marshal's engineers, holding the rain over the road and letting it go thin over the fields.

<font color="#77f8c0">"Yeah,"</font> she said. <font color="#77f8c0">"When the last of them's past the marker. It's their beat. They can have it on the way out instead of on the way in."</font>

---

Chisa put her bowl down at some point during that and put two fingers on the crate and left them there for about a minute.

<font color="#77f8c0">"What."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"The north string slackened."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"Which — "</font> Ciaccona put her mug down. <font color="#77f8c0">"The one off the third seat. The one that went past the eighth tower and you would not walk."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"It was taut for a month and it was held at both ends, and it went tighter the night the Magistrate put a dish down at an empty place, and it has been tighter every day since." </font> Chisa turned her hand over on the crate. <font color="#ff8a8a">"At the eighth beat of the bell it went slack. It is still there. It goes as far as a road with people on it, and it stops there, and there is nothing pulling on the other end of it any more."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"And the other one? The one that goes up?"</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"In the house since the fifteenth. Drinks the tea."</font> A beat. <font color="#ff8a8a">"It is sitting on the duckboards at the end of this shelf and it has been four degrees cold all afternoon and it is about eleven inches from a woman with a red hat, and I have known where it was every minute since the twenty-seventh and I have not said so, because it was not mine to say."</font>

---

Cartethyia got up at some point and went round the crates and stopped behind Amy and put a three-inch paper person down beside her bowl.

Amy looked at it.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"That is the fifth one."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Mm."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"That is the fifth one, from the passage floor, at half past twelve at night, six weeks ago, which you handed me and refused to identify, and which has been on my windowsill next to the rig ever since where I have looked at it every single morning like an *idiot* — "</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"It's true now."</font>

Amy stopped.

Cartethyia sat down on the crate corner with her hands in her lap, small, five feet of her, with a crystal laurel and a burnt sleeve and a plant with four brown leaves.

<font color="#b8ffff">"It's me."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"…Thyia."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"The paper people are for people who might disappear. That's the whole rule, I didn't invent it, everyone gets one." </font> She turned it over with one finger. <font color="#b8ffff">"I made one for myself the night the household voted me a room, because I thought — well. I spent twenty years naming things so they wouldn't disappear and I have never once been sure the rule works on the person doing the naming. So I made mine and I gave it to you to hold, because I have never in my life been able to hold my own, and I said I'd tell you when it was true."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"And it's true now because — "</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"I said my name on his stage."</font> Cartethyia's chin came up. <font color="#b8ffff">"On my knees, in the mud, with two directions fighting over me and a prayer in my mouth from a seminary that owned twenty years of me — and I said *Cartethyia*, out loud, at a god's theatre, and it was the smaller name and it was mine and it *held.*"</font>

Amy picked the paper person up very carefully, the way you pick up something that has been on a windowsill for six weeks.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"I'm keeping it."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Obviously you're keeping it, why would I give it to you if — "</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"I'm keeping it *and* I'm going to be insufferable about it."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"…Oh, that's fair, that's — yes, that's the deal, I accept the deal — "</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"EAT."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"*I am eating!*"</font>

---

Somebody carried Chixia her bowl at the cradle line, and Abby went with it, on the principle that food moves and he goes where it moves. He held forth at her for six minutes about the boot, and about the ford, and about a valley with snow in it that he could not place, and Chixia — flat on her back under orders with her leg up — argued back at full volume about all three, and the two of them got on immediately and catastrophically.

Then he stopped in the middle of a word, the way he does, and was asleep.

Chixia's red patrol cap was on the rail above her head. It stayed on the rail. By the time anybody looked again there was a small white weight asleep inside it, warm, with the gold still turning slowly a finger's width off the fur; and on the other side of the same rail the air was four degrees under; and the warm line and the frost line met somewhere in the middle of a cap that was chosen at a Bureau exam by a girl who put a resolute checkmark in the least wanted box on the form.

Nobody joined those three facts up out loud.

Chisa heard the whole arrangement from eleven feet away and reported none of it.

---

Hsin ate on a rice sack, at the end, with her tails arranged behind her and a bowl in both hands, and she took the last hawthorn out of her sleeve halfway through and looked at it and put it back, which nobody except Mei noticed.

<font color="#c86596">"Your knight told me my sword-carrying colleague ought to put her sword away."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"She's got a very direct approach to sovereigns."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"She has an *excellent* approach to sovereigns. It is the only approach that has ever worked on me and I have spent four hundred years constructing elaborate defences against the other kind."</font> Hsin ate. <font color="#c86596">"Rover. When you come to Mengzhou, do not bring me a delegation. Bring the household and the plant and the girl with the microphone and the wolf who throws food at people to stop them making speeches. I have an academy full of extremely clever men who have never once been thrown food at and it is showing in their work."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"When are we coming to Mengzhou?"</font>

<font color="#c86596">"When you have a reason,"</font> Hsin said, <font color="#c86596">"and you will have one before the eighth month, and I am not going to tell you what it is, and you are going to find that infuriating, and I would like you to notice that I have not lied to you once."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Noticed."</font>

<font color="#c86596">"Good. Hold on to that. It is going to be the only handrail on the stairs."</font>

---

Geshu Lin ate nothing and sat at the table anyway, on the ground with his back against a crate, at the end where Jiyan was, and the two of them said almost nothing for about forty minutes, which several people afterwards described as the loudest part of the meal.

At one point Jiyan put his bowl down and picked up the spear.

He held it out, across, the way you hand somebody a tool.

<font color="#d4b896">"I can't take it."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"I know."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Then why — "</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Because I have been carrying it for three years and I have never once offered it,"</font> Jiyan said. <font color="#b0c4de">"I told myself that was custody." </font> He put it back down, point in the mud, upright. <font color="#b0c4de">"Offering it would have meant admitting there was somebody standing here to offer it to. You have refused it. That is the transaction, and it is done, and now the spear is mine on your terms. You left it with me. That is the reason I carry it, and it will go on being the reason."</font>

Geshu Lin looked at the spear for a while.

<font color="#d4b896">"You filed the counterweight."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"Twice. In the second year. It had gone off by about a half-grain and it was pulling right."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"…It always pulled right."</font>

<font color="#b0c4de">"It pulls straight now."</font>

<font color="#d4b896">"Show me."</font>

So a general who had been dead for three years spent eleven minutes of the last six hours of his existence sitting in the mud at a field kitchen watching a man who had stood beside him for three years demonstrate the balance of a spear, and interrupting him, and being wrong about the grip once, and being told so.

---

Mei sat in the middle of it with a bowl in both hands and her daughter's shoulder against her arm and Iuno's coat over her own knees because Iuno's coat was drier than Mei's coat, which had been a subject of debate.

She got about halfway down the bowl and then she put it on her knee and turned it a quarter-turn by the rim and settled it, and everybody who knows her saw her do it and nobody said anything, and Jinhsi, four crates down, did the identical thing to a mug within the same minute and also said nothing, and Iuno watched both of them do it and filed it, again, for the second time in a month.

<font color="#ffdead">"How long have they got."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Eight hours to an eighth of current density,"</font> Baizhi said. <font color="#a171d1">"They will reach the north gate at about the seventh hour of the evening. What happens after that is a question about a substance nobody in this valley has ever studied, and I intend to be standing at the gate with a slide when it is answered."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Is it going to hurt them?"</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"No."</font> She gave it flat and at full strength, the way she gives everything. <font color="#a171d1">"I have listened to two thousand of them die tonight through a relay. That was pain. What is coming is a rain stopping."</font>

Mei nodded slowly, and drank her tea, which was Earl Grey and hot, because Iuno keeps her treaties.

Then she said, to the table, in the tone of a woman who has run out of large things to say and has got down to the size of thing she can actually manage:

<font color="#ffdead">"Same time tomorrow?"</font>

She says it about twice a week. She has been saying it for as long as anybody in that household can remember, with no idea at all where she got it, and the one person alive who knows has never told her, and it goes out and it lands on the table and people say *sure* or *no, I'm on the early shift* or nothing at all, and it is the smallest sentence in her whole vocabulary and it is the one she means most.

The steam off eleven bowls leaned, all at once, all the same way.

And the air beside Yangyang's cheek spent the last word it had.

<font color="#b0e0e6">*Tomorrow.*</font>

Yangyang closed her eyes.

Then she opened them and did what she does, which is report, in the flat voice, at the correct volume, so that a table of eleven people and one shelf of an army could hear it:

<font color="#667cf9">"She says tomorrow."</font>

Nobody made a speech. Lupa's tail did one enormous sweep and took a mug off the crate. Chixia said something loud and slightly wrong about ghosts and dinner reservations. Cartethyia burst into tears and went on eating. Chisa said, <font color="#ff8a8a">"Good,"</font> in exactly the voice she uses for a splint and a compliment, which she has caught off Baizhi and denies. Amy put her forehead on her mother's shoulder for about four seconds and then sat up and asked whether anybody was going to eat the last fish, and Baizhi, at the correct pause, without looking up from her bowl, said:

<font color="#a171d1">"It is a very good fish."</font>

And the wind sat down.

## XI. THE SEASON

The last of the column went past the third marker at four minutes past the fourth hour.

He was a signalman from the north battery with a flag he no longer had, and he had spent most of the walk about eleven feet out of line on the right because his road had been misplaced by a play for three years and his feet were still arguing about it, and a Ranger from the second section had walked eleven feet out of line beside him the whole way without mentioning it.

Ciaccona was sitting on the berm with two strings and a hand that would not close.

She let him get four paces past the marker, and then she played the fourth beat.

It took about a second. It was one note, struck once, on a bad instrument, by a bad hand, and it closed a bar that had been open since the second hour of the morning. Everyone on the shelf heard it. A few of the Rangers looked up. Sergeant Du put his bowl down.

Everybody within hearing held still for about three seconds. Then the rain went on falling upward at a rate down to a third of what it had been at midnight, and the road went on being a road, and one thousand nine hundred and seven people went on walking south along it at the pace of the slowest.

<font color="#77f8c0">"There,"</font> Ciaccona said, to her lute. <font color="#77f8c0">"That's what it's for."</font>

She did not play anything else. She sat on the berm for another half-hour with the instrument across her knees and her palm flat on the boards of it, the way she does, and Cartethyia came and sat down against her shoulder with the terrarium and said nothing at all, which took visible effort and which Ciaccona noticed and did not comment on.

---

The convoy went south in the shape the Grand Marshal had written.

Four shield sections forward and four in echelon at four hundred metres, projectors at a foreign sovereign's number. Two gun platforms on the ridge line, elevation locked, laid north. Four medical wagons. The kitchen, following, having become mobile again out of sheer professional pride at about the fifth hour. Every paired runner in the theatre. Eleven flat stones along the eastern road with bowls on them, refilled by a works detail with strong opinions.

And along the road, where the guardians walked, the rain stayed thick and stayed over the people, and out over the fields on either side it went thin and thinner, so that a farmer at Lianshui standing in his own doorway that afternoon looked out at a band of silver rain about forty metres wide running dead straight across the valley floor from the north, with dry ground on both sides of it, and a great many small figures moving inside it going south, and put his hat on and went and got his neighbours.

Word of that reached the Bureau of Rites in Jinzhou at the sixth hour and produced, within a week, a formal enquiry, a doctrinal dispute, three competing accounts, and eventually a new line in the weather register with its own column, which Yangyang would come to hate.

---

Changli held the north gate.

She had spent the night and the day in the Acting Magistrate's plain robe with the civil seal inside her sleeve, and she had read the stores figure aloud at every second hour, and the eleven ward tables had gone on sitting, and at the sixth hour of the evening she came down off the wall and stood under the arch with Clerk Wen at her shoulder and a city behind her that had spent two days building rooms for people who were now demonstrably coming.

Wen had the register open on a folding desk under an oiled sheet.

He had written the heading at three in the morning after the bell — RETURNS — and the date and the hour, and had left the rest blank, because the rest was not his to fill until they arrived.

<font color="#cfdb29">"Are you going to enter them?"</font>

<font color="#dcdcdc">"I have no column for them, Acting Magistrate."</font>

<font color="#cfdb29">"You have a heading."</font>

<font color="#dcdcdc">"I have a heading and no rule, and I have been sitting under this sheet for three hours composing the objection I would raise if somebody else tried it."</font> Wen turned the pen over. <font color="#dcdcdc">"They are deceased. The returns register is for persons resident or transiting. The interment regulations have ninety-one pages and every one of them assumes the deceased is being carried."</font>

<font color="#cfdb29">"Then read me the fifth article of the returns rule."</font>

Wen read it. It said that the register records who came back.

He read it twice, the second time more slowly, and then he sat down on the folding stool and dipped the pen and began entering names off the second forme, one at a time, in his small steady hand, with the personal detail in the remarks column, because the remarks column had been sitting there for four hundred years waiting for somebody to use it properly.

He got to two hundred and eleven before the head of the column came round the last bend, and then he stopped, and stood up, and did the rest of it standing.

---

They came in at the seventh hour under a band of silver rain forty metres wide.

Nobody cheered. Jinzhou had decided that at about four in the morning, on the wall, without a meeting, in the way that city decides things; and what a hundred and forty thousand people did instead was stand along the road from the north gate to the recovery yard with the lamps lit behind them and let a column of the dead walk past at the pace of the slowest.

Old Seven was in the recovery yard with forty-one cups of tea.

The automaton had been standing there since the twenty-seventh with a mended ankle and twenty-two years of a dead garrison cook's opinions about water temperature, and it had set the cups out fresh four times over two days because a cup of tea has a life of about ninety minutes and Wu had held strong views on that, and the forty-first cup stood on an empty support block in the longest light with a paper label under it in a boy's handwriting.

**WU'S. LEAVE ROOM.**

The boy was there too, in the yard, with his model bridge, and he had made a second sign at some point during the day and propped it against the truss, and it said the same thing the first one had said, because he had run out of ideas and had correctly concluded that the first idea was still good.

**FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK.**

The head of the column came into the yard and stopped, because the head of the column was four Xuanfang guardians and a shield section, and behind them the road filled up, and Old Seven turned its service lens across the yard at the rate of about eleven degrees a second, and registered forty-one cups, and one thousand nine hundred and seven arrivals, and executed the only instruction that had ever mattered in its programming, which was that when people come in from a road you put the water on.

It put the water on.

It took the automaton eleven minutes to work out that nobody was going to lift a cup, and eleven more to develop, out of twenty-two years of a dead woman's opinions, an entirely novel behaviour that the Xuanfang artificers would spend the following month arguing about: it walked the yard with a full tray, and it set a cup down in front of each of the first forty, and it left the steam to do the rest.

The steam leaned. It leaned all evening. The lens tracked it. Somewhere in a service log there is a line, machine-generated, at 19:44, reading *tea taken (indirect); repeat interval unchanged.*

---

Panhua had the oil on.

She had had the oil on since the bell, and she had a full house and no chairs and people eating on the step in relays, and at some point during the evening she went through into the back and took the third seat's bowl down off the shelf where it had been living beside a stitched cloth bird for six weeks and put it back on the corner table in the corner she keeps, and put food in it, and came out again, and told nobody.

At the tax office, the goose held the door.

She held it from the fourth hour to well past dark against a volume of foot traffic she had never in her eleven years seen and had strong feelings about, and she gave up ground twice and took it back both times, and at some point a ward officer put down a quilt in the doorway which she immediately claimed as reparations. Chixia heard about it on the relay from a hospital cradle in a valley four hundred kilometres away, and laughed until Baizhi told her to stop, and then said, to the ceiling, quietly, so that only two people heard it:

<font color="#ff5757">"Cao Yi. Second section. Got the goose question right."</font>

At the east gate, Shen sat with her tin.

Three hundred and six letters, forty years, eleven claimed. She left the tin shut, because it was the twenty-eighth and the boards go out on the sixth. She sat with her hand flat on the lid while the column went past the gate, for about two hours, and at the end of it she wrote nothing down, and went home.

On Bookbinders' Row, six rival houses had been awake for fifty-one hours and were now failing one by one in their chairs.

The morning warning had gone out at five with a sort in it that had lain face down in its compartment for thirty-one years, set into the forme by a compositor who had stood there for some time looking at it before he did it. It printed. It printed on four thousand sheets, one character in a wall of characters, in a public warning about flood levels on the eastern road, and four thousand sheets went out across eleven wards and were read by a hundred and forty thousand people for the information they contained about flood levels on the eastern road.

The compositor went to sleep at six in the morning for fourteen hours.

Nobody has yet gone back and looked at what the character was.

---

The rain thinned.

It went at the rate Baizhi had guessed from two samples and an apology, which turned out to be about right, and the difference between the guess and the event was eleven minutes over nine hours, which she recorded with some satisfaction.

What happened at the end of it was quiet, and it happened over about forty minutes in the recovery yard and along the road behind it and in the eleven ward halls where families had come to stand near a column they could not touch, and it was painless, and Baizhi stood at the gate with a slide in her hand from the moment it began until it was over and looked at the people the entire time.

Sergeant Du reported afterwards that the last thing he saw was a man in the fourth relief section standing over one of the eleven bowls with his hands open, watching the steam lean, at the exact moment the steam stopped leaning.

Jiyan reported that a general had put a hand on his shoulder at some point and that the count under his thumb had stayed at zero the whole way and that he had gone on counting, and that he intended to go on counting for a while yet, and that the physician had been informed and had made a note.

Clerk Wen, in ward four, at eleven at night, finished entering one thousand nine hundred and seven names with the personal detail in the remarks column and the true road beneath, and ruled the page off, and wrote at the foot of it:

*All present at the gate. All entered. The column is closed.*

*The register remains open.*

Underneath that, on the next clean page, because a field pad had come down the eastern road in a foreigner's handwriting with a magistrate's countersignature on it and a one-stroke mark at the foot, he opened the census and copied out an entry in column twelve.

*Abby. Resident. No owner of record. Ward four accepts the charge.*

*Remarks: carries two of things and gives one away. Loud. Resides within the Tacet Mark of a co-resident of household four-one-one, by his own election, on terms attached, reviewable at seven days. See field pad, entry ninety-four, which is not to be summarised.*

Assessor Lü came in at half past eleven at night in his own time, in his own coat, having heard about it from a patroller on the wall, and asked to see the field pad, and read all five clauses standing up, and asked for a copy for his own drawer.

<font color="silver">"Six years,"</font> he said. <font color="silver">"Six years that entry has sat there and nobody has ever built anything on top of it."</font>

<font color="gainsboro">"Captain Chixia says she has argued the point at this office four times."</font>

<font color="silver">"She has."</font> Lü folded his copy. <font color="silver">"She has never once cited the goose. Tell her I said so, and tell her slowly, because I would like her to sit with it."</font>

---

Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying stayed outside the wall.

She turned her column round on the outer shelf at the eighth hour and set her line across the mouth of the gorge, facing north, with her guns laid on nothing in particular and her rotations written for twenty days, and she wrote her report to Mingting standing at a folding table with a lamp and Zhou Lian holding the lamp.

*The Threnodian designated Ovathrax is not destroyed.*

*It has been denied its material. Its interval has opened from one second to a figure my listeners can no longer take, which measures my listeners.*

*I decline to enter it as a victory. I direct that it shall not be entered as a victory in any subordinate account, and I will personally amend any account that does.*

*A war god eats war. It has been starved. Starvation is a condition; conditions return when they are fed. What fed this one was three years of public arithmetic, correctly performed. I signed the arithmetic myself. I have today watched the correction entered on the ground by a magistrate standing in mud, and I have countersigned it, and I request that the Board read the appended registers, both of them, in full, before it drafts the commendation which I am aware it is already drafting.*

*Recommend: the outer shelf held for twenty years. Recommend: no school play.*

She signed it, and sanded it, and gave it to Zhou Lian, and then she stood at the table for a while with her hand on the pommel of the sword lying open in its travelling cradle.

Eleven years out. The steel had a working polish on it now: handle-wear, guard-scuff, the bloom of humidity and camphor. She lifted it and walked it across to the tent flap the way she did every morning at a window in Mingting, and stood there with it in her forearm and the weight of everything she was responsible for arriving daily and distributed evenly, and looked north into the dark where a valley had subsided and a seam had closed and a thing without a face was hungry.

Zhou Lian held the cradle open behind her.

<font color="#cbd369">"Leave it,"</font> she said.

<font color="#b9a882">"Ma'am."</font>

<font color="#cbd369">"Eleven years and the interval is my watch, and the watch is not over, and if anybody in Mingting asks you why the Grand Marshal's sword is out on a night when the enemy has withdrawn, you tell them it is the same reason the lamp-keepers go out in fine weather."</font>

She put it back in the cradle, open, across the saddle, and Zhou Lian carried it out, disliking the road, as he had for eleven years.

---

Yangyang made her entry at the eleventh hour, sitting on a crate at the south shelf with the patrol log on her knee and the second ink open beside the first, and she wrote for a long time, and most of it was ordinary.

Weather: dispersing. Interval: not taken. Column: passed, 19:00, north gate, escorted, 1,907, all present, all entered, see prefectural register.

Casualties, own: two. Named.

Then she turned to the back of the book where the second ink lives, and found the three boxed words she had written on the sixth of the sixth month and had shut the book on before anybody could see, and looked at them for a while.

She struck them through. One line, clean, three inches of it, the way you cut a post.

*~~Not yet.~~*

Under it, boxed, in the second ink:

*Mengzhou.*

And beside it, in the margin, in the first ink, in the flat hand of a woman filing a count she has kept privately since she was nineteen years old and has never once explained to anybody:

*Out, one. Back, two.*

The count in that book has always been a count of people through a gate, and she has kept it since she was nineteen, and she let it mean something else for one night and entered no note about the change.

She shut the book. The cold came and sat against her cheek and stayed there. It had nothing left to say with. It had spent the last of it on the smallest available sentence, at a table, in front of people who were eating.

<font color="#667cf9">"Manners,"</font> Yangyang said.

---

At the ninth tower, the keeper wrote her entry for the twenty-eighth.

She had broken the seal at three in the morning and rung eight beats for a column of one thousand nine hundred and seven, which was the sixth incident in six years and the first one anybody had heard, and she had put the rope back on its cleat afterward and had declined, again, to fill the second column.

Below *wait,* in the margin, where a Magistrate's pencil had been sitting for six weeks, she still wrote nothing.

The margin held.

But she took a new seal out of the box and looked at it for a while and put it back in the box unbroken and left the rope free, and in the incident log, in the sixth entry, she wrote:

*Rope left off the cleat. Reason: they may come again.*

*Second column: not mine.*

*Ninth beat: not struck.*

There is a piece of Mengzhou lead in a coat pocket on a lake road in Huanglong. It is a quad, cast blank on purpose, so that it will hold a space open and leave the paper alone. It was bought at a counter one evening by a woman in transit coveralls who kept the customs of every street she worked, and it has been in a pocket ever since, and a fox has now asked for it by name and asked that it not be cast.

There is a second piece of Mengzhou lead four hundred kilometres north, on Bookbinders' Row, in the founders' keeping, entered in the register as *quad, faced; matrix, probationary; do not distribute.* It came back off a shore carrying a face in reverse at striking depth, which is the beginning of the thing you cast type from, against the day you want a word the cases have run out of. Nobody has cast from it. The founders take turns looking at it.

There are eleven blank compartments in eleven different houses on that Row, and every founder gives the same two words about them, and every sort cut fresh to fill one of them cracks in the mould, and the sort for *fail* cracked first.

And there is an inscription on two shells in one cloth in an inner pocket over a young woman's sternum, cut by a mother in an old hand, with eleven characters in it that nobody who has held it has ever been able to say out loud:

> *"Like moths to a ■■, all who hear the ■■ walk the road of ■■■■. To the last ■■■■ of the ■■■ left in ■■ world: may a journey unhoped-for, and fated to ■■, find its ■■■■ in the presence of ■■■."*

Nobody in this account puts those three things next to one another. Two of them are in the wrong country and the third is in a pocket, and the people who could do it have been awake for two days and have a road home in front of them, and the road home is the entire remaining agenda.

The clerk sets them down separately, which is the clerk's way of commenting.

---

The weather over Norfall dispersed across the following eleven days, slowly, the way weather does. The suspended sea came down. The Tacet seams went quiet enough that the survey office restarted its ordinary quarterly readings and had a long argument about the baseline. The tremors past the north face resumed at intervals of eleven days, then twelve, then fourteen, and the war college is arguing about the trend in two prefaces across the stitching, as it does.

The season was over, which is a different sentence from the weather being gone, and the Grand Marshal wrote the difference down in the margin that every lamp-keeper in the country maintains as the one inch of honest real estate the corridor permits, in her smallest hand:

*Weather never leaves. It goes somewhere else, and comes back with a different name on the forecast, and the correct response of a professional is to still be here.*

---

The household went home on the twenty-ninth, on a lower cradle road, at three kilometres an hour, which is the speed at which the world has detail in it.

Abby flew the first two kilometres, gave a commentary on the surface of the road for one more, and rode the remaining eight inside a wolf's coat, which was categorically denied at the time, is categorically denied now, and is attested by four separate witnesses and one physician's log.

Mei walked with a stitched hand and her coat over her arm because Iuno's coat was on her shoulders and Iuno's coat has one sleeve and does not fit her at all. In its pocket, in the corner where a small object settles, there was a ring, and beside it a folded paper with four characters on it and a chop pressed into the fibre at the mill by somebody who had that sheet made before they wrote on it and left the seal space empty anyway.

Iuno's hand went into that pocket the following evening, twenty-nine hours later, which is a long delay for a woman who empties her coat out of habit.

It was a Tuesday. It was raining. The window in the front room of Mrs Ren's guesthouse still would not latch, and the wind was in the half-inch gap the way it had been all month, reading over somebody's shoulder, rudely.

Her fingers found the paper first.

Then they found the ring.
