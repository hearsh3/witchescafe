# Before Supper

Mei felt the change beneath her hand. She had been counting the uneven breaths against her hair; somewhere along the way, she lost her place.

Iuno's fingers moved slowly over the back of her head. They caught in a tangle near her collar, paused, and began working it loose. Mei closed her eyes. When the fingers withdrew, she tipped her head toward them.

“You can leave that until morning,” she murmured. “I want your hand back.”

Iuno rested it against her hair again. Mei let out a breath that warmed the hollow beneath her jaw.

The room had grown dim enough for the pale parts of Iuno's shirt to stand out. Mei could see the rise of the dressing through the fabric, and below it the ordinary slope of a body she knew how to lie beside. She shifted her hand up from the torn hem. Iuno's breath caught as the palm settled between her shoulders.

“Have I found somewhere sore?”

“You've found somewhere I wanted you to touch.”

Mei lifted her face. Their noses met at an awkward angle, and she drew back just far enough to look at Iuno properly.

“Come down a little. You're making me climb.”

Iuno bent toward her.

The kiss landed at the corner of Mei's mouth. Mei turned into it, her hand coming up to Iuno's cheek, and for a moment Iuno forgot to breathe. She had been watching Mei's face so carefully that the closing of her eyes felt like being allowed to sit down.

When Mei drew back, Iuno followed her a little.

Mei saw it. Her thumb moved beside Iuno's mouth, over the place she had wiped earlier. Then she kissed her again.

Iuno's hand slipped to the back of her neck. The small weight of it brought Mei closer, until they were leaning against each other with the dressing held carefully between them. Mei's nose was cold. Her cheek was warm and damp. Iuno turned her face into it and stayed there, breathing against her skin.

“I love you, Iuiu,” Mei said. “Some days I wish you'd made it easier for me to say.”

Iuno's fingers stopped against her neck.

Mei waited. She could feel the answer being considered in the movement of Iuno's jaw, the lips parting beside her cheek. After a while, Iuno lowered her forehead to Mei's shoulder.

Mei held her there.

Outside, the porter wheeled his empty cart away. One wheel struck the same loose stone twice as he turned it, and somewhere below Mrs Ren called after him about tomorrow's delivery.

Iuno's hand began moving again, slowly, gathering the loose hair away from Mei's collar.

“I love being here with you,” she said. “I still catch myself being surprised that I get to.”

Mei closed her eyes at the sound of her voice. The hand on Iuno's back tightened, drawing the shirt into shallow folds.

They stayed like that until Mei's foot began to slide off the mattress.

She tried to recover it by shifting her hip. The movement pulled Iuno sideways; Iuno braced her hand behind them, and a sharp breath came through her teeth.

Mei straightened at once.

“We should lie down. I've been trying to fit both of us into the space left by a book.”

“The book has enjoyed an excellent afternoon.”

Iuno looked past her at the pillows. Mei followed her gaze, picked up the book, and stood with it in her hands, considering the crowded chair.

“I had a system when you came in.”

“It has moved through several promising phases.”

Mei gave her a tired, offended look. Iuno felt her own mouth soften, and the pleasure of it hurt in a place she had already spent the afternoon trying to reach.

Mei put the book on the floor against the wall. Then she took off her boots, setting them side by side beneath the chair, and came back to help Iuno with hers.

Iuno drew one foot under the bed.

“Come here first.”

Mei looked up from where she had crouched.

“Your boots are going to end up on the blanket.”

“I can get them off sitting here. I would like you beside me.”

Mei's hand rested for a moment on Iuno's knee. Then she stood and sat close enough that their thighs touched. Iuno worked at the fastening of her boot with her living hand, her brow drawing together in concentration.

The High Priestess watched the struggle with mounting interest.

*We chose these for their elegant fastenings, darling. Do remember that when you write the complaint.*

Mei leaned forward and held the leather steady. Iuno freed the clasp, and the boot came off with a small, graceless tug. They managed the second more easily.

“There,” Mei said. “We can blame the blanket on something else now.”

She put the boots beside hers and climbed onto the bed.

Iuno had learned the available positions over several nights. The dressing wanted room; the crystal wanted supporting; her back objected to the wrong angle after a few minutes and continued objecting long after she moved. She lowered herself onto her living side while Mei folded the towel beneath the edge of the pillow.

“Try that. Baizhi had it about this high yesterday.”

Iuno settled, drew a breath, and moved an inch toward the wall.

Mei lay facing her. Their knees met beneath the coverlet. She reached for Iuno's hand and brought it between them, then looked at the bandage where it disappeared under the collar.

“Will you tell me if I lean on something?”

“I will. You have a very determined elbow.”

Mei tucked it closer to herself. Iuno watched the movement and brushed her thumb across Mei's fingers.

From this close, she could see the mark the towel had left beside Mei's eye. She touched it with the back of one finger. Mei's eyelid fluttered, and she pressed her face briefly into the touch before lifting her gaze.

“I wanted this before you came upstairs,” Mei said.

Iuno's hand rested against her cheek.

“I kept thinking I could hear you on the stair. I opened the door once and frightened the man with the onions.”

“That may explain the care he took when I passed him.”

“He was carrying a sack in each arm. I offered to help, and he backed into the wall.”

A small laugh escaped Iuno. Mei smiled at it, then her eyes shifted toward the floor.

Iuno felt the smile leave her own face.

They listened. Below them, water splashed into a basin, followed by Chixia explaining something at length. Her voice rose on a familiar indignation and carried through the boards.

Mei moved closer. She kissed the place beneath Iuno's eye and rested her forehead there.

“I keep doing that,” she said.

“I've begun watching you do it.”

Mei's hand tightened around hers.

Iuno could feel how easy it would be to draw her nearer and wait until her attention returned to the bed. She had done it a moment earlier. Mei would turn toward a touch; she loved being touched. Iuno knew the places that made her close her eyes.

She left her hand where it was.

Mei lay listening until Chixia's voice fell to an ordinary volume. Then she looked back at Iuno.

“I'd like to kiss you again.”

Iuno's eyes filled so quickly that she had to blink before she could find Mei's face clearly.

“Come closer, sweetheart. I can manage the rest of the distance.”

Mei came closer.

This time Iuno felt the small hesitation as it happened: Mei's mouth still against hers, her hand flattening over the shirt, the breath held a moment too long. Iuno opened her eyes.

Mei was looking at her.

“I remembered the balcony.”

Iuno stayed still beneath her hand.

“You used to look at me like that when you thought I'd gone to sleep,” Mei said. “I'd open my eyes and you'd be watching.”

Iuno could remember the chair. The damp edge of Mei's hair against a cushion. Her own hand on a cold cup, staying until she heard somebody approach the door.

“You caught me several times.”

Mei looked down between them. Her fingers had found a button near Iuno's collar and were resting against its edge.

“I was pleased with myself. I thought I was finding out something you wanted to keep secret.”

Iuno breathed carefully. Mei's thumb moved across the button, then her hand spread against the cloth again.

“I'm glad you told me about looking back,” she said. “I keep thinking about it. I expect I'll remember it the next time I pick up my sword.”

Iuno could feel the mattress beneath her palm, rough through the sheet. She pressed her fingers into it.

Mei lifted her eyes.

“And I still wanted to kiss you just now.”

Iuno reached for the hand on her shirt. She brought it to her face and held it there, her eyes closing against Mei's fingers.

The tears went into the space between them. Mei wiped one away with her thumb, then let the next reach the pillow.

They lay facing each other while the room darkened.

After a while, Mei drew the coverlet higher. She settled her head beside Iuno's and rested her hand on her waist, keeping the cloth smooth beneath her palm. Iuno could feel the warmth of her all along the bed. When Mei moved a foot to find a comfortable place, she made room for it.

For several minutes their breathing found the same pace. Then a door opened below, and Mei took a deeper breath.

“I should get up soon.”

Iuno watched the dim outline of her face.

“Could you stay until I've got my arm under me? I would like to sit up with you.”

Mei moved back to give her room. They rose awkwardly, gathering the coverlet between them. Iuno's hair caught beneath her elbow; Mei freed it and left her hand on Iuno's back until she was settled.

The room felt cooler upright.

Mei rubbed her face with both hands, then bent for her boots. Iuno watched her fasten them. One lace had worked its way beneath the heel of the other boot, and Mei had to lift both feet before she could sort it out.

“I'll take the cup,” she said. “Are you finished with it?”

“I am. Leave the towel here; I'll put it somewhere to dry.”

Mei picked up the cup and stood. She waited beside the bed, looking down at Iuno with the handle hooked loosely over one finger.

Iuno caught the free hand and kissed the backs of her fingers. She could feel the little bones beneath the skin, the movement as Mei bent them toward her mouth.

Then she let go.

At the door, Mei turned.

“I liked being here.”

Iuno's hand rested in the warm place on the coverlet.

“I'll have the lamp lit when you come back.”

Mei nodded. She opened the door carefully, keeping the cup clear of the frame, and went into the passage.

Iuno listened to her reach the stair. The fourth board creaked; then the steps continued downward.

She took the towel to the chair and spread it over the back. The map slid to the floor when she moved the lamp, and she bent to gather it, holding her shoulder carefully. By the time she straightened, Mei had reached the room below.

Her voice came through the boards, warm and a little uncertain.

“I've been wanting to come and sit with you. Would that be all right?”

Iuno stood beside the chair, holding the map.

She waited for Yangyang's answer.
