## I. The Blind Watcher
### *Qiuyuan, on the road from Rinascita to Mingting*

The frequencies of a dead man's prayer had a color Qiuyuan could still see when he closed his eyes.

Fenrico's faith had been scarlet — arterial, saturated, the hue of a man who believed with his circulation. In the cathedral of that dying mind the scarlet had pulsed with every confession: I chose this. I would choose it again. History shall remember me. And Qiuyuan, whose Forte read the world in frequencies the way literate men read words, had stood in the red and taken the deposition and delivered the verdict — *history's truth is inscribed in stone; let those who come after judge* — and walked out carrying the First Relic and a color he could scrub off his hands and not off his retinas.

The ship from Rinascita made port at the south coast in the first week of winter. He took the overland road to Mingting because the overland road takes eleven days and eleven days was what the debrief required: one day per confession, one per frequency, one for the scar.

The scar was Geshu Lin.

The corridor had a protocol for scars. You filed them. You named the operation, named the loss, named the arithmetic: one agent's capacity weighed against one hero's survival, measured against the theater's outcome, resolved in the theater's favor. Geshu Lin had gone into the Gorges of Spirits to hold the Threnodian Ovathrax at the frontier, and the holding had held, and the holder had vanished, and the arithmetic said: *acceptable.* One general, spent. One gate, saved. The ledger balanced. The corridor filed the balance under *completed, satisfactory* and issued Qiuyuan his next deployment, and Qiuyuan had packed his kit and gone to Rinascita and done the work and come back carrying a relic and a dead man's color and the thing the corridor had no protocol for, which was:

He had seen a woman punch a god and demand her friend back, and the god had listened.

Mei. The Rover. The Arbiter. He had traced her company's story through the Avinoleum from bullet casings and residual frequencies the way he read everything — by the light things left behind — and the light she left behind was golden, and it did something to his reading that eleven days of road had failed to metabolize. Her frequency refused the arithmetic. He could see it still, overlaid on his retinas beside Fenrico's scarlet: a golden signature that said, in every room it had passed through, in every fight she had fought, in every person she had caught or carried or refused to let go of: *the loss is unacceptable.*

The corridor said: one general, spent.
The golden frequency said: *get him back.*

The corridor said: the ledger balances.
The golden frequency said: *balance this* — and it said it with a fist through a god's sternum, and the god had healed two women, and the god had died, and the dying had freed a nation, and the nation was arguing about fish now, and the fish were fresh.

On the sixth day of the road, outside a coaching inn in Chongzhou, he sat on a bench at dusk and watched the mountain pass descend toward the capital and ran the frequencies one more time.

Geshu Lin's last recorded signature: silver-white, martial, the precise hue of a blade drawn with full knowledge of the cost. The frequency had terminated at the Gorges at a timestamp the Bureau logged as *mission conclusion.* Terminated. A clean stop, the way a candle stops: one moment present, the next, past tense.

Qiuyuan had believed it. The Bureau's instruments were excellent. The instruments said: gone. The corridor said: acceptable. The agent filed the assessment and signed his name and the name was the last act of his obedience and the first act of something else, and the something else was this:

In Rinascita, a woman had been told her daughter was gone — lost to the Nihility, dissolved into the void, measured absent by every instrument the city owned — and she had gone in after her. She had dragged six women and a shrine maiden and a giant into the end of the world, and at the bottom of everything she had found a girl sitting with her knees to her chest asking not to be saved, and she had held out her hand and said *jump,* and the girl had jumped, and the catch had been heard on the surface, and the instruments had revised their readings.

The instruments revised their readings.

Qiuyuan sat on the bench at dusk in Chongzhou and thought about instruments. The Bureau's instruments were excellent. The Bureau's instruments had said Geshu Lin was terminated, and the corridor had filed the termination, and the filing was the protocol, and the protocol was the state, and the state's arithmetic said *acceptable,* and Qiuyuan had signed.

And in Rinascita, a woman's arithmetic had said *unacceptable,* and she had gone into the void, and the void had revised.

The question the road was metabolizing — the one that would arrive at the capital with him, and sit in his apartment in the dark, and be the rest of the protocol — was whether Geshu Lin's frequency had terminated, or whether the instruments had simply stopped being good enough.

He ordered tea at the inn. The innkeeper brought jasmine, which was wrong — he drank longjing, always, the habit of a man whose rituals were the architecture of his concentration — and he drank the jasmine anyway, because an Arbiter across the sea had served Earl Grey from a portable porcelain service to a prophet who hated Earl Grey, and both of them had drunk it anyway, and the drinking of the wrong tea had been the whole theology.

On the eighth day he composed his report. The report was excellent. It contained the frequencies, the depositions, the relic's provenance, and the verdict on Fenrico, and it was filed under *completed, satisfactory,* and the corridor accepted it, and the filing was the protocol.

In the margin of the report — the margin that was his, that Agent Qiu Han in a different wing of the same building kept for different reasons, that every honest officer in the corridor maintained as the one unlicensed inch of real estate they owned — he wrote four characters:

未結案。 *Case open.*

The case was Geshu Lin.

The margin was the beginning.

## II. The Auditor
### *Censorate dispatch, Mingting to Jinzhou, re: anomalous filings*

> **CENSORATE — ORDER OF AUDIT.**
> *Subject: the Magistrate of Jinzhou. Nature of inquiry: fiscal and doctrinal irregularities, cross-referenced from garrison filings, census amendments, and one (1) foreign-origin confectionery expense logged under 'sundry, shrine.' The auditor is instructed to assess, report, and recommend.*

Jiang Ru arrived at Jinzhou's south gate on the noon shuttle, between a crate of persimmons and the mail, and disliked the city on professional principle before the gate guard finished checking her seal.

The city smelled of fish and lake water and military discipline, which was acceptable, and also of something the Censorate manual calls *atmospheric deviation,* which the manual defines as *the quality of a jurisdiction whose filings do not match its practice,* and which Jiang Ru had learned, over eight years of audit work, to detect the way other women detect perfume: from across the room, before introductions.

The file on Magistrate Jinhsi ran forty pages. Jiang Ru had read it twice on the shuttle and marked it in four colors. Blue for fiscal, red for doctrinal, green for structural, and — her own addition, unauthorized, the auditor's version of the agent's margin — amber for *interesting.*

The amber sections were:

One. The sluice-song. A foreign mnemonic, Septimont origin, taught to the garrison and the ward schools for evacuation purposes. Filed under *civil defense, cultural exchange.* The war college's objection had been overruled by General Jiyan, whose overruling authority derived from the Sentinel Jue's direct appointment, which superseded Mingting's standard general selection process, which the Censorate had flagged twice and which had remained, both times, beyond audit jurisdiction, because the Sentinel's appointments were classified *meteorological.* The Censorate had stopped flagging meteorological items. The Censorate flagged everything else.

Two. The fourth rank. The Magistrate had proposed — and implemented, without waiting for the proposal's return through channels — a fourth tier in the Resonator classification system. 民用, *civil service.* Below the combat tiers. Rated for healers, tide-callers, mnemonic singers, a dairyman whose Forte sweetened milk. The fiscal impact was modest. The doctrinal impact was: the youngest gate in Huanglong had declared, by administrative fiat, that a nation which sorted its people by their relevance to the worst day was missing the people who made the other days livable.

Three. The dam amendment. Contingency Nine, revised. Two hundred breaths. A lower-city-born hand on the sluice. The amendment had been sealed with the Magistrate's chop and forwarded to the Secretariat and the Secretariat had forwarded it to the Emperor's office and the Emperor's office had co-signed it, and the Censorate, arriving six months after the co-signature, was auditing a document that had already been approved by every authority it answered to.

Jiang Ru climbed the yamen steps with her file and her four inks and found the Magistrate at the morning report, flanked by her general and the general's adjutant, in a room whose furnishings said *frontier* in every particular — the iron inkstand, the map table, the operational wall — and whose single decorative item was a teapot.

The teapot was Huanglong standard, blue glaze, government issue. Beside it, in a cloth sleeve, a second pot: foreign, porcelain, painted with camellias.

"Auditor Jiang." Jinhsi stood. She had the bearing of a woman who had been expecting this visit and had decided to greet it standing. "Tea?"

"I'm on duty."

"So am I. The duty serves tea." She poured from the government pot. "The Censorate's order references anomalous filings. I've prepared a briefing. The anomalies are documented, contextualized, and filed in proper order. You'll find I've made your audit as easy as the audit permits."

Jiang Ru sat. She drank the tea. It was good. The goodness was suspicious — government tea is, by specification, adequate — and she said so.

"The ward schools have been experimenting with the leaves," Jinhsi said. "A teacher in the terrace district has a Forte that improves drying temperatures. Class C. Fourth rank. She contributes to civic supply."

"You're auditing me."

"I'm briefing you. The difference is the tea."

Jiang Ru opened the file. Blue, red, green, amber. The amber was the problem. The amber was where the anomalies lived, and the anomalies were where the city had stopped being Mingting's jurisdiction and started being its own argument, and the argument was the thing the Censorate had sent her to measure, and the measuring was her craft, and her craft said: the anomalies are consistent. The sluice-song works. The fourth rank fills a gap. The dam amendment has been co-signed. Every deviation from doctrine points in the same direction, and the direction is: away from the corridor.

"The confectionery expense," Jiang Ru said. "Filed under 'sundry, shrine.' Explain."

"The Academy President in Lahai-Roi sends sour candy to our infirmary. Our infirmary nurse stocks it for ranking-season patients. The expense is negligible and the filing is accurate: the candy is kept at a shrine to the proposition that a child who has burned herself exceeding her own limits deserves to eat something sour and remember the cost."

Jiang Ru looked at the Magistrate. The Magistrate looked back. The room held the look the way the dam holds the lake: by engineering, under pressure, at a cost both parties understood.

"The Censorate will recommend review," Jiang Ru said. "The filings are irregular."

"The filings are honest."

"These are different qualities, Magistrate."

"They are. One of them keeps a city standing. One of them keeps a corridor orderly." Jinhsi refilled the cup. "Auditor. You've read the dam amendment. You've seen the drill timetable. You've heard the children singing the sluice-song in the ward schools. Let me ask you one question, and then the briefing is yours."

Jiang Ru waited.

"When the water comes — and it comes, Auditor; we are the youngest gate; the water always comes — who would you rather have at the sluice? An officer whose filing is regular, or an officer whose filing is honest?"

The tea was good. The goodness was the argument.

Jiang Ru drank it. She made her notes. She filed her preliminary assessment that evening, in four inks, and the amber column — the one the manual had no code for, the one she had invented herself, eight years ago, because the world kept producing things the manual's three colors missed — read:

*The Magistrate is irregular. The irregularity is structural. The structure holds. Recommend: observation. Duration: open.*

She underlined *open.* She did this with the same gesture Agent Qiu Han used when she kept a file thin, and Keeper Pei used when he trimmed a lamp he had already trimmed. The gesture of a person who understood the corridor's length and had decided, for today, to be one lamp longer than the corridor required.

## III. Falling Rain
### *the Grand Marshal's correspondence, kept in a sword's shadow*

The sword Falling Rain had been unsheathed for eleven years, and this was, the Grand Marshal had been told, a metaphor.

She was sixty-one. She had commanded armies since twenty-nine, when the last Grand Marshal died holding a bridge and the Emperor had looked down the roster and found her name at the bottom, which was where the competent hid, and she had been hiding competently since. The sword was her predecessor's. The tradition said: unsheathe it on appointment, sheathe it at retirement or death, and the interval is your watch. Eleven years. The steel had taken on a working polish — handle-wear, guard-scuff, the bloom of humidity and camphor — and some mornings, when she lifted it from the stand and walked it to the window, she felt it in her forearm the way the lake-dragon must feel the water: as the weight of everything she was responsible for, distributed evenly, arriving daily.

"Falling Rain" was the sword's name. Her own name was Li Zhaoying, and she kept it the way keepers keep lamps: trimmed and functional, in a room without ornament.

The dispatch from Rinascita sat on her desk beside the morning's reports, and she had read it the way she read battlefields — by the negative space, by what the filing left out, by the shape of the absence between the documented facts.

Documented: Qiuyuan, ISA, deployed to Rinascita at her direction. Documented: Fenrico's deposition, the First Relic, the verdict. Documented: the company, the "pack," six women, their names and Fortes and their operational record, which read like poetry and scanned like nothing in the war college library.

Undocumented: the thing about them that had made her send Qiuyuan in the first place.

She had been in the field office when the western dispatches first came through, three years ago. A routine monitoring report, Rinascita section, one paragraph: *a castaway, amnesiac, has toppled the Order of the Deep with five companions. The Sentinel Imperator has dissolved.* She had circled the paragraph and put it in the reading stack and forgotten it for a week, and then, at three in the morning, sleepless over a supply requisition, the paragraph had come back and sat on her desk and refused to leave.

*With five companions.*

Not with an army. Not with a mandate. Not with an institution, a budget, a doctrine, a corridor, or a filing system. With five companions, and the companions were a bard, a gladiator, a poisoner, a knight, and a seer, and the seer had erased her own memory and the knight had been sealed in a tower for twenty years and the gladiator had been abandoned by her family and the bard had been exiled for telling the truth, and the poisoner had killed her own predecessor because the alternative was worse. Every one of them damaged. Every one of them, by the war college's rating system, a liability. And together they had walked into a theocracy and dismantled it from the inside out, and the god itself — four thousand years old, chained, corrupted — had spent its last strength on them, and the spending had been the god's first free act in a generation, and the act had freed a nation.

Li Zhaoying had set down the supply requisition and picked up the dispatch and read it three more times and then written, in her smallest hand, in the margin her office permitted:

*She takes great interest in your pack.*

The *she* was herself. The *interest* was professional. The margin was wider than the ISA's.

What she had seen in the dispatch — the thing the war college could not scan, the thing the filing system had no drawer for — was a unit type she had spent thirty years looking for and had never found in her own army. A company held together by trust. Six women who had earned each other at the full price and kept earning, who fought for each other because fighting for each other was the only strategy they had, and who had discovered, by accident or by grace, that the strategy scaled.

She had commanded divisions. She had held walls. She had signed the orders that sent Geshu Lin to the Gorges and signed the assessment that called the outcome *acceptable,* and the signing had been correct, and the correctness had tasted, in her mouth, the way she imagined Qiuyuan's iron tasted in his: like a coin spent on the right purchase at the wrong price.

The Rover's company did not sign those orders. The Rover's company went into the void and pulled people out.

This was tactically absurd. It was strategically unsound. It violated every principle the war college taught, and every principle Li Zhaoying enforced, and every line of the arithmetic that kept the youngest gate standing. The acceptable loss was the gate's foundation. You spent the one to save the many. The many stayed standing because the one was spent. The arithmetic balanced because the arithmetic was built to balance, and the building was the corridor, and the corridor was the state.

And a woman in Rinascita had refused the arithmetic, and the refusal had saved a god, and the saving had freed a country, and the country was arguing about fish, and the fish were fresh.

Li Zhaoying poured her tea — longjing, always, the habit of a woman whose rituals were the architecture of her command — and composed her reply to Qiuyuan's report. The reply was brief, and it went through channels, and the channels were the corridor, and the corridor was the protocol:

*Report received. Assessment: satisfactory. The relic is secured. Resume standard deployment.*

And in the margin — the margin that was hers, that every lamp-keeper in the country maintained as the one inch of honest real estate the corridor permitted — she wrote:

*The Magistrate's amendment reaches me. Two hundred breaths. A hand on the sluice that hates to turn. The war college calls it sentimental. The war college has never stood at the sluice.*

*The Rover's company will arrive in Huanglong. When they do, see that they are met. Teapot. Bench. The Mingting protocol: 'the kettle is on.'*

*I would like to see what an unacceptable arithmetic looks like, applied to our wall.*

She sealed the margin note separately, in the personal envelope the Grand Marshal's office maintained for communications outside the corridor — a privilege granted by the Emperor, revocable at pleasure, used sparingly, kept like a lamp. She addressed it to no one in particular and filed it where filed margins go: into the custody of the sword, which is the shadow the light keeps, which is the watch, which is eleven years old, and counting.

## IV. The Dairyman
### *a minor testimony, filed under the fourth rank*

His name was Lo Sheng, and his Forte made milk sweet, and the classification of this gift had consumed, to date, three agricultural inspectors, one Censorate sub-auditor, and the whole of his local magistrate's lunch hour.

"It's cheese," he said to the sub-auditor, for the fourth time, at his own kitchen table. "Good cheese. The cows are normal. The feed is normal. I milk them, and the milk stays sweet. It's been doing this for twelve years."

"The application says *Forte, civil, unclassified.* The form requires a description."

"Sweet milk."

"For the technical specification—"

"*Sweet. Milk.*"

He had been the second person in his valley to register under the fourth rank, after the tide-caller and before the grandmother who could find water with her feet. The registration office was a desk in the garrison annex in the county seat, staffed by a young woman who had introduced herself as the frontier outreach clerk and who had processed his form with the specific gentleness of someone who understood that the form was the first time the state had said *you count* in a language that fit.

She had boxed him CIVIL and written in the margin: *Lo Sheng. Forte: affinity for casein proteins under Resonator expression. Tactical value: nil. Nutritional value: considerable. Note: the cheese is exceptional.*

The sub-auditor from Mingting, arriving three months later to review the fourth-rank filings, had read the margin note and made the face that capital people make when they encounter language that works and cannot be regulated.

Lo Sheng offered him cheese. The sub-auditor ate it. The eating was the audit. By the time the second round arrived — the good stuff, the aged wheel, the one the mountain inn had been buying since his mark surfaced — the sub-auditor was writing in his report and eating with his left hand, and the writing and the eating were arriving at the same conclusion, which Lo Sheng could have told him three hours ago if the form had a box for it:

Some Fortes hold the wall. Some Fortes hold the lunch. Both holdings are load-bearing, and the lunch, on a given Tuesday, saves more lives.

He walked the sub-auditor to the shuttle and gave him a wheel for the road. The sub-auditor accepted it with the gravity of a man accepting evidence, and Lo Sheng watched the shuttle climb the pass toward the capital carrying a round of cheese that would arrive on somebody's desk in the Censorate and be eaten, and the eating would be the strongest argument the fourth rank had yet produced, and the argument would taste of grass and patience and a mark on a man's wrist that the state had spent forty years calling *inert* and his cows had spent twelve years calling *delicious.*

## V. The Inscription
### *graffiti on the inner wall of the sluice-gate housing, Jinzhou dam, found during maintenance, author unknown*

七聲到石拱，
高路港區行，
低路窟區過，
奶奶說你好，
風兒也問安。

*the high road for the harbor wards,*
*the low road past the pit,*
*Gran says hi,*
*the wind says hello.*

> **FINAL MARGIN.**
>
> *The country's arithmetic runs in two columns. One is the corridor's: the acceptable loss, the balanced ledger, the filed protocol. The other is the margin's: the open case, the amber ink, the cheese, the sluice-song, the child's nail on the dam's stone.*
>
> *The corridor is four hundred years old. The margin is older. The margin is as old as the first keeper who trimmed a lamp past the need for trimming, the first agent who kept a file thin, the first magistrate who stood at the water and asked a different question.*
>
> *One column says: the loss is acceptable.*
> *The other says: prove it.*
>
> *The proof is a woman in a foreign city, punching a god. The proof is a man on a bench in Chongzhou, drinking the wrong tea. The proof is a Grand Marshal's sword, eleven years unsheathed, casting a shadow wide enough to hold a margin.*
>
> *The proof is a wind, heading west, carrying a letter, spending her last sorts on the fare.*
>
> *The arithmetic is changing. The corridor knows. The corridor has always known. The lamps are still changed on the hour, in soft shoes, and the mirror at the midpoint still shows you your own face — but the face, lately, has been asking questions the corridor can hear, and the corridor, lately, has been one lamp longer than it used to be, and the lamp leans east, and the leaning is a word the corridor has spent four hundred years filing under weather, and the weather, it turns out, has opinions.*
>
> *Filed under: the acceptable margin. Case: open. Duration: however long the tea stays warm.*
