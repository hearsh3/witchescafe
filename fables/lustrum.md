### being the household return of a party of seven at the youngest gate, and the disposal of what the city had no column for
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

*The tide comes level and both banks widen; the wind sits fair and one sail hangs. … Where will the letter home be delivered? With the geese going back, somewhere near Luoyang.*

---

> **ON THE WORD.**
>
> ***Lustrum*** *has three meanings, and the third one spoils the dignity of the other two, which is why it is the useful word.*
>
> *First: the rite that closes a census. When the counting was done, the censors led the offering three times around the whole assembled people — every citizen standing in one field, in the open, counted — and then made the sacrifice, and the count was closed. The verb for it is* condere, *which is also the verb for founding a city and for putting a thing away safe. A count that has not been walked around is a count that is still going on.*
>
> *Second: the interval. Five years, from one closing to the next. It is the unit in which a state measures how long it has been since it last looked at itself, and every language that keeps such a unit has discovered independently that the honest figure is longer than the official one.*
>
> *Third: a den. The place an animal lies up between huntings — a wallow, a haunt, a form pressed into the grass by something heavy that means to come back to it.*
>
> *The ward offices of Huanglong keep the first sense in their instructions and the second in their budget. They keep the third without knowing they keep it, in the column where they write down who sleeps where.*

---

> **[Ward schedule 4, sixth month. Enumerator's instruction, issued over the Magistrate's chop, clause C in Her Excellency's own pencil.]**
>
> *A. The count opens on the first and closes when it closes.*
>
> *B. Enumerators will take the return at the household, in the household's hearing, and will read it back before it is signed. A return read in an office is a rumour.*
>
> *C. The first census after any dark is of who came back. Column* 返 *is added this year and is to be completed for every household. Where the enumerator cannot establish an origin, the enumerator will complete column* 返 *anyway.*
>
> *D. Column twelve stands as before.*
>
> *E. Nobody is to be counted twice. Everybody is to be counted.*

## I. 戶 — HOUSEHOLDS

The *Lucky Bird* got two hundred metres into the city of Jinzhou and stopped, because Jinzhou is built up a rock in nine terraces and the road turns into a stair at the second one.

There is a bollard at the foot of that stair. It is granite, it is waist-high, it has stood there since the rebuilding, and the whole harbor ward turned out at eleven in the morning on the thirtieth of the fifth month to watch a twelve-tonne apricot-and-tooth-yellow decommissioned mobile dental surgery arrive in front of it and find out the truth about it.

Chisa put the handbrake on and sat back.

<font color=coral>"That's a wall."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"There's a whole *gap*, look at the gap, we've done worse than that gap in Shimen—"</font>

<font color=coral>"Granite one side, shrine the other, and four centimetres of mirror I'd like to keep."</font>

Cartethyia had the window down and was conducting negotiations with four separate people at once, which is what happens to a fluent speaker in a crowd, and the four separate people were all telling her the same thing in four registers of politeness, and the thing was: *you cannot bring that up there.*

<font color=skyblue>"They say we can't bring it up there."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I had gathered."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"They're being *very* nice about it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"They're being nice because there's a votive on the back,"</font> said Lupa, from the roof rack, where she had been since the dam road. <font color=indianred>"Half this street's already put its hands together at us."</font>

That was accurate.

The tarp had worked a corner loose in the morning wind on the descent, and the corner of a white ceramic wing had showed under it, and by the time the truck reached the second terrace the whole lakefront had decided, city-wide and without a meeting, that the foreigners had brought a votive.

On the bonnet, by then, there were: four loquats. A bundle of incense in a paper sleeve. Two plums. A child's drawing of a bird, weighed down by a stone the child had also brought. And one thousand-cash note folded into a triangle, which Iuno found at the base of the wiper and looked at for some seconds with an expression Amy afterward described as *the face you make when the bank apologises.*

<font color=royalblue>"Somebody has tipped us."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Somebody's tipped the *angel.*"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The angel is nineteen and lives in my house."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"—is standing *right here*, has ears, and would like it noted that the money's hers."</font>

---

Patroller Captain Chixia arrived at eleven minutes past, off the muster yard, at a trot, with her hair coming out of its tie and a whistle in her fist that she had already decided against using.

She reached the bollard and took the situation in one long sweep — truck, stair, crowd, wing — and did the thing that had made her a captain at an age which still annoys two people in the barracks.

*"Right,"* she said, to the street. *"Whose is the fruit."*

Nobody claimed the fruit. Nobody was ever going to claim the fruit.

*"Then it's mine now and it's going on the sill at the ward office, and if it's yours you know where the ward office is."* She swept the bonnet into her arms in one pass, handed the drawing back to the child unread, kept the stone, and pointed her chin up the stair. *"Freight apron, dam side. Only flat in the ward that'll take her and it's got a rail and a tap. Go round the harbor road, past the second landing, red gate, and ignore the sign on it."*

Cartethyia translated at about ninety percent.

<font color=royalblue>"What is on the sign?"</font>

Chixia looked at Iuno properly for the first time — one-armed, crystal, tea-service posture, foreign, patient — and made a decision about her in under a second, which is how Chixia makes all of them.

*"No unauthorised vehicles."*

<font color=royalblue>"And we are authorised."</font>

*"You are now."*

<font color=royalblue>"By whom."</font>

*"Me. Loudly. In front of witnesses."* Chixia hoisted the fruit. *"I'll write it up after lunch and it'll be fine, and if it isn't fine I want it minuted that it was me and none of you."*

There was a pause while that went through Cartethyia and came out the other side.

Lupa dropped off the roof rack onto the cobbles, walked over, put a wrapped packet on top of the fruit in Chixia's already-full arms, and walked back to the truck.

Chixia stood in the middle of the harbor ward holding four loquats, two plums, a bundle of incense, a stone, and a packet of something warm.

*"...What is it?"*

<font color=indianred>"Pork."</font>

*"Why?"*

<font color=indianred>"You were carrying stuff."</font>

*"That's—"* Chixia turned to Cartethyia. *"Is that a reason?"*

<font color=skyblue>"With her? That's the whole reason. That's her at maximum."</font>

Chixia looked at the packet a moment longer than she needed to.

Then she yelled at the crowd to clear the stair, and led a truck round the harbor road at a walking pace with her arms full of other people's fruit, and never once looked back to check that it was following — which Chisa noticed from the driver's seat and filed, because Chisa reads people by what their shoulders decline to do.

---

The freight apron on the dam side is flat, drained, about the size of a threshing floor, and has a standpipe, a rail, and a view straight down forty kilometres of water.

They put the *Lucky Bird* nose-out at the rail, and Amy went up the side and unlashed the tarp properly and folded it, because half-covered is worse than either, and four metres of white ceramic angel knelt on the sample bed in the sun with her flight blades folded flat in two descending ranks and the acid green showing at the shoulder.

The whole apron went quiet.

Then a man from the tobacco shed opposite crossed the road, put a plum on the tailgate, and went back, and that settled it, and the afternoon proceeded.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I've been a votive before."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I know."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm saying I've *done* this. I know what week two looks like."</font> Amy sat down on the folded tarp with her elbows on her knees. <font color=mistyrose>"Week one it's plums. Week two somebody's grandmother has a bad night and comes down here at three in the morning and talks to a *machine* about her *son*, and I'm asleep four hundred metres away in a rented bed, and then it's mine. It just is. It doesn't ask."</font>

Mei sat down on the tarp next to her and put her boots out.

<font color=moccasin>"What do you want to do?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I don't know yet."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Okay."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That's it? *Okay?*"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You said a real thing, so I took it."</font> Mei bumped her shoulder. <font color=moccasin>"Tell me when you know. I'll carry something."</font>

Amy leaned into the bump and stayed leaning.

Behind them the air came up over the parapet and went along the whole length of the apron once, unhurried, the way somebody walks a room they intend to be in for a while, and the corner of the folded tarp lifted and settled, and forty kilometres of lake went on taking the sky.

---

Assessor Lü came at half past three with a satchel, a folding stool, and the numbered schedules for ward four.

She was twenty-nine. She had walked up from the tax office in the harbor ward. The ward office had sent her specifically, on the grounds that she had form: it was Lü who had made the entry six years ago that put a goose into the census of the city of Jinzhou, in a week she declines to discuss, and the entry had stood, and had since been cited twice.

She set the stool down four paces from the tailgate, sat on it, and looked up at four metres of kneeling angel for exactly as long as she required.

Then she opened the satchel and got out the schedule and a brush.

*"Household return. Ward four, freight apron. I read it back before you sign."* She inked the brush. *"Who speaks?"*

Cartethyia put her hand up like a schoolgirl, which she was aware of, and did anyway.

*"Good. Slowly, please. I write in the old hand and it takes a moment."* Lü did not look up. *"How many stoves?"*

<font color=skyblue>"...Sorry?"</font>

*"Stoves. Fires. Places where food is cooked and shared."* She still did not look up. *"A household is a stove. Everything else on this form is decoration."*

Cartethyia turned round to the tailgate, where seven people were variously sitting, standing and lying.

<font color=skyblue>"She wants to know how many stoves."</font>

<font color=indianred>"One."</font>

<font color=teal>"There are two burners."</font>

<font color=indianred>"There's one *stove.* The second burner does rice, it's mine, it doesn't count."</font>

<font color=coral>"I cook on it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"On sufferance. Mine. That's a *loan*—"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"One,"</font> said Iuno.

Lü wrote it down.

*"Good. Then I spend the rest of the afternoon on people instead of arithmetic. Head of household?"*

Cartethyia rendered it.

The tailgate went quiet in a specific way.

Six people looked at Mei, in the manner of six people who have been looking at the same person for two years. Mei looked at the lake, and then at Iuno, and then very obviously at the lake again.

Lü lifted the brush clear of the paper so it would not blot, and waited with the patience of a woman who has taken four thousand returns and knows this exact silence by its shape.

*"Different question,"* she said. *"When there is trouble at a counter, which of you goes?"*

The answer came out of five mouths at once, and it was the same answer, and Amy's arrived a half-second ahead of everyone else's, because Amy is fast and because she had been waiting all afternoon for a chance to make her aunt sit still and receive something.

Iuno, on the tailgate, went entirely quiet.

*Well,* said the High Priestess, in the small warm room behind her sternum. *There you are, darling. Thirty years capturing rooms from the shadows, and the trick turns out to be standing in a car park while they vote.*

*Be quiet.*

*I shall not. I have been waiting for this since Kelvet.*

*"Name,"* said Lü.

<font color=royalblue>"Iuno Caecilia Severa."</font>

*"...Say that at the speed of a person dictating, and not at the speed of a person accustomed to being obeyed."*

<font color=royalblue>"...Iuno. Caecilia. Severa."</font>

*"Thank you."*

---

It took an hour and ten minutes, because Lü read every line back, and because Cartethyia had to carry three different ideas of family across two languages without editorialising, which she did, mostly, at a cost the others failed to see until later.

Chisa's card was of the old municipal issue from a city that had been shut for twenty years, and was verified by telephone in nine minutes — four hundred percent faster than the frontier had managed, a fact Chisa stated aloud in a level voice, causing Lü to make a small pleased mark in the margin of her own schedule that had nothing to do with the census.

Cartethyia was entered twice on one line, one person and two presentations, under a rubric Lü found in the back of the instruction book. It exists for actors and for the priesthood and nobody in ward four had used it in eleven years.

<font color=skyblue>"Which one goes first?"</font>

*"Whichever one buys the vegetables."*

<font color=skyblue>"...That's me."</font>

*"Then that one goes first."*

Amy gave her registered name and three others unprompted, with dates, on the theory that a form should be fed properly; and Lü wrote all four into the remarks column and then read all four back to her in order, flatly, without comment. And Amy — who had spent a decade being one name for a city and had jumped out of it with her arms wide — heard the four of them said out loud in a stranger's clerical voice on a freight apron in the sun, and had to go and stand at the rail for a minute.

The wind at the rail came off the water and crossed her face at the old spring pressure and went along the parapet.

<font color=mistyrose>"Yeah, yeah,"</font> Amy told it. <font color=mistyrose>"I'm fine. Go on."</font>

Then Lü reached the relation column.

*"Relation to head of household. I take them in the order I have them."* She dipped the brush. *"The wolf."*

<font color=indianred>"Pack."</font>

Cartethyia opened her mouth to build something and Lü held up two fingers.

*"Give me the word she said. Leave the sentence you were about to make."*

<font color=skyblue>"...There's a word for a hunting group that eats together and leaves nobody."</font>

*"We have one of those. It is used of soldiers and of thieves and it will do."* She wrote it. *"The musician."*

<font color=teal>"Household. Put household and don't make me be clever about it, it's been a long road."</font>

*"The one with the scissors."*

<font color=coral>"Same."</font>

*"The knight."*

<font color=skyblue>"Oh! Um. I live there, and I'm the one who reads the signs, and she taught me how to hold a pen properly when I was doing it like this—"</font> Cartethyia demonstrated, gripping an invisible pen in a fist, <font color=skyblue>"—and she never once made a face about it."</font>

Lü wrote for some time.

*"The girl."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Niece."</font> A beat, in which Amy came back from the rail and sat down on the tailgate and looked at nobody. <font color=mistyrose>"Put niece."</font>

*Oh,* said the High Priestess.

*Later.*

*Darling, she said it in a foreign language, to a stranger, in a box, where a clerk can read it out in a court in forty years. That brat has never done a single thing by accident in her life.*

*I know.*

*"And the last one,"* said Lü.

Mei was sitting at the corner of the tailgate with her heels knocking the step and both hands round a cup she had carried for an hour without drinking.

<font color=moccasin>"Put whatever fits."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Put it in her words,"</font> said Iuno.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuiu—"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"You will get one of these every ten years for the rest of your life, and this is the first one, and I would like the first one to be true."</font> Iuno had her hand flat on the tailgate. <font color=royalblue>"Say the thing. I'll sit here."</font>

Mei turned the cup a quarter-turn on the wood, settling it.

<font color=moccasin>"She's the one who named me."</font>

Cartethyia rendered it. Lü's brush stopped for the length of one breath, which in that woman is an earthquake, and then went on.

She wrote two characters in the relation column. They are flat and legal and mean *co-resident*, and she would have written them for two carters sharing a loft.

Then she moved the brush to the remarks column and wrote for considerably longer than two characters, and left that part unread, and closed the schedule.

*"Your copy is at the ward office in nine days."*

<font color=royalblue>"May I ask what went into the remarks."</font>

*"You may ask at the ward office in nine days."* Lü stood and folded the stool. *"It is my column. Everybody gets one. It is the only part of this form that has ever been of the slightest use to anybody, and the Bureau has been trying to abolish it since before I was born."*

---

Lodging took until dark and was harder than the census, for the reason Iuno had been dreading since the dam road, which is that Jinzhou had already decided to be generous.

The guesthouse above the east market had four rooms and took them without a deposit. The proprietor, a widow named Ren, with a bad hip and a very good ledger, quoted a rate at the door — and then, halfway through Cartethyia's translation, looked past all of them at the freight apron where a white wing was holding the last of the light, and revised the rate to nothing.

<font color=royalblue>"No."</font>

*"It's my house."*

<font color=royalblue>"It is your house and this is my household and we are a family with a truck."</font>

*"You brought her up the dam road at a walk. The whole ward saw it."*

<font color=royalblue>"Because the road is bad and she weighs two tonnes."</font>

Mrs Ren looked at Iuno for a moment with the particular attention of a woman assessing a bolt of cloth by feel.

*"Then you'll take the two upstairs rooms free and pay for the two down,"* she said, *"because upstairs gets the noise off the market and I've not let them in a year. Argue with me about downstairs and I'll take the lot off you and you can sleep in your truck like a crew."*

There was a silence.

<font color=teal>"Take it,"</font> said Ciaccona, from the stairs, without opening her eyes. <font color=teal>"She's a better negotiator than you, she does it in fewer words, and I want a bed."</font>

Iuno took it.

Then she walked down to the ward office in the dark, found it open, because ward offices in a census month are open, presented herself to a duty clerk who had been having a slow evening, and asked to have the two upstairs rooms formally assessed at the schedule rate for the ward, in writing — so that the amount forgone by Mrs Ren could be entered as a remission and claimed by Mrs Ren against her own assessment.

It took forty minutes. The duty clerk had to fetch a book.

*"Nobody does this,"* he said at the end of it, stamping.

<font color=royalblue>"Somebody should. She is out of pocket for a kindness, and I decline to be the reason a woman with a bad hip subsidises a foreigner."</font>

*"...I'll have to explain this to my supervisor."*

<font color=royalblue>"I'll write you the sentence."</font>

She wrote him the sentence. He read it twice. He put it in the file.

---

The lamps came on while she was walking back up through the east market — from the north side first, because the switching at Jinzhou is done by hand and has been for eighty years — and the gold ran down the terraces one street at a time at the pace of a man with a pole, and Iuno stopped in a lane with vegetable water running past her boots and watched a city light itself by muscle.

*Well, darling,* said the High Priestess.

*Yes.*

*You are the head of a household of seven in a walled town four thousand kilometres from anywhere you have ever held power, you have just spent forty minutes arguing a landlady's tax down on her behalf, and you have the face of a woman who has been handed a present she cannot lift.*

*Yes.*

*Say the good part.*

Iuno stood in the lane and let the lamps come on around her.

*They all said the same name. At the same time. Without deciding to.*

*Yes they did.*

*I have engineered senates. I have never once been elected to anything.*

*You still haven't, darling.* The glass turned a quarter-turn by the stem. *That was worse. That was affection.*

## II. 名 — NAMES

The east gate of Jinzhou faces the terraces and takes the morning first, and under the outer arch, in the strip of shade the arch keeps until nine, there is a stall with a cloth awning, a low table, a stool for the customer and a stool for the writer, four brushes, a stone, an ink cake, a brick to hold the paper flat, and a woman who has been writing other people's correspondence there for forty years.

Her name is Shen. Nobody uses it. The ward calls her the gate writer, and the schedule of charges is on a board repainted so many times that the numbers stand proud of the wood.

She works mornings. She is impossible after eleven, on account of her eyes, and has never pretended otherwise, and there is a queue by seven.

On the third of the sixth month, at eight in the morning, in the second week of the plum rains, with the arch dripping, a twelve-tonne apricot-and-tooth-yellow truck came round the outer wall and stopped six metres from her table, and a woman got out of it and stood there.

Shen finished the letter she was on. It was for a man who wanted to tell his brother that the roof was fine. She charged him for eleven characters, read it back, and sent him away.

Then she looked at the truck.

Then she got up off her stool — which the queue noticed, because she does not — and walked over to the cab door and stood in front of five black characters on black enamel and read them.

She read them at the pace of a person reading a lintel. Then she read them again at the pace of a person reading a name.

*"Who wrote this?"*

Cartethyia, at the front bumper, translated.

<font color=moccasin>"I did."</font>

*"When."*

<font color=moccasin>"Over about a year. One at a time. My hand does it when I'm not watching."</font>

Shen took that the way she takes everything, which is with no visible reaction whatsoever, and went back to her stool and sat down and got out a fresh sheet.

*"Sit."*

---

She copied all five onto the sheet, large, in the middle, with a lot of white around them, the way you set out a thing that is going to be argued about.

*"Five characters. I charge by the character to write. I charge the same to read, because reading is the harder half, and I stopped apologising for that in the sixties."* She capped the ink. *"Five."*

Mei put the coins on the table.

Shen counted them without touching them, which Chisa — sitting on the bumper with her chin up, listening to a stranger's hands from nine metres — would afterward describe as the single most professional sound she had heard in Huanglong.

*"The first is a surname,"* said Shen. *"A real one. Held by real households, mostly two prefectures south of here, some of them along the river. I have written it perhaps two hundred times."*

<font color=moccasin>"Okay."</font>

*"The second and third are a formula."* She put her finger under them. *"They go on lintels. They go on gate-posts. They go on grave-tablets. I have written this pair into something over four thousand letters in my life and I could set them in the dark."* She looked up. *"They mean: there is someone."*

Cartethyia's voice did something on the third word and came back.

*"Over a door it means the house is occupied. Somebody is in. Don't pass by."* Shen's hand stayed flat on the paper. *"On a stone it means what you would expect. And as a given name it happens after a bad year — a family loses everybody, and the next child born is called that, and it goes into the register, and on paper it looks ridiculous, and nobody who was there thinks so."*

The arch dripped.

<font color=moccasin>"Which one is it?"</font>

*"I write letters,"* said Shen. *"I don't decide what people meant. Anyone who tells you they can do that from characters on a door is selling you something. I have watched four of them do it at this gate and outlive their reputations."*

<font color=moccasin>"Guess."</font>

*"No."*

<font color=moccasin>"...Yeah. Fair."</font>

*"The last two are a place,"* said Shen.

Mei sat very still.

*"It is small. It has a river bend in it and it is named for the bend, which is what half of them are called."* Shen turned the sheet a quarter-turn so it faced Mei, which she does for every customer, and which made the woman across the table look at her own hands for a second. *"It never got a road, so it never got onto a map, so as far as the Ministry is concerned it does not exist and has not since the survey. About forty thousand are in that condition. The ones that got a road got printed. The ones that didn't got remembered by the people who left."*

<font color=moccasin>"Do you know it?"</font>

*"I have written to it once."*

The queue behind them had gone quiet in the way a queue does when it decides to stay.

*"Nineteen years ago. A woman came up from the fish market and paid me for forty-one characters, and I wrote her letter and posted it myself at the ward box, because she asked me to, because she did not trust herself to walk past the box without opening it again."* Shen wiped the brush. *"It came back in the spring. Undeliverable, no such district. I kept it, because I keep them. She never came back for it. I have three hundred and six letters in a tin at home that were paid for and written and never got there, and that tin is the only part of this trade I have ever considered giving up."*

Mei put both hands flat on the table.

<font color=moccasin>"Say the whole thing out loud."</font>

*"All five?"*

<font color=moccasin>"All five, together, like it's a person."</font>

Shen said it.

She said it in the flat unbeautiful voice of a professional reader, at the speed of a clerk reading a line off a register — surname first and native place last, the way you write a person down in a country that writes people down — and the arch took it and gave it back once off the stone, and a truck's engine ticked as it cooled, and somewhere up the wall a bird went off.

Mei sat there with her hands flat on the table.

Cartethyia, standing behind her, left that part untranslated, because there was nothing in it to translate.

---

The queue waited. Nobody moved off. A man near the back put his basket down.

Shen let it run. Forty years at a gate teaches you what to do with a silence, and what you do with it is get a fresh sheet out and square it and pick up the brush and wait with your hand loaded — because a person in that condition either gets up and walks away, or dictates.

<font color=moccasin>"How much for a letter?"</font>

*"By the character."*

<font color=moccasin>"To that place."</font>

*"It'll come back."*

<font color=moccasin>"Yeah. How much."</font>

*"By the character,"* said Shen, *"and there's no discount for a bad address. I've been asked."*

Mei laughed — once, wet, all the way out — and wiped her face on the back of her wrist and sat up.

<font color=moccasin>"Okay. Write this."</font>

*"Thirty-one,"* said Shen, counting. *"That's a short one. Most run to a hundred and forty, because they're paying for the visit and not the letter."*

<font color=moccasin>"Can I have two?"</font>

*"Two copies is two prices."*

Shen wrote the second copy and left the question of the door alone.

The first she folded and addressed in the form the post uses for districts that no longer exist — the last office that has a name for the country it is in, and then the name — and put into her own sleeve to walk down to the ward box herself, because that is what she had done nineteen years ago and she does not keep two systems.

The second she handed across the table flat, uncreased, ink still soft, and Mei took it in both hands.

*"One thing,"* said Shen.

<font color=moccasin>"Mm."</font>

Cartethyia got four paces past the awning and stopped in the middle of the wall lane with the folder against her chest.

She had been the one in the house.

Twenty years in an inverted seminary with the door shut and the lamps going out one at a time, and a girl in there stitching dolls and saying their names out loud so the dark could not have hers, calling out into a building that nobody was walking past — *there is someone in, there is someone in, there is someone in* — and there had been, the entire time, and nothing had ever called back.

And then a tired man in a cold country, who had done everything correctly and stamped everything twice and struck out *two* and written *nine* and told nobody, had stood on a step without his coat on with a cup in his hand and watched a truck go under a pole and said it back to a door.

She cried in the street for approximately forty seconds. Her fists were at her sides and her chin was up and she made no sound at all, which is a thing you learn in a place where sound is an admission, and which she has never entirely unlearned and does not intend to.

Then she wiped her face with the flat of her hand, twice, once each side, and went up to the market and bought lemon cakes, the ones with the sugar crystals on, and paid without haggling for the first time on that continent, and carried them back to a freight apron and put them on the tailgate and said they were from a stall by the gate.

She kept it. She kept it that day and she has kept it since, on the grounds that it had been said to the air and to nobody in that truck.

The record contains it because the gate writer keeps a ledger of every item she is paid for, and the ledger is a public document under the ward's own by-law, and no clerk in the history of Jinzhou has ever thought to read one.

## III. 業 — OCCUPATIONS

The plum rains came properly on the fourth, and the city put its awnings out and got on with it.

**Iuno** went to the youngest gate for her figures.

Mornye's chart wanted river level against stated discharge, fortnightly, on pre-addressed sheets with bands and dates on them, and the date on the first band had gone past three weeks earlier in a tobacco shed in the rain. Iuno had written the explanation in a letter and posted it at the Jinzhou office on the second, at a counter, in person, and had watched it go into the sack, and had then walked out and stood under the eave for a moment in a manner Amy elected not to comment on.

The gauge at the youngest gate is at the Lower Sluice on the dam side, in a cut in the concrete, with a ladder down to it and a hut at the top with a stove in it.

The man in the hut had a bad back, a lidded cup, and a book with thirty-one years of readings in it in the same hand throughout, going from a young hand to an old one across the middle of it without ever changing shape.

He reads the level three times a watch. He has the third watch, which is the one nobody wants, and has had it so long that the roster no longer offers it to anybody else.

*"Fortnightly,"* he said, when Cartethyia had explained the sheets. *"You'll want discharge from the works office. They post it on the board. It's wrong."*

<font color=royalblue>"How wrong?"</font>

Iuno looked at thirty-one years of a man's handwriting.

<font color=royalblue>"May I copy both columns."</font>

*"If you copy them properly and don't tidy them."*

<font color=royalblue>"I have never tidied a figure in my life,"</font> said Iuno, <font color=royalblue>"and I have done considerably worse things than tidy figures, so you may take that as a boast with some weight behind it."</font>

Cartethyia rendered about seventy percent of that. The man laughed, once, and then put a hand in the small of his back and said something short and unrepeatable about the weather, and went and got a second cup.

She sat in the hut for an hour and a half with the rain going across the lake in curtains, copying two columns onto a data sheet for a woman four thousand kilometres away, and at the end she asked for his name, in her own three bad words of the language, which she had made Cartethyia drill into her on the road for exactly this purpose and had used four times so far.

He told her his name.

It was two syllables. It was an ordinary name, held by a great many people, and it went into the corner of the sheet as the source, which is where sources go.

*Ah,* said the High Priestess, on the ladder.

*Don't.*

*Darling, a woman with a pink smile offered you that name across your own kitchen table in the small hours, and you said* it'll keep, *and it kept, and you have just bought it for three badly-pronounced words and a cup of his tea.*

*Yes.*

*How does it feel?*

*Like something I earned,* said Iuno, *which I am told is the whole point, and which I am still not used to.*

**Ciaccona** found the seventh step on the fifth day and stayed on it four hours.

The northeast cistern stair goes up from the second landing to the water gate in thirty-one steps, and the ward schools sing that the seventh is broken.

The song is old. Nobody can date it. It comes with a hand-clap on the fourth line and a stamp on the seventh, and every child in the harbor ward learns it at four and can still do it drunk at sixty, and the line is: *the seventh's gone, so mind your feet.*

The Bureau of Works has repaired that step three times in living memory. Each time, the evacuation drills produced pile-ups at the seventh step, caused by four hundred children skipping a step that was sound. Each time an inspector wrote a note, and the third inspector wrote the sentence the Bureau has since had to defend twice at the frontier court:

*The song is older than the stair. We build to the song now. It is cheaper.*

The step is currently broken. It was broken deliberately this spring by a mason with a certificate, in the presence of a witness, to a specified depth, and the work order is on file.

Ciaccona sat down on the sixth step with her lute across her knees and looked at the seventh for a long time.

<font color=teal>"Say that again."</font>

The Bureau clerk who had been sent to explain it — a nervous young man with an umbrella and a folder — said it again, and Cartethyia translated it again, and Ciaccona put her head back against the wall.

<font color=teal>"They broke it on purpose."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"To a specified depth."</font>

<font color=teal>"There's a *drawing.*"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"With a scale bar on it."</font>

She sat with that.

Four hundred years of playing. Four hundred years of buying rounds in un-touristed taverns and playing badly on purpose until somebody got annoyed enough to tell her what the city actually believed. And a hundred kilometres of cold road spent explaining to a knight, patiently, at length, more than once, why she had written Ragunna an anthem with a rough seam in it and refused to fix the seam. *A song that belongs to everybody can't be confiscated.* She had believed it the way you believe a thing you have decided to believe.

And here was a stair in a foreign country that had been rebuilt to match a song, three times, by an office with a budget, because the song had outvoted the masonry.

<font color=teal>"Ask him what happens if they stop singing it."</font>

The clerk answered at some length.

<font color=skyblue>"He says nobody's stopped singing it."</font>

<font color=teal>"Ask him *if.*"</font>

Cartethyia asked. The clerk thought about it properly, standing in the rain under an umbrella with a folder going damp under his arm, and gave the answer that Ciaccona wrote into the mock-epic that night, in the margin, with a small uneven star beside it of the kind her father used to draw:

*"Then we'd mend it, and in about nine years somebody would fall down it, and in about forty a child would make up a song."*

She played on that stair for two hours in the afternoon and taught eleven children the wrong words to a Ragunnan drinking song. Four of them still have it. One of them will teach it to a granddaughter in sixty years with three words changed, and the song will be about a stair.

**Amy** got on the air on the sixth.

The freight channel in that valley runs off a relay on the dam face, and everybody uses it and nobody licenses it, and at eleven at night, sitting on the roof rack between two water drums with a folded red kite at her elbow, Amy keyed the rig and said the words that had opened a decade of broadcasts in a city that needed them.

Four sets came back. Then nine. Then something she stopped counting.

The blue-on-white Mingting consortium unit was two hundred metres away on the same apron with its cab tilted and Little Guo underneath it, and Little Guo went up a ladder in the rain to get a better line and shouted eleven words of a language he does not speak across two hundred metres of concrete at a girl he had been talking to for eight days at an eighty percent loss rate, and got it eighty percent right, and Amy laughed so hard she had to put the microphone down.

Then, at half past eleven, she said the other thing.

<font color=mistyrose>"Okay. Housekeeping. There's an angel on the back of the yellow truck at the dam apron and she's mine, and I want to say some stuff about her, and then I'm going to play a record."</font>

## IV. 籍 — WHERE THEY ARE REGISTERED

Yangyang went up to the yamen on the sixth of the sixth month and made her report.

She had promised it in the snow, standing at attention in front of a window with the lake grey below. *I'm finding out. I'll report to you when I have it whole.* — *To me alone.* — *To you alone.*

She had it whole.

Jinhsi received her sitting down, which she does for reports she expects to run long, with the file shut and the rain on the glass and the lake underneath gone the colour lakes go in the plum rains, which is the colour of the inside of a shell.

It took forty minutes.

Yangyang gave it in the order she had built it. The incident ledger. The underlinings. The correlation study handed to her in a clinic doorway on an equinox. The ribbon washed clean in a trough. The incense that bent toward her in wet grey air. The third bowl and its leaning steam. The half-note finished at the frost month. The lake turning over at the fourth watch with great courtesy, which she had asked for and which had been done.

Then she gave the part with no evidence attached, which is the part she had been unable to file for a year, and she gave it standing, because she found she could not do it sitting.

That it is a person.

That it came out of another story by a road nobody has surveyed, with its doors blown open, and paid the crossing in its own name and its own words, and arrived here with a purse that holds one word at a time and spends them like principal.

That for twenty years it carried this country's weight, because this country hands its weight to the weather as a matter of custom and has done since before the reform; that it took all of it; and that it carried the whole load down to the lake at the fourth watch and gave it to the only thing in Huanglong big enough to hold it.

That it now has a name, half of which was written in wet sand by a foreigner's hand that cannot read what it writes.

That it is staying.

That the Almanac Bureau has entered it as **接風**, the Welcoming Wind — *arrives with guests; met with a meal, as the word requires; stays* — ruled under *weather*, struck, and ruled again under *family*, and that the second ruling stands.

Jinhsi listened to all of it without once moving her hands.

"Is it dangerous," she said, at the end.

"Yes, Excellency."

"To us?"

"To itself." Yangyang held the gaze. "It has no capacity to refuse work. It has been running errands for a whole country for twenty years, and nobody ever offered it anything, and it never once asked. If we put it on a roster it will kill itself doing the roster, and it will keep that to itself, because it has no word to spare for telling us."

"Your recommendation."

"Feed it, Excellency."

The rain went across the glass.

"That is the Bureau's recommendation as well," said Jinhsi. "The Bureau got there from the etymology and you got there from a year of watching. I have two independent findings on my desk this week and they agree, and one of them was arrived at by a woman who cooks."

She turned a page she had not been reading.

"There is a party in the Censorate who will read your report and see a resource."

"Yes, Excellency."

"There is a party in the Ministry of Rites who will read it and see a cult, and want the naming of it, and want a building."

"Yes, Excellency."

"They will not read it," said Jinhsi. "It goes in the drawer with the schedule. The seal is mine. The copy is yours."

---

Yangyang was at the door when the flat voice came again, quieter, off the record, addressed half to the window, the way it had come once before in the snow.

"Outrider."

"Excellency."

"On the night after the kite, the lake dreamed forward."

Yangyang stopped with her hand on the frame.

The lake dreams backward. It has dreamed backward for four hundred years and every reign has a shelf of it — a library burning that has never burned, floodwater standing at second-floor windows on dry streets, a red cap coming up out of black water, a girl at a black post who never lands the cut. The runners map it. The Bureau files it under memory, and the frontier court has ruled twice that memory is admissible and prophecy is not.

Yangyang took her hand off the frame and came back into the room and stood at attention, which was the only thing available to her.

"Once. For about the length of a held breath." Jinhsi's eyes stayed on the water. "The Ministry of Rites has requested the contents and been refused. The refusal is on paper. The paper says *the Magistrate declines*, and it says nothing else, and it is going to go on saying nothing else."

"Understood."

"You may have it."

Yangyang stood in the doorway.

"I dreamed the ninth beat," said Jinhsi.

The towers of the youngest gate are nine.

Eight of them answer. They have answered since the rebuilding — tower to tower along the wall, one beat each, for a column counted returning — and the garrison log gets its two words, and the ward hears it and knows to put water on.

The ninth stands on the north face. It looks up the north road at the mountains that have been counting since the frost month, eight days apart and then seven, and it has not been beaten since the dark, because the column it is kept for has not come back down that road, and a tower does not beat for a road with nobody on it.

Eight beats went along that wall at full morning on the thirtieth of the fifth month, for a column of one, on Her Excellency's own order, and the ninth was left where it has always been left.

"In the dream it beat," said Jinhsi.

"...Was it good, Excellency?"

Jinhsi turned round.

"That is the correct question," she said, "and it is the reason I am telling an Outrider instead of a Minister, and the answer is that I do not know, because the lake showed me the sound and declined to show me the road."

She looked at Yangyang for the length of a held breath.

"I have doubled the kitchen budget and ordered lamp-oil in quantities that will be raised at audit, and I will lose that argument on paper and win it in fact. The reason is that whichever road that beat is standing at the end of, this city will need feeding afterward, and I have decided to have the rain in before the fire this time." A pause the exact width of a magistrate deciding to be a person. "It is the only preparation available to me that is honest. Everything else is guessing in a robe."

"Excellency."

"Go and eat something, Outrider. That is an order, and it is the second one I have given this week, and both of them were that."

"I have four hundred words, and my daughter's friend has ten thousand, and she has carried this household on her back for nine days, so I have given her the afternoon off." Iuno folded her hand on her knee. "You speak my language, Excellency. You did it in the first six seconds and you did it well and you were watching to see whether I would notice."

Jinhsi poured tea.

The lustrum was walked on the twenty-third.

Huanglong keeps it as a practice with no name attached: when the count closes, the register goes round the wall.

The Ministry of Rites has twice offered to make it a procession and has twice been declined, on grounds recorded in the same office forty years apart: *the point of it is that it is a walk.*

What happens is that four clerks from the yamen carry the ward returns round the top of the wall, in the order of the wards, at the pace of people carrying paper. The towers beat as they pass, tower by tower, one beat each. At each tower a clerk reads the ward's figure out loud to a garrison officer who writes it into the tower book, and then they walk on.

It takes about two hours. It is extremely boring. Half the city comes out.

Three.
