# Second Helpings

The rice arrived while Cartethyia was still trying to persuade Lupa that the camellia could live in a soup bowl.

It came in a wooden tub with a cloth over the lid. The woman from the kitchen put it on the long table beneath the side awning, looked at the occupants of the benches, and fetched a second serving spoon.

“The rice stuck a little at the bottom. Whoever likes that part can have it.”

Three hands went up.

Suisui looked at Yangyang's.

“Ah. You still do that.”

“I enjoy the browned rice. You used to scrape it into your own bowl before bringing the pot out.”

“The person washing the pot enjoys certain privileges.”

“I washed it on Thursdays.”

Mei leaned toward Amy. “We should have asked which sister to sit beside.”

Amy had already passed her bowl forward. “I've chosen the woman holding the spoon. Experience has been very useful to me.”

The cook gave her a piece of crust almost as wide as her palm.

They moved over from the gatehouse benches by stages, bringing cups, coats, the lute, and the bowl which had acquired a plant. Lupa carried the rice tub the final few feet when the cook changed her mind about where it should stand. Chisa felt along the near edge of the table, found a splinter, and covered it with a folded cloth before sitting down.

Suisui took the place at Yangyang's left. She had a clear view of the dressing above her sister's collar and the hand coming slowly back from the rice tub.

Yangyang settled her bowl before Suisui could reach for it.

“Could you pass the mushrooms, jie? I'd like to choose the rest myself.”

Suisui held the dish for her. Yangyang took three, considered, and took a fourth.

Across the table Lupa drew the greens closer. “Put some of these beside them. The stems are good.”

“I had intended to.”

“Then we're having a very agreeable lunch.”

Lupa ate a mouthful large enough to keep her occupied while Yangyang arranged her own plate.

Mei sat down carefully, one hand on the bench. Iuno watched the hand until it relaxed, then moved her cup out of the way so Mei could lean against her.

“That's better,” Mei said. “I'd like my next adventure to involve one of those chairs with cushions tied to every part.”

“I have seen you fall out of a chair,” Amy said. “You were reaching for a bun. We would need to consider the whole arrangement.”

“The bun was farther away than it looked.”

“I was beside it, Ma. You could have asked me.”

Suisui glanced up. She was still learning which exchanges called for intervention.

Mei reached across Iuno for the bread basket, felt the pull beneath her ribs, and let her hand settle on the table.

“Would you pass me one, sweetheart?”

Amy chose a bun, tore it open to check the filling, and gave her the larger half.

“Sweet bean. That should keep you interested in this end of the table.”

Iuno received the other half. Amy took a whole one for herself.

Suisui began to eat.

The mushrooms were slippery and wonderfully ordinary. Someone in the kitchen had been generous with ginger. Yangyang fished a piece out of the sauce and put it beside her rice, where she used to put things she was saving for the last mouthful.

Suisui had forgotten that too.

She looked away before the sight could fill her eyes again. Ciaccona was trying to take a dish from Cartethyia, who had one hand inside her pack and the other around the plant bowl.

“I can hold your lunch while you find it,” Ciaccona said. “You appear to have packed the entire road above whatever you're looking for.”

“There was a piece of cloth in here. I thought I could put it under the roots.”

“There's a piece of cloth in your lap.”

Cartethyia looked down.

“That's a particularly good place for it. Thank you.”

She laid the bowl on the table. Ciaccona drew the camellia's one drooping leaf clear of the mushroom sauce.

“Ciaccona,” Suisui said, testing the name. “May I call you Cia as well? I'm afraid I learned several of your names while someone was shouting for you.”

The bard smiled at her over the leaf.

“Please do. You'll find this household offers frequent opportunities to revise.”

“I remember yours from Yangyang's room. I meant to say it properly then.”

“You can say it over lunch. I much prefer the occasion.”

Suisui passed her the mushrooms. Ciaccona took them with her better hand, and Cartethyia quietly shifted her cup nearer the other one.

A breeze came beneath the awning. The Wind stirred in Suisui's scarf, following the warmth from the rice. Yangyang drew the wider bowl into the clear space between them.

Steam bent toward it.

For a little while Suisui watched the cold settle there. Then she loosened her scarf and let it fall over the bench beside her.

“You can come back when you feel like it. I shall be here for at least another serving.”

The spoon beside her turned a fraction.

“I heard that,” she told the bowl.

Yangyang looked down at her rice, smiling.

At the far end, Chisa put her heel against a table leg. The next time Lupa reached for the greens, the dishes stayed level.

“There's a short leg on this side,” Chisa said. “Could somebody give me the folded paper beneath the water jug?”

Amy passed it across. Chisa doubled it once, bent down, and fitted it under the foot.

Cartethyia tested the table with one finger.

“That was bothering me through the entire argument about the bowl.”

“You could have mentioned it,” Chisa said. “I was listening to you think about drainage.”

“I thought Lupa was moving it with her knee.”

Lupa looked along the table at her. “If I want your plant off the dinner table, you'll be able to tell.”

She took the bowl and placed it in the patch of sun at the end of the bench. Cartethyia leaned after it to check the leaf.

Then the cook brought eggs with chopped scallions, and the question of drainage lost its audience.

***

They reached the second helping slowly.

The first had required too much attention: getting the bowl comfortable, finding a position for an elbow, discovering the split in a lip through a mouthful of salted broth. By the second, Amy was describing an alteration she intended to make to the transport's luggage restraints, and Mei was objecting to being included among the luggage.

“You leave your seat whenever somebody drops something,” Amy explained. “Your restraint will need a little bell.”

“I would ring it for service.”

“I know. I've been considering that disadvantage.”

Suisui laughed with a mouthful of rice and had to put down her bowl.

When she reached for it again, her sleeve caught the wrapping beside her hip. The Prism rolled against her thigh. A narrow gleam showed through the cloth.

Amy's attention followed it.

“Suisui, could you tell us about that? The one Yangyang held seemed to be answering the one inside the cage. We had time to work out about half of what we were holding.”

Suisui steadied the parcel.

“Hsin gave it into my keeping. It's a portion of her power. I have her permission to use it for finding and protecting Yangyang.”

“I heard her through it,” Yangyang said. “She spoke while everything around me was held still.”

Suisui turned toward her so quickly that the cloth slid farther open.

“I was going to tell you this afternoon.”

“She asked me for a vow. I explained what I intended to do with the two Prisms, and she let me try.” Yangyang looked at the light against her sister's hand. “She told me what she offered you for my safe return.”

Suisui's spoon lay between her fingers. She put it across the rim of her bowl and missed. It fell onto the table.

Mei moved the sauce dish before the spoon could reach it.

“What did she ask for?”

Suisui looked along the table. Amy's hands rested beside her plate. Lupa had stopped reaching for the rice. Beside Iuno, Mei waited with the fallen spoon in her hand.

Beyond the awning, a porter was asking which storehouse wanted the empty sacks. The kitchen woman leaned out of the window to direct him. He gathered them up, talking as he went.

Suisui followed him with her eyes until he reached the corner.

Iuno set down her cup.

“We can take this through to the little room beside the kitchen. Liangyu offered it when the sun moves around. The rest of our lunch can come.”

“Would that be a great deal of trouble?”

Lupa rose with the rice tub. “I've carried this three times already. We seem to be getting on.”

Chisa gathered the cups. Cartethyia retrieved the plant. Amy took the water jug and tucked the folded cloth under her arm.

Mei held out Suisui's spoon.

“I would like to hear it,” she said. “You can finish eating while you tell us.”

Suisui took the spoon. Then, because Mei was still standing beside her, she let her take the bowl as well.

***

The little room held a table, a bench beneath the window, and an impressive quantity of onions tied in strings.

They fitted themselves around these. Ciaccona took the stool by the door; Lupa and Chisa sat on the floor with their backs against the wall. Amy put the rice within reach of both. When everyone had settled, Cartethyia passed around a plate of eggs which had nearly been forgotten outside.

Suisui unwrapped the Prism.

Its light fell across a ring left by somebody's tea. She moved it onto the dry cloth.

“I met a fragment of Hsin in the pagoda. Dumpling led me there. She offered me a wager.”

The little bird settled on the window ledge, clicking once as its wings closed.

“If I agreed to the price of bringing Yangyang home, I would surrender the authority I'd copied to reach her, and spend the rest of my life in her service. If I held out until her incense finished, I would receive the Prism, permission to use it, and instruction.”

Iuno's gaze lifted from the crystal to Suisui.

“Your remaining life was the stake?”

“She was very precise about that. She wanted me to hear petitions with her.”

Amy put down the egg plate. “You had to beat her at this before she'd show you how to find your sister?”

“I had challenged her. Rather grandly. I told her about all of you and what happened at Mt. Firmament. I called your strength something I could put on the table.” Suisui looked at Mei. “She asked me what I would pay myself.”

Mei shifted her sore hand beneath Iuno's.

“I'd have liked to be there when you needed an answer from us.”

“So would I,” Suisui said. “By then the door was behind me.”

Yangyang drew the wider bowl onto her lap. The Wind had followed them in and settled near its rim.

Suisui watched the steam lean.

“Hsin said she could make a certain passage to you by using everything shijie has left of herself. The shell would help her find the way. You would arrive alive.”

Her fingers closed around the loose cloth.

“The Wind would be spent.”

Yangyang held the bowl very carefully.

On the floor, Lupa's ears had drawn back. Chisa laid a hand beside hers.

“She would need the Wind's agreement too,” Suisui continued. “My part was to give Hsin a black stone and ask her to do it.”

Cold moved along the bowl's rim toward Suisui's wrist.

She opened her hand beneath it.

“You were ready to help me.”

The pressure rested against her palm. Yangyang's thumb moved once over the pottery.

Mei looked at the little space between the sisters.

“I'm glad you're both here to tell us.”

Suisui bowed her head. “I nearly gave her the stone.”

Her voice had become so quiet that the small sounds from the kitchen reached them clearly: a knife against a board, water poured into a pan.

“She showed me Yangyang at home. Years of her coming to the table, getting older, letting me mend her clothes. I could almost remember living there. And then she showed me the roads where people died. Each time I found something to question, she brought me closer to the thing I already feared.”

She lifted her eyes to Yangyang.

“I knew what the stone would do. My hand went toward it anyway. I was so tired of wondering whether I would see you.”

Yangyang set the bowl on the table. She took her sister's hand between both of hers, awkwardly around the dressings.

“Hsin told me you refused. I'm glad you've told me how difficult it was.”

Suisui's mouth trembled. “I had been preparing a rather better account.”

Iuno reached for the water jug. Mei helped her tip it into Suisui's cup.

“When we first found each other, I used to prepare those,” Iuno said. “I could keep a woman occupied for an evening with the arrangement of the facts. Eventually she would have to go to bed.”

“I was very susceptible,” Mei said. “She made tea.”

Iuno glanced at her. Mei's thumb moved against her wrist.

Suisui accepted the water. A little escaped onto her fingers as she drank.

“You knew she was doing it?”

“We learned,” Lupa said from the floor. “We had some loud evenings.”

Iuno settled the jug between her feet.

“I hurt Mei by choosing what she was allowed to know. I also kept her from reaching the Wind, years before any of us sat at this table.”

Suisui looked at the bowl. The cold above it had gone still.

“I have things to answer for with both of them,” Iuno said. “You can ask me about my part when you wish. For now, I'd like to hear how you brought your sister out of that room.”

Mei's hand remained over hers. Suisui looked at their joined fingers, then at Yangyang holding her own.

“I asked Hsin what the Prism could do while everyone remained alive.”

She drew breath more easily this time.

“It can distinguish frequencies and carry a call through the Shroud. It can find a passage if the person at the far end answers. It has some strength to protect them while that happens. All of that uses power Hsin meant to have returned to her true self.”

Amy leaned forward a little. “Did she tell you how much she had left after giving it to you?”

“Enough to guide us out and answer a call through it. She said the rest needed discussing with her true self.”

Amy glanced at the crystal. “Does she hear us between those calls?”

“That's something else I have to ask her.” Suisui looked at Iuno. “She may have heard all of this.”

“Then she'll have time to prepare her answers,” Iuno said. “I'd be pleased to receive them over a similarly comfortable lunch.”

“Then that belongs on the page with the other things we're asking her.” Amy reached for her Terminal, hesitated at its cracked face, and accepted the pencil Chisa held up.

Suisui watched her write.

“I thought you would be angrier with me.”

Lupa shifted against the wall. “I am angry that you had to sit there. Hearing about it has given me a very clear idea of where I'd like to put that board.”

“Lupa,” Iuno said, “somebody would have to retrieve it afterward.”

“Hsin has plenty of staff.”

A laugh escaped Suisui, sudden enough that she had to cover her mouth.

The Wind moved against the inside of her wrist. She brought the other hand close around the cold.

“I'm also glad you told us how close your hand got,” Lupa added. “I've had days when somebody needed to catch mine.”

She took the plate of eggs from Amy and held it up to Suisui.

Suisui chose a piece. It took a while before she could eat it.

***

“There is something else,” Suisui said.

Amy held the pencil above the paper.

“Hsin opened the gates for Yangyang. She knew we would come after her. She said a visit could be postponed.”

Mei's fingers tightened beneath Iuno's hand.

Outside the window, a man wheeled an empty barrow past, one wheel squeaking with each turn.

“She knew Yangyang might die,” Suisui said. “I asked her that directly.”

Yangyang's hand left the bowl. For a moment she held it over her knee, fingers slightly bent. Then she laid it flat.

“I wondered why they opened just then. I could still see Chixia through the passage.”

Lupa put her plate down.

“We were already coming. She heard us make the plans.”

“I said that to her. She spoke about the guardians, how little reach she had left, the powers she needed inside Xuanfang. She said she chose a bond she believed would hold.”

Mei looked at Yangyang. “We'll ask her together.”

Yangyang's lips parted. She looked from Mei to the covered window, then back to her sister.

“I want her to hear what happened on the road. Mingyu's mother lives here. Mingyu told me she would be waiting by the columns as they came in.”

Suisui reached toward her and waited with her hand open. Yangyang leaned into her shoulder.

“She died in the mill yard,” Yangyang said. “Yanlang died there too. They helped bring the village through.”

Cartethyia lowered her eyes to the plant on the sill. A small clot of soil had fallen onto the wood. She gathered it with her fingertip and put it back.

“Liangyu will know who needs telling,” she said. “I'll go with you when you speak to her.”

Yangyang nodded against Suisui's shoulder.

Amy drew a line beneath the things she had written.

“We can send the part about the gates to Jinhsi with the next dispatch. She asked Hsin about it before we left.”

“Let Suisui read the account first,” Iuno said. “Those are her answers from Hsin. And we still need to learn who ordered the attacks themselves.”

Suisui looked up.

“You think there may be more to that?”

“I'd like Hsin to tell us exactly what she set in motion and what she knew was happening already. She has made those very different questions.” Iuno glanced toward the Prism. “She can answer them in a room with enough chairs for the people asking.”

The kitchen door opened. The cook stood there with a small pot in both hands.

“There's a little broth left, if you're staying a while.”

Mei rubbed a thumb over Iuno's knuckles and reached for her bowl.

“We are. Thank you.”

The woman poured from the pot. She asked Yangyang to lift her spoon, waited while Suisui helped her move the bowl, and put the last ladleful into Lupa's.

“Leave the dishes. I'll collect them when I've finished out there.”

She shut the door with her hip.

For a while they ate. Suisui found she could manage the eggs now. Yangyang remained against her between mouthfuls, shifting only when the position pulled at her shoulder.

Across from them, Mei bent over her broth. Amy moved the bread basket nearer without interrupting what she was writing.

Suisui watched all three of them for a moment, then looked down at the luminous parcel.

“And the man at the Skyworks? How did Muyu get another piece of her power?”

Amy turned the sheet around and began on the back.

“That's the question I keep coming back to. The two crystals answered each other. The machinery around his took that answer and sent it through whichever passage was open.”

She put her bowl on one corner of the page, drew a small cage, and frowned at it.

“Imagine that has nine arms. I'm sparing myself the other seven.”

“I can supply a vivid recollection,” Ciaccona said. “One spent a considerable time trying to remove my head.”

Amy added an indignant little mark beside the cage.

Suisui leaned nearer. Yangyang followed the pencil with her eyes.

“When we hit the thing, it sent the blow somewhere else,” Amy continued. “Later it began sending them toward the captives. Yangyang found a way to make its own call go back into it, and we closed the ways it could use to get out.”

“The return is still holding,” Chisa said. “The machine also has Nyx's lance through it and the loading frame locked around it. The Wardens have the places to watch.”

“Nyx can remain very still when she has a worthwhile occupation,” Amy said. “She's currently doing better than several people I could name.”

Mei had begun to shift on the bench. She stopped, then continued until her sore side had room.

“I'm getting comfortable. You can watch if you like.”

“I have been. Your audience approves of this development.”

Suisui's mouth curved. She looked back at the drawing.

“Can Muyu reach the controls from somewhere else?”

Amy laid the pencil down across the cage.

“I haven't found a way he can. I want the people who know the Skyworks to examine the connections before I give you a firmer answer. The extraction spindles are off, and the people are out.”

“Some of them had died before we reached their restraints,” Yangyang said. “Their voices were still being used.”

She raised her hand toward the Prism, then rested it beside the cloth.

“I heard something of Muyu before that. An artisan at Yuan Fortress, among the dead and dying. He was frightened. Then he found something he could do with the voices, and I could feel the relief in him.”

Suisui looked at her.

“Yuan Fortress was two hundred years ago.”

“I know. That's part of what I want to understand.” Yangyang's fingers moved across the cloth's edge. “I heard him inside what remained of that time. I saw him in the Skyworks today. There's a great deal between those two things for us to find.”

Ciaccona leaned back on her stool.

“Hsin has been here through those years. She can begin by telling us when she last had an agreeable conversation with him.”

“I'd settle for when she last had any conversation with him,” Iuno said. “Her standards for an agreeable one have given me cause for concern.”

Amy wrote *Muyu—Yuan Fortress* and left room beneath it.

“He fitted a stop under one of the levers that would have let us close the galleries,” she said. “I had to break it before I could pull the lever down.”

Yangyang looked at the sketch again. Her face grew tired.

“When he went toward the door, I tried to reach him with my sword. I thought I could finish it before he touched anything else.” She drew her hand back into her lap. “He let me see a clear way across the floor. I followed it straight into a wall.”

Mei's gaze went to the bandage at her wrist.

“By the time we came in, he'd taken the lower lift. We found the landing on the way out.”

“Do you know where it goes?” Suisui asked.

“We know where to begin looking,” Mei said. “The workshops have people who know those routes, and some of them are being treated here. We can ask when they're able to talk.”

Lupa picked up the loose end of the rope at her belt. She worked a knot out with her thumb while Mei spoke.

“And when we find him, someone stays with Yangyang. Someone watches the way he came in. I'm interested in making his next departure difficult.”

“I'd like someone watching his hands,” Yangyang said.

Lupa looked up. “I can enjoy that job.”

Suisui found herself looking for the woman who would decide what happened next. Mei was reading Amy's page. Iuno had caught a missing word and was pointing to the space where it belonged. Chisa asked Amy to add the service passages, and Amy moved her bowl to make room.

Yangyang reached across her sister for the pencil.

“I'd like to put down what I saw of the lift. My hand can manage a little.”

Amy gave it to her and held the page steady.

Suisui stayed beside them. When the paper slid against the grain of the table, she put her empty cup on the corner.

“Hsin asked you for a vow,” she said to Yangyang. “What did you promise?”

Yangyang finished the short line she was drawing.

“To remember that I was one of the people coming out. To ask the others for what I needed.”

Mei's head lifted.

“She said something of her own first,” Yangyang continued. “There was a word I couldn't hear. It had the same kind of gap as the inscription in the shell. I told her what I meant while I still had everyone in sight.”

The Wind brushed the side of the pencil. Yangyang let her hold it level for the last stroke.

Suisui looked from the moving pencil to the wrapped light.

“We still have to find someone who can help with her words.”

“That's on my page too,” Amy said. She showed her the front.

Below the questions about power, the gates, and Muyu, she had written *Founders. Wind's voice. Bring the lead as it is.*

Suisui read the line twice.

“I should like to come when you ask about that.”

Cartethyia shifted the plant so Suisui could see Amy better.

“Please do. You'll know some of the things she might want to tell us.”

“I shall have to be careful what I admit to knowing.”

The Wind lifted the very end of Suisui's scarf and laid it across her mouth.

Yangyang laughed. It hurt enough that she held her side, and Suisui immediately put down the cup to steady her.

The scarf slid free. Suisui caught it, rubbed the soft edge between her fingers, and began laughing too.

***

By late afternoon the onions had acquired company.

Three damp shirts hung from the opposite rail. Amy's socks occupied the windowsill. Cartethyia's camellia had been transferred into a small earthenware pot supplied by the cook, who wanted her soup bowl back and had strong opinions about plants sitting in water.

Cartethyia listened to every one of them.

Liangyu brought sleeping mats and word of the southern dispatch. Yangyang's short account went with it, along with Suisui's reading of the sentences about Hsin and a message for Baizhi and Chixia. Amy held the page while Yangyang signed it. Suisui watched her add, beneath the formal lines, that she wanted to hear Chixia's voice as soon as a call could get through.

Later, Cartethyia went with Yangyang to speak to Liangyu about Mingyu's mother. They sat together behind the infirmary for a while. When they returned, Yangyang accepted a place on the bench and slept with her head against the wall.

Suisui sat beside her. The Wind rested in the open collar of the spare coat.

Around them the afternoon found enough little tasks for everyone. Lupa carried water. Chisa repaired a torn strap. Ciaccona put her lute on a folded blanket and spent half an hour doing very little with her eyes closed.

Mei slept through most of it, holding Amy's hand on her own knee. When her head began to fall forward, Iuno guided it onto her shoulder and settled there herself.

The cook found them like that when she came for the dishes.

Suisui opened her mouth to offer help.

Then she looked down at Yangyang, whose cheek had slid against her sleeve.

“Could you leave that tray until later?” she asked. “I've rather filled my lap.”

The woman nodded toward the sleeping bench.

“Supper will keep for a little while too.”

She took the empty rice tub and left them the room.

***

Cartethyia found a place for the camellia beside the window in the room she and Ciaccona had been given.

Then she moved it an inch to the left.

Then she turned it.

“The new leaf should face the morning,” she explained.

Ciaccona sat on the edge of the bed, working a pebble out of her boot.

“The morning will have a very good view. Come here and let me see what's happened to your knee.”

Cartethyia came, carrying the little watering cup. Ciaccona took it from her and set it on the floor.

The scrape had been cleaned twice. It looked much the same each time, with a patch of red where the edge of the dressing rubbed when Cartethyia bent her leg. Ciaccona eased the cloth away from that corner.

“There. Try moving it now.”

Cartethyia bent her knee experimentally. Then she let her leg rest across Ciaccona's lap.

“I'd like to leave it here a while. The rest of me can come closer if there's room.”

Ciaccona moved the boot off the bed.

“I've been making room for you since the grain store. You may have noticed the rather excessive hand gesture.”

Cartethyia sat beside her and laid her head on her shoulder.

“I kept looking at your fingers. There was dust falling between us, and I was trying to see them through it.”

Ciaccona put her arm around her. Her own hand lay open on Cartethyia's knee, the fingers slightly swollen.

“I thought of climbing down,” she said. “I was trying to decide where I could fit. You filled a remarkable amount of the available space.”

“I wanted to get smaller. Every time I tried, the engine moved.” Cartethyia touched the bard's fingers one by one. “I wanted you to be cross with me about the terrarium. I could remember exactly how you'd sound.”

“I had several remarks prepared. Seeing you rather interfered with them.”

Cartethyia considered this.

“You could try them now.”

Ciaccona looked at the earnest face turned toward her, the cleaned patch beside her jaw, the hair still damp above one ear.

She kissed her there.

“Perhaps in the morning. My standards tend to recover after breakfast.”

Cartethyia turned into the next kiss. Her hand found Ciaccona's waist, and for a while the bard's good intentions concerning the dressing received very little assistance.

When they drew apart, Cartethyia kept her forehead near hers.

“I want a day when we can go somewhere because you've heard the food is good.”

“I have excellent information on that subject.”

“And I want to sit at the table until you're ready to leave. I keep thinking of places I meant to take you. Sometimes I was only saving them for a day when we could both be pleased about it.”

Ciaccona brushed the damp hair back.

“I can be pleased over lunch while my wrist hurts. You've seen me manage considerably more complicated feelings over a bill.”

Cartethyia smiled. Then she looked at the hand on her knee.

“I was frightened today. I know I said it down there. I wanted to say it where you could hold me afterward.”

Ciaccona drew her closer, until Cartethyia had both arms around her and the room had become mostly hair and the smell of borrowed soap.

“I was frightened too,” she said against her temple. “I want to sleep with you where I can put my foot against yours. Kindly arrange your grandeur accordingly.”

“I can be this size all evening.”

“That will suit the bed beautifully.”

They sat until someone in the passage called for a spare towel. Cartethyia lifted her head.

“We have two.”

“One of them is under your plant.”

“I'll give them the dry one.”

She returned a moment later to find Ciaccona holding out the nightshirt she had been sitting on.

Cartethyia took it, caught the offered hand as well, and kissed the fingers she had watched through the dust.

***

Lupa had brought the rope upstairs.

She sat on the floor beside her sleeping mat with the severed end across her knee. Chisa heard the fibres scrape against her thumbnail each time she tried to twist them together.

“Would you like me to join it?” she asked.

Lupa rubbed the end once more before holding it out.

“Please. I keep making the same mess.”

Chisa sat opposite her. She found the two cut edges by touch and laid them side by side. Lupa watched her fingers.

“It's a short piece,” Chisa said. “You'll want to replace the whole length when we reach a shop.”

“I know where the good one is. Near the harbour. The man there tried to sell me three coils last time.”

“You were carrying everybody's bags. He probably thought you needed them.”

Lupa gave a small laugh. “He was persuasive. I even bought a pulley.”

Chisa brought the fibres together. The work asked for a steadiness she could still give, sitting with her feet on a warm floor. When she had finished, she put the join in Lupa's palm.

Lupa tested it gently, then laid it aside.

“When the rope came back empty, I kept pulling,” she said. “There was a bit of Mei's belt in the knot. I knew what I had in my hand.”

Chisa waited while Lupa rubbed her thumb across her palm.

“I could smell her in three directions. I wanted one of them to be right so badly that I went straight into the fog. Then I came around that bench and there you both were.”

“You put my hand on your sleeve afterward,” Chisa said. “That helped me.”

“You were shaking.”

“So were you. I could feel it through the cloth.”

Lupa looked toward her, then down at the rope again.

Chisa uncapped the small bottle the healer had given them. She held out her hand for Lupa's burned one.

“I had been following your steps for months,” she said as she spread the salve. “In that place I heard you walk away while you were still beside me. It made me afraid of using everything I usually trust.”

Lupa turned her hand so Chisa could reach the edge of the burn.

“You found the room.”

“Cia kept playing. You kept talking. I could follow the changes.” Chisa's thumb slowed over the rough skin. “I'd like you to talk for a while tonight. You can tell me about the pulley.”

“You'll regret inviting that. It had a brass wheel, and the fellow expected me to pay for the shine.”

Chisa smiled at the warmth arriving in her voice.

When the dressing was secure, Lupa took the bottle and held it up.

“Your collar next. Turn around.”

Chisa moved between her knees and gathered her hair forward. Lupa spread a little lotion along the skin below the collar, careful where the strap had rubbed. Her hands were warm from the room and the last of the water she'd carried up.

“I wanted to ask you to do this,” Chisa said.

“You can ask earlier. I'd have found somewhere to sit.”

“I'd like to stay here after you've finished.”

Lupa's fingers paused at her nape. Then she worked the lotion gently along the other side.

“I've got an entire floor to offer you. The mattress appears to have been stuffed with the floor as well.”

Chisa leaned back against her chest. Lupa's arm came around her middle, loose enough for her to settle comfortably.

“This is good,” Chisa said. “You can start with the man at the shop.”

Lupa rested her chin near her shoulder and began describing him. She gave him an extravagant voice which Chisa knew at once had been invented for her benefit. By the time he reached the price, the harbour had acquired several additional customers and an argument about the rope required to restrain a particularly troublesome daughter.

Amy laughed from the passage as she went by.

“I heard that part. Save the ending; I'm coming back for my socks.”

“Bring the little towel when you do,” Lupa called. “I've had this woman's hair in my mouth for five minutes.”

Chisa moved the hair. Then she settled farther into Lupa's arm and listened to the rest of the story.

***

Suisui found the comb in the pocket where she had put it before leaving Jinzhou.

She stood holding it for a moment. Then she laid a towel across Yangyang's shoulders and drew the first loose section of hair over it.

“Tell me when I pull.”

“You have improved since the practice-sword years.”

“You sit considerably better.”

The Wind lifted a strand clear of the towel. Suisui held it between her fingers while she worked through the knot below.

They had put the shells in their cloth beside the bed. The Prism rested farther away, wrapped on the small table by the door. Dumpling had chosen the top of the wardrobe and was conducting a slow examination of a loose nail.

Yangyang watched the bird in the little mirror. Her own face looked strange beneath it, clean at last and tired around the mouth.

“Hsin let you see us growing old,” she said.

Suisui's hand slowed.

“At home. I was mending this cuff.” She touched the coat hanging on the bedpost. “You had Ma's flute. Your laugh had changed a little.”

Yangyang looked up.

“I heard myself like that at the cliff. There was rain in the courtyard. You had grey by your temple.”

“I should look very distinguished.”

“You were still doing my hair.”

Suisui drew the comb through to the ends and laid the smooth lock over Yangyang's shoulder.

“In Hsin's room, I asked whether you could leave. The evening would keep you just as I saw you. I stood there thinking how happy I could be for a very long time.”

Yangyang met her eyes in the mirror.

“I like coming home to you.”

“I know, darling.” Suisui swallowed. “I want to become better at enjoying the visits while you're having them. I spend half of them wondering when you'll be leaving.”

“You could come to Jinzhou more often. Baizhi has the key, and there are shops you would enjoy. Cia would go to the opera with you if you asked.”

“I learned her name properly over mushrooms today. It feels very soon to begin leading her astray.”

“I think she would manage.”

Suisui laughed and began on the other side.

Yangyang waited through three strokes of the comb.

“At the cliff, I nearly came apart,” she said.

The hand holding her hair became very still.

“My Resonance opened too far. I could hear other lives. I thought I could reach Chixia if I followed shijie into the air.”

The Wind settled against the towel at her shoulder.

Yangyang raised her hand to that place. “I understood how much you wanted to reach her. The whole road seemed to be there. Then I thought of coming through my own window, and I wanted to be the person sitting inside.”

Suisui put down the comb. She came around the stool and knelt in front of her sister, gathering the two hands carefully into her lap.

“Who reached you?”

“The bird. Then Liangyu and the Wardens. I caught her sleeve.”

Suisui bent over their hands. Yangyang could see the parting in her hair and the damp place where washing had loosened a strand.

“I was afraid to tell you,” Yangyang said. “You've spent so long trying to get here.”

Suisui looked up.

“I'd like to know the things that frightened you on the way. Even when I have to sit down to hear them.”

“You're already on the floor.”

“An excellent precaution.”

Yangyang's laugh shook. Suisui put her cheek against the hands in her lap, and Yangyang bent forward as far as her ribs allowed.

The Wind passed between them and settled beneath Suisui's chin.

For a while the older sister held what she could reach. Then Yangyang's fingers moved against her cheek.

“Could you come up here? I'd like to lean on you properly.”

They moved onto the bed. Suisui fitted a pillow behind Yangyang's back, let her settle against her, and brought the loose towel around the cold at her collar.

“I wanted to tell you I'd made friends,” Yangyang said after a while. “In the letters. I kept giving you their names and the places we'd been.”

“I enjoyed those parts.”

“I wanted you to know I was happy with them. I was also lonely some evenings. I thought it would sound as though they were treating me badly.”

Suisui stroked the hair she had finished combing.

“You can tell me about an evening. I can read it and wish I were sitting beside you.”

“Then you would come all this way.”

“I should like to do that sometimes. There's a room to picture now, and a key somebody will lend me.”

Yangyang turned her face against her shoulder.

Suisui waited until she had settled, then touched the damp edge of the towel.

“Mei is very dear to you.”

Warmth spread all the way to Yangyang's ears.

“She is. We've spoken about it. She loves Iuno, and she likes spending time with me. I asked her to be clear about what she wants.”

“What could she tell you?”

“About some of it. She said she had more to think about.” Yangyang's fingers closed on the towel. “I still want to take the walk.”

Suisui considered the back of her sister's head, resting where it had rested through a great many earlier difficulties.

“Then I'd like to hear about the birds afterward. And how you felt when you came home.”

Yangyang raised her head a little.

“You may receive a long letter.”

“You know how much I enjoy your handwriting.”

The Wind stirred beneath the towel. Suisui turned toward the cold.

“That includes you. When we have some words for you, I intend to become a very demanding correspondent.”

A corner of the towel brushed her lips.

“You may mention my faults. Your sister has already established the practice.”

Yangyang took the corner gently and laid it across her palm.

“I'd like to hear about the pears first, shijie. I've been trying to imagine the kitchen. Tomorrow, if you're rested, we can begin with what you can show us.”

The cold gathered at the centre of her hand. It remained there, a little weight held steady.

Suisui found the comb again.

“And I should like your opinion on whether we leave her hair loose tonight.”

The Wind lifted the ribbon off the towel and carried it to the bedpost.

Yangyang watched it arrive.

“You could have waited for my opinion as well.”

Suisui kissed the top of her head, smiling into the clean hair.

“Go on, darling. We're both listening.”

***

Amy had claimed the pillow nearest the wall by the time Mei and Iuno finished discussing where the water basin should go.

She sat on the bed with her knees drawn up, trying to work her fingers through a knot at the back of her head. Her borrowed nightshirt had sleeves long enough to cover her hands. Every few attempts she had to push them up again.

“There's a patch of the Skyworks in my hair,” she announced. “I'd like somebody with better access to take possession of it.”

Mei sat beside her and turned her gently by the shoulders.

“Come here. I thought we washed most of the mountain out downstairs.”

“This part had an excellent defensive position.”

Iuno brought a damp cloth from the basin. She laid it over the knot while Mei loosened the strands around it, then sat on Amy's other side with the comb.

Amy settled between them. Her shoulders came down by degrees.

“You'll have to leave the pin out tonight,” Iuno said. “Your scalp has had quite enough arrangements.”

“I fixed yours.”

“You did. It's the finest piece of engineering still attached to me.”

“I'm putting that in writing when my sleeves stay up.”

Mei rolled one sleeve twice and pressed the fold flat. Iuno handed her the cloth, took the freed section of hair, and began combing from the ends.

At the first tug, Amy tipped her head back to complain. Mei put her hand against the back of it, and Amy's weight stayed there a moment longer than the complaint required.

“You can lean,” Mei said. “I've got a very substantial wall behind me.”

Amy looked at the place where her mother's nightshirt lay over the healed wound.

“Which side is comfortable?”

“This one. Keep your elbow above my waist.”

Amy moved carefully. Mei drew her closer by the shoulder until she had her daughter's cheek against her chest and both of Amy's feet gradually taking over Iuno's side of the bed.

Iuno lifted one foot and placed it on the blanket she had folded for the purpose.

“There. Your mother gets your head. I appear to have acquired the touring end.”

“That end walked a very long way today.”

“Its difficulties have been reported to the management.”

She laid her hand over Amy's ankle. Amy let it rest there.

Mei drew the damp cloth gently along her hairline. A little grey dust came away on the corner.

For several strokes, Amy watched her mother's hand.

“When we got here, everybody kept touching you,” she said. “I knew why they wanted to. I kept watching where their hands went.”

Mei put the cloth down.

“You were watching at the benches too.”

“I was trying to decide when to tell them to move. Every time you made a sound, I thought it was happening again.” Amy pressed a finger into the seam of Mei's sleeve. “I've spent the whole afternoon wanting you to look after me. Then you'd sit down carefully and I'd start watching your stomach.”

Iuno's hand moved once along her ankle.

Mei bent to kiss the top of her head.

“I'm sore. I can wash your face and help with your hair. I can hold you right here.”

Amy lay still against her.

“I'd like you to complain when it hurts,” she said. “You can make a tremendous fuss. I'll recognize you immediately.”

Mei laughed, and the laugh pulled at her side. She drew a slow breath through it.

“There. That hurt. You'll have to tell the jokes farther apart.”

Amy's fingers tightened in the sleeve, then loosened.

Iuno reached for the discarded cloth.

“Your turn, darling. There's still a line of dust by your ear.”

Mei turned her face obediently. Iuno wiped it with the clean corner while Amy watched from below.

“She missed that in the washroom,” Amy said. “I was going to tell her and then someone took the last towel.”

“We've recovered from the setback,” Iuno said. “Will you lift your foot? I'm sitting on the hem of my own clothes.”

Amy moved both feet and nearly lost her place against Mei. Mei steadied her. Iuno freed the hem, all three of them shifted again, and the bed gave a prolonged creak.

They listened until it finished.

“I think that was an opinion about the wall,” Mei said.

“It was an opinion about three people trying to occupy one pillow,” Iuno replied. “Pass me the other one.”

Amy reached for it and caught sight of her bare ankles.

“My socks are still in the onion room. And Lupa promised the end of the story about the rope seller.”

She stayed where she was for another few breaths, then pushed herself upright with Mei's shoulder beneath her hand.

“Keep my place. I have very little faith in either of you around an unattended pillow.”

“Bring your cup back too,” Mei said. “You left it on the stairs.”

“The stairs were a temporary arrangement.”

Iuno held out the folded towel Lupa had asked for.

“Take this to her. If the story reaches the purchase of the pulley, do come and tell me.”

Amy grinned. She took the towel, stepped into the borrowed slippers, and went out with one heel already escaping.

Mei watched the door swing almost shut.

“She's going to bring half the house back.”

“We have a little time to enjoy the pillow.”

Iuno put it behind Mei, then leaned against her with a sigh that made Mei turn at once.

“Would another pillow help your shoulder?”

“I'd appreciate one. The ache has also reached the point where I'd like someone to be sympathetic.”

Mei pulled Amy's pillow into the space beside her and kissed Iuno's temple. “I can begin there.”

She eased an arm around Iuno's waist and let her choose how close to come. Iuno settled against her, living shoulder beneath Mei's cheek, the dressed crystal clear of the bedding.

For a while they listened to Amy's slippers retreat down the passage. Her voice reached Lupa's room, followed by the deeper reply and Chisa saying something which made her laugh.

Mei moved her hand over Iuno's.

“I wanted to tell you about tea before everything happened. Yangyang asked what I meant by holding her hand.”

Iuno turned her palm upward beneath Mei's.

“I remember you coming home. You spent some time putting a perfectly ordinary parcel of barley in the right cupboard.”

“I was thinking about how to begin.” Mei looked down at their hands. “I told her I love the life we have. You, and Amy leaving things on our bed, and coming home together. I also told her I wanted to spend time with her. I meant both.”

“And when you saw her today?”

“I wanted to hold her. I was relieved, and then she looked at me, and I wanted her to keep looking. I liked making her blush.”

Iuno breathed out slowly. Her thumb moved along the inside of Mei's wrist.

“I enjoyed hearing you laugh with her. I also felt jealous when Amy described your afternoon. I've had time to experience a rather broad selection of feelings.”

Mei turned toward her.

Iuno watched the concern enter her face, then touched her cheek.

“Stay here while I finish, darling. I'm still finding the words.”

Mei kissed her palm and waited.

“I like being the woman you come home to. I like knowing where your shoes will land and how long you'll spend looking for the tea I put in front of you. Today I spent hours wondering whether we would ever have another evening like that.” Iuno glanced at Amy's pillow. “Then we got here, and I wanted a great many things all at once.”

“I'd like to hear them.”

“I want your company tonight. I want you to tell me when your feelings for Yangyang change. And while we work out what we can each be happy with, I'd like us to keep kisses between us.”

Mei nodded, slowly enough that Iuno could watch her think.

“I can do that. I'll tell Yangyang what I can offer before we make another afternoon of it. She should have time to decide what she wants too.”

“I'd appreciate that.” Iuno let her hand rest against Mei's jaw. “The walk sounds lovely. I hope you get to see the birds.”

“I still want to take you places as well.”

“I should hope so. I've worn one new earring through an appalling quantity of dust.”

Mei's mouth softened. She touched the blue stone carefully, then kissed the skin below it.

*There we are,* the High Priestess murmured. *We can be sensible and have someone kiss our ear. An encouraging discovery.*

Iuno turned toward Mei's mouth. The kiss took its time, interrupted once by a little adjustment of the pillow and then resumed with Mei's hand warm at her waist.

When they drew apart, Mei rested her forehead against hers.

“I wanted you in the sluice,” she said. “Amy was doing so much. I wanted somebody to hold her while I worked out what to do.”

“I wanted to get my hand around yours. I kept remembering the canvas coming away.” Iuno's fingers closed in Mei's sleeve. “When the Wind went ahead to find Suisui, I told her where I'd wait. I sat there thinking you might still be reaching for me somewhere.”

Mei drew her closer.

“I was. You were all in so many directions.”

They held each other through the sound of a door opening farther along the passage. Mei's hand moved slowly over Iuno's back. Iuno let her head rest against her neck.

After a while, she said, “Will you help me take the pin out? Amy has done a magnificent job. I should like my scalp back now.”

Mei found it by touch. She worked it loose, caught the falling hair, and had just set the pin on the table when Amy knocked with her elbow.

“I have the cups and some bread. Lupa bought the pulley. Chisa says she left it with the man because she was already carrying too much.”

Iuno began to laugh.

Amy pushed the door open and surveyed the bed, the released hair, and her pillow in Mei's lap.

“I expected this. Lift that corner; I'm coming in.”

***

The bread had come wrapped in a clean cloth, with a little dish of sweet bean paste tucked into the fold.

Amy put it on the bedside table and began distributing everything else. Her socks went on the chair. The cups went beside the bread. Iuno's pin went into its pouch after Mei reached past it twice.

“You're organizing us,” Iuno observed.

“I'd like my breakfast tomorrow to contain a reasonable quantity of hairpins.”

Mei reached toward the plate.

“How many would you allow me?”

“You can negotiate with the cook. I'm having this piece.”

Amy sat between them and broke the bread. She gave Iuno the piece with the thinner crust, remembered Mei liked the thick edge, and took the soft middle for herself.

Mei accepted hers with a pleased little sound.

“I missed eating in bed with you.”

“We had breakfast in bed last week, Ma. You dropped something down your sleeve.”

“I missed it a great deal today.”

Amy looked at her, then reached for the paste and spread some on the crust in Mei's hand.

Iuno took her own share from the dish. They ate with the blanket across their knees. Through the open window came the sound of the watch changing and a woman calling up for someone to shake the last washcloth out before hanging it.

Amy began telling them about the rope seller. In Lupa's version, he had apparently recognized a champion immediately. Chisa had supplied the information that Lupa had pointed to herself on a poster.

Mei laughed with her whole face, keeping one hand against her side. Amy slowed down long enough for her to recover before describing the pulley.

Iuno finished her bread and brushed a crumb from Amy's sleeve.

“That part will improve greatly when Cia hears it.”

“Carte was listening through the doorway. By breakfast we may have an official account.”

Amy reached for her cup. Her hand shook against the handle, and she held it with both hands until the movement eased.

Mei rested a palm against her back.

Amy drank, set the cup down, and looked at the space they had prepared on the bed.

“I'd like to sleep here tonight,” she said. “I keep closing my eyes and hearing the wood move. I think I'll want you when I wake up.”

Mei put the plate aside and opened her arm.

“Come and try this side. We can move the pillow if you need it higher.”

Amy settled slowly, feeling for a place where her mother's body could take the weight comfortably. Iuno folded the spare blanket beneath her knees, then drew the cover over all three of them.

“I want you here too,” she said. “If you wake first, say my name. I'm likely to complain about the hour while I'm finding your hand.”

Amy turned her head on the pillow.

“You can be very convincing when you complain.”

“Years of practice. You'll appreciate the quality as you grow older.”

“I'm twenty-nine when it matters.”

Mei brushed the hair off her forehead. “You were nineteen when I asked who wanted the last sweet at supper.”

“That mattered as well.”

Iuno's laugh warmed the back of her neck.

They took some time getting comfortable. Mei needed to lie higher. Amy's foot found the cold wall. Iuno had to sit up again to move the cup before somebody's hair went into it, and afterward Mei helped arrange the pillow clear of her shoulder.

When they had settled, Amy had her head in the bend of Mei's arm and one hand resting in Iuno's.

Mei began humming under her breath. A small tune wandered through it, lost a turn, and found another way to the end.

“You're making that up,” Amy murmured.

“It's a new verse. Very exclusive.”

“It needs work on the middle.”

Iuno touched her thumb to Amy's knuckles. “You can advise her over breakfast. I should like to hear how it ends.”

Mei hummed the last part again, giving it a particularly elaborate finish. Amy smiled against her sleeve.

A soft knock came at the door. Suisui stood in the opening, carrying the little lamp from the corridor.

“Forgive me. Is there somewhere I can leave this? Yangyang would like a little light through the door, and I thought somebody might trip over it on the floor.”

Iuno nodded toward the narrow shelf beside the frame.

“Put it there. It reaches both rooms.”

Suisui lifted the lamp into place. Its glow ran along the passage and entered over their blanket in a thin golden strip.

“Thank you,” she said. Then she looked at the three of them and smiled. “I should like to sit with you at breakfast.”

Mei raised her head from the pillow.

“Come early. Amy knows how to get the best rice.”

“I was planning to sit beside her.”

“A sound decision,” Amy said sleepily. “Bring your bowl.”

Suisui returned to her room, leaving the door open to the lamp.

Mei settled again. Iuno reached across Amy to ease the blanket over Mei's shoulder, then brought her hand back where Amy's fingers were waiting.

Downstairs, the cook put tomorrow's rice to soak.
