## I. The Dispatch
### *Mingting, the Secretariat*

The relay came through at the fourth watch, and Wen carried it up the stairs in her nightclothes because the relay operator had used the priority seal, and the priority seal meant the Minister would want his orchid watered on the left side first, which was the side that received bad light and better news.

Shen Qiu read the dispatch standing, in the lamp-room, in his house robe, with his reading glasses on his nose and his watering can in his left hand.

"Septimont confirms. The *Gilded Swallow* has sailed. Five passengers, one cutting, one porcelain tea service." He lowered the page. "Wen. The protocol."

"The tea is already in transit, Minister. Sealed jars, Huanglong leaf. They left with the autumn shipment."

"The other protocol."

"Which other—"

"The one I haven't written yet." He watered the orchid. "Draft the following. To the Grand Marshal's office, with the Secretariat's chop: 'The kettle is on. Who pours?' To the Magistrate of Jinzhou, same chop: 'Guests approaching. Their filing is irregular. Their irregularity is structural. The structure holds.' To the Ministry of Sentinel Affairs: 'The approved count remains seven. Advise the filing system.'"

"The Sentinel Affairs note will cause—"

"The Sentinel Affairs note will cause the Minister to open his ninth drawer and close his seventh bottle, and both reactions are correct. Sign everything." He set down the can. "One more. Personal, unsealed, by courier to the harbormaster at the southern port."

Wen readied her brush.

"'Five guests. Irregular. One drinks Earl Grey from a portable porcelain service, which you will find insulting and endure. One has a wolf's tail and will terrify the customs queue, which you will manage. One carries a lute and will play it on your dock, which you will allow. One carries a camellia cutting in a terrarium, which you will stamp as agricultural import, class decorative, and wave through. And one—'" He paused, and the pausing was the orchid, and the orchid was the old man thinking, and the thinking was the Secretariat's finest product.

"'And one has gold eyes and will look at your harbor the way a person looks at a country she has been walking toward her whole life. Give her room to look. That's all. She has been given very little room, in the places she has been. We can start there.'"

He blotted the page. Outside, Mingting's dawn came up the way it always did: bells from the river wards, then bells from the hill wards, then the whole city ringing agreement with itself, and the agreement, this morning, had a note in it that Wen could hear and the Minister could hear and the orchid, for all she knew, could hear — the note a country makes when its weather is about to change, and it has decided, for once, to leave the windows open.

## II. The Atmospheric Reading
### *Jinzhou, the Gorges of Spirits*

Qiuyuan stood at the rim of the Gorges at dawn with his instruments and his margin and his wrong tea, and the frequencies said the same thing they had said every dawn for three months: Geshu Lin's signature was terminated.

And the wind said otherwise.

It came off the north face in a thin rush, tasting of stone and ice and the particular ozone of a battlefield that has been fought on and rained on and fought on again, and Qiuyuan's Forte translated it the way it translated everything — into light, into color, into the visual grammar of the world's hidden weather — and in the light at the edge of the Gorges, at the farthest reach of his reading, where the instruments said *noise* and the corridor said *acceptable:*

A thread. Silver-white. Martial. The hue of a drawn blade.

He had been seeing it for six weeks. A thread, at the limit of resolution, that appeared at dawn and faded by the first watch, that followed the contour of the north face the way a river follows a valley it carved, that matched — to within one spectral line — the last recorded signature of a man the Bureau had filed under *terminated.*

The instruments said noise. The instruments were excellent. The instruments had a resolution ceiling, and the ceiling was the Bureau's budget, and the budget was the corridor, and the corridor said: the reading is atmospheric, the atmosphere is weather, and weather is filed and forgotten.

Qiuyuan filed and remembered.

He wrote in his margin: *Day 97. Thread present, dawn window, north quadrant. Duration: forty-three minutes. Spectral match: 99.2%. The 0.8% discrepancy may be instrument error. The 0.8% discrepancy may also be a man who has been holding a wall for three years and whose signature has weathered.*

He capped his pen. Below the rim, Jinzhou woke in its bowl of water and mountain, and the sluice-song came up from the ward schools — seven stairs, Gran says hi — and on the far side of the Gorges, past the dark, past the noise, past the Bureau's resolution ceiling, something held.

This morning's dispatch found him at the garrison canteen. Jinhsi's hand on the cover note: *The Secretariat confirms arrival. The kettle is on. The Grand Marshal asks who pours.*

And beneath, in the Magistrate's smaller script: *Your atmospheric readings. Ninety-seven days. Have you found what you're looking for?*

He turned the note over. On the back, in the same hand: *She breaks scripts. That is the dispatch's whole biography. She went into the void and the void revised. You are standing at the rim of a void and asking it to revise. The arithmetic is the same. The scale is different.*

*When she arrives, bring her to the Gorges. Let her read the air. Her frequency refuses acceptable losses, and the Gorges have been accepting one for three years.*

*Let us see what her arithmetic does to our weather.*

He put the note in his coat, beside the margin, beside the wrong tea. The dawn thread had faded by now, as it always did. But the fading, he had learned, was a property of his instruments, and his instruments had a ceiling, and somewhere past the ceiling, past the 0.8%, past the corridor's resolution:

A blade, still drawn. A wall, still held. A man the filing system had called *acceptable,* holding the gate at the end of the world, waiting for someone whose reading had better range.

## III. The Fox's Mail
### *Mengzhou, Bookbinders' Row*

The sorts arrived at dawn.

The night porter found them in the 來 case — the character for *come, arrive, approach* — and the case was full, impossibly full, sorts stacked two deep, some of them in faces he recognized (the Row's own type, familiar as family teeth) and some in a metal he had seen exactly once before, in the tray the wind had paid her evening's fare from. The wind's own sorts, spent and recovered, re-entering the cases.

He counted. Thirty-one. One short of the grid the fox had posted last winter (留, *stay*, full sheet, the broadsheet that had stopped the drifting). He checked every case in the compositing room, shelf by shelf, and found the thirty-second sort at the bottom of the 風 case — *wind* — face down, worn almost flat, the thinnest character he had ever handled. He picked it up with the bamboo tongs, the way the Row handles type that has been through weather, and read it.

梅。*Plum.*

He set it in the 來 case with the others and went to the stone and began compositing, because the sorts had arranged themselves into a sentence on the way in, and the sentence was the fox's mail, and the mail was the city's morning edition, and the morning edition, this morning, read:

*The plum arrives at the youngest gate. Mengzhou sets the table. The Row prints the welcome. The road through Xuanfang is open.*

*Bring many hands, she was told. She brings six, and one is a wind's.*

He printed the broadsheet — one copy, on paper light enough to fly — and posted it on the Row's bulletin board, where the 留 broadsheet had hung all winter, and stood back and read it in the clear light of the morning, and the paper lifted once in a draft from the east, and settled, and held.

Then he swept the compositing stone, as his grandfather had, as the courtesy required. And on the stone, in hawthorn residue, a new annotation in the paw-print he knew better than his own:

*Clear the guest room over the garden. She'll want the window facing east. Hsin's invoice: one (1) opera ticket, premier seating, and a cup at Master Ping's. The hinge cup will do. Add it to my tab.*

He swept the annotation, as the courtesy required. He kept the tab, as the partnership required. And the hawthorn candy he set on the stone that evening was the good kind, the kind from the terrace stand where the vendor's daughter counted change for foxes, and by morning it was gone, and the stone was clean, and the partnership was forty years old, and counting.

## IV. The Leaning Lamp
### *Jinzhou, the crest wall of the dam*

Keeper Pei heard the weather change at the second watch.

The lamp at the northeast shoulder — the one that had been leaning east for three years, the one he trimmed every night and logged as *maintenance, habit* — stood straight.

He looked at it. He looked at it a long time. He had kept this lamp through three years of leaning, through a wind that came and went on its own schedule, through a warmth that arrived at the flame and stayed a moment past its own momentum, and the leaning had been his address, and the address had been the wind's, and the whole arrangement had operated on a protocol older than the Bureau: you tell a lingering wind your dead, and the wind carries and keeps.

The lamp stood straight, and Pei understood. The wind was gathering. The wind was pulling itself in from every draft and every doorway and every eave and every parapet where she had lingered, and the pulling was the spending, and the spending was the fare, and the fare was for the last crossing: west to east, sea to gate, letter to hand, the appointment she had been saving her remaining sorts to keep.

He trimmed the lamp. It stood straight. The trimming was for her, and the straightness was for him, and the difference was the whole of what they had shared, three years of it, told and kept.

"Safe crossing," he said to the air. And then, because he was a keeper and keepers keep what they keep: "I'll hold the lamp."

He wound the wick. He cleaned the glass. He set the flame to the brightness the night required, and stood at the post, and watched the lake, and the lake was calm, and the calm was the dragon sleeping, and the dragon was dreaming of a city that would soon hold six strangers and a wind and a war god and an arithmetic that did sums the corridor had filed under *unacceptable,* and the keeper at the wall held his lamp and trimmed it and called the trimming faith, and the faith was the infrastructure, and the infrastructure held.

## V. The Lake
### *between the dam and the deep, at the turning of the watch*

Far below the keeper's lamp, in the roots of the range, the dragon turned over in his sleep.

The displacement was gentle, enormous. One wave crossed the whole surface of the lake, warm as tea, and the lamps along the crest wall guttered once, together, and steadied, and the night watch logged: *weather: calm; lake: attentive.*

Jue had been counting. This is what the horn does — the first joint, the cutting edge, the one who holds the branching timelines in his memory like plates in a printer's shop. He had been counting the versions of this arrival since the wind first crossed his water, three years ago, carrying a compass needle that pointed at a woman he had been dreaming about in someone else's story.

Fourteen versions ended at the gate. In twelve of those, the city held, and the word *held* did the same work it always did — look at your hands. In two, the gate opened the wrong way. He kept all fourteen in the deep, turning them over, the way a keeper turns a lamp, checking the oil.

This version — the one running, the one arriving — had a property the other fourteen lacked. In this version, the woman who was coming had already refused the arithmetic twice, and been refused by the arithmetic once, and had revised the instruments once, and had punched a god once, and had been caught four times, and had caught others six, and the counting of the catching was Jue's whole interest, because the catching was the variable the other versions had missed.

In twelve versions, the city held because a blade held. In two, it fell because a blade fell. In this version, the city would hold because a hand caught. He was almost certain. The almost was the 0.8%, and the 0.8% was the weather, and the weather had opinions.

**THEY'RE COMING,** Jue said, to the water, to the roots, to the seven-way frequency that ran below the filing system's reach, the one the Ministry logged as *tectonic, normal.*

Across the country, in the warm stones of a shrine terrace, a fox lifted one ear.

*I know,* Hsin said. *I set the table.*

**THE WAR GOD COMES BEHIND THEM. THE NINTH DRUM. THE DEBT-COLLECTOR.**

*I know that too. My wall is walking. I sent them early.* A pause with four thousand years in it. *She was told to bring many hands. She brings the kind that catch. It is a different arithmetic, kinsman. I've been watching it compound.*

**THE CORRIDOR WILL FILE THEM.**

*The corridor will try.* The fox's amusement moved through the deep frequency like a fish through dark water. *The corridor has been trying to file weather for four hundred years. Weather grows a second verse in the dark. The Secretariat knows. The Magistrate knows. The girl on the dam knows, the one who stands at the northeast shoulder at first light, checking a distance for someone particular.* A tail flicked, somewhere above the stones, in the warm evening. *Even the corridor knows. The corridor is one lamp longer than it used to be. The lamp leans east.*

**AND THE WIND?**

*The wind is spending her last sorts on the fare.* Hsin's voice went quieter, which meant it went older, which meant the Heart was speaking from the joint where the body had broken and the breaking had been, by the architect's design, the strongest edge. *She will arrive with them, or ahead of them, or inside the letter she is carrying, and the arriving will cost her the rest of herself, and the Magistrate's girl will be standing at the northeast shoulder, and the wind will find her, and the once will happen.*

A long silence in the deep. The counting of versions.

**SHE CARRIES A NAME SHE HAS HALF-FINISHED,** Jue said. **THE SORTS FOR THE REST OF IT CRACKED IN THE MOLD.**

*Then the mold is wrong.* Hsin stood, and stretched, and was several shapes at once the way she was when she stopped being careful, and then she was a fox-eared woman with hawthorn on her breath, looking out at the Bulwark, where the third generation was already marching, shield-shapes moving against the stars, old faithful iron walking toward the youngest gate because a fox had asked them to, forty years ago, and they were still answering. *We'll cut new sorts. The Row has the founders. The girl has the hand. Between them they'll set the whole name, and the setting will be the mold, and the mold will hold, because the mold will be Jinzhou, and Jinzhou holds. That is the word. Look at your hands.*

The lake was quiet. The lamps burned. The keeper trimmed.

And in the deep, the dragon who commanded time looked at every branching future — every version, every plate, every city-that-falls and city-that-stands — and chose the one where a hand catches, and held it, and the holding was the whole of him, and the holding was the gate, and the gate was open.

**COUNT THE RETURNING,** Jue said.

And the city, waking into dawn, began.

## VI. The Harbor
### *southern port, the morning of*

The harbormaster read the Secretariat's dispatch twice and the personal note three times, and went down to the dock at first light, and the dock was already occupied.

Qiuyuan, ISA, stood at the end of the longest pier with his instruments and his margin and his coat collar turned up against the sea air, reading the eastern horizon the way he read everything: by the light things left behind.

"You're early," the harbormaster said.

"I'm on duty."

"Your duty is in Jinzhou."

"My duty," Qiuyuan said, "is atmospheric." He lowered the instrument. On the eastern horizon, where the sea met the sky, a sail had appeared — small, foreign-rigged, making for the harbor on a wind that should have been westerly and was, by every reading available, dead calm. The sail moved anyway. The sail moved against the weather, the way kites move against the wind's own grain when the wind is carrying them personally.

The harbormaster looked at the sail. He looked at Qiuyuan. He looked at his own dispatch, with its five guests and its portable tea service and its instruction to give a gold-eyed woman room to look.

"How many cups?" the harbormaster asked.

"Seven," Qiuyuan said. "Five on the ship. One in the wind. And one—" He lifted the instrument again. The spectral reading was golden, and warm, and it arrived at his retinas the way dawn arrives at water: all at once, everywhere, refusing the horizon's arithmetic. "One for the harbor."

The harbormaster went inside and put the kettle on.

The sail came in on wind that was carrying it. The harbor was ready. The kettle was on. The gate was open. The lamp was trimmed. The sorts were set. The table was laid. The wall was walking. The dragon was counting. The fox was smiling. The margin was wide enough. The corridor was one lamp longer. The sluice-song was in the children's mouths. The cheese was in the Censorate. The tea was in the jars. The file was open. The candy was sour. The bench was wide enough for nine. The bell-ringer was climbing.

And the wind — the wind that had been a woman, and before that a soldier, and before that a girl in a courtyard cutting at a post she would spend her whole life failing to reach — spent her last sort on the easterly that filled the sail, and the sort was 家, and 家 means *home,* and the sail came in.
