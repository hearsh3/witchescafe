# INTERLUDE — RETURNING CALLS

The rain came down in the usual direction on Wednesday morning.

Mrs Ren’s guesthouse had one long-distance terminal, one chair placed beneath it, and a line of seven people extending through the front room and halfway up the stairs. Iuno had booked the first hour in four-minute blocks. Amy had altered the booking to read **HOUSEHOLD, WITH DEPENDENTS**, then drawn a star beside it in violet pencil.

Lupa read the amendment over her shoulder.

<font color="#ff9742">“Abby lives inside Mei and eats off everybody’s plate. Put him under livestock.”</font>

A gold eye opened above Mei’s Tacet Mark.

<font color="#ffe3f1">“I HAVE ACHIEVED RESIDENCY UNDER COLUMN TWELVE, WOLF. YOUR ADMINISTRATIVE MALICE STOPS AT MY BORDER.”</font>

<font color="#ffdead">“He learned jurisdiction yesterday,”</font> Mei said, sounding proud.

Iuno pressed the call key before the discussion acquired case law.

---

In Lahai-Roi, Helios shone across a dormitory floor covered in open textbooks, gun-cleaning cloth, and three shades of spray paint. Lynae reached the terminal first. Rebecca came over the back of the sofa and landed beside her hard enough to shake the image into squares.

<font color="greenyellow">“Turn the camera. I’m counting everybody myself.”</font>

Amy took the terminal from Iuno and walked it down the line. Lupa raised two fingers. Ciaccona displayed her plum-colored hand with theatrical distaste. Cartethyia crowded into frame with her terrarium. Chisa lifted the bowl she was eating from. Mei waved her stitched hand, earned six simultaneous glares, and lowered it again.

Rebecca counted under her breath. At seven, her shoulders dropped.

Lynae had remained very still through the tour. A crescent of dried amber paint cracked across one knuckle when she flexed her hand.

<font color="palegoldenrod">“The public bulletin used the phrase ‘orderly supernatural withdrawal.’ Then every military channel went quiet for six hours. I had a bike fueled, Rebecca had ammunition on my desk, and Professor Mornye had already worked out which wall she planned to drive through.”</font>

<font color="greenyellow">“The vent gives us a clean entry,”</font> Rebecca said. <font color="greenyellow">“We would’ve made great time.”</font>

<font color="#ff9742">“Save the fuel. We’re coming home after the physicians finish complaining.”</font> Lupa leaned close enough for one red eye to fill the screen. <font color="#ff9742">“You both ate?”</font>

Lynae angled the terminal toward two empty noodle cartons.

<font color="palegoldenrod">“Rebecca called this breakfast. I supplied vegetables in self-defense.”</font>

<font color="greenyellow">“The onions were green. That counts twice.”</font>

Abby rose over Mei’s shoulder in a shower of small gold facets. Rebecca’s grin arrived with such force that Lynae shifted the terminal away from her face.

<font color="greenyellow">“Oh, that guy rules. Bring him back before some lab gets ideas.”</font>

<font color="#ffe3f1">“THE SHORT WOMAN HAS EXCELLENT DISCERNMENT.”</font>

Rebecca pointed at the screen. <font color="greenyellow">“He gets my chair. Anybody tries to put him in a jar, call me first.”</font>

Chisa’s head turned at the change in Lynae’s breathing.

<font color="coral">“We are safe,”</font> she said. <font color="coral">“You can unpack the bag under your bed.”</font>

Lynae glanced down. Her bare heel nudged a canvas strap farther into shadow.

<font color="palegoldenrod">“It’s staying packed until I hear your bikes at the gate.”</font>

The operator’s clock flashed red. Rebecca slapped one palm to the glass. Lupa placed hers against the field terminal, broad fingers covering half the little hand on the screen.

Then the circuit moved on.

---

Chisa carried the terminal to the window. Elsewhere in Lahai-Roi, ordinary rain tapped a noodle stand’s awning.

Her mother answered with the camera aimed at a noodle stand and two steaming bowls.

*“Kuchiba Chiyo,”* she said, clear and level, giving the name before the image settled. *“Tell me what your hands are doing.”*

<font color="coral">“My left hand is holding breakfast. My right hand has the terminal. The bandage is dry.”</font>

*“Who had hold of you when the ground became a stage?”*

<font color="coral">“Lupa had my belt. Cartethyia had the red cord. When the floor released us, Ciaccona was close enough for our shoulders to touch.”</font> Chisa ran her thumb once along the bowl’s rim. <font color="coral">“I used the sewing blade on a soldier’s bowl. He carried it broken for three years.”</font>

Chiyo set her chopsticks across the second bowl. *“Write the hour and every person tied to that bowl. Bring the page to the table on Thursday.”*

<font color="coral">“Du carried it, and his friend was Rui. I will bring them.”</font>

The stand owner entered the edge of the picture, listened, and placed a third empty bowl beside Chiyo’s elbow.

Chiyo glanced at it. Her fingers touched the warm rim.

*“Your road adds seats.”*

<font color="coral">“It has become crowded.”</font>

*“Good. Crowded people still need lunch. Come hungry.”*

Chisa’s mouth moved at one corner. The screen went dark while the third bowl still held the light.

---

Augusta answered on the second ring.

She sat at her kitchen table in Septimont with a campaign map folded beneath a seed catalogue. Two cups steamed beside the terminal. Galbrena’s hand entered from offscreen, turned the second handle toward Augusta, and withdrew.

<font color="#cbd369">“Your dispatch accounted for the column. It gave me three lines concerning you.”</font>

<font color="#5881fe">“Baizhi wrote the medical appendix. Six pages, with diagrams.”</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">“Iuno threw her ring at Ma,”</font> Amy said from Iuno’s left. <font color="#ff8ff4">“It struck the sword hand and broke the stage direction. I entered it under field improvisation.”</font>

Mei leaned in from the right, cheek almost against Iuno’s. <font color="#ffdead">“Excellent throw. Very romantic.”</font>

*We once addressed the Senate from a balcony of black marble,* the High Priestess sighed. *Now we conduct jewelry reviews between our family’s heads.*

Augusta’s gaze dropped to Iuno’s hand. The returned ring caught the kitchen light. A length of copper wire beside the seed catalogue curled slowly around Augusta’s forefinger and formed a small open circle.

<font color="#cbd369">“How is your shoulder?”</font>

Iuno turned the crystal toward the lens. A clean dressing covered the place where rain had drawn blood from it.

<font color="#5881fe">“It has sensation. Baizhi regards the development as medically significant and personally irritating. The pain is manageable.”</font>

<font color="#ffdead">“She slept eight hours,”</font> Mei contributed.

<font color="#ff8ff4">“She also drank water before tea. I supervised.”</font>

Iuno looked left, then right. <font color="#5881fe">“I had prepared a concise report.”</font>

Augusta lifted her cup, and the iron line of her mouth softened into private amusement.

<font color="#cbd369">“Huanglong held you in one place for four minutes and taught you to call afterward. I approve of their doctrine.”</font> She pressed the copper circle flat with her thumb. <font color="#cbd369">“Come to my table when your physician clears the road. Bring the resident, the ring, and every page Amy has filed. Angel will set the places.”</font>

From somewhere beyond the picture, Galbrena said, “The long table seats nine.”

Augusta looked toward her. The wire bent into a second circle.

<font color="#cbd369">“Bring another chair,”</font> she told the room, and ended the call with her hand resting beside the spare cup.

---

The Ragunna circuit opened onto the Guild office after closing.

Zani sat behind the intake desk with a fresh folder open. Phoebe held a pastry over its lid and watched the crumbs with clerical concentration. Laevatain occupied two chairs and had placed her boots on a third. Her stabilizer spheres turned at an easy pace around her waist.

Zani’s pen touched the page. <font color="#d8b36a">“Start with the phrase ‘consent with a stated limit.’ I want the clause text, its witnesses, and the review date.”</font>

<font color="#5881fe">“The full document reached your office eleven minutes ago.”</font>

Zani checked the receiver tray. A roll of paper had reached the floor and continued beneath Laevatain’s chair.

<font color="#d8b36a">“Your copy arrived. This resident has generated more governance than three coastal towns.”</font>

Phoebe moved the pastry over her plate. <font color="#f2c4dc">“How did the road feel when the rain thinned?”</font>

Amy took that one. <font color="#ff8ff4">“Quiet enough to hear boots. Some of them talked. Some watched the lamps. A signalman walked outside the line, and a Ranger matched him step for step.”</font>

Phoebe’s fingers closed around the little sun locket at her waist. She nodded once and set the pastry down whole.

Laevatain leaned toward the screen. <font color="#b9a882">“Show me the weapon hand.”</font>

Ciaccona held up the bruised palm.

<font color="#b9a882">“You get the strings changed by somebody else. Four days. The hand gets a holiday even if the bard throws a tantrum.”</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">“My tantrums scan beautifully.”</font>

<font color="#b9a882">“They scan in common time. Take the holiday.”</font>

A gold muzzle pushed into the bottom of the picture.

<font color="#ffe3f1">“I REQUIRE A RAGUNNESE EMBASSY, THREE MEALS, AND AN EXPLANATION OF WHY YOUR TIEFLING HAS FILED ME UNDER DEPENDENTS.”</font>

Zani opened a second folder. Phoebe’s face lit with frank delight. Laevatain swung her boots to the floor.

<font color="#d8b36a">“This goes in column twelve,”</font> Zani said. <font color="#d8b36a">“Resident, self-directed, dictation accepted, standard rates.”</font>

Abby drew breath for a speech. Iuno ended the call and wrote *diplomatic incident deferred* beside the time.

---

Ciaccona waited for the cheap hour, then called everybody.

The terminal divided itself into windows. Carlotta joined from a Council desk with three secretaries moving behind her. Cantarella appeared among new camellia stakes at Porto-Veno. Brant answered from a ship’s galley; Roccia hung over his shoulder upside down. Buling brought her copper bell. Hiyuki sat beneath the open eaves of her shrine with snowlight on the floor. Zani routed the Guild back in and charged the call to Commission One.

The moment the faces settled, they began speaking across one another. Carlotta demanded a usable account for the morning edition. Brant offered a toast involving three ports and a cannon. Cantarella asked Cartethyia to turn her head toward the light so she could inspect the cut at her temple. Roccia wanted every detail about the stage collapsing. Buling rang twice for quiet and achieved another layer of sound.

Ciaccona rested her battered lute against her knee.

<font color="#77f8c0">“I saved one beat for the end of the road. Here it is.”</font>

Her injured hand made the note rough. The relay carried the roughness faithfully: through the Council chamber, the greenhouse, the galley, the Guild, and the shrine.

Hiyuki raised her bell and answered once.

Cantarella’s hand remained on a camellia stake. Carlotta took a fresh sheet from a secretary and wrote the first line herself. Brant lowered his cup. Buling closed her fingers around the bell’s waist. At the Guild, Phoebe reached beneath the desk and found Zani’s hand.

Cartethyia listened over Ciaccona’s shoulder, waited for the circuit to breathe, and whispered, <font color="#b8ffff">“The bridge is still a little flat.”</font>

Ciaccona turned her head. <font color="#77f8c0">“I fought a war yesterday. Grant me until rehearsal.”</font>

Roccia’s laughter reached them first. The rest followed, each at its own delay, and filled the terminal’s small speaker to distortion.

---

Uncle Cosimo answered from Egla Town with flour to both elbows and the bakery ovens bright behind him.

Cartethyia sat up straighter. The crystal leaves of her laurel turned once.

<font color="#b8ffff">“Uncle, I said my name in a god’s theatre. It held all the way through.”</font>

Cosimo folded the dough beneath his palms and gave it a quarter-turn.

“Your front door has carried that name for twenty-three years. The hinges remain sound.” He studied the bandage at her temple, then the terrarium under her arm. “How many are coming to supper?”

<font color="#b8ffff">“Seven from the road, two at the dormitory, and Abby lives inside Mei. He eats four portions when excited.”</font>

Cosimo reached for a second bowl of dough.

“I’ll make twelve. Roads develop appetites.”

<font color="#b8ffff">“Please make the lemon cakes with the sugar crystals. Cia will claim one and call it quality control.”</font>

“Rosa already started them. She heard the evening bulletin and doubled the batch.” He dusted his hands, came close to the terminal, and laid his flour-white palm against the glass. “Come through the door when you arrive, child. I’ll keep the ovens warm.”

Cartethyia put her smaller hand over his.

The clock gave them twenty seconds. They spent every one of them there.

---

Far beyond the relay maps, at the black-sand house of the Banat al-Rih, seven cards had lain across the long table since the first drumbeat.

The Chariot held two cords taut. Death carried gold along one shear. Strength knelt in rain under her smaller name. The Hanged Man kept an open space in the music. Judgement had lost a bright facet from one shoulder. The Star bent over the Fool with a relay at her ear.

Black Swan turned the last card when Ciaccona’s rough note reached the sea.

Herta dated the margin in her third color of ink. Viviane kept two fingers in a shallow bowl and felt the rings widen. Phrolova stood in the doorway through the whole of her hour. Mina came from the kitchen carrying a stack of plates.

“How many came through?” she asked.

Herta looked from the cards to the names arriving across her page, one careful line at a time.

“The Jinzhou register closes the column at one thousand nine hundred seven. Their household gained one resident. The world has begun answering.”

Mina set the long table.

Outside, the wind moved east through the grass. In Jinzhou, Lahai-Roi, Septimont, Ragunna, and Egla Town, kettles went on in five different kitchens, each for its own reason, each at the same hour.
