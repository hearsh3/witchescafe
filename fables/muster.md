# Muster
### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

天下興亡，匹夫有責。
*The rise and fall of all under heaven — even the common person bears responsibility.*
— Gu Yanwu

---

## I.

Mei read the letter twice on the rooftop with her daughter watching and her hands perfectly steady, which was how you knew.

The type was honest. The ink was deep-pressed, set by founders who read the sorts aloud as they went in, because that was the courtesy. And between the girl's careful sentences — the willows, the dam at dusk, the patrols noting a gust indoors — there were blanks. Characters the type could not hold. Sorts that cracked in the mold, the way certain words crack when they have traveled too far from their country.

*To the one the wind is looking for —*

The girl wrote about her city the way people write about a person they are trying to introduce to a friend. With pride. With the particular careful brightness of someone who knows the friend will see the cracks and is asking them to look at the window boxes anyway.

She wrote about the lake. She wrote about her sister's letter, the practice post, the mark her teacher would have liked. She wrote about standing on the northeast cliff at dawn in a hat her friends had chosen, checking the distance for someone particular.

She wrote: *I have left my window open on windy nights. I do not know what I am waiting for. I think you do.*

And then, at the end, set in a hand that had practiced the sentence for a season before committing it:

*The water is showing us a year that belongs to someone else. The patrols write "weather" in the cause column. I am writing to you because weather is not a cause. It is a warning, and warnings are for the people who can still move.*

Mei folded the letter along its original crease.

Amy leaned in. <font color=mistyrose>"Ma?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Get everyone up."</font>

---

## II.

They gathered the way they always gathered: badly, loudly, with tea.

Lupa arrived first because Lupa always arrived first, which she denied, which was itself a kind of arriving. Ciaccona came from the practice room with rosin dust at her collar and her braced hand held slightly away from her body as though she were carrying something fragile, which she was. Cartethyia came with the stick and two pastries and a question already forming that she would ask in the wrong order. Chisa came last and sat closest to the door, which Lupa noticed and Chisa let her.

Lynae leaned against the kitchen counter with her arms crossed and her weight on one foot, the stance of someone who has already read the room's temperature and is waiting for it to be said.

Iuno stood behind Mei's chair, which was as much beside her as the room's geometry allowed.

Mei put the letter on the table. Then the shell. Then the kite — red paper, light bamboo, its tail wound neatly around the spar, the way you pack a thing you do not intend to fly again.

<font color=indianred>"The wind,"</font> Lupa said.

<font color=moccasin>"The wind."</font>

<font color=indianred>"When."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Three weeks ago,"</font> Iuno said. <font color=royalblue>"On the balcony. Past midnight. She spent a word getting through the door."</font>

Lupa's eyes moved to Iuno and stayed there for a beat that had teeth in it.

<font color=indianred>"Three weeks."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I needed to hear all of it before I brought it to this table."</font>

<font color=indianred>"And the rest of us needed three weeks of sleep."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Yes."</font>

The sound Lupa made was very quiet and involved her jaw. She looked away. She looked back.

<font color=indianred>"Go."</font>

Iuno told it. All of it. The balcony, the guest cup, the steam that leaned the wrong way. The wind's voice — thin, purchased syllable by syllable from a budget going to zero. The name: *Ovathrax.* The Threnodian of War. Coming to the youngest gate, to a city on a lake called Jinzhou that was already being remembered by something it had yet to meet.

And the wind's correction, offered at nearly the last of the night's purse: *Her to choose.*

The table absorbed it the way tables do. Someone's cup went cold. Nobody touched the pastries.

<font color=skyblue>"How big,"</font> Cartethyia said.

<font color=royalblue>"A Threnodian. Full-class."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Like —"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Like what we walked into."</font> Iuno's voice stayed level, which cost the same every time. <font color=royalblue>"A war god. A walking siege. Jinzhou has walls, a garrison, and a Sentinel, and the wind watched them hold once and spent herself to nothing making sure there would be a second time."</font>

Chisa's hand went to her throat and came back down.

<font color=teal>"What's the ask,"</font> Ciaccona said.

<font color=moccasin>"There's no ask."</font> Mei had her chin on her hand, looking at the kite. <font color=moccasin>"That's the whole shape of it. A girl at a window writing to someone she hasn't met. A wind carrying a letter because it's the last thing she can carry."</font>

<font color=teal>"Mei."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"No ask. Just the facts."</font> Mei sat up. She picked up the shell and turned it in her hands the way you turn a stone you've been keeping in your pocket for years. <font color=moccasin>"Jinzhou's going to burn. The people in it are going to fight. And a woman who turned herself into weather so she could cross between worlds spent her last real words getting a letter here so that I'd know it was happening."</font>

<font color=indianred>"And?"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"And I'm going."</font>

She said it the way she said everything — too fast, too bright, a decision that had arrived in the gap between heartbeats and was already being loaded onto a bike.

<font color=royalblue>"We knew,"</font> Iuno said. To the room. To the cup. <font color=royalblue>"We have known since the kite cleared the passage."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Cost,"</font> Lynae said, from the counter, in the voice she used for estimates.

Everyone looked at her.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm not objecting. I'm pricing."</font> Lynae pushed off the counter and pulled a chair around backward, arms across the back. <font color=palegoldenrod>"Transit to Jinzhou: three weeks by the Mingting route, if the marshal's berth is still warm. Materiel: what we carry. Intelligence: one letter, one shell, and a ghost's word. Medical: one physician with a rolling stool, who is not coming. Tactical posture: seven-to-nine walking into a theatre we've never seen, against something that eats cities."</font>

She held up one finger.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"And the bill comes back to this table, to the people sitting at it."</font>

Silence. The ventilation changed shifts.

<font color=coral>"Seven,"</font> Chisa said.

Lynae's head turned.

<font color=coral>"You said seven to nine. You've already done the roster."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm in pricing. I price everything."</font>

<font color=coral>"Seven going. Two staying."</font>

Lynae said nothing, which was itself a ledger entry.

---

## III.

The list went around the table the way lists do in that house: with argument, with tea, with Cartethyia asking a question that turned out to be the right one at the worst possible time.

<font color=skyblue>"Who stays."</font>

The kitchen got very specific.

Mei and Iuno: obvious. The letter came to Mei. Iuno followed Mei. That arithmetic had been settled on a purple evening with an arrow and was not up for review.

Lupa: obvious. Lupa went where the pack went, and the pack went where Mei went, and every link in that chain was forged at a temperature that did not invite renegotiation.

Ciaccona and Cartethyia looked at each other across the table with the fluency of people who had been conducting this particular conversation for nine years and several lifetimes' worth of walking together.

<font color=teal>"Don't."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I'm coming."</font>

<font color=teal>"You have a stick."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I have a stick and three blades and a goddess who was *fourteen centimeters taller than Amy's mech*, Cia."</font>

<font color=teal>"Shorter. She was shorter."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"THE MEASUREMENT STANDS."</font>

Ciaccona closed her eyes. Opened them. Her braced hand sat on the table between them, palm-down, the way you set down a card you wish you weren't holding.

<font color=teal>"Ninety minutes a day,"</font> she said. Very quietly. <font color=teal>"We'll be walking into something that takes longer than ninety minutes."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I'll carry the timer."</font>

<font color=teal>"That's not —"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I'll carry it. The way I always carry it. And at ninety minutes I will take it from you, and at ninety-one minutes you will argue, and at ninety-two minutes you will stop, because I carried you down a bridge and I'll carry you down anything else, and that is also a stick."</font>

Ciaccona put both hands in her lap and looked at the ceiling.

<font color=teal>"Fine."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Say it in a normal voice,"</font> Lupa said.

<font color=teal>"I will outlive all of you."</font>

Amy had been sitting on the counter with her legs swinging, watching. She looked at Mei. Mei was looking at the kite.

<font color=mistyrose>"I'm going."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"We haven't gotten to you yet."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"We don't need to."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Amy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

The thing that moved between them was old and heavy and had a blue door painted on it.

<font color=moccasin>"You just got back."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I know."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Three weeks."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I can count."</font>

Mei's jaw worked. Her hand found the shell again, turning it, turning it.

<font color=royalblue>"She has five meters of restored guard platform and a lance,"</font> Iuno said. Practical. True. <font color=royalblue>"She has a set of rules this table wrote together. She has hands on her in the dark and a felt doll and a clock and Chisa on the frequency."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's not the —"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"That is the argument, and you will let it be the argument, because the other one is yours and you cannot make it for her."</font>

Mei looked at Iuno. Iuno held the look. Behind her sternum the High Priestess was doing arithmetic.

Amy slid off the counter.

<font color=mistyrose>"I walked into the dark because you taught me that's what love looks like."</font> She said it levelly, chin up, in the voice that was hers and also the voice of a woman in a cabin with wisterias who never learned to stay put. <font color=mistyrose>"You're going to walk into a war for the same reason. If you try to leave me behind, I'll follow. You know I'll follow."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's what I'm afraid of."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Good."</font> Amy picked up a pastry and bit into it with absolute finality. <font color=mistyrose>"Be afraid. Bring me anyway."</font>

And then Chisa, who had been sitting closest to the door and farthest from the argument, said:

<font color=coral>"I'm going."</font>

The room paused. Chisa saying *I'm going* had a different weight than the others, and everyone at the table knew why, and nobody said it yet.

<font color=coral>"Before anyone asks,"</font> she added. <font color=coral>"I'll talk to my mother."</font>

---

## IV.

The door to her mother's apartment was on the third floor of the east wing, and Chisa had memorized every creak in the corridor the way she memorized everything: by sound, by the spaces between sounds, by the quality of absence that particular surfaces left in the air around them.

She knocked.

Her mother opened the door the way her mother opened everything — with both hands and a question already forming. Chisa could hear the shape of it: *Tea?* or *Dinner?* or *Have you eaten?* The questions a woman asks when she has had her daughter back for eighteen months and is still checking that the fact will hold.

<font color=coral>"Can I come in?"</font>

The door widened. Chisa went in.

Her mother's apartment smelled like pressed cloth and barley tea, the same two smells it had smelled of since Chisa was old enough to distinguish them. There were clocks on the shelf — four of them, keeping time — and Chisa stood in the doorway and heard them tick, each at its own interval, the way a room full of clocks will always insist on being a room full of individuals rather than a chorus.

She sat at the table. Her mother put the kettle on without being asked.

*The scissors are her mother's, floral-handled.* Chisa carried them at her hip the way other people carry keys — the instrument so constant it had stopped being separate from the hand. She had cut gods with them. She had cut a tower into six hells with them. They had belonged to a woman who signed a form above a line that said *the signature is a formality*, which Chisa had thought about for a long time.

<font color=coral>"I'm going north."</font>

Her mother's hands paused on the kettle.

<font color=coral>"To Jinzhou. With the pack."</font>

The kettle went onto the ring. The ring clicked. Her mother sat down across from her and folded her hands in her lap, and Chisa heard the fold — the precise small sound of knuckles settling against knuckles — and remembered it from a Prefecture office a long time ago, where a woman in a formal jacket had sat across from another woman in a formal jacket while a man seated a clasp against a girl's throat, and the one folded her hands, and the other signed.

<font color=coral>"I wanted you to hear it from me."</font>

Silence. Four clocks.

Her mother said something Chisa had been expecting and something she had not, and the something she had not was: *"Take the good shears."*

<font color=coral>"What?"</font>

*"The good ones. The ones your grandmother kept for company. They're in the cedar chest under the winter quilts. I moved them last spring."*

<font color=coral>"Those are yours."</font>

*"They were always going to be yours."*

Chisa sat with that for a while. The kettle built itself toward sound.

*"Are you taking them because you want to, or because someone asked?"*

<font color=coral>"Both."</font>

*"Good."*

The kettle sang. Her mother got up and poured, and the tea arrived in the two cups it always arrived in — one with the chip at the rim, one without — and Chisa took the chipped one without discussion, because she always took the chipped one, and her mother let her, and this was the oldest form of their conversation: the part that happened in the handling of objects, below the register of words.

<font color=coral>"I left once without telling you."</font>

*"You left at sixteen with a form and a clasp and you told me with your chin."* Her mother drank. *"I have spent a long time with that chin."*

<font color=coral>"I should have —"</font>

*"You are telling me now. That is the improvement. I am measuring it. Finish your tea."*

Chisa finished her tea.

At the door, her mother did the thing she always did: both hands on Chisa's shoulders, brief and specific, a weight that arrived and departed in the same breath. And then — the thing she had done once, at the Prefecture, that Chisa could not remember the sound of:

She said something.

Chisa stood in the corridor afterward, scissor-loops warm against her hip, and listened to the four clocks ticking through the closed door, and carried the shape of the sentence the way she carried everything: by the space it left.

---

## V.

Lynae said it to Mei on the loading dock at three in the morning, because Lynae conducted all her important business at three in the morning on loading docks. It was a personal brand.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm staying."</font>

Mei leaned against the rail and studied her with the uncomplicated attention she gave to problems she had already solved and was waiting for the owner to notice.

<font color=moccasin>"Sigrika."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Sigrika."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"You could bring her."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Into a war theatre, with a candidacy she hasn't decided whether to keep and a best friend who walked into a lake and a second best friend who turned out to be centuries old and wearing a student's face."</font> Lynae kicked the rail once, the way she kicked things when she was being precise. <font color=palegoldenrod>"She needs a floor."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Yours."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Mine's the only one she'll sleep on."</font>

The false stars hung in their wrong constellations. Somewhere below, the tram line changed shifts.

<font color=moccasin>"The pack's never split."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"The pack's never had someone to stay for."</font>

Mei turned the rail with her palms. <font color=moccasin>"Rebecca?"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Asked her. She's in."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"In for staying."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"She said, and I quote: 'Somebody in this family has to be where the mail comes back to.'"</font>

Mei smiled. It hurt in the particular way true things hurt.

<font color=moccasin>"I'll miss the card games."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You'll miss losing."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I'll miss losing."</font>

Lynae pushed off the rail and turned to go, and stopped, and said the thing she had come to the loading dock to say, which was a different thing from the thing she had said first, the way in Lynae's economy the bill always came after the meal:

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Come back."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Lynae —"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm not asking. I'm invoicing. Seven of you walk out; seven of you walk back in. That's the terms. I don't care about the war and I don't care about the wind and I don't care about the god. I care about the number."</font>

She went inside.

Mei stood on the dock with her hands on the rail, looking at the false sky and the city breathing its slow electric hymn beneath it, and counted seven, and found that the number sat in her chest exactly the way a number sits when someone else has written it down.

---

## VI.

In the kitchen, past midnight, in the interval between one argument and the next, Iuno put the second cup down on the table and sat across from it.

*The ring,* the High Priestess observed. *You have been carrying the question for four weeks. The logistics are becoming self-conscious.*

*After,* Iuno replied.

*After the war.*

*After everything.*

*That is what the man on the hill said, darling, and the calendar kept going.*

Iuno looked at the cup. Mei's cup. The cardamom already waiting, because premeditation was the only love language she had ever been fluent in.

*The doctrine,* the Priestess reminded. *We do not plan the people. We plan the luggage.*

*A ring is luggage.*

*A ring is a delivery date. And delivery dates require officers. Living ones.*

Iuno touched the stump where the crystal caught the kitchen light and scattered it. Somewhere in the mineral, a facet held a frequency that was hers alone — instrument-grade, true, a note that could be cut from the arm and set and worn, and carry the sound of her into an indefinite future. She had run the material analysis three times. She had made sketches. She had hidden the sketches inside a filing system so baroque that even the High Priestess had admired its paranoia.

*Before,* Iuno said, correcting herself, the way you correct a course. *Before we go.*

*Mm,* the Priestess said. *That's new.*

*If I bring it back, it's a promise to return. If I leave it with her and don't return, it's a promise kept anyway.* She turned the crystal in the light. *Either way, the order stands.*

The Priestess was quiet for a long moment, which was unprecedented, and warm, which was rare.

*That's the best arithmetic you've ever done,* she said.

Iuno picked up the cup and carried it down the hall, on schedule, unhurried, spending.

---

## VII.

On the last night, Amy found Chisa on the drying terrace.

The laundry hung in the warm vent-draft the way it always hung: Iuno's blue, Lupa's undershirts (grey, regulation, betraying nothing), Cartethyia's socks, Ciaccona's cuff-burned shirt. Among them now, new: Amy's small sleeve, still with the tag. She'd have to take the tag off before they packed. She kept meaning to.

Chisa sat on the reinforced crate with her hands empty and her scissors in their loop.

<font color=mistyrose>"You're going to miss them,"</font> Amy said. Not the laundry. The clocks.

<font color=coral>"I'm going to miss a great many things."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Your mother."</font>

Chisa turned her mug a quarter-turn on the concrete. <font color=coral>"Yes."</font>

Amy sat on the second crate. It held. She looked out at the reservoir, where the lights were breaking up in the water, and thought about counting, and thought about the void, and thought about a woman humming three notes through a bathroom door.

<font color=mistyrose>"I used to think about what I'd do when I got back,"</font> she said. <font color=mistyrose>"In the void. Lists. Long ones. All the food I'd eat. All the places I'd go. The people I'd hold onto and never let go."</font>

Chisa waited.

<font color=mistyrose>"And then I got back, and I held onto everybody, and three weeks later we're packing for a war."</font> She looked at her hands. Still solid. Still here. <font color=mistyrose>"I thought there'd be more time in the middle."</font>

<font color=coral>"There's always less time than the list."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Yeah."</font>

The ventilation cycled. Down the line, a shirt cracked once in the draft.

<font color=mistyrose>"Chisa."</font>

<font color=coral>"Mm."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Did she say something? Your mother. At the door."</font>

Chisa looked at the mug.

<font color=coral>"She said something I can't repeat because I can't hear the sound of it anymore. I know the shape. I know where it went. It's here."</font> She put her hand flat against her sternum. <font color=coral>"But the sound left the way sounds do when you try to hold them too tightly."</font>

Amy sat with that for a while.

<font color=mistyrose>"Sumika."</font>

<font color=coral>"Mm."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"The same thing."</font>

<font color=coral>"The same thing."</font>

They sat on two crates on a terrace above an underground city, two women who had each lost a voice and kept the space where the voice had been, and the laundry moved around them in the warm dark, and neither of them filled the silence, because the silence was the point.

After a while, Amy reached over and took the chipped mug out of Chisa's hand, refilled it from the kettle on the utility ring, and gave it back. The gesture was Iuno's, learned by osmosis, performed at the wrong temperature, and Chisa drank it anyway.

<font color=mistyrose>"I'll be loud on the way out,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"So you know it's me."</font>

<font color=coral>"I'll know."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"How?"</font>

<font color=coral>"You walk like your mother. Heavy at the heel."</font> Chisa turned the mug. <font color=coral>"She doesn't know. Don't tell her."</font>

Amy smiled — bright, crooked, inherited.

<font color=mistyrose>"Deal."</font>

The false stars held steady. The city breathed. And somewhere far above the engineered sky, past corridors of stone and the long rise of the Riseway and the real air beyond, a wind too thin to be felt was leaning south, the way a compass leans, toward a kite string that had already been caught.

The manifest was filed by Iuno Covella in her usual hand, which included the unit designation of a guard platform she personally objected to and was overruled on by a show of hands, and a column for REMARKS in which the entry for every departing member read, identically: RETURNING.*

*The clerk notes that the column was added to the standard form. It has not been seen on previous manifests.*

*The reply, if any, postdates this printing.*

*The court keeps the register open.*
