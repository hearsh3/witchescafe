# Muster
### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

勸君更盡一杯酒，西出陽關無故人。
*Finish this last cup of wine — west of the pass, there will be no old friends.*
— Wang Wei, "Song of Weicheng"

---

## I.

The group chat had been renamed at four in the morning. GOING EAST (BRING SNACKS). Mei's doing, obviously. By six-thirty, Ciaccona had muted it, Lynae had unmuted it, and Cartethyia had sent eleven stickers of a bird she had seen on the ventilation ledge.

The list went up at six on a piece of yellow paper, taped to the kitchen wall between the spice rack and the calendar, in Mei's handwriting.

**ROAD TRIP RULES**
**(Effective Immediately)**

1. One bag each. Instruments count as persons.
2. Snack stops every four hours. MANDATORY.
3. Driver rotation. Everybody takes a turn. YES this means you.
4. The group chat name is FINAL. Stop changing it back.
5. First person to complain about the route is on dinner duty for the week.
6. We are having fun. This is a rule. See above re: mandatory.

Amy found it at twenty past six, toast in hand, her hair doing something Mei would later describe as *structurally optimistic.*

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

The shower was running.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma, rule three says everybody. Carte drove a cart into a ditch outside Ragunna. There were witnesses."</font>

The shower cut off. Mei appeared around the doorframe with soap in one eye and a grin that preceded every bad decision she had ever survived.

<font color=moccasin>"She'll practice."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Into what. A second ditch."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Character building."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"For the DITCH?"</font>

Mei winked with the wrong eye and disappeared. The shower resumed.

Amy stood in front of the list with her toast. Rule six. *We are having fun. This is a rule.* She ate the crust, brushed the crumbs off her shirt, and went to wake up Lynae.

---

Lupa came down at seven, read the list twice, pulled the pen from behind her ear, and added in red:

**7. Mei is forbidden from naming anything else until we cross the city line.**

Her tail gave a single lateral sweep — on the record — and she poured herself coffee.

---

By eight the kitchen had developed the particular acoustic quality of nine people discovering a document at different stages of alertness and grievance.

<font color=teal>"Instruments count as *persons.*"</font>

<font color=indianred>"She's trying to be inclusive."</font>

<font color=teal>"My lute has survived four hundred years, two divine catastrophes, and a shipping crate. It has earned a seat. I am disputing the phrasing."</font>

<font color=indianred>"You're disputing the fun."</font>

<font color=teal>"I am disputing whether fun requires *paperwork.*"</font>

Cartethyia arrived from the stairwell with a leaf in her hair and the camellia terrarium under one arm and her walking stick under the other.

<font color=skyblue>"Oh! There's rules!"</font>

<font color=teal>"There *are* rules. Carte, the subject is plural."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I want to add one. Can I add one? Rule eight: we stop for birds."</font>

<font color=indianred>"We are stopping for birds exactly zero times."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Interesting ones! The eastern corridor has a migratory species I've only seen in drawings—"</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Rule eight is approved,"</font> Mei called from the hallway. She had soap on her collar and both shoes on the wrong feet, and she was already heading for the door.

<font color=indianred>"Where are you going."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Supply run. Market opens at eight-thirty. Amy, Carte, Naeun, you're with me."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"It's *Lynae,*"</font> said Lynae from the top of the stairs, in a voice that carried the specific exhaustion of having explained this before. She was wearing a jacket covered in spray-paint constellations and holding a shoe in each hand.

<font color=moccasin>"It's Lynae, and we're leaving in five."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"I'm in my socks."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Four."</font>

The door banged. Cartethyia looked at Ciaccona. Ciaccona looked at Cartethyia's terrarium.

<font color=teal>"Leave the plant."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It needs sunlight."</font>

<font color=teal>"We live underground."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It needs *attempted* sunlight."</font>

Cartethyia left the terrarium on the counter, kissed the top of Ciaccona's head in passing, grabbed her walking stick, and followed Mei out. Ciaccona's hand went to the exact spot on her hair where the kiss had landed and stayed there for approximately two seconds longer than she would admit under oath.

Lupa watched the door swing shut. She took a slow pull of her coffee and looked at the people remaining in the kitchen: Iuno at the table with her terminal and a tea that had been steeping for exactly four minutes; Chisa by the window, scissors resting across her knees, tracking the footsteps receding down the corridor by weight and pace and distance; Rebecca on the counter with her legs folded, eating cereal out of the box with her fingers.

<font color=indianred>"Right."</font> Lupa set her mug down. <font color=indianred>"While she's gone. Maps."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Kitchen table. Ten minutes."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Five."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Eight."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Six, and you make the tea fresh."</font>

Iuno looked at the pot. Looked at Lupa. The calculation took about a second and a half.

<font color=royalblue>"Done."</font>

## II.

The room Mei had given her was on the south side, second floor, with a window that looked out on a ventilation shaft and forty feet of bioluminescent lichen.

Rebecca sat cross-legged on the bed and looked at the bag.

The bag was canvas, olive green, the size of a backpack from home if home existed. Lupa had left it on her bed that morning with a grunt and zero explanation. It had a buckle, two straps, and a front pocket.

Rebecca opened it. Empty.

She closed it.

She pulled the dresser drawer. Three shirts. Two had been Amy's before Amy's body solidified and her shoulders came in wider. The third was from a shop Chisa had walked her to last week — Chisa navigated the city by counting steps and listening to vendors, and she had stood beside Rebecca at the counter and said <font color=coral>"The grey one,"</font> and Rebecca had bought the grey one because Chisa's advice cost exactly one sentence and was always right.

Two pairs of pants that fit. She'd arrived in chrome-studded shorts and a jacket that smelled like cordite and a world with a sky. Lynae had laughed at the shorts for three days and then handed her cargo pants without comment.

The jacket. She held it. It was from home and it was the only thing that was.

She folded it and put it in the bag. Shirts. Pants. A pair of socks Cartethyia had knitted badly, each one a different length, because Cartethyia had learned to knit in a dark room by touch and had carried that calibration into the light. The left sock had a hole at the toe and a small embroidered flower where the hole had been repaired. Rebecca had asked why she'd bother fixing it instead of knitting a new one, and Cartethyia had looked at her like she'd suggested drowning a kitten.

The marker set. Twelve colors, the green worn down to a nub from the cabin wall. She'd drawn Lucy, David, Maine, Dorio, Pilar, Kiwi, Sasha. On a wall in a cabin in a world where their names meant nothing to anyone except the one person in the room who had watched them die.

She put the markers in the front pocket.

Toothbrush. The crystalline comb Iuno had given her that she was half-convinced was worth more than her entire previous net worth. A hairband that Amy had left on the bathroom counter and that Rebecca had taken and Amy had watched her take it and said nothing.

Everything she owned in this world fit in one bag with room to spare.

She sat on the bed with the bag zipped between her knees. The lichen outside pulsed blue, then green, then blue again, cycling on whatever schedule underground cities used for a sun.

From downstairs: Ciaccona's lute. Three notes, the chord that followed, three notes again. The practice room vent opened into the south wall of the second floor, which meant Rebecca had been falling asleep to that lute for weeks, and would never in her life admit she needed it.

The front door banged open two floors down. Mei's voice, carrying. Something about lemon cakes and the wrong stall. Cartethyia's higher register cutting across. Amy laughing.

Rebecca pulled the bag's straps tight, tested the weight — light, because everything she'd been given weighed almost nothing — and set it by the door.

<font color=greenyellow>"Done,"</font> she said, to the room.

The lichen pulsed.

## III.

The Phosphor Market opened an hour after Helios came up, by which point the vendors on the coral level had arranged their wares under the bioluminescent canopy and the eel-smoke from the food stalls had settled into its daily argument with the ventilation.

Mei walked the way she always walked in a market: too fast, stopping without warning, backtracking three stalls because something caught her eye, bumping shoulders with strangers who all forgave her immediately on account of the eyes.

<font color=moccasin>"Dried fruit. Road bread. And the good jerky from the stall with the blue awning, the old woman who calls everybody 'petal.'"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"That stall closed."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"When."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma, it closed while you were—"</font> Amy caught the word and walked around it the way they'd agreed: furniture in the room, named once. <font color=mistyrose>"A month ago."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Find me a replacement jerky vendor. I trust your judgment."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You trust my palate."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Same instrument. Carte, stay close. Last time you went to a market alone—"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I got a very good price on the spices!"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You paid triple,"</font> Lynae said. Her hands were in her jacket pockets and her eyes were doing the thing they did when she was counting exits, inventory volumes, and vendor margins simultaneously. The gyaru paint job was fresh — neon accents on her nails, two new stars sprayed on her cuffs — but her feet moved through a crowd the way a person's feet moved when they'd spent years entering rooms they were planning to leave through the window.

<font color=skyblue>"I paid what they were worth!"</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You paid what they were worth to a person with zero haggling instinct and a face that reads 'I will feel guilty about this for three days if I walk away.'"</font>

Cartethyia's walking stick tapped once, the small indignant tap.

<font color=moccasin>"Lynae is on price. Carte is on quality. Amy is on snacks. I'll handle bulk."</font> Mei clapped her hands, the field-commander gesture, delivered with the gravity of a woman deploying troops toward dried apricots.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"What's my budget."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Less than the thing is worth."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"That's every budget."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Then you're already on it."</font>

---

They split at the fruit tier. Mei and Cartethyia went toward the bulk vendors at the east wall, where the crystal outcroppings grew close enough to the stalls that the fruit caught the mineral light and looked candied. Amy vanished into the snack row with the focused efficiency of a person who had spent a decade learning what she liked from a menu of nothing.

Lynae hung back.

The Phosphor Market was three tiers of carved coral, bioluminescent vine overhead casting moving green-gold across everything. Vendors called in the melodic Lahai Roi dialect — the common tongue buried underground long enough to develop its own gravity, pulling vowels low and stretching consonants. The smell was eel-smoke and crystal-pollen and the metallic tang of recycled air pushed through too many lungs.

Lynae's fingers found the edges of the cards in her jacket pocket: her student ID (real, earned, four months old), her transit pass (legitimate), her old Fractsidus cipher key (deactivated, kept because she couldn't figure out how to throw it away). She used to carry nine forms of identification, three genuine. She had one now, and it had her real name on it, and the photograph looked like her because she'd been making her real face when they took it.

The first vendor Cartethyia approached was selling dried persimmons at a markup Lynae could calculate from six stalls away.

She walked over.

The vendor was a broad woman with crystal dust in her hairline and a smile that widened when she saw Cartethyia's face — an open one, trusting, the kind of face that made vendors' mornings.

*"Persimmons, dear? Just in, picked before the light cycle, best quality on the tier—"*

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Six per bundle?"</font>

The vendor's smile paused.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"They're three at the stall on the tier below. Same supplier. I can see the crate stamps from here."</font>

The vendor's smile reconsidered.

<font color=skyblue>"Lynae."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"What."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"She was being nice."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"She was being nice at a sixty percent margin."</font>

*"Four,"* the vendor said.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Three and a half, and two extras per bundle for the trouble of me pointing out the crate stamps."</font>

The vendor looked at Lynae. Lynae looked at the vendor. A brief, professional, amused moment — two people who understood the arithmetic of a room reaching an understanding.

*"Three and a half. No extras."*

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Done."</font>

Cartethyia stood between them holding two bundles of persimmons with the expression of a person whose good time was being optimized against her will.

<font color=skyblue>"I liked the interaction the way it was."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"You liked paying double."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I liked the *talking.* She was going to tell me about her grandmother's orchard."</font>

Lynae took a persimmon from the bundle and bit into it. Good. Sweet. The kind of sweet that meant the supplier dried them slow.

<font color=palegoldenrod>"Ask her now. You've got the time, and she only respects you more."</font>

Cartethyia turned back to the vendor, who was already leaning on her stall with the particular posture of a woman who had a grandmother story ready and a customer willing to hear it.

Lynae peeled off and went looking for road bread.

---

Mei found Amy three stalls deep into the snack row, holding two bags of salted plum candy and examining a third with the critical attention of a sommelier.

<font color=mistyrose>"The darker ones have more salt."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Is that good."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Depends. Lupa wants salt. Iuno likes sour."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"When did you learn Iuno's snack preferences."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"She left a candy wrapper in the bathroom. Sour plum. Twice."</font> Amy held up both bags. <font color=mistyrose>"I'm getting both."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"That's nine people's worth of candy."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Chisa eats when she's reading. Cia snacks when she's composing. Becca eats everything all the time. This is a three-day minimum."</font>

Mei watched her daughter sort through candy with the operational precision of a person who had once provisioned a decade of solitude in a place where provisions were made of light. Amy knew exactly how much everyone ate, when, and what they reached for first. She'd learned it by watching, from inside a silence where watching was all there was.

Amy looked up and caught Mei's face.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Mm."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Your eyes are doing the thing."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"My eyes are shopping."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Your eyes are making a feelings face at me over candy."</font>

Mei bumped her shoulder. The physical contact was the answer, the way it always was: shoulder, hip, a hand on the back of the neck. The language she spoke when the other one ran out.

<font color=moccasin>"Get the toffees too. Carte eats slow."</font>

Amy grabbed a bag of toffees and added it to the pile. <font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Mm."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"This is actually fun."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Rule six."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"I know the rule. I'm saying it's working."</font>

Mei grinned. The golden eyes did the thing — the holding-on-tight thing.

<font color=moccasin>"Wait till you see the bikes."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"What about the bikes."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"I got them detailed."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma. You got nine motorcycles detailed for a deployment to a war zone."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Road trip."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"War zone."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"*Road. Trip.*"</font>

Amy paid, slung the bag over her shoulder, and chose her mother's version of the word because the other one was there either way.

---

## IV.

The kitchen table had been cleared of everything except the map, Lupa's coffee, Iuno's tea, and a plate of the road bread Mei had left before heading out.

The map was Iuno's work: Academy archive cartography, hand-annotated in her precise blue script, the route east marked in silver ink.

Lupa studied it the way she studied ground: distances first, cover second, choke points third.

<font color=indianred>"The eastern corridor."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Past the surface outlet at grid seven, overland through the Disputed Reaches, then the mountain pass into Huanglong territory. Jinzhou sits at the bottom of a valley with the dam above and the lake below."</font>

<font color=indianred>"How long."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"On bikes, if the weather holds, twelve days. Fourteen if we stop for Carte's birds."</font>

<font color=indianred>"She'll find birds."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"She'll find birds."</font>

Lupa traced the route with one claw. The Disputed Reaches were a stretch of overland territory between the underground network and the eastern nations — monitored, populated, technically in cease-fire with everything that had claws. Discord activity was logged and variable. The Academy classified the corridor as *navigable with precautions,* which was the institutional way of saying *bring weapons.*

<font color=indianred>"Supply caches."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Two Collective relay stations, here and here."</font> Iuno tapped. <font color=royalblue>"Stocked for transit teams. Medical kits, rations, communication terminals. I've filed our passage."</font>

<font color=indianred>"When."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Tuesday."</font>

<font color=indianred>"She announced the road trip today. You filed four days ago."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

Lupa's tail moved once: the slow sweep of a verdict.

<font color=indianred>"You've been planning this since the vote."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I've been planning this since the boots."</font>

The boots. The pair by Iuno's door, laced to the second eyelet from the top, cleaned and resoled twice, standing in a room with three weeks of dust on everything else. Lupa had been the one who noticed the dust. She was always the one who noticed.

*Darling,* the High Priestess observed. *She tracks you by your housekeeping.*

*She tracks everyone by everything. That's why she's here.*

*That's why she's HERE, at this table, doing logistics with you, instead of being the woman you put a spear through.*

*We settled that.*

*And now we're planning campfire routes with her. Character development is RUINOUSLY expensive, darling.*

Iuno poured tea. Lupa pulled the map closer.

<font color=indianred>"Nine riders. Three who can fight on flat terrain, four on rough. One who's been solid for a few months. One who's from a different dimension."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"And Cartethyia, who can become a twenty-foot divine instrument of war if sufficiently motivated."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Carte is a hinge. She swings when she wants. I can predict the direction. The timing is harder."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"The timing is: when someone she loves is inside the arc."</font>

Lupa's ears came forward. Iuno had said something tactical, and Lupa marked it.

<font color=indianred>"Amy."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Ninety-nine point six. She is a weapon. She's also Mei's daughter. She's also nineteen."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Nineteen and ten."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Nineteen and ten. And she'll go where Mei goes, and Mei will step in front of whatever's there, and we will spend the entire journey keeping both of them alive while they take turns trying to protect each other."</font>

Lupa took a piece of road bread and bit into it.

<font color=indianred>"Same as always."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Same as always."</font>

They sat with the map between them. Iuno's crystalline stump caught the kitchen light and scattered small blue sparks across the contour lines. Lupa ate bread.

<font color=indianred>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=indianred>"The route through the Reaches. There's a northern variant that adds two days but avoids the choke at the gorge."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"I see it."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Two days is two days of Mei pretending this is a vacation. More campfires. More stops. More time for the kid to be a kid before she has to be a weapon."</font>

Iuno looked at the northern route. Two extra days in the open. Two extra nights around a fire. Two more mornings of Mei waking up with her daughter real and the war in front of them and both of those things true at the same time.

<font color=royalblue>"Take the northern route."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Already marked it."</font>

Lupa pulled the pen from behind her ear and drew the alternate line. Her handwriting was red and sharp and exactly legible, the hand of a person who had filed field reports in worse conditions than this kitchen. Iuno watched her trace the route north, up around the gorge, through terrain marked as forested and largely unmonitored.

<font color=royalblue>"You wanted the extra days too."</font>

<font color=indianred>"I wanted the extra campfires."</font> Lupa set the pen down. <font color=indianred>"My fires are excellent."</font>

*She builds them deliberately too large, so everyone has to sit close,* the High Priestess noted. *If you point this out—*

*Heat distribution. I know.*

<font color=royalblue>"Your fires are very large."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Heat distribution."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Naturally."</font>

They finished the road bread. Lupa rolled the map. Iuno washed the cups.

## V.

The practice room was on the basement level, carved into the coral substrate where the resonance was best. Ciaccona had claimed it three days after they moved in by the simple expedient of playing in it so often that nobody else tried, and had since added: a stool, a shelf for sheet music, a chipped mug that lived on the shelf and was used exclusively for thinking, and a small uneven star drawn in the corner of the wall, like the ones her father drew.

She sat with the lute across her knees and the notebook open on the stool and a title written at the top of the page:

*Travel songs. For the road east.*

Under it, one entry:

*da-da-da?*

The three notes. Amy's notes. The ones that had sat in her keeping for a decade, unplayed, because she'd believed playing them would use them up. She'd been wrong. They were waiting to be played with someone.

She played them. Three notes. Then the resolution. Then three notes again.

The road trip playlist. Mei had asked for it over breakfast two days ago, casually, the way Mei asked for everything — as though it were a small request made of a friend, and the friend could say no, and the no would be fine, and she would go on grinning and find another way. The grin was honest. The *finding another way* was honest too. Ciaccona had said yes because she could hear the thing underneath the grin, and the thing underneath it was: *I need the noise to cover the quiet, and I trust you to make the right noise.*

So. Road music. Songs for nine people heading east on motorcycles toward a god of war in a city they'd only read about in a letter.

She wrote:

*2. The Carnevale reel. Lupa's request. The one from the piazza — the one that made her howl.*

*3. Amy's piece. Full arrangement. Both voices.*

She stopped writing.

The problem was that she could hear the trip already. She heard it the way she heard everything: as a song. Nine frequencies moving east in convoy. Mei's bright E. Lupa's grounded A-flat. Iuno's precise B. Amy's high C, young and clear, wobbling at the edges when she was frightened. Carte's frequency shifted depending on which body she was wearing, which restructured the whole convoy's harmony every time she transformed — musically fascinating and logistically impossible to arrange for.

She wrote:

*4. Something for the fire. Low. Strings and voice. For the nights.*

She underlined *nights.* Then she wrote beneath it, in smaller script:

*When Mei is asleep. When the performance stops. Play the real thing then.*

Then she crossed that out, because writing it gave it an audience, and the real thing only worked when nobody was arranging it.

Footsteps on the stairs. She knew them by weight and rhythm: Cartethyia, in the smaller body, walking stick tapping every other step. Tap, step, tap, step, with a slight drag on the left from the hip the physio was working on.

<font color=skyblue>"Cia?"</font>

<font color=teal>"In here."</font>

Cartethyia appeared in the doorway with a persimmon in one hand and the walking stick in the other. She had changed out of her market clothes and was wearing the oversized shirt she wore around the house — the one that had belonged to Ciaccona first, and that Ciaccona had stopped claiming because some arguments are concessions dressed up, and she knew which ones she had already lost.

<font color=skyblue>"Oh, you're working."</font>

<font color=teal>"I'm making a list."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"The playlist! Can I see?"</font>

<font color=teal>"Absolutely and categorically."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Is the knight's anthem on it?"</font>

<font color=teal>"It's a travel playlist, Carte. The knight's anthem is a battle hymn for a specific deity."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"It has a good beat."</font>

<font color=teal>"It has a LITURGICAL beat."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Those are the same!"</font>

Ciaccona's mouth twitched. The twitch that Cartethyia lived for: the exact moment the sharp-tongued cynic lost a fight with her own amusement.

<font color=teal>"Sit down."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"You said you were working."</font>

<font color=teal>"I said sit down."</font>

Cartethyia sat on the floor beside the stool, leaning her walking stick against the wall. She ate the persimmon in small bites, the way she ate everything: slowly, tasting each one, because for twenty years every bite had been a confirmation that taste existed, and the habit stayed.

Ciaccona played. Three notes. The resolution. Three notes.

<font color=skyblue>"That's Amy's."</font>

<font color=teal>"Mm."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Are you going to play it on the road?"</font>

<font color=teal>"When it matters."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Everything matters."</font>

Ciaccona stopped playing. She looked at Cartethyia on the floor with the persimmon and the walking stick and the stolen shirt and the crystal thorns catching the basement light, and she felt the chord that Cartethyia always was: a major seventh, bright and unresolved, open at the top, waiting for the next note to arrive and claim it.

<font color=teal>"Fine."</font> She started writing again. <font color=teal>"The knight's anthem goes on the list."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"Really?"</font>

<font color=teal>"At the end. After the music."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"The knight's anthem IS music."</font>

<font color=teal>"The knight's anthem is a hymn composed by a temple committee who thought *majestic* meant *loud.*"</font>

<font color=skyblue>"I can make it louder."</font>

<font color=teal>"I know you can."</font>

Cartethyia finished her persimmon and leaned her head against Ciaccona's knee. Ciaccona's free hand found the top of her head, and the star in the corner of the wall caught the light, and the lute hummed against the coral, and the notebook said:

*5. The knight's anthem (arrangement: stripped back, one voice, one lute. She has earned the real version).*

## VI.

The dinner happened because Mei came back from the market at four, set three bags on the counter, and said:

<font color=moccasin>"Everybody cooks something."</font>

The kitchen regarded her.

<font color=moccasin>"One dish. Anything. From wherever you're from. I want a table."</font>

<font color=indianred>"There are nine of us and four burners."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Then it's a logistics exercise."</font>

<font color=indianred>"It's going to be a fire."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Then we'll eat outside."</font>

Lupa's tail moved. The single lateral sweep of a formal objection. But her hands were already reaching for the olive oil, which meant the tail was filing the protest while the rest of her had voted yes.

At half past four the kitchen had exceeded its designed occupancy by a factor of two, and the ventilation system had begun to lose arguments.

Lupa occupied the far burner with the territorial confidence of a woman defending high ground. She was making something Septimontian with anchovies and rosemary and bread that she tore by hand, and the smell of it was already colonizing the corridor.

<font color=teal>"Is that the thing from the piazza."</font>

*That was a calibration issue.*

*It was DEEPLY calibrated.*

<font color=mistyrose>"Cia."</font>

<font color=teal>"WHAT."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Let her cook."</font>

Amy said it from the second burner without turning around, stirring the chocolate with the steady rhythm of someone who had made this recipe a hundred times in a place where the ingredients were made of light and the taste was a memory of a taste.

Ciaccona closed her mouth. Cartethyia beat the batter. The construction sound resumed.

---

Chisa arrived at the kitchen at five with a bag of ingredients she had purchased that afternoon, walking her route from the shop in exactly three hundred and twelve steps — a number she'd counted once and carried since.

She set down: miso, tofu, spring onions, a jar of dashi, and a block of something dark and fragrant that Mei identified as kombu.

<font color=moccasin>"Miso soup."</font>

<font color=coral>"From scratch."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Is there another way."</font>

Chisa looked at her. Mei got the answer from the look.

She worked at the cutting board with the efficiency of a person who navigated her entire life by weight, sound, and the angle of a blade. The spring onions fell in even rings. The tofu came apart in blocks her hands measured by pressure. She worked fast, quiet, clean, and the only sounds were the tap of the knife and the shift of her scissors in their sheath — worn across her back to the kitchen the way she wore them everywhere: balanced, there.

---

Lynae cooked at the far end of the counter, as far from the main chaos as the kitchen geometry allowed.

She was making something she hadn't made in four years, from a recipe memorized at twelve from a woman who was dead, in a kitchen that was gone, in a city she'd been trained to deny she was from.

Tteokbokki. Rice cakes in gochujang sauce. The kind of thing her mother made on cold nights, the kind of thing Lee Naeun ate before Lynae was born.

She'd bought the rice cakes at the Phosphor Market from a vendor who sold eastern staples to a customer base of approximately nobody, and who had looked at her with the attention of someone recognizing a compatriot. Lynae had paid in cash and walked away, and then walked back and bought twice as much.

Her hands remembered the recipe. Cut, sauce, stir. The gochujang popped and spat. She had mascara on and the flinch was a professional decision: mercenary training. You do the thing and you watch it spit and your face stays where it is.

<font color=greenyellow>"What's that."</font>

Mei cooked last.

She had spent part of the afternoon studying a recipe from the Academy archive, filed under *Regional Cuisine, Eastern Nations, Huanglong, Municipal District: Jinzhou.* Zongzi — sticky rice wrapped in bamboo leaves, filled with pork and chestnut. A festival food. A travel food. Something you could carry.

She worked in the corner by the sink with the bamboo leaves soaking in a bowl of water and the rice measured out in Iuno's porcelain cup, which she used because it was exactly the right size for one portion, and because it made Iuno tighten her jaw in the specific way that meant she was in love and furious about the porcelain.

The recipe was in institutional shorthand: *Wrap tight. Steam forty-five minutes. Serve with soy.*

She wrapped. The first one came apart. The second held but looked geological. The third was recognizable as a thing a human had intended.

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma."</font>

The table.

Lupa's Septimontian bread with anchovies and rosemary, torn and oiled and warm. Chisa's miso, clear and deep, the tofu cut in blocks so even they looked printed. Lynae's tteokbokki, brick-red and glistening, with a warning label she'd written on a scrap of paper: *SPICY. NOT MY PROBLEM.* Cartethyia's lemon cakes, which had — against all prediction and several laws of thermodynamics — become lemon cakes: small, dense, golden, with sugar crystals on top that Ciaccona had contributed from the pantry without comment because Carte's cakes needed sugar crystals and pretending otherwise served nobody. Rebecca's toast, assembled with the grim efficiency of a combat loadout: dark bread, cheese, sliced persimmon from the market, and something green she'd found in the back of the refrigerator and identified as edible by eating some and waiting. Mei's zongzi, eight of them, tied diagonal, steamed exactly forty-five minutes. Amy's hot chocolate in a pot with a ladle, because the recipe made too much and always had. And in the centre of the table, alone, Iuno's teapot — placed with the expression of a woman who had contributed what she had and dared anyone to mention it.

They sat.

Lupa took the head because nobody argued. Mei beside Amy. Iuno beside Mei. Ciaccona and Cartethyia together, Carte's walking stick hooked over the back of her chair. Chisa at the corner where the acoustics were best — she'd mapped the table by how voices carried from each seat and chosen the one that let her hear everyone at once. Lynae beside Rebecca, because the two of them had developed a proximity that looked like irritation and functioned as orbit.

Mei looked at the table.

Nine plates. Nine people. Food from Septimont and Honami and Korea and Night City and Jinzhou and a temple where a priestess had once set fire to rice and been rescued by a teenager with a towel. Lupa's bread steaming. Chisa's soup clear. Carte's cakes defying the laws. Rebecca's toast, somehow, the thing everyone reached for first.

<font color=moccasin>"Rule six."</font>

Everybody looked at her.

<font color=moccasin>"We're having fun."</font>

<font color=indianred>"Eat."</font>

They ate.

The lemon cakes were terrible and everybody knew and Amy ate three. Lynae's tteokbokki made Rebecca's eyes water and Lupa's ears pin flat, and Lupa ate the rest of the bowl and asked for the recipe in a voice stripped of all bark. Chisa's soup was the thing Mei kept coming back to between everything else, spooning it slowly, the way you drink something that puts the ground back under you. The zongzi held. They were dense and plain and would taste nothing like whatever Jinzhou made of them, and everyone ate one and Iuno ate two without comment and Mei noticed and Amy noticed Mei noticing and ate a third just to even the count.

The hot chocolate came out last, ladled into cups. Too sweet. Too thick. The cinnamon Amy had found behind the empty space where the flour had been.

<font color=mistyrose>"To the road trip."</font>

<font color=indianred>"To the road."</font>

<font color=skyblue>"To the birds!"</font>

<font color=teal>"To the playlist."</font>

<font color=coral>"To the food."</font>

<font color=palegoldenrod>"To getting there."</font>

<font color=greenyellow>"To the toast."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"To coming back."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"To all of it."</font>

## VII.

Late. After dishes. After the table cleared and Lupa did her sweep of the building — doors, vents, exits, the habit of years — and Chisa carried her scissors to bed and Ciaccona carried Cartethyia's terrarium up the stairs because Carte's hip was stiff and neither of them mentioned it. After Amy's door closed and the sound of the bed adjusting, springs and weight, for a person who was solid now and learning the heft of it.

Mei stood in the kitchen with the list.

She pulled the pen from the counter where Lupa had left it, and beneath rule seven she wrote:

**8. We come back.**

She capped the pen. Looked at the yellow paper. Rules one through six in her handwriting, rule seven in Lupa's red, rule eight in hers again. The tape at the upper corner was peeling. She smoothed it down.

Footsteps in the hallway: light, precise, one arm's worth of balance in the gait. Iuno appeared in the doorway in the shirt she slept in, the crystalline stump bare, her hair down.

<font color=royalblue>"You're up."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Adding a rule."</font>

Iuno came to the wall and read it.

*We come back.*

*Oh,* the High Priestess said, very quietly. *Oh, darling.*

Iuno put her hand on Mei's back, between the shoulder blades, where the warmth sat.

<font color=royalblue>"Good rule."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Best one on the list."</font>

They stood in the kitchen with the rules between them and the direction ahead of them and the house behind them. The lichen outside pulsed blue, green, blue, the underground breathing its slow electric night. East of the city line the corridor opened onto surface and sky and a road toward a city in a bowl of water where the willows came out three days ahead on the west bank, and the lamp-grid lit from the north side first, and a woman in an Outrider's hat waited on a cliff at dawn with a question she'd been asking the wind all season.

<font color=moccasin>"Iuno."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Mm."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Thank you for taking the northern route."</font>

Iuno's hand went still on her back.

<font color=royalblue>"Lupa told you."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"Lupa tells me everything. She takes a while, is all."</font>

<font color=royalblue>"Two extra days."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"One more cup?"</font>

<font color=royalblue>"It's midnight."</font>
