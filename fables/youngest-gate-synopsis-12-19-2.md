# THE YOUNGEST GATE

### Books Twelve through Nineteen — A Continuous Account

## XIV. THE SEA ABOVE

*(Gate 12 — the night of the twenty-seventh)*

The plum rains stopped falling and began to climb. They rose along the Tacet seams, joined at the height of a second-storey window, and by dark a sea hung over Jinzhou where the stars belong. In its centre a brightness gathered and closed its last seam of darkness, and the moon of another night sat whole in the water above a hundred and forty thousand people. Jinhsi had copied Jué's prophecy in her own hand at fifteen: *When the moon turns full, they shall return.* Men in the streets called the Sentinel's name. A wounded Ranger at the terrace school said they had seen that light at Norfall three years ago, and told Chixia to get everyone away from the windows.

North of the city, General Jiyan's soldiers were fighting on ground occupied by two armies, because the Retroact Rain gives back what the earth has kept and Norfall's earth had kept the last campaign. Doctor Tang issued an antidote and made the general swallow his own dose while she counted his pulse, and the doctrine of the camp reduced to one line: **every order goes through an officer standing beside the people receiving it.**

Then Geshu Lin stood across the map table, rain running into a split in the leather beside his throat that Jiyan had stitched himself while the general dictated an order over his head. He said the crest was within reach; that another season meant another levy and another row of boys waiting for a surgeon's hands; that he had wanted to finish it. Jiyan had once carried a man with a shattered jaw between two ranks preparing to charge, and the man's hand had beaten against his back the whole way, trying to make him understand something. He answered that his soldiers followed because Bao came back for them at Shuanghe and because Chao told them when the ground was failing, and that each time they survived an order they learned what his next one was worth. He put his weapon across the narrow approach instead of taking the crest. Seventeen of nineteen came out of the north culvert alive; two came out under covers, and he knelt and straightened a bent tag corner and pressed it flat.

Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying reached Mingting with Falling Rain in its open cradle and released the western guns to Jiyan's discretion. Jinhsi asked the capital for medical wagons with lifting equipment, because some of the people coming toward her were very heavy. In Xuanfang, Sentinel Hsin had seven heavy combat platforms and a problem: the rain remembered their old crews, and one had saluted a captain dead before Jinhsi was born and put its shield through a workshop wall. *Three of them have spent forty years learning to recognise a voice*, she told the circuit, *and tonight every voice they have ever loved is standing on the road.*

The Welcoming Wind carried Jinhsi's recovery plan west and slipped through the open vent of the unit called Old Seven. When a dead sergeant's voice called and the great foot lifted, Hsin held the ankle housing — and a small lower shutter, fitted last winter to reach an oil line, opened against her shoulder, and from inside the shield Old Seven said, in the voice of a woman who had once served its post, *You'll be warmer here. Stay until the weather passes.* Hsin read Jinzhou's offer aloud to five crews standing beside their units and asked Old Seven for its answer before the brace came off; it lowered its shield to the yard and asked for the long spanner.

Five marched, and gun crews on the shelf found room to stand upright under iron. Then Old Seven's left foot went through the crust of the eastern shelf and its ankle came apart around the old bearing. Chisa walked the recovery cradle up the service road with her blade on the verge, finding damaged paving by feel; Lupa leaned into the guide bar in a borrowed coat Panhua wanted back with all its buttons. Yangyang met the cradle at the east arch, and the dispatch came out of the lower shutter first, damp and folded along its return road, and warm air brushed the inside of her sleeve and stayed. *I've kept your place by the stove.* At the freight apron, the boy whose model bridge lived on the head teacher's shelf showed the wounded machine how the middle span lifts, and Old Seven levelled its service platform to keep the little road straight.

## XV. ELEVEN PAGES, AND A ROOM FULL OF RAIN

*(Gate 13 — the same night, under the Grand Library)*

Jinhsi had sent the annexe key with no note, and the lock turned smoothly, which meant somebody had oiled it and prepared the way before Mei asked. Inside, eleven folders held the First Resonator's evaluation completed in a steady hand and cancelled in the same hand with the same ink — long strokes drawn hard enough to groove the paper, each starting a quarter-inch before the words and ending a quarter-inch after. No censor's mark anywhere. Somebody had sat at a desk and struck out every word she had just written, so the record would show that a thing had been there and had been removed on purpose. Mei knew the motion the way she knew the puzzle floor at the Court of Savantae and the blast door that had read her palm. *That's my hand. I did this at home too.*

Behind a seam in the back wall was a chamber of glass holding hundreds of raindrops suspended at chest height — the same impossible patience as Heisen's hillside prototype, and older. *She was very thorough*, Mei said, and said *she*. *And someone left a door.* Amy asked it flat, with no cushion, the way Iuno gives numbers: **Was Ma wrong?** Mei said the woman had made sure nobody would find her reasons either. Iuno, who knows the difference between erasure by committee and erasure by one person who understood exactly what she was removing, said the rain had held it anyway. Mei put her palm out under the nearest drop the way a person checks whether it is still coming down. It fell, and gold came through her fingers like sunrise through a shut door. *She was wrong. I want to know.*

The column burst outward and the glass threw back a sky four centuries gone: the Rover in black and gold with Spectro spiralling between her fingers, and behind her Jué descending, amber eyes burning in unison with hers. Mei's face, Mei's eyes. *She's taller than me*, Mei said; Amy told her she was sitting down, which she was, without having decided to.

Then a gate guard was standing behind them wearing her own face with somebody else's ease inside it, and Amy had her against the wall before anyone spoke, and the woman did not resist and did not breathe until she remembered to. **Nivora**, Grand Architect of the Fractsidus — the Charon of the Dark Tide, a woman called Triss before that — told Mei she had just watched herself light a sky and crack open a god's jaw and had sat down, and should ask the right question. So Mei took the pronoun out of it. **Was it wrong?** Nivora said nothing for four seconds, which from a creature who talks for a living is a confession with the words removed. Then she gave it back in the same shape and admitted she could not say yes — and reached for an exit built out of true materials: the little wind was extinguishing herself, none of them were watching, and Jué was missing.

Mei felt the shape of the offer. Sword up, place beside the dragon, save everyone alone. She stayed on the cold floor. *I have people up there. They don't need me to save them. That's the whole point of them.* Nivora said that four hundred years ago she would already be running. Mei agreed, closed her fingers, and said: **I'm not her.** Amy felt a borrowed pulse skip under her knuckles. Asked her name, the shapeshifter opened her mouth twice and produced nothing.

On the City Hall terrace Mei handed the key back and reported eleven sealed pages, all redacted, the hand old. Jinhsi wrote *Key returned. Annexe inspected. Contents previously redacted. No actionable material* — every word true, every word a mercy accepted, because the alternative was a Censorate memorandum, a Board of War request about the disposition of an asset, and by the ninth month a Grand Marshal with a policy about Mei. Then Mei remembered another moon over Septimont in the Dark Tide, when she and Iuno had learned the price of lies, took Iuno's hand, and gave the whole of it. *I chose not to want that. What is your counsel, Magistrate?* Jinhsi said that four years ago she had written that if the arithmetic wants a person as payment the arithmetic can have the city, and had not stopped meaning it; that without Jué the cost was hers; and that her counsel was not her wish, and her wish did not enter the room.

Mei told her the rest: the Grand Architect had wanted her to choose alone, and wanted the same for Jinhsi. Amy, from the doorway: *There was no job, Ma. I held a door for ten years because nobody told me I could put it down.* And Iuno asked how many people were in the room. Five. How many had been in the room when Jinhsi wrote that the arithmetic could have the city. **One.** Changli's mouth opened and closed, and she said six words she had first said to a girl of eight drowning in books too large for her: **Do as you wish. I am here.**

And the Magistrate of Jinzhou said *I don't know*, and her voice broke on the second word. Three hundred and twelve people without a floor; a prophecy copied at fifteen completing itself over her roof; a god silent twenty nights; every wish ever brought to her hall turned into walls and grain and lamp oil, and no answer available for this one. Changli's knuckles went white on the table edge, because seven years had taught her exactly how close she was allowed, and she held there and said they would find one tomorrow, and that tonight she had said it in a room, and that was enough for tonight. Later Mei named the other number — two casualties — and asked whether she had been wrong. Amy gave the honest concession: *Yeah. Maybe.* Iuno said, *Say the rest*, and it came out rough and true: **I'm glad I didn't.** Neither told her it was all right, because it was not, and neither told her she was wrong, because she was not.

## XVI. IN KIND

*(Gate 14 — the twenty-eighth)*

Jinhsi wrote the opening of a public address five times and discarded all five, because every version arranged the city into a single listener, and the actual city had four thousand one hundred and twelve people down from eleven villages and a tax-office goose asleep against a crate of resonance couplings under a blanket somebody had given it. Changli, who had prepared forty-six such addresses as Secretary-General and watched each acquire a different meaning in every room it reached, told her to use tables instead. Eleven ward meetings by the second hour, two of them created because poorer wards objected to being combined with a wealthier street, and Jinhsi approved both objections. She read figures at the harbour guildhall with Panhua's congee beside her papers, and fifty-three people watched her take the first spoonful, and the room's shoulders came down by a measurable amount. Then the city answered in its own units — rooms above a salting shed if the city scrubbed the floor, a tannery tank released overnight, twelve ash staves out of thirty because the rest were promised to flour barrels — and beside each entry Iuno drew the one-stroke mark she had invented before sunrise: **consent with a stated limit.**

Three seals arrived from the capital: the Board of War recalling every Mengzhou platform, Internal Security suspending Senior Operative Qiuyuan, the Grand Marshal keeping Norfall closed. Jinhsi spent the readings Changli gave her — an emergency compact, a summons making Qiuyuan a material witness in an attempted unlawful execution, a repair bond, an indemnity for Xuanfang's exposure — and wrote to Li Zhaoying that she had assumed the obligations under her seal and requested confirmation, amendment, **or personal relief from office by a hand willing to assume them.**

Then a smaller sheet slid out from under the Board packet: an eleven-year chronology of Sentinel irregularities carrying a reference number from Changli's former office and her own certifying initials. The lake's backward answers had begun eleven years ago; Jinhsi's archive said four. Changli explained that the Council had opened a fitness inquiry when Jué chose a fifteen-year-old, and that she had moved the anomaly into the fourth year so the administration would have an established record before the silence could be used to un-choose her — and cited the two articles of the Code she had broken herself. Jinhsi folded the chronology along its existing crease with great care, set the full account for the sixth hour with Clerk Wen present, and said that until then the capital's orders required a former Secretary-General and she required her Counselor, and that trust would have its own meeting.

At the shelf, Qiuyuan put his own token on the gate table. Hsin answered the Board of War in writing with a paw-print beneath it: she had given her word when this woman carried fewer characters than a sentence; the woman had crossed four seas and come back with many hands; her line walked until the promise was answered. Then the phantom drum sounded and Geshu Lin came through his ranks in ceremonial armour with his hair arranged as on memorial prints and a wound placed with an artist's understanding of where an audience would see it. Sergeant Du identified his line — second act of *The General at Norfall*, staged in the ward schools every winter — and Hsin heard the breath after each sentence and named it: somebody had fed him a role until the role learned to march.

So the household went to the theatre. The seventh revision had removed the chorus's names to meet a school runtime and printed a request that the audience complete the final beat by standing. The actor playing Geshu Lin had taken the role because the first actor had made his brother a chorus member — Shen Ke, who hated pears and made their mother cook them separately, signalman, north battery, recovered from the west sinkhole road. The script sent him east; his unit had gone west. Then Chisa found an indentation beside the final line of the oldest prompt book, and charcoal dust raised letters the first director had written and removed before publication: **THE GENERAL LOOKS BACK. THE COMPANY REMAINS VISIBLE.** The printed direction read: **THE GENERAL FACES NORTH. BLACKOUT.** *I've faced north for three years*, the actor said. *By blackout, the stage belongs to me.* Cartethyia set named figures along the true roads and said, quiet and country-plain, that he keeps receiving an empty stage, and that is a very long time to hold a pose.

At the sixth hour Jinhsi read a Council draft that had called her *the juvenile appointee* seven times in twelve pages. *You were fallible and arranged my government around your infallibility.* *I did.* She left Changli's resignation in its envelope, suspended her access to sealed prophetic material, ended her authority over the registry channel, and required the traitor's name: **Luo Zemin**, registry examiner, a daughter at Huaxu, a debt manufactured for the purpose — for whom she ordered a hearing, protection, and counsel. By the fifth hour the shelter remainder had fallen from three hundred and twelve to two hundred and thirty-one, at the speed of bolts fitted and stoves tested and people deciding what their own doors could hold. On the terrace, the field beneath **COURSE OF ACTION** stayed empty. *My office keeps offering me a proclamation. One voice, one seal, one city arranged to receive it.* Mei said the rain chamber favoured its own picture too, and put her at the centre with a sword and everyone else out of frame. They left the field open.

## XVII. THE STOVE, AND A COLD DUMPLING

*(Gate 14A — that evening)*

*I can't see the way back*, Mei told the stove. She could feel the shape that wanted to pull her in and could not feel the door out the other side. Ciaccona named the missing thing — the erased direction, and whoever had decided the dead stop mattering once they have served their use — and Cartethyia gave the whole work in one sentence: *You have to convince them differently.*

Then Lupa put her flask down with the finality that makes a room notice. Cartethyia had solved it in a sentence and everyone had nodded, and the two who died last night were still dead. Mei said she knew; Lupa asked whether she did, with her tail entirely still, which meant something. So Mei said it small: *I'm choosing not to stand in the door. Having one person do it is what got us here in the first place. Even if I could bring them back, I would still choose to sit here, beside all of you.* And then the question underneath — whether remembering them gave her the right to live while they fought and died. Lupa said the word *rights* like something found on a road: Rui had not had the right to die; he had forty years and a bad stomach and a sergeant whose belt needed grabbing. Amy said one person in one place is not the math that keeps people alive, and she knew, because she had been that person. And Iuno put the real question on the table in the voice she keeps for numbers: *Do you trust us to be in the rooms you're not standing in?* *I do. And I trust everyone on the frontline as well.* Iuno's hand came off the table into her coat pocket, where a ring's filed edge pressed back against her fingers.

Afterwards Mei found the Grand Architect at a dumpling stall with a bowl she was not eating, sat down, and took one of her dumplings. Why the stage direction? *I learnt from the best*, Nivora said, carrying it the way you carry something expensive. *Lady Arbiter. The original was much better.* Because it had worked: the Lady Arbiter crossed every page she ever wrote about herself, left the company behind, faced north, and won. *She was wrong.* *She was effective.* *That's not the same thing.* And something under the borrowed voice went quiet in a way that was not performance: *No. It never was.*

Mei put a dumpling in front of her hands and told her to eat it or say what she had been waiting for. Nivora took the bite, set her chopsticks down parallel with the etiquette of a great many tables, and said: **for someone to take up the pen.** Whose pen? The borrowed eyes went to the mark on Mei's hand and did not answer. Mei said, without ceremony, *The company remains visible*, and let her go. Reporting it later, Mei said the Fractsidus build with true materials and so does everyone at that table; and Changli, doing arithmetic she had spent seven years learning, said the only time a player hands over a stone is when they have something better to do with their next move. *Yeah. That's what I'm counting.* Which meant Norfall — where the general faces north, alone, a picture being built for more than Geshu Lin.

## XVIII. THE COMPANY REMAINS

*(Gate 15 — twelve hours before the north road opens)*

Jinzhou turned Mei's suggestion about sleep into a rota. Lupa wrote names on the duty board before anybody with a seal could turn rest into a matter of character; Jinhsi ruled that City Hall recognises a rest order issued by a competent person during an emergency; Panhua put a bowl over the shelter return and told the Magistrate that magistrates get a column too. The citizens of Ward Nine arrived for their meeting with their own chairs, found their Magistrate asleep under a bathhouse blanket, set the chairs down quietly, and had a carpenter fix the cot's loose crosspiece while she slept.

On Bookbinders' Row, six rival houses set one register: names, then one verified personal detail beside each name, then the true roads. Amy ran the receiving desk — *give me the name, your relation, and one thing an official history would waste* — and collected a cook who salted with her left hand and threw dice with her right, a cartographer with accurate roads and fantastical chickens, two brothers filed under one date with one identity tag between them whose sisters argued through five possible knots and agreed on the fifth. At two in the morning a proof came off the press with seven names missing from the middle of complete lines, the paper still holding the pressure of the type; the rear lamps went out in sequence and a Fractsidus compositor came out of the dark in an apron hung with filed-smooth type sorts. Lupa caught the wrist, Chisa cut the pin out of the air, and when footsteps crossed the roof Lupa stayed with the families crouched under the composing stones and let the Patrollers take the chase, and it cost her, and Chisa heard the cost in her grip. The prisoner said, with somebody else's mouth, *The company clears during the speech.* Cartethyia held up Captain Mi Renshu's blank figure, and a shaking young man said the captain used to rescue moths from washbasins with a cup and a postcard, on campaign as well — and the blank on the proof darkened from the outer strokes inward until the name stood complete. By dawn the Row had a rule: two readers per forme, one hand copy per proof, one personal detail per name, and every blank kept in place with its source and the hour it was found.

On the south shelf the paired doctrine held — **ONE VOICE CALLS. ONE VOICE ANSWERS. A THIRD KEEPS THE ROAD.** When the rain raised a dead captain beneath Bao's gun, Du caught her harness and made her name her six living people and their route home, and by the sixth name the phantom had rain through his chest. When old command tones reached the guardians, Hsin made the present crews state their names and the lifted foot came down inside the line. A Fractsidus infiltration ended in forty-seven seconds and seven prisoners, because Qiuyuan heard six living frequencies wrapped in smooth absences before the scouts saw anything and Hsin caught the seventh with a tail; and when a speaking seal issued Geshu Lin's voice, Jiyan answered by naming Shen Ke and Mi Renshu into the ranks, and Qiuyuan split the seal along a seam too fine for the eye.

The shelter number fell by doors. When an innkeeper withdrew her dining room because coal could only be guaranteed to dawn, Jinhsi handed back the key and paid for the inspection time from emergency cash, saying public power already had the school and the guildhall and the yards and that her household kept its own threshold; two hours later the innkeeper returned with six brazier bricks from her sister's kiln and the room through noon.

At dawn Jinhsi finished Changli's account — thirty-one pages of minutes — and then made her **Acting Magistrate of Jinzhou**, because that officer knew eleven wards, seven ministries, the north compact and the habits of every office arguing over their survival. The terms: the civil seal until Jinhsi resumed office in person or a Decree Council proceeding installed a successor; Wen holding the prophetic key and countersigning anything touching Jué, the registry, or Luo Zemin's household; requisition of private rooms requiring the ward table's concurrence; stores, casualties, shelter and roads read publicly every second hour. And in her own hand at the foot: **THE COMPLETE ACCOUNT BETWEEN MAGISTRATE AND COUNSELOR REMAINS SCHEDULED UPON RETURN.** She said why she was going north herself: Geshu Lin had died under Jinzhou's command and had received three years of answers written at a safe distance from him. *The question travels in my mouth; the answer returns to eleven tables.*

Amy asked for the distances on paper, because Ma drifts when people need catching and Auntie predicts around corners. Iuno gave every member two recall voices, one from the field and one from the city, so that if the performance took a voice the second would remain outside its grammar. Lupa added a handhold; Chisa stitched short red tethers that release when the person holding them chooses. At the theatre Ciaccona removed the audience's cue and the company stayed seated, and one minute forty-one seconds later Ovathrax supplied the missing beat from the north — so they ran the first edition with the messenger restored and the named figures on their true roads, and when the actor turned to look back he found a stage full of people doing separate work, and his next breath came from his own chest. Ciaccona wrote the change in and **left the fourth beat open.**

The remainder reached four — the Ge family and a donkey named Minister who accepted tribute and ignored policy — and Chixia found them a courier stable and a family room with a ground-floor entrance, and the official remainder reached **zero**: four thousand one hundred and twelve individual entries, forty-seven arrangements ending within two days, nine stoves to repair, signed with those conditions attached, because zero can travel with its debts showing. Six minutes later the register reopened for a daughter born in the weavers' hall. Li Zhaoying amended the Norfall restriction and gave the objective: separate the dead from the performance, keep a named road south available to every living member, preserve each dead soldier's capacity to answer apart from the chorus, identify the Fractsidus control structure, deny Ovathrax the completed cue — with named recovery obligations, roll by roll, and her own line at the bottom. At the tables before departure an old man from the north terraces put down a wooden chopstick and said his son Gao Shun had clicked it against his bowl when he wanted another serving and had believed all his life that his mother missed it. *Tell him she heard every time.* Jinhsi took it across both palms.

## XIX. BENEATH THE CRESCENT

*(Gates 16 and 17 — the march, and the first ten minutes)*

They walked north four hours at the pace of people carrying paper. The Welcoming Wind pressed hard against Yangyang's cheek the whole way, and it was the frightened press: ahead of them Chixia argued formation theory in a red patrol cap worn at the careless angle of somebody who has owned one for years — and the Wind had seen a red cap once before, floating in still water in a city that drowned in another printing. *She knows they are fine*, Yangyang told Mei. *She keeps seeing the other thing anyway.*

On the north lip, **Cristoforo** set his torches by hand as markers rather than light. He had run this production across seven prefectures over eleven years and regarded all of it as instructive juvenilia. Scar asked why they were watching; because the Blessed Maiden had spent twenty years in the dark naming dolls, and the bard carried a blank card with his marks on it and had written nothing, and the Lady — he smiled, finding the beat — **brought everybody.** Below, Hsin observed to a sensor array with no opinions that the amnesiac did not know she was the most dangerous thing in the valley, that the ones beside her did, and that they were not afraid of it: they were watching her hands.

Jinhsi stepped onto a flat stone and checked a speaking horn's mouthpiece for rain, and Cristoforo brought his fingers down. The charge under the medical track split Mei's tongue against her own teeth; Chixia hit the Magistrate at the waist and carried both of them behind the command board through iron pellets and took one in the calf that stopped against bone. The second charge drove Master Gong Mi face-first into the cradle road and put a stone splinter through the throat of **Engineer Deng Hao**, who had been reaching for him. Mei reached for Amy, and the red tether jerked from the other direction, wrapped round Iuno's fist. *Your daughter has Ciaccona beside her. Take my shoulder and stand up with me.* Mei obeyed, and the whole shape of the day was in that.

Amy took the felt doll of her mother out of her pocket, gave her number as fifty-seven, and went in; and **Nyx Ravenhollow** rose off the mud — five metres of restored Startorch armour, ten flight blades left of eleven — and asked to be pointed at whoever was shooting at their paperwork. Cristoforo had chalked three destinations inside his fighters' cuffs: white for the register cases, red for the Magistrate, gold for Mei's right hand. Mei saw the straight road open — six bodies, the berm, the valley floor, the ridge, the man with two fingers raised — and her mark lit until the tendons showed through her glove. Iuno leaned her crystalline shoulder into Mei's back and gave her a smaller task: two fighters on the right. Mei took the first onto Iuno's raised knee, put her marked palm over the second's muzzle and lost a strip from the side of her hand, and went down among the map stakes with a knife stopping against her rib while Iuno's living hand closed the man's collar across his throat.

Lupa held a hook chain while Chisa listened it through three kinds of stone and sheared the anchor at the third pace. Cartethyia batted a fire flask out of the air, took burning pitch across her sleeve and the glass of her camellia terrarium, and Fleurdelys came up out of her in one surge. Baizhi told Engineer Pei that Deng Hao had died, that his tag stayed on him and his body went inside the shield line, and that four people could lift the slab on her count. Jinhsi knelt in the mud with both hands round Chixia's calf and her own scarf folded into a pad, and Chixia told her the shooters had waited for the horn and had wanted her standing in that exact patch, and Jinhsi tucked the field seal deeper into her coat and said she could work from the ground.

The attack peeled away against a reserve rotation that changed faces while keeping its shape. Scar reached the command board and said Cristoforo had promised him a proclamation, and was told that Jinzhou currently meant six medical cradles, a damaged west road and a relief section arriving behind him, and that he could file a petition during office hours. Jiyan read the field return: one dead, seventeen wounded, both register cases intact. Jinhsi turned over the ruined first page of her address, found clean paper on the back apart from one blood print, and wrote **Deng Hao** at the top of it. Told she still owed them the inspiring part, she looked at the shelf — Deng carried behind a shield with an orderly's hand over his tag, Yangyang and the Wind delivering pages out of the mud corner-first — and said they already had their instructions, and the city could hear the account when the road carried it home.

Cristoforo applauded once and told Nivora that the actors had brought private habits to public roles, which is where a production begins to live: Act One had achieved its proper ending, and everyone now understood that the floor was real. Then the rain turned over, and Deng Hao's tag rose out of a palm and pointed north, and one thousand nine hundred and seven pairs of feet settled onto their marks.

## XX. THE COMPANY REMAINS VISIBLE

*(Gate 17, Act II)*

They came at parade speed on a road the Retroact drew upward out of the mud in silver lines, with faces of memorial bronze, and rings spreading through the suspended sea wherever a boot landed, as if the dead were walking on the underside of a lake. Nobody crossed the berm alone; Du steadied a Ranger who had turned toward sunlight on a remembered rail by asking him to name his six and say what Wei was carrying in his left hand.

Then Cartethyia knelt at the forward berm with her splinted thumb and laid the first figure down and spoke the way a person names a dish she is serving. *Shen Ke. North battery, signalman. Recovered from the west sinkhole road on the second day. He hated pears. His family cooked them in a separate pot.* The ink darkened and the character on its chest lifted in a pale amber glow at the temperature of a lamp left on, and sixty paces out a signalman's arms rose a little because a stage property suddenly had weight, and his head turned an inch east toward the road that was actually his. The chorus pulled him back. The inch remained. Ciaccona put her first note between the third drumbeat and the held space, where the march had no room for it, and twelve soldiers lost a step, and through the gap the rain showed a muddy encampment held together by meal rotations and an argument about which way the supply road turned at the second ridge.

Mei's gold climbed her fingers unasked, and where it touched a hanging drop the drop turned and became a lens: a man asleep against a supply crate with a half-eaten plum on his knee, a woman counting ammunition with her lips moving, two soldiers under one blanket arguing whose turn it was to fold. *They're in there. Under the armour. Under the march.* Iuno told her the light was extending past the berm and to pull back six inches and sustain from there, and it steadied. Qiuyuan put his Forte through the layered frequency — stage directions compressed into destiny, memorial applause become a permanent audience, school recitations smoothing every rough edge off a living voice — and found under it a young man breathing hard through a split lip, at the cost of fresh blood along the blade's flat. *The general is aware. The performance does not silence him. It drowns him in company.*

Chisa supplied the arithmetic: a stranger's name holds a figure one to two seconds; a family member's voice holds it six. And Amy, who had run a city off a radio for ten years, built the architecture in her hands: **all channels, all wards — families with a name in the register, speak your detail into the nearest receiver. One sentence.** A photograph inside a canteen lid, replaced twice because the steam ruined it. A father frightened of geese who went the long way round the tax office. A husband whose left boot wore faster because he dragged his foot when thinking. A grandson who carried a river stone for a year because a girl who moved away had said she liked the way he listened. Armour peeled, and Cartethyia went on placing figures and crying and placing figures, and the ink held, because she had used the good ink.

Jinhsi carried the field seal and one sheet in Clerk Wen's hand to the forward berm and held it up facing the valley, the way a magistrate holds a ruling. *The direction was removed from your script three years ago, and I am restoring it. Your company remains visible. Shen Ke is carrying a flag on the wrong side of the valley because the play put him there. Mi Renshu is looking for a moth she cannot find because her gauntlets are painted on. Your city has been asked, and your city has answered.*

Geshu Lin's head turned a degree — and every head in the formation turned with him, because the play had built them into one body with one face. Three hundred and twelve stood slightly apart with their faces warming into firelight. Ciaccona left the fourth beat open, and for one and a half seconds no drum filled it and no direction covered it. Then Hsin repurposed four tactical loudspeakers for a recital, the voices of Jinzhou reached the middle ranks, and the number reached **five hundred and twelve**. Scar told Cristoforo to close the beat; he declined, because he had built Geshu Lin a solitary ending on the strength of the Lady Arbiter's legend and she had answered with a crowded one. Nivora said he was describing an audience for a god. *Yes. And they have brought chairs.*

## XXI. THE PLAY THAT ATE ITS REVIEWS

*(Gate 18 — the Sonoro Sphere)*

He came down off the ridge and clapped four times, exactly, one per named grouping the household had pulled loose, so everyone would understand they were being marked; he meant the praise and asked that it be minuted. Then he thanked Ciaccona for the four seconds she had refused to fill, said a rest is only silent if nobody puts anything in it, and snapped his fingers.

The rain turned over. Every drop that had held a plum on a knee emptied and refilled with the last twenty minutes of somebody's life — nineteen hundred and seven of them, at once, as material. Shen Ke got four hours of roadbed across his hips; Gao Shun got the eleven minutes he had spent trying to breathe with a lung filling, quietly, because there were men worse off within earshot and he had been raised not to make a fuss at table. And the arrows stopped being phantoms on the way down: an orderly took a spear through the shoulder, and **Ranger Cao Yi**, second section, twenty-six, who had spent the walk north arguing that the tax-office goose was a protected species, was named aloud within four seconds of dying, because that was the doctrine now. *The chorus was the morphine*, Cristoforo told the valley, with tenderness. *It is very cheap and it works. You have lit the room where the pain is happening, and now they can see the whip.* Iuno put an arrow through his raised hand; he looked at the wound the way a man looks at a review and thanked her for the correct instinct.

Amy asked the wards whether to cut the channel. *Cut it*, Iuno said instantly, out of thirty years of reflex — heard it land — and withdrew it in the same breath: *that was the old thing; it is not mine to cut.* Jinhsi told the city plainly what it was hearing and that every ward table had the switch and nobody's account was reduced by taking it. Eight wards stayed on; Ward Nine cut, and the clerk entered why — fourteen children present, no second room; Ward Eleven stayed at a quarter volume under the one-stroke mark for consent with a stated limit, used for the ninetieth time in two days. Then Shen Ke found the channel, because he had been a signalman, and said his name had been spoken and had not stopped; and his brother the actor, in a costume he had not taken off in nineteen hours, leaned into a receiver in an empty theatre and found he had no line, and said the only thing he had: *Ma still cooks them in the other pot, A-Ke. Every autumn. She won't be told. I'm not going anywhere.*

Mei asked Lupa for six metres of rope and the stone Jinhsi had stood on, and got both after two full seconds of public inquiry, and read names off the register in the flat cadence of a clerk at a ward meeting. Arrows came; Lupa shifted her centre of gravity three inches with the rope; Chisa sheared two out of the air by sound and took a third across the ear and stayed. A grandmother in Ward Eight supplied her daughter's second canteen before Mei reached the line; a boy in Ward Six said his father's name and the phantom wearing it dropped his spear. So Cristoforo said one word — *Stage* — and the interval fell sixteen seconds early, and six copper wires in the ridge ignited with grammar. The Retroact made the directions historical, so the valley had always been a theatre and the people in it had always been a company, and the stone under Mei's boots developed a grain running downstage to wings.

**THE LADY ARBITER TAKES HER PLACE AT CENTRE STAGE. SHE HAS ALWAYS BEEN HERE.** Her feet moved half a step, and when the direction doubled her fingers closed on the hilt. **THE WOLF GUARDS THE THRESHOLD. SHE DOES NOT CROSS**, and Lupa's whole body tried to become a door, and her fire came up her arms the way it does when she cannot reach somebody, and lit nothing. **THE ORACLE WATCHES FROM THE WINGS**, and Iuno was placed eight feet right — the position she had occupied her entire adult life, near the door, seeing disasters three moves ahead, far enough away that nobody could accuse her of participating. Her body hurt less there; she could see the exact angle at which the legend would close and the number who would survive, and the number was good; and her mouth opened and produced nothing, because oracles observe.

So she stopped trying to speak, took the ring out of her coat — one facet sawn from her own crystal shoulder — and threw it at her partner's drawing hand. It struck Mei's knuckles and the sword stopped rising, and Iuno said one word that fell outside every genre the playwright had ever written, because it was Lupa's word for keeping a thing small and real and doing it on a day that does not matter: **"*Tuesday.*"** The Sphere stuttered around it like type skipping a sort that will not fit the forme.

The others got cages built from true things. Ciaccona watched her own hands complete his fourth beat in his key. Cartethyia's knees found the boards and real prayers from the real seminary that had owned twenty years of her arrived in her mouth, because the cruelty was selection rather than invention. Chisa's red string became the most theatrical object on the field — and Chisa, who cannot read a face and therefore cannot read a stage direction, listened underneath and heard six heartbeats on the ridge and a gap between the false floor and the real one. **THE DAUGHTER—** hung incomplete for a full second, because Amy had already been the Star and the anchor and the sacrifice and had jumped out of every one of them; then it found the role she could not refuse: **THE DAUGHTER WATCHES HER MOTHER BECOME THE LEGEND**, which she had done for ten years from inside the dark, and the audience is where his power lives. **THE GATE DOES NOT ASK FOR HELP, BECAUSE HELP COSTS THE GATE ITS PURPOSE**, and Jinhsi's field seal went cold in her hand. **THE RED CAP FLOATS**, and Chixia's cap turned the colour of a thing seen through dark water, and the Wind screamed a sound stripped past language, and Yangyang caught its collapse against her chest with the temperature dropping eight degrees beside her while Chixia shouted at a cold she has never been able to see that she was right there and loud and not going anywhere. And on the boards that ran south under her guns, **THE GRAND MARSHAL FIRES**, and Li Zhaoying's fingers touched the flag cord and she understood in the half-second before the lanyard that her shells would cross a stage full of dead people whose families were listening. Her hand stopped. *Belay firing.* And when the direction argued: *I said belay.*

Each of them broke their own cage. None of them broke anyone else's. Cristoforo swept his cane across five hundred and twelve soldiers whose personal details the march had simply learned — the man carving peach-pit birds in ceremonial plate, the woman singing off-key inside the chorus's harmony, every fact making the production richer — and said the thing he had come eleven years to say. *You cannot break a narrative by refusing your role. You can only break it by refusing the form. And the form is* — **ONE PERSON. ONE CENTRE. ONE STORY.** And a voice said one word, low and warm and older than the valley: **引征.**

## XXII. THE LANDLORD

*(Gate 18, continued)*

Hsin stepped off San Jie's shoulder and arrived on the boards the way a signature arrives on a document, closed her paper umbrella with enormous deliberation, and the grain of the wood turned ninety degrees under every foot in the valley. She itemised the compliment — the copper inspired, the incorporation mechanic the finest piece of Sonoro engineering in four hundred years — and then: *You also built it inside my house.* A Sonoro Sphere in Huanglong falls under the citation of Huanglong's Sentinels, and Mengzhou holds reciprocal authority over Spheres touching its sovereign interests; five of her guardians and their crews and her alliance with the Welcoming Wind were all inside his production. She could reach it because he had built it around her, and he had built it around her because he had taken her for decoration. She named that as homework he had not done, and put out his wires one at a time while she said so.

The stage stopped extending. The directions did not release; they could not *complete*, because a sentence requires a period and the period belongs to the room. Mei's hand unclenched. Lupa's leash lost its anchor. Iuno gasped, hoarse and undignified, and said nothing with it, and the nothing was hers. The terms: every living person who had not built the Sphere leaves; the dead remain, being nobody's to move; the choreography goes with them, because the choreography requires her floor. An empty theatre, he said. *I leave you the parterre. You wrote a play about what happens when one person stands alone. Let us sit together and watch what happens when she doesn't.*

The boards vanished. Mei hit real ground with a blank page going soft in her hand and a sword nobody had drawn for her, and the ring lay in the mud between her knees, and she picked it up and asked nothing about it, because asking would have made it a Moment. Nivora signalled and twelve agents and three Discords came down to buy time, and the valley became a battlefield, which differs from a stage in that every choice costs something nobody scripted. Scar went for the register cases and met Lupa with her fire clean and hers again, fought her nine real seconds, opened a fold in the air and went, saying Mei would still come when she knew what she was; and Qiuyuan, from one knee with a blade wound between his fourth and fifth ribs, taken standing after refusing the clean revenge the Sphere had offered him because testimony happens in company, said: *She knows.*

Then Jinhsi stepped off the berm onto real mud with the field seal, Gao Shun's chopstick in her belt, and the first-edition direction copied in her own hand, and Jiyan walked beside her at the distance a field commander keeps from a civil authority in a combat zone. The broken formation had gaps and she walked through them the way a magistrate walks a ward, looking at each person, because she had come to look. *I am Jinhsi, Magistrate of Jinzhou. Three years ago your city signed your loss as acceptable. I am here to read the correction.* And she read it flat: **The general looks back. The company remains visible.** Geshu Lin turned his head, fully, and his company was there.

What the rain gave him then was the sixth dispatch — clean paper, correct initials, correct seal, filed under the category that means *we know, we have calculated, hold another line*, with the cost expressed as a range that included everyone behind him, by category. *The city held*, he said. *On what.* Jinhsi looked at him and not at the names falling between them, and said she could not make it right: she could tell him the truth and stand there while it fell and keep the register open and put Deng Hao's name in it that night, and those were the things she could do, and they were not enough. Then he looked past her at Jiyan — at his own spear held at his own angle by the man who had reached him four hours late and had spent three years carrying everything he left behind as a form of apology — and the rain showed the seventh dispatch arriving two days late, carried nine li in the dark by a runner whose horse threw a shoe, saying *hold* to a position that had held thirty-one hours past the point where the word applied to living men. *You kept my spear. You held my wall and you* — and his voice broke on a frequency belonging to a man of twenty-seven — ***you lived.*** And he drew and swung.

In the same heartbeat, four metres away, Mei walked toward Iuno and reached for her hand, and the Wind saw it. It had been going east, because east was where Mei was; and at the shore it had felt a low immovable note come out of the west with the precision of a deliberate hand adjusting a variable it had already modelled, and had pressed against it while a body on the wet stones slid west. It had crossed the dark between printings spending everything the crossing costs, and arrived, and found her already loved, already held, already someone else's. Nivora had put her hand into the rain and opened the channel, and the shore lay in the air over the valley for everyone to see. Iuno said it before she could be asked: *That was me. I was in Septimont. I was running the plan. I saw the wind going east and I filed it under Weather. I kept her west.* The cold did not change, because it was not a revelation — the Wind had known, and had come anyway. She began *your city —* and stopped, because finishing it would have been for her own benefit. The Wind spent a word on the shore: ***Late.***

Then Yangyang's own blade came out of her scabbard at the angle a swordswoman had learned in a life she no longer had a name for, and went for Iuno's throat. Iuno saw it three moves early, as she has seen everything for thirty years, and did not act. She looked at Mei, who was already drawn toward the blade with the instinct that has killed her twice and that her daughter copied into a god's dark — and the look was the old one by a fraction of a degree, and the fraction was everything, because it did not say *I have this.* It said *I trust you to stay where you are.* **Mei stayed.** And Yangyang caught the line — the intention, the trajectory from shoulder to throat — by joining it and running alongside, because the stroke was hers, taught to her by the person swinging it, corrected at the elbow a hundred times over a year by a presence that never explained because the explanation lives in the muscle. She brought her right elbow in half an inch: the exact adjustment she had been given silently every night by something that loved her enough to fix the same fault every time and never once say why.

The cut turned and struck crystal. The crystal had never bled — eleven years and a hundred and seventy-three names compressed into an iridescent stump worn bare because a deserter held together with wire had told her it was proof she did not run. The blade opened it along the grain, and what came out was liquid warmth, amber-gold, running into rain that carried the memory of the shore and the note that had moved the body west: the price discovering it had not finished being paid, and would never finish, and you stand in it anyway. Meanwhile Jiyan had left his spear point-down in the mud and put his open hand up — the medic's first instinct, which predates rank — and the blade opened his palm from heel to fingers along the line a surgical needle enters, and his hand closed on the general's wrist, where a medic takes a pulse. The count was zero and had been zero for three years, and he counted anyway. Then the blade fell and rang once on the stone beside the late dispatch, the sound a weapon makes when it is put down on purpose; and a young man's hand with a cookfire burn across the knuckles came onto Jiyan's shoulder and said the word a soldier uses when the wound is real and the pride can wait: ***Medic.*** *Here*, Jiyan said.

The cold beside Yangyang shattered — the Wind's own teaching applied to itself by the student, at the moment doing it correctly saved someone — and separated, present and distinct and very small. Yangyang dropped the blade and caught it against her chest with both arms: **I've got you.** The Wind spent its second-to-last word on the shore: ***Here.*** Iuno pressed her living hand over the fracture and looked across four metres of rain. *I'm here.* *I know*, Mei said, and did not cross, and did not need to. Where the crystal's warmth met the rain it went south along the Tacet scars to the ninth tower, whose bell had been silent since the dark because silence is what you keep for someone who has not come home; and it trembled in its housing, and the keeper wrote the fifth entry in six years: *The bell answered.*

## XXIII. THE ROAD

*(Gate 18, concluded)*

The ground split at the northern edge, and what came through was not a body or a voice or a god. Ovathrax has no face; it has weight. Stone folded upward in tiers, carrying three years of unfinished war and thirty-one unanswered hours and a commendation that fits on one line and a school play running every winter and a city that wrote *acceptable* in a column and closed the file. The drum settled into one second, one second, one second, and the weight pressed south toward everyone, because war does not select. Seventeen people stood in it, and Mei said, in the voice of a woman distributing a shift: *They've been holding three years. It's our turn.*

Jiyan's whistle carried the oldest word in the field manual. ***Relief.*** *Your section is relieved. Your company is relieved. The relief column has arrived, three years late, and I will account for every hour of that delay, and you are going home.* Geshu Lin said he could not hold it; Jiyan said he had his spear and his surgical kit and his doctrine, and had held them three years, and they were his, and he was giving them back.

Hsin sent four guardians into the gap in shield configuration nine — *the one we tested and you told me wouldn't work; it works* — and two hundred tonnes of sovereign metal stepped between the living and the weight of war. Amy brought Nyx up, closed the eastern road, and told her mother she had the door; her mother, without looking back, said she knew. Lupa hit the forward line with fire that cleared weight rather than enemies, and the road appeared: mud and stone and twelve ordinary kilometres to Jinzhou's north gate. Ciaccona played the ugly chord — the working chord she had once described on a roof, sixteen people in a low room with wet plaster, the one that never resolves because a room full of people eating only gets louder and then somebody brings more chairs — and the march stuttered, and into the stutter Cartethyia drove a name from the mud: *Sun Qiang! He carved peach-pit birds and left them on people's bedrolls! He's going home!* And a soldier looked at his own palms and turned south, one step, into the road.

Li Zhaoying's batteries opened at three hundred metres above the valley floor — shooting at the sky, because the stage was gone and the dead were people in a road and the target was the weather pressing down on them. *Make it flinch.* It flinched, and the dead walked in the flinches. Qiuyuan found the seams: the war had been fed on official remembrance, and what it had eaten had given it structure, and structure has seams. Baizhi named the condition — unmanaged grief, an overclock — and Chisa found the boundary between the grief and the griever and used the other thing her blade does. She stitched. *Here. That's the edge. You can put that part down.* Fleurdelys held the western break where the stone folded fastest — *I've been here before. I was born here* — and her light joined the dark rather than fighting it, which is what she had learned at the bottom of everything. Yangyang gave the Wind the work instead of the grief, and it held the rain off the corridor for eleven minutes it could not afford, until she said: *That's enough. You are not the sky. You are a person. You have a seat at a table. Come home.* And the Wind came down into her sleeve and kept its last word, because keeping it meant having it tomorrow, and having it tomorrow meant tomorrow existed.

Mei stood in the road where a keeper stands when she is lighting the next lamp — halfway between the last one and the next, visible from both — and turned her mark around and pointed it south, and the gold lit the road with the warm visibility that tells a person walking in the dark that they are on a path and somebody thought to light it. A man in broken armour brushed her sleeve as he passed, and she let him pass. Iuno counted, flat, no run-up, no face, because Mei had asked for numbers that way; Amy counted on her beat so no number could be taken. The guardians bent, and each crossing cost a blow absorbed, a shell fired, a bar held, a seam found, heat that could not be replaced. The thirty-three whose names the sources had lost went through with their bordered blank spaces around them, and nobody filled the space, because the space was theirs. Geshu Lin said he should be last; Jiyan said he would be, and that they would walk together, and that the spear stayed with him because the general would want a hand free for the chopstick. The count reached one thousand nine hundred and seven.

In the amber box, Cristoforo did not applaud, for the first time in eleven years, because applause is for performances and what he had watched was a shift change. *It's not an ending*, he said. *No*, Hsin agreed, opening her umbrella. *It's a Tuesday.* And at the ninth tower the keeper put her hands on a seal that had been intact through six incidents and three refusals, and broke it, and the bell swung full for the first time since the dark: eight beats, at full morning, for a column of one thousand nine hundred and seven. It reached a harbour vendor who put down three extra bowls unasked; Bookbinders' Row, where a compositor found a sort that had lain face down for thirty-one years standing upright in its compartment and put it in the forme; Panhua's, where a lamp leaned toward a kept seat; the east gate, where Shen touched the lid of her tin and said nothing; and Ward Four, where Clerk Wen opened the register to a clean page and wrote one heading — **RETURNS** — and left the rest blank. Changli heard it under the north gate and said: *Clear the road. They're coming home.* Nobody cheered. They set the tables.

## XXIV. STAND DOWN

*(Gate 19 — the morning after)*

After the bell's eighth beat there was a space where a ninth would have been, and it stayed empty. Then the drum: one second, then two, then four, eleven, twenty-two, forty — and then it broke off the count and went somewhere Qiuyuan would have to follow to hear. *It has stopped asking.* The tiers came down like a stack of wet paper with the weight taken off, and the suspended sea let go of its borrowed moon. Bao, on an ammunition box with her strapped thumb held against her chest, delivered the analysis that went into three separate written accounts that week: *孤掌難鳴.* One hand makes no sound. *It needs somebody to hit. We stopped holding still.*

Jiyan read the field return with his cut hand already through with blood: two dead, Deng Hao and Cao Yi; forty-one wounded; nineteen prisoners, four carried in by the people who had shot them, because anyone reaching the recovery markers got treated before custody was recorded. **Missing: none** — and he said that part twice. Chixia recognised Cao Yi as the kid who had argued the whole line down about the tax-office goose, said he had been right, and asked for it written down as a bequest; and Iuno, bleeding amber out of her arm, asked for the spelling. Baizhi meanwhile measured the rain twice eleven minutes apart, caveat attached: it halves every four hours. Twelve kilometres; nineteen hundred people walking at the pace of people who have been standing for three years. Iuno asked her to say the sentence aloud so neither of them could arrange it. *If they are still on the road when it goes, they will go on the road.*

Then the negotiation. Nivora came down off the ridge and waited to be acknowledged, which nobody who had watched her for four hundred years would have predicted. Jinhsi named the article: Deng Hao's homicide, inside her boundary, on that man's signal. Hsin said she was correct in every particular, that her article reached the boundary of the writ and stopped, and that she was going to release him. Qiuyuan objected in the only register he owns, which is calm — eleven years, seven prefectures, a stolen seal, a Grandmaster buried under a name no longer his, a blade in his ribs and a private verdict refused out loud with the blade still in him. *I came to testify. Testimony requires a defendant.* *It requires a record*, Hsin said, and promised it entire with her chop on it, and named the substitution at its own size so he could keep the difference and hold it against her, which she expected and called his right. The price was said at ordinary volume and reached the relay recording as a flat tone with no formants in it. *That is expensive.* *It is.* *I accept it.* *You accepted it before I finished, which tells me the thing I actually wanted, and which you have now given me for free.* It took under a minute, and Iuno said afterward only that two extremely old creatures had agreed on something at the speed of people who have rehearsed against each other for a very long time, and that the speed was the information.

Cristoforo told the Magistrate she had read the direction and had not written it. So she wrote it standing in the rain with the paper going soft under the pencil: *The finding of the third year, that the losses at Norfall were acceptable, is vacated. The company of General Geshu Lin did not disappear. One thousand nine hundred and seven persons were present with him and remained present. Their names, personal details and true roads are entered in the two registers appended, which are to be filed as one document and are not to be summarised.* She folded it into the seal case. *There. It is written. Get off my valley.* He bowed properly and went north — the wrong direction for a released man — and Iuno said out loud that there is one road on the far side and it runs west and then north, and at the end of it is a city that prints things. Nivora walked with him to the lip, stopped, turned back once, and found the woman she was looking for already looking at her; and neither raised a hand, because it was not that kind of accounting. Asked what she had paid, Hsin said she had bought something with it, that those are different transactions, and: **Ask me in Mengzhou** — and then, over her shoulder with all nine tails still: *When you come, bring the quad. Do not have it cast. Bring it as it is.*

Li Zhaoying came up the gorge and had the problem before anyone said it, because the negative space of this battlefield was a road with a clock on it. *Then the road is the weather, and my reserve has just become a convoy.* The Board of War's forms have no column for the escort of one thousand nine hundred and seven persons deceased three years previously and currently walking, so she wrote the true thing in the space provided and let the audit come, reading every clause aloud as she wrote it: shield sections forward and in echelon, projectors at the setting supplied by the Sentinel of Mengzhou's engineers **and no other**; guns laid north and on nothing south of them; the kitchen dismounted. *The column is under the civil authority of the Magistrate of Jinzhou for the entire distance. Military authority extends to the ground, the road, the water and the enemy. It does not extend to the column.* The engineer Fang Qiu explained the setting — the total quantity of Retroact is fixed and thinning, and guardians spaced along a road can stop it dispersing sideways, thin over the fields and thick over the road, the same water either way, put where the people are — and Amy rendered it on the relay as *an umbrella held upside down*, and Hsin said four hundred years of her life's work had just been explained in six words by a nineteen-year-old with a microphone.

Cartethyia told the Grand Marshal her sword was out and that they had won. Li Zhaoying said the sword goes away on her retirement or her death, that the interval since it came out eleven years ago is her watch, and that today the tradition was a forecast. Then she said the rest deliberately, so everyone in earshot would put it in their letters home: the thing eats war; they had taken the war away from it; they had **starved** it — and in about nine months somebody would write this up as a victory and put it in a school hall, and that is precisely how the last one got fed. *So it's hungry.* *Permanently, in my professional opinion. A condition comes back the moment somebody feeds it.*

Jinhsi walked back along the line against the direction of travel and found the fourth relief section at the second marker. Gao Shun's bare right hand was still moving through a half-inch rhythm it had kept for three years with nothing to use it on. He reached for the chopstick and his fingers closed on rain, and tried twice more, and put the hand down. She gave him the correction — his mother had heard every time — and he worked out a piece of arithmetic that had been in a drawer since he was nine: *She used to put the pot back on. Before I did it. She was ahead of me the whole time.* So she walked him to the field kitchen and asked for a bowl, and the sergeant said they don't eat, and she said no, and put the seal case on the corner of his table so the next sentence would have furniture under it. He considered the shape of his afternoon and said that if they were doing one they were doing all of them, because he was not having a column go past his kitchen where some got a bowl and the rest looked at it. They set a flat stone at the roadside, and Jinhsi clicked the chopstick once against the rim, and the steam came up and leaned toward him and stayed leaning while a hundred and ten people walked past behind them and kept walking. By noon there were eleven stones with bowls along the eastern road, and the supply return reads, under *issued against no requisition*: *Grain, one hundred and forty measures. Reason: the column.*

Qiuyuan took his deposition with a foreigner as sworn clerk, who objected to an oath requiring her to affirm she had no interest in the matter; Jinhsi struck the clause and wrote above it, ***Interest declared and recorded.*** Under the tarpaulin, Geshu Lin heard a blind man read him back to himself — a catch on the third beat of his breathing, from two ribs cracked badly at eighteen, heard nine years ago in the dark on the Wuting road at four paces, when Qiuyuan had been sent to kill him under Grandmaster Liang Dongyuan's seal and had refused because what he heard was a man with cracked ribs telling three farmers to go up the bank. Then Iuno, without looking up: *Describe the seal.* Vermilion, square, a nick out of the **lower left** corner with the ink run into it. Qiuyuan's head came up: the Grandmaster's chop carries its flaw in the *upper* left, from a fall, and he had refused to have it recut on the grounds that a flawed seal is harder to forge than a perfect one. A second chop existed, cut from an impression by somebody who put the flaw on the wrong side, and the Agency passed it, because nobody in the Agency looks at the corner of a seal. And then: *The dispatches you received at Norfall. Did any carry that same wedge?* Geshu Lin was quiet long enough that Qiuyuan turned his head. **The sixth.** The page he had had in front of him every hour for three years — and he had never once looked at the corner, because nobody in a command tent does. Jinhsi used the blind-deponent form, written for people who cannot write, for a person who cannot hold, entered the anticipated objection to admissibility in advance as reasonable, and said that on its own it was a piece of paper about a corner, and that beside a chop-cutter's book and a printing house's accounts and nineteen prisoners asked the same question separately by a patient person, it was where the rest starts.

## XXV. TWO SEAS, AND ONE MORE RESIDENT

*(Gate 19, continued)*

Iuno's crystal stump lay bare in the medical cradle with an amber deposit setting along both edges of the fracture. *It has feeling in it. Eleven years of ache in weather is a fact about barometric pressure. This is a fact about a surface.* Asked to describe it: *A hand on a scar.* Baizhi wrote it verbatim, and said she had three columns and none of them took it — a discharge her instruments would accept, an emotional event outside them, and a wound everyone including the patient had entered as concluded, which made it a filing error of eleven years' standing. Then she produced the healing folder: **to the hour, across eleven separate observations, unchanged since the twenty-first.** *A rate that changes once and then holds is a rate that changed for a reason. I have no reason. I have a date.* Nobody said the date aloud, and nobody said what had been filed at the ward office that day. *I keep the page.* *You keep the page.* And somewhere in that same stretch, out of an entire day, Mei chose the eleven minutes in which every single person on that shelf had a job, and put the ring back in Iuno's coat pocket. Nobody saw her do it.

At half past twelve Mei brought Yangyang the sea shell that had come down out of a false sky over Lahai-Roi on the spine of a red kite. *This is yours. It was carried to me. There's a sentence that came with it and the sentence was an address and the address was for a shore I never got to, and the shell was cargo, and I'm the only person on this shelf who's ever been cargo, so I know one when I'm holding it. Somebody's mother wrapped this.* Yangyang put it to her ear and heard a tide, and wind under the tide, and at the very bottom, faint as a lamp four rooms away, a second tide in a different mother's rhythm. Then she went into her kit-roll past forty-one letters bound with a boot-lace and unwrapped a cloth bundle tied the way a person ties a thing they intend to untie once a year. A shell: pale, sea-worn, the same size, the same curve, the same three ridges, the same worn patch where a thumb had been for a long time before either of them was born — a coming-of-age gift from her mother, left with Suisui because Yangyang had already gone to the registry office and her mother was too proud to post it.

Baizhi got down on the duckboards with her lens and found the same wrap-marks, the same four creases, and the same small careful abrasion where somebody with unhurried exact hands had taken the sharp edge off a shell before giving it to a child, the way you dress a wound. Each inner face carried an inscription in a fine old cut. Mei's had blanks in it, taken out by the crossing; Yangyang's had characters in every one of the blanks, and she had never been able to read a single one of them, because her mother had told her it was an old cut and she would get it when she was older, and had died the following winter. Jinhsi could name three and would be guessing at two of those, and said they were the old Mengzhou cut from the hollow art — and the people to ask were the founders on Bookbinders' Row, who have eleven blank compartments where those exact sorts belong and whose fresh-cut replacements crack in the mould. Ciaccona told her not to say the one she thought she had: if it is that word it will still be that word on Thursday, and if it is not, she has put a word in somebody's mother's mouth and there is no getting it back out. Yangyang held one shell to each ear. Hers held one sea. Mei's held two. *…Ah*, she said, very small, and everybody understood that the flat voice had reached the end of what the flat voice can do.

Then Chixia said the thing, because she is the only one whose job description is saying things loudly to people who would rather she did not. Same shell, same mother, same wrapping — **and she has been fixing your elbow.** For a year, every night, same stroke, same fault, same half-inch; and nobody knows a fault like that except the person who has it, and a stranger would want twenty years to find the drift and another five to learn where to push, and she had it on the first night in the dark without being told. And she cut the post: *Someone visited. Everything is still here. The guest left a mark your teacher would have liked.* Three inches deep. One stroke. The measure Yangyang had left home without. Then Chixia turned her face toward the cold patch, got the angle about eight degrees wrong as she always does, and said hello to a woman she had been arguing with for a month.

Baizhi held her thermometer in the cold for ninety seconds and wrote the number. Then she turned back through every entry since the second month with a temperature in one column and a struck-out word and a blank beside it, and wrote the same word into each empty cause column, six times, in her small exact hand. The word was a name, and it belonged to the woman sitting three feet away with a shell in each hand. She closed the book on it and said, in exactly the voice she uses for a splint and for a compliment, that she had been carrying an unexplained four-degree gradient for four months and had now explained it, that it was the most satisfying afternoon of her professional life, and that she would prefer nobody comment on it. *You're crying.* *I am standing in rain.* Yangyang wrapped both shells in the one cloth, put the bundle in the inner pocket over her sternum, and said the only thing that got through: *You could have told me. I know what it costs. I know you have one left.* And she set new terms: **Keep it. I'll do the talking for both of us until we get to Mengzhou and find somebody who can cut you a new one.**

Then they heard him before they saw him, live on an open channel: a small clear voice at considerable volume noting that it had said the ford was too deep, had said it at the ford, had said it *in* the ford. He was the size of a well-fed cat, properly white, with fur the rain ran off, long ears held sideways doing a fair share of the flying, gold jewellery floating a finger's width clear of him and turning on its own schedule — and in the middle of his forehead, small and clean and unmistakable to every Resonator present, a **Tacet Mark**. He landed on the highest crate, folded his ears, and told everybody they might sit down. Everybody was already sitting down. *Excellent start.* He had taken Lupa's boot into a culvert and brought it back with a stone in it, on the grounds that you cannot give somebody an empty boot; Chisa reported flatly that between the ford and the third marker there had been one set of footsteps, and that Lupa had put him down so he could come in on his own; and Lupa looked at the middle distance for four full seconds and said, *It followed me*, and that is the version Jinzhou tells to this day.

He ate for forty minutes, and eleven times he picked up two of something and gave one away to whoever was nearest. Asked why, he said, *Because one is for the person behind* — and stopped, and could not say behind whom. Amy went very still and named **Tao Jingrui**, second gun crew, who carried two canteens because one was for the person behind her who forgot, and whose father had said so on the relay at four that morning. He had no idea who that was. *I know*, Amy said. *Eat your fish.* Ciaccona's theory was that he was the leavings — the war had eaten three years of official remembrance and kept it down, because a commendation that fits on one line is exactly the shape a war is built to hold, and then it had been fed nineteen hundred people's actual habits and could not use a single one of them. Baizhi said the theory was consistent with the frequency and wrong, because he sheds water, casts a shadow with an edge, carries a structure that structures take time to make, and remembers things from before yesterday. *Name one.* *Snow*, he said instantly, and looked as surprised as everybody else; there had been no snow in that valley that year, and she wrote *snow* in her book and underlined it. Cartethyia went quiet for eleven minutes, which for her is a medical event, looking at gold turning off a small white throat and a clean mark on a small white forehead — then glanced up at her own crystal laurel, which floats clear of her head and turns on a schedule of its own, put two fingers against her own forehead, took them away, and said she would say it in Jinzhou, with the door shut, to Ciaccona.

Then he crashed mid-sentence, and Baizhi did the division and took it to Iuno, and neither had to say it: **same curve as the rain. Eight hours.** He woke all at once and asked who did, and Jinhsi told him plainly and at once, because she had spent six years watching what happens to people told these things late. *That is extremely inconvenient. I have plans.* *Tell me one.* *Dinner.* *Then I am going to try very hard to get you to dinner, and I would like you to understand that I mean the whole sentence and that I have no idea yet how to do it.*

Hsin named the one container within four hundred kilometres rated for a resonance of that class, and Mei — who had worked it out at about the second spoon — sat down on the duckboards, which put her at his height, and stated the problem for the record. *This hand has been reaching for things since yesterday morning. It reached for a dragon. It reached for a sword on a stage. It reached for nineteen hundred deaths and it would have taken every single one of them if anybody had let it. So I don't trust the reach. I trust all of you. Somebody tell me the difference between taking him in and putting him in a jar, because from in here they feel identical.* Lupa got there first, against herself: she had carried him four kilometres inside her coat because he was small and going to die and she wanted him not to, which is exactly the reason a person puts a thing in a jar, and she had no business deciding where he slept. And then: *There's a clause* — the one she had bought in a foreign day-book with the only number that belongs to Mei's body. *I wrote it for you. I want it to cover him.* It covers residents. *Then make him one.*

Jinhsi said that was the entire difficulty, because he was a hazard by classification, a person by behaviour and a foreign body by law — and then Chixia sat bolt upright and shouted **THE GOOSE**, winning from a field hospital an argument she had lost four times at the ward office. Column twelve; Assessor Lü, sixth year, entry two hundred and eleven: an animal, resident, no owner of record, holding a door at a government office, with a remarks field. Chisa recited the entry from memory, and Chixia discovered that the woman who cannot see faces had read the file she had been fighting for six years. The terms were drafted and Iuno read every one aloud twice: residence within the Tacet Mark of the co-resident, by the party's own election; he goes in because he goes in, and nobody carries him and nobody reaches; he comes out on request, by him or by the household physician, the head of household, or the gladiator who brought him down off the north cut; review every seventh day, in a room, out loud, with the party present; *if he wants out and the co-resident is the only person who can hear him, that is a jar*, entered at the gladiator's request with her name against it; the co-resident may decline at any point, and declining costs her nothing; and, in the party's own words read back twice at his insistence, *Three meals. Minimum. And I am to be woken for all of them.* At the foot, in one stroke: **consent with a stated limit**, for the ninety-fourth time in two days.

Every proposed name was refused at rising volume until Lupa asked what he wanted and he said *I want it to be mine*, and Mei said, sideways, in the middle of somebody else's sentence: *Why don't you just call yourself Abby?* And Cartethyia observed that he had been saying *absolutely no* rather a lot. The gold stopped turning. *Hmm… Abby… Abra… Abraxas?* Iuno wrote it, boxed it, and asked the room. The Magistrate did not know the word; the bard said it scanned like a name and did not sound like anywhere she had been; the Sentinel of Mengzhou took four seconds and then said, pleasantly, that she had no comment to make at that table — and Iuno wrote in the margin: *Sentinel of Mengzhou declines. Four seconds.* *Abby's a good name*, Mei said. *It fits in a bowl. You can keep the other one for later.*

Then she put her hand down on the duckboard, palm up, mark showing, and made the offer: it is warm and crowded and there are about nine of them in there already and two of them argue, and he can come and he can go, and she was going to sit there and was not going to reach. And she did not reach, for forty seconds, in front of everyone who had watched her stand in a road she had lit and let a dead man walk past without catching him. He came off the crate in a slow spiral, landed on her open palm weighing about as much as a wet coat, still talking, and put his forehead against her hand. The two marks touched, at the size of a lamp being lit in a room, and the gold came up warm along the branching lines and held three seconds at the temperature of a hand on a scar, and settled; and the duckboard was empty and wet with one pebble left on it, carefully, for whoever came next. *He's rummaging*, Mei reported, with an expression that had no precedent in four hundred years. *He has moved something that has been in the same place since Rinascita.* Is it painful? *It's rude.*

## XXVI. THE MEAL, AND THE FOURTH BEAT

They ate at the third hour on the south shelf, in the rain, off the ground: Rangers with printers' apprentices, Xuanfang crews with Mingting infantry translating insults for one another in a spirit of professional courtesy, and nineteen prisoners under escort at the far end getting the same food, which Jiyan had put in the order in writing at the top. Forty steamed fish had come up the eastern road on a cart from Ward Two with a note tied to the handle: the ward had heard the name Li Guorong on the relay and heard what he did about fried fish, and this was what the ward had to send, and it was to be steamed, and anybody who fried it would be answered to. *Eat the fish*, Lupa said.

Abby came out at four minutes past, mid-sentence, which was the first test of his second clause and passed on its own. Later he drifted down beside Sergeant Du, looked at the neat pile of pepper Du had picked out of his own bowl, asked nothing, and added two pieces to it. After a minute: *Who is it for?* *Bloke called Rui.* *Is he coming?* *No.* *…Then he gets the pile*, said Abby, as though settling a point of law. Du had been carrying two halves of a rice bowl tied with a bootlace for a month — Deng Rui's bowl — and Chisa, who had been navigating by their click against his canteen since the walk north, had mended the fracture that morning with the half of her blade that sews and handed it back whole with a bite out of the rim, because the missing crescent had gone into the mud at a highland camp four hundred kilometres away. He had said he could not work out what you do with it. *You were keeping it in the shape it was in when he stopped. That is a good reason and you can stop now.* By the following week the pepper pile had reached the highland camp and become a thing the second section does without explaining, and new arrivals who ask are told it is for Rui.

Amy filed the post-action on the callsigns: both return calls worked, and Iuno's went first when the boards came down — one syllable off the ground, out of a woman with three minutes of enforced silence in her and a voice like a rasp, and it crossed the whole stage and landed. *She turned her head on the first syllable. She has never once done that for a title.* Amy was sending the doctrine to the war college, who would hate it, because the whole doctrine is *have two people who are allowed to insult you*; and Jiyan said to send it to him first so he could put his name under hers and they would hate it in a more useful direction.

Ciaccona had the blank Fractsidus card under a mug, its three grey marks stopped since the ninth hour, and drew one thing on it in two strokes: a whole-bar rest with the value written underneath, *because if you leave a rest without a value some idiot fills it in.* It was going back to him one day, and he would know exactly how much she meant by it, because he is the only person alive who reads that fast. Chisa put two fingers on the crate and reported that the north string had slackened — the one off the third seat at Panhua's that ran past the eighth tower, taut for a month, held at both ends, tighter since the night the Magistrate put a dish down at an empty place. *At the eighth beat of the bell it went slack. It goes as far as a road with people on it, and there is nothing pulling on the other end of it any more.* And the other string, the one that goes up and has been in the house since the fifteenth and drinks the tea, was sitting on the duckboards eleven inches from a woman with a red hat, and she had known where it was every minute since the twenty-seventh and had not said so, because it was not hers to say.

Cartethyia put a three-inch paper person beside Amy's bowl — the fifth one, handed over six weeks ago with no identification and kept on a windowsill ever since. *It's true now. It's me.* The paper people are for people who might disappear; she had made her own the night the household voted her a room, because twenty years of naming things had never once made her sure the rule works on the person doing the naming, and had given it to Amy to hold because she has never been able to hold her own. And it was true now because *I said my name on his stage. On my knees, in the mud, with two directions fighting over me and a prayer in my mouth from a seminary that owned twenty years of me — and I said Cartethyia, out loud, at a god's theatre, and it was the smaller name and it was mine and it held.* Elsewhere, Chixia's red cap ended up on a cradle rail with a small white weight asleep inside it and the air four degrees under on the other side of the same rail, and nobody joined those facts up out loud. Hsin gave her invitation — no delegation, but the household and the plant and the girl with the microphone and the wolf who throws food at people to stop them making speeches — and said the reason would arrive before the eighth month, that she would not say what it was, that Mei would find that infuriating, and that Mei should notice she had not lied to her once.

Geshu Lin ate nothing and sat at the table anyway, and for about forty minutes he and Jiyan said almost nothing, which several people described as the loudest part of the meal. Then Jiyan held the spear out across, the way you hand somebody a tool. *I can't take it.* *I know.* *Then why —* *Because I have been carrying it for three years and I have never once offered it. I told myself that was custody. Offering it would have meant admitting there was somebody standing here to offer it to. You have refused it. That is the transaction, and it is done, and now the spear is mine on your terms.* *You filed the counterweight.* *Twice. It was pulling right.* *…It always pulled right.* *It pulls straight now.* *Show me.* So a general three years dead spent eleven minutes of his last six hours watching a man demonstrate the balance of a spear, interrupting him, being wrong about the grip once, and being told so.

Mei turned her bowl a quarter-turn by the rim and settled it, and four crates away Jinhsi did the identical thing to a mug within the same minute, and Iuno watched both and filed it for the second time in a month. Asked whether the ending would hurt them, Baizhi said no: *I have listened to two thousand of them die tonight through a relay. That was pain. What is coming is a rain stopping.* Mei drank her tea, which was Earl Grey and hot, because Iuno keeps her treaties. And then she said the smallest sentence in her vocabulary and the one she means most: **"Same time tomorrow?"** The steam off eleven bowls leaned, all at once, all the same way. And the air beside Yangyang's cheek spent the last word it had. ***Tomorrow.*** Yangyang closed her eyes, opened them, and did what she does, which is report, in the flat voice, at the correct volume, so a table of eleven and a shelf of an army could hear it: *She says tomorrow.* Nobody made a speech. Lupa's tail took a mug off the crate. Cartethyia burst into tears and went on eating. Chisa said *Good* in exactly the voice she uses for a splint and a compliment. Amy put her forehead on her mother's shoulder for four seconds and then asked whether anybody was going to eat the last fish, and Baizhi, at the correct pause, said: *It is a very good fish.* And the wind sat down.

## XXVII. THE COLUMN COMES HOME

The last of the column passed the third marker at four minutes past the fourth hour — a signalman walking eleven feet out of line because a play had misplaced his road for three years and his feet were still arguing about it, with a Ranger walking eleven feet out of line beside him the whole way without mentioning it. Ciaccona let him get four paces past and played the fourth beat: one note, struck once, on a bad instrument by a bad hand, closing a bar open since the second hour. *There. That's what it's for.*

The convoy went south in the shape the Grand Marshal had written, and the rain stayed thick over the people and went thin over the fields, so that a farmer at Lianshui looked out at a band of silver rain forty metres wide running dead straight across the valley with dry ground on both sides and small figures moving south inside it, and put his hat on and went and got his neighbours. Word of it produced, within a week, a formal enquiry, a doctrinal dispute, three competing accounts, and eventually a new line in the weather register with its own column, which Yangyang would come to hate.

Changli held the north gate in the Acting Magistrate's plain robe, having read the stores figure aloud at every second hour while eleven ward tables went on sitting. Clerk Wen had his heading and no rule, and three hours' worth of the objection he would raise if somebody else tried it. *Then read me the fifth article of the returns rule.* It said the register records who came back. He read it twice, sat down, and began entering names off the second forme with the personal detail in the remarks column, because that column had been sitting there four hundred years waiting for somebody to use it properly. He got to two hundred and eleven before the head of the column came round the last bend, and then stood up and did the rest of it standing.

They came in at the seventh hour under a band of silver rain forty metres wide. Nobody cheered — Jinzhou had decided that at about four in the morning on the wall, without a meeting, in the way that city decides things — and what a hundred and forty thousand people did instead was stand along the road with the lamps lit behind them and let a column of the dead walk past at the pace of the slowest. Old Seven was in the recovery yard with forty-one cups of tea, the forty-first on an empty support block under a label reading **WU'S. LEAVE ROOM**, and the boy was there with his model bridge and a second sign he had made because he had run out of ideas and correctly concluded the first idea was still good: **FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK.** The automaton registered forty-one cups and one thousand nine hundred and seven arrivals and executed the only instruction that had ever mattered in its programming, which is that when people come in from a road you put the water on. It took eleven minutes to work out that nobody would lift a cup, and eleven more to develop, out of twenty-two years of a dead cook's opinions, an entirely novel behaviour: it walked the yard with a full tray, set a cup in front of each of the first forty, and left the steam to do the rest. A service log carries a machine-generated line at 19:44: *tea taken (indirect); repeat interval unchanged.*

Panhua had the oil on and a full house, and at some point took the third seat's bowl down off the shelf where it had been living beside a stitched cloth bird for six weeks, put it back on the corner table, put food in it, and told nobody. At the tax office the goose held the door from the fourth hour to well past dark and claimed a quilt as reparations; Chixia heard about it on the relay four hundred kilometres away and said quietly to the ceiling: *Cao Yi. Second section. Got the goose question right.* At the east gate Shen left the tin shut, because it was the twenty-eighth and the boards go out on the sixth, and sat with her hand flat on the lid for two hours while the column went past. On Bookbinders' Row the morning warning had printed on four thousand sheets with a sort that had lain face down for thirty-one years, read by a hundred and forty thousand people for the information it contained about flood levels on the eastern road, and nobody has yet gone back to look at what the character was.

The rain thinned at the rate Baizhi had guessed, and the difference between the guess and the event was eleven minutes over nine hours. What happened at the end was quiet, took about forty minutes, and was painless, and she stood at the gate with a slide in her hand from the moment it began until it was over and looked at the people the entire time. Sergeant Du reported that the last thing he saw was a man in the fourth relief section standing over one of the eleven bowls with his hands open, watching the steam lean, at the exact moment the steam stopped leaning. Jiyan reported that a general had put a hand on his shoulder at some point, that the count under his thumb had stayed at zero the whole way, and that he intended to go on counting for a while yet. Clerk Wen finished entering one thousand nine hundred and seven names, ruled the page off, and wrote: *All present at the gate. All entered. The column is closed. The register remains open.* Then he opened the census and copied out column twelve: *Abby. Resident. No owner of record. Ward four accepts the charge.* Assessor Lü came in at half past eleven at night in his own coat, read all five clauses standing up, asked for a copy for his drawer, and said Chixia had argued the point four times at that office and had never once cited the goose — and to tell her so slowly, because he would like her to sit with it.

Li Zhaoying stayed outside the wall, set her line across the mouth of the gorge facing north with rotations written for twenty days, and wrote to Mingting: *The Threnodian designated Ovathrax is not destroyed. It has been denied its material. I decline to enter it as a victory. I direct that it shall not be entered as a victory in any subordinate account, and I will personally amend any account that does. A war god eats war. It has been starved. Starvation is a condition; conditions return when they are fed. What fed this one was three years of public arithmetic, correctly performed. I signed the arithmetic myself. Recommend: the outer shelf held for twenty years. Recommend: no school play.* Then she told her driver to leave the sword out of its cradle, because the interval is her watch and the watch is not over, and if Mingting asks why the Grand Marshal's sword is out on a night when the enemy has withdrawn, it is the same reason the lamp-keepers go out in fine weather. Yangyang, meanwhile, turned to the back of her patrol log, found the three boxed words she had written on the sixth of the sixth month and shut the book on before anybody could see, and struck them through with one clean line, the way you cut a post. ~~*Not yet.*~~ Under it, boxed: ***Mengzhou.*** And beside it, in the flat hand of a woman filing a count she has kept privately since she was nineteen: ***Out, one. Back, two.*** At the ninth tower the keeper wrote nothing under the Magistrate's *wait*, and the margin held; but she took a new seal out of the box, looked at it, put it back unbroken, and left the rope free: *Rope left off the cleat. Reason: they may come again.*

## XXVIII. TUESDAY, AND WHERE IT STANDS

The household went home on the twenty-ninth by the lower cradle road at three kilometres an hour, which is the speed at which the world has detail in it. Abby flew two kilometres, gave a commentary on the road surface for one more, and rode the remaining eight inside a wolf's coat, which was categorically denied at the time, is categorically denied now, and is attested by four witnesses and one physician's log.

Iuno's hand went into her coat pocket twenty-nine hours later, which is a long delay for a woman who empties her coat out of habit. It was a Tuesday, and raining, and the window in the front room still would not latch, and the wind was in the half-inch gap the way it had been all month, reading over somebody's shoulder, rudely. Her fingers found the four-character paper first — good stock, an empty seal space, a chop pressed into the fibre at the mill by somebody who had that sheet made before they wrote on it — and then the ring. She sat down on the edge of the bed to look at them, which was the mistake, because it obliged her to do the arithmetic she had been declining to do since half past nine that morning: that the ring had left her coat on a stage because a Sphere had taken her voice and throwing it was the only sentence she had left; that Mei had carried it nine hours through a war and a dispersal and a deposition and a meal and a road, in a fist with a hole in the palm; and had put it back, without saying anything, without waiting to be seen doing it.

Mei hit her from the side — too fast, at the pace of a woman who crossed a room in three steps and did not plan the last two, arms round from behind and about a quarter tighter than a person needs. It was the bill for a whole day of standing still: four metres of mud not crossed, a road lit and a dead man let past, a hand kept flat on a wet board for forty seconds while something small made up its own mind. Iuno got the ring closed in her palm on the way down, noted the rib and the four stitches and the physician's card that says *do not grip*, put her living hand over the arm across her chest, and let it be that for a while. Then she raised a scheduling concern instead, at length: a daughter through one board and a coat of size, a fourth step that announces anybody who uses it, a wolf on the stairhead who can hear a cork come out of a bottle from the market, and a window with a gap in it, and an aunt in the gap, who reads her correspondence and turned a page back on Sunday. The curtain moved once in a way that had nothing to do with the dam wind, and then settled with what could reasonably be described as dignity. *So. Now we have a daughter, and a talking cat in our bed.* *He's not in the bed.* *He is in you, and you are in the bed. I have done the syllogism twice.* Mei said her body was soundproof and offered to test it that night, and Iuno laughed the ugly one, the one that comes up out of a person who has held a shelf full of people together on a raw throat since three in the morning; and through the wall a girl of nineteen heard her mother laugh, put her headset down, and lay grinning at the ceiling. Iuno put the ring back in the coat pocket herself, later, with the paper on top of it, and hung the coat on the hook by the door where it lives. It was Tuesday. There would be another one.

---

At the start of this stretch, seven foreigners on a ninety-day stamp stood under a sea that had assembled itself over a city, with the drum past the north face at six minutes and closing, three hundred and twelve people without a floor, a Magistrate who had answered every wish ever brought to her hall and had none for this one, and a general three years dead marching an army back toward the valley that killed it inside a play his own city had written to make the dying legible. Mei knew her body remembered doors her mind could not reach, and did not know whether the woman who had made those memories was somebody she wanted to be.

By the end, the number of people in Jinzhou without a roof is zero, reached by four thousand one hundred and thirteen individual entries with their debts and temporary terms written underneath, and the register stays open for births and arrivals and changed terms. The casualty finding of the third year is vacated, on the ground, in the weather, in a magistrate's own small square hand, with two registers appended and an instruction that they are not to be summarised. One thousand nine hundred and seven people walked twelve kilometres home under a band of silver rain held over them by four foreign machines, and were entered by name with a personal detail and a true road by a clerk who stood up to finish. A bell silent since the dark has rung eight beats and its rope is off the cleat, because they may come again.

**Mei** has seen what she was — gold, at the centre of a finished sky — and declined it twice out loud, keeping the part that was hers: she stands for them, and she does not stand alone. She read a register under arrows, lit a road with her mark, let nineteen hundred people walk past without catching one of them, and stayed across four metres of mud while the two people she loves most were cut, because Iuno looked at her with faith. Her healing is still halved from a date nobody says aloud; something small and loud now lives inside her Tacet Mark on written terms, with a clause naming the difference between a household and a jar; and she put a ring back in a coat pocket without being seen. **Iuno** was placed in the wings by a grammar built out of her own thirty years and got out by throwing away the most expensive object she owns and saying a word about a weekday; told the Wind *I kept her west* with no argument left to make for herself; bled amber from a wound she had entered as concluded; and counted a column of the dead home, flat, no run-up, no face. Her coat still holds the ring and a folded paper whose seal space wants a chop she has not chosen.

**Amy** ran a war off a relay the way she once ran a city, refused every role until the playwright found the one that was true, and got out of that by giving her mother's counting a second voice. **Lupa** spent her hard-bought clause on somebody who was not family yet, naming her own reason for wanting him as the same reason a person puts a thing in a jar. **Ciaccona** held a rest open for hours, watched her hands play somebody else's music, and answered a blank card with two pencil strokes and a value. **Cartethyia** said the smaller name at a god's theatre and it held; her fifth paper person is on Amy's windowsill as true, and she has one thing left to say in Jinzhou with the door shut. **Chisa** found the seam between grief and the griever, mended a bowl kept in the shape it was in when its owner stopped, and heard the north string go slack. **Yangyang** applied a year of silent corrections to the hand that gave them, carries two shells in one cloth with eleven unreadable characters, and does the talking for two.

**Jinhsi** let a city answer in its own units, gave the civil seal to the woman who had forged a date for seven years, kept the personal account in its envelope with a date on it, read a restored direction to a dead man, took a deposition from a witness who would be gone by evening, clicked a chopstick against a bowl at a roadside, and wrote the correction standing in the rain. **Changli** held a gate and a city for a night and a day and has one room left to sit in. **Jiyan** returned a spear by offering it, got the word *medic* back after three years, and is still counting a pulse that has been zero the whole time. **Hsin** locked a playwright inside the room he built, ejected his cast, and traded him back for a price nobody heard and something she will name in Mengzhou. **Li Zhaoying** belayed her guns rather than become the military ending, forbade the word *victory* in any subordinate account, and keeps her sword out of its cradle. **Qiuyuan** refused the clean revenge with a blade in his ribs, testified in company, and left with a corner of a seal, a wedge in the wrong place, and a chop-cutter to find. **Geshu Lin** looked back, and his company was there, and he walked south last with a medic beside him.

**Ovathrax** is not destroyed. It has been starved, its interval has opened past what any listener can take, and it has stopped asking, which in that frequency means a thing has run out of surfaces to press against; and a Grand Marshal has written down that it will find one. **Cristoforo** walked north over the ridge toward the only road on the far side, which runs west and then north, and at the end of it there is a city that prints things. **Nivora** turned back once at the lip and was already being looked at, and neither of them raised a hand. **Scar** went through a red aperture with two wounds and no register, still promising that Mei will come when she knows what she is.

On Bookbinders' Row, eleven compartments are still empty in eleven houses, every fresh-cut sort still cracks in the mould, and a faced matrix sits in the founders' keeping marked *do not distribute*. A quad of Mengzhou lead, cast blank so it will hold a space open and leave the paper alone, is in a coat pocket on a lake road, and a fox has asked for it by name and asked that it not be cast. Two shells lie in one cloth over a young woman's sternum with eleven characters in an old Mengzhou cut that nobody who has held them has been able to say. Nobody has yet set those three things beside one another.

The plum rains have the rest of the season to run. The weather over Norfall dispersed across eleven days; the tremors past the north face resumed at eleven days, then twelve, then fourteen. The wind is in the half-inch gap of a window that will not latch, entirely out of words, with an appointment in Mengzhou before the eighth month. The ninth tower's rope is off its cleat. And in a rented front room, on a Tuesday, in the rain, a ring is back in a coat pocket with a folded paper on top of it, hanging on the hook by the door where the coat lives, waiting for an ordinary evening.
