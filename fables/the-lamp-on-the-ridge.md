# THE LAMP ON THE RIDGE

## 燈下客

*(the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth, the eastern road, last approach)*

---

The eastern road runs straight for the last two kilometres before Jinzhou's outer shelf, and the full stretch is visible from the irrigation ridge east of the canal. This is the kind of fact a person learns about a place before they sit down on it.

Lupa saw the lamp at the fifth hour.

It sat against the failing sky like a pulse with no body: warm, amber, steady, and on a ridge where the evening had already taken everything else to grey. She had been walking the convoy's outer edge since the third marker with her spear across her shoulders and her ears set forward, and the inventory of things on that ridge had been empty all afternoon. Then it had a lamp in it.

Her tail went still.

Chisa, eleven paces back and finishing a bowl, said:

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Since the second bridge. He walked to the spot at a pace that suggests he chose it in advance. There is a small animal with him that has been asleep for most of the last hour."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"How long."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Two hours. Possibly three. There was a moment at the second marker where I thought I heard him arrive, and then I corrected myself, and then I corrected the correction, and now I am eating."</font>

Lupa took the spear off her shoulders.

<font color="#ff9742">"Two hours."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"I have already agreed that you are going alone. I agreed with it before you said it. This is faster."</font>

Lupa went up the irrigation ridge on the east side, where the drainage channels cut shallow steps into the slope, and she went at a pace that had already passed through threat and arrived at curiosity, because three hours on a ridge with a lamp and a sleeping cat is the behaviour of a man who has made an appointment with a view and intends to keep it.

---

He was sitting cross-legged on the flat stone at the top of the ridge with his coat open and a lamp in his lap. The lamp was brass, old, green at the vents, with a crack running diagonally across the upper pane that someone had mended with a fine wire join. It held a flame the colour of water in shadow — barely there, already there, the way a lamp looks when it is simply present and listening.

A grey cat sat at his left knee, washing a paw with the methodical patience of a creature that had been watching a man stare at a road for two hours and had used the time to develop a position on the subject.

Lupa stopped at twelve paces. She put the spear butt in the ground.

The man looked up.

Mid-twenties. A jaw that had settled into a permanent state of quiet amusement with the general direction of the afternoon. Hair tied back with a string that was managing about half its job and losing ground on the rest. His right eye caught the last of the sky and held it at an angle that was a fraction too sharp, the way a lens catches light rather than receiving it, and around his left eye, close against the lid, a faint ring of discolouration that Lupa clocked as a resonance trace.

One eyebrow went up. The other stayed where it was. A faint smirk followed, roguish, the expression of a man who has been found and is choosing to enjoy it.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Evening."</font>

His voice had Mengzhou in it: that particular cadence where every sentence gets composed at a writing desk and delivered at a noodle stand.

<font color="#ff9742">"You're sitting on a military convoy's approach, in eyeshot of four guardians and a gun platform."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"I counted four."</font> He said it conversationally, the way a man at a teahouse remarks on the quality of the water. <font color="#8af2b2">"The one on the left flank is running her projector about six per cent above the others' setting. She's either compensating for something in the ground on that side, or somebody missed the calibration, and given that those machines are walking in formation I'd guess she's doing it on purpose, which is the more interesting answer."</font>

Lupa's ears came forward.

<font color="#ff9742">"That's Wu Mei. She grounds through her right heel."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Ah."</font> He tucked the information away with a slight tilt of the head, the way a person pockets a receipt. <font color="#8af2b2">"Then she's very good."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"What are you doing up here."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Jingran. Nethermancer, when the title fits. Writer, when the deadlines catch up."</font> He set the lamp down on the stone beside him, carefully, the way someone handles a thing that is both an instrument and a family member. The flame in it brightened for a moment when his hands left it and settled back. <font color="#8af2b2">"Currently both of those are somewhere behind me on a hill road, and I'm sitting on a ridge watching the most extraordinary thing I have seen in my professional life, so they can wait."</font>

The cat at his knee stopped washing its paw and looked at Lupa. One ear came forward. The other stayed where it was, which in a cat is a complete assessment rendered without paperwork.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Yuebing,"</font> Jingran said, indicating the cat with two fingers. <font color="#8af2b2">"My Echo, my dining companion, and the reason I have never once finished a manuscript on schedule. He ate my third chapter this morning and threw it up in my travelling pack, which is the single most effective piece of editorial feedback I have ever received."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"From Mengzhou."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"From Mengzhou. Seven days on the hill road, three of them in rain."</font> He pulled his coat closed against the evening, a gesture that arrived late, as though the chill had been queuing and he had only just got round to it. <font color="#8af2b2">"Your Sentinel's name came through our commerce relay four days ago. The printing houses decoded it before the government clerks — happens about once a year, produces strong feelings on both sides. By the second morning there were eleven apprentices in the foundry yard arguing about what she'd bought, and this thing in my lap had been pulling south at a frequency I have never heard from it in my life."</font>

He touched the lamp on the cracked pane.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Three generations in the family. It has an opinion about every dead person within four hundred kilometres. Seven days ago it went quiet for the first time since I inherited it, and then it started again on a note I had to look up in my master's index, and the note pointed south."</font> He gestured at the road below, where the silver band of rain was coming south at the pace of people who had been standing for three years. <font color="#8af2b2">"So I came south."</font>

---

The column was about a kilometre and a half from the outer shelf. The guardians held their projectors at the setting Fang Qiu had given the Grand Marshal's engineers, pulling the thinning rain into a corridor just wide enough for the walkers, and the light through it had the quality of a window being closed by degrees — luminous, and narrowing.

Lupa looked at the road. Then at the lamp. Then at the right eye, where the Tacet Mark sat in the iris like a hair-thin seam of white-gold.

<font color="#ff9742">"What do you see."</font>

Jingran went quiet. It was a specific quality of quiet — the silence of somebody whose instruments are running. Lupa had spent enough time with Chisa and Baizhi to recognise it: a person reading the room through a sense that shared a language with nobody in it.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Through the eye, or through the lamp?"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Both."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Through the lamp,"</font> he said, and his hand went to the cracked pane again, tracing something across it that Lupa could not follow, <font color="#8af2b2">"nineteen hundred and seven individual frequencies. Each one is a condensation of the habits a person kept while alive. The breath catch. The hand gesture. The way somebody holds a bowl or picks the pepper out of their soup."</font> His fingers stopped. <font color="#8af2b2">"Your Threnodian ate three years of official remembrance and kept all of it down, because a commendation that fits on a line is exactly the shape of thing a war is built to hold. And then somebody fed it the actual people — the private inventory, the habits, the parts that fall through any filing — and it choked. What walked out of that valley this morning was what the war spat back up."</font>

He looked down at the road.

<font color="#8af2b2">"The part that falls between the lines."</font>

Lupa's tail moved once, low and slow.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Through the eye is a different instrument."</font> His right eye shifted register. The white-gold tracery in the iris brightened, and the pupil went to a depth that had no comfortable analogy. <font color="#8af2b2">"Through the eye I see the density. About a fifth of what it was this morning. They'll make the gate; whoever took the samples had it right, and the decay curve has been clean all afternoon."</font> A pause. <font color="#8af2b2">"Except for the last two readings."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"What happened in the last two."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"The curve flattened."</font> He said it with the specific intonation of a man delivering a finding he has checked four times and still finds personally inconvenient. <font color="#8af2b2">"The rate was constant from Norfall to the second bridge. Clean exponential, textbook decay, a beautiful measurement. And then, about three kilometres out from the gate, the rate started to slow. The density is still falling. But it is falling more slowly the closer they get to the wall."</font>

Lupa waited.

<font color="#8af2b2">"The seam under the road runs all the way into the city. I can feel it through the ridge, and at this end — at the home end — the frequency coming up through it is thicker. Denser. As though the ground remembers that this is where people come back to, and it is holding the rain a little longer because of that."</font> He picked up the lamp and turned it over once in both hands, the way a man turns an instrument he has lived with long enough that the motion has become a form of thought. <font color="#8af2b2">"I have absolutely no idea if that is physics or sentiment, and I am going to be very annoyed if it turns out to be the second one."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"The physician will want that."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"The physician?"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Baizhi. She took the samples. She'll want the flattening."</font>

Jingran filed the name — behind the eyes, in the place where things come back later with ink on them.

<font color="#8af2b2">"I would very much like to meet a woman who takes a rate off two samples in a valley and gets a curve that clean."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"She apologised for the sample size."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Of *course* she did."</font> One brow went up again. <font color="#8af2b2">"Professional modesty over a result that good. I already like her."</font>

---

The cat stretched, long and boneless, and climbed Jingran's coat in three efficient movements to settle on his left shoulder, where it arranged itself into a shape that suggested long practice and firm opinions about the allocation of space. The faint ring of resonance around Jingran's left eye pulsed once, softly, in the rhythm of a sleeping thing.

Lupa studied the arrangement for a moment. The cat rode his shoulder the way a scout rides a lookout — ownership going both ways.

<font color="#ff9742">"You came seven days on a hill road to watch."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"I came seven days on a hill road because my master spent forty years crawling into Sonoro Spheres with this lamp, pulling out the things people left behind. A broken stele. A voice in a wall. The way a woman folded cloth."</font> He tapped the cracked pane. <font color="#8af2b2">"He said a Nethermancer's job is to carry the part that got dropped. The part that was never in any record, because the records are busy with something else."</font>

He looked down at the road, where the last sections were coming south under a rain that held its silver because four guardians were telling it to, and the light through it fell on faces that were finishing.

<font color="#8af2b2">"This is nineteen hundred and seven people walking home with their habits in their hands, and by tonight those habits are going into the ground, and every official who writes about it is going to write about the column and the gate and the register with the names in it. Good records. Important records. And they will be the whole of the filing."</font> He turned the lamp so the pane faced the road. <font color="#8af2b2">"I do the rest."</font>

He raised his right hand, palm out, toward the road, as though framing a distance.

<font color="#8af2b2">"There is a man in the fourth section who is walking with his hands open and his fingers going, about half an inch, in a rhythm. I've been watching him for twenty minutes. Whatever that motion was, it mattered enough that it survived three years inside a god's production and came out the other side. And it is going to go into the seam tonight, and the records will carry his name and his rank and the date, and the rhythm in his fingers will go with the rain."</font>

He let his hand come down.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Unless somebody writes it down."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Chopstick."</font>

Jingran's head came round.

<font color="#ff9742">"The movement. He clicks a chopstick against a bowl when he wants more soup. His mother heard it every time."</font> Lupa's ears went back, forward, settled. <font color="#ff9742">"The Magistrate carried a chopstick four hours up a mountain road and gave it back to him at the second marker. She clicked it against a bowl and a kitchen sergeant put four big pots on because he said he wasn't having a column go past where some of them got a bowl and the rest looked at it."</font>

She shifted her weight onto the spear.

<font color="#ff9742">"Somebody already wrote it down."</font>

Jingran was quiet for several seconds.

The single brow came up. The smirk that usually followed it stayed in its holster. What settled over his face was a stillness, and behind it the expression of a man who has been studying a puzzle for seven days and has just been told that somebody solved it while he was on the road.

<font color="#8af2b2">"…Who."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"The Magistrate."</font>

He sat with that. The lamp in his hands brightened for a moment, as though it too had been told something.

<font color="#8af2b2">"The Magistrate carried a chopstick into a valley so she could give it back to a man whose fingers still knew the motion."</font> He said it slowly, turning each word the way he turned the lamp. <font color="#8af2b2">"And somebody put pots on."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Eleven of them. Along the road. With a works detail."</font>

Jingran looked down the last stretch of road toward the wall, where Changli stood under the arch and a clerk had a register open on a folding desk, and the column came south in the silver rain like a single long sentence arriving at its period.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Then this is a very different kind of place than I expected."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"It's Jinzhou."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"It's Jinzhou,"</font> he repeated, and the way he said it had the particular weight of someone updating a map they have carried for a long time. The cat on his shoulder made a sound. <font color="#8af2b2">"And they already have the bowl."</font>

The lamp had been doing something else since she arrived. Jingran had chosen to leave it alone.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Your fire has passengers."</font>

The teahouse cadence folded itself away. What was left was the instrument — the right eye reading, the lamp confirming, a Nethermancer looking at something his profession had taught him to recognise and his manners had taught him to leave alone until the conversation could hold it.

Lupa's tail stopped.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Two frequencies. Both human, both dead, both seated in your Forte."</font> He traced the cracked pane with a thumbnail. <font color="#8af2b2">"The older one has bonded to your own vibration — she's been in the fire so long I'd have to look twice to separate you. The second came later. He rides the combustion the way a soldier rides a transport. Upright. Conscious of the door."</font>

Her ears pinned. Came forward. Settled.

<font color="#ff9742">"Mya. And Avidius."</font>

He held the first name like he held the lamp. The flame brightened once, turned a colour that had no business in a lamp — something between viburnum and old copper — and held.

<font color="#ff9742">"She was a gladiator from Ragunna. Twenty years inside a machine built on her bones. What came out the other end asked me for the crown of glory, and I gave it to her clean, through the heart, and she stepped into it herself."</font> Lupa's hand rested on the spear shaft — resting, the way load-bearing walls rest. <font color="#ff9742">"The payment she asked for was witness."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Avidius was a senator. They wrote his legend for him and put a dead king in his body. He held it — all the corruption, every spawn on the field — and asked for an ending the playwright hadn't written."</font> She shifted her weight onto the spear. <font color="#ff9742">"He said witness me. I threw."</font>

The lamp hummed below hearing. The flame went through three colours in quick succession. The third one Jingran could name, and chose to hold privately.

<font color="#8af2b2">"In Mengzhou,"</font> he said, and his voice had the weight of someone picking up a heavy thing with both hands, <font color="#8af2b2">"the protocol for corrupted comrades is mercy, administered and sealed. The file closes. The Nethermancer walks to the next site."</font> He turned the lamp over. <font color="#8af2b2">"My master did it nine times. Nine entries, given to the archivist, filed in green drawers in a room with controlled humidity. On each anniversary he would sit in that room for one hour. Then he would leave, and the drawers stayed closed."</font>

He looked at the place on her spear where the fire would come.

<font color="#8af2b2">"You opened the drawer."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"I burned it."</font> Her chin came up. <font color="#ff9742">"I put them in my fire. I gave them my arms and my forward motion, and when the flame comes off my hands one of the throats in it is hers, and I did that on purpose, because ghosts get carried or they get dropped, and I have seen what this world does with the dropped ones."</font>

Yuebing climbed down from Jingran's shoulder, walked three paces toward Lupa, and sat in the gap between them. He washed a paw. In the mathematics of cats this is a proprietary claim on the intervening space, filed without witnesses and without appeal.

<font color="#8af2b2">"My master spent forty years telling the Academy that the dead are a conversation. The Academy called his position sentimental and filed his papers in a drawer of their own."</font> He tapped the cracked pane. <font color="#8af2b2">"He should have met a woman who gave the conversation a spear and brought it to a fight."</font>

Lupa's tail did one sweep. Slow. The kind of verdict that does not go through chambers.

<font color="#ff9742">"They're company. They hunt with me. Especially the dead."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"A drawer preserves. A fire transforms."</font> He said it with the care of a man filing a revision to a document he had carried his entire professional life. <font color="#8af2b2">"My master's nine entries are in the archive. Their frequencies are intact. Their habits are catalogued. And they are exactly as dead as the day he sealed the file."</font>

He looked at the two frequencies in his lamp — one bonded, one seated, both warm — and then at the woman who had put them there.

<font color="#8af2b2">"Yours are fighting."</font>

Yuebing, having rendered his verdict on the space between them, walked back to Jingran and climbed his coat to the shoulder in three efficient movements. Both ears forward. The deposition was filed.

---

He stood. He was taller than the sitting posture had suggested, and he moved the way his cat did — from still to full height in one motion, with nothing wasted between. The lamp came up with him, held low at his side, and the flame went the colour of old paper in good light.

<font color="#8af2b2">"I'll stay up here until they're through the gate. The last section has about fourteen people who are going to reach the arch with less than an hour of rain on them, and I want to see what the seam does with the frequencies when they let go. It may tell me something useful. It may tell me something useless in a very interesting way."</font> He tucked a strand of escaped hair behind one ear with the hand that was not holding the lamp. <font color="#8af2b2">"Both of those go in the book."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Your name again."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Jingran. Of Mengzhou. 'Cat of the Nether Lamp,' if you read the serials, which I hope you do, because my editor has been sending me increasingly threatening correspondence about readership figures and I could use the numbers."</font>

Lupa pulled her spear out of the ground.

<font color="#ff9742">"Lupa."</font>

She said it flat, the way she says everything that matters.

Jingran filed it. Same place, same motion: behind the eyes, in the drawer where names go before they come back in ink.

<font color="#8af2b2">"You came up a ridge by yourself to check on a lamp with a cat."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Yesterday it was a boot in a culvert. I've had worse."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Ah."</font> The smirk arrived, properly this time, and it carried the warmth of a man who had been sitting on a ridge for three hours watching something he loved. <font color="#8af2b2">"I was on the ridge above that culvert, actually, earlier this morning. I saw her go up to check it. I stayed out of the way. She had a spear and an expression, and I made a professional decision about which of those to respect."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Smart."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Alive,"</font> Jingran corrected. <font color="#8af2b2">"I would like to remain so. My sister has a great deal to say about it and she is very good at saying things."</font>

Lupa looked at him for a long moment. Her tail did one sweep, low, involuntary, which she would contest.

<font color="#ff9742">"Tell the physician yourself."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"Hm?"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"The flattening. The readings. Everything you just told me about the seam getting thicker near the wall."</font> She turned, already two steps down the ridge. <font color="#ff9742">"She's the one with the slide. She'll want a data set from a man who's been sitting on a ridge for three hours with an instrument older than her grandparents. And bring the lamp."</font>

<font color="#8af2b2">"I always bring the lamp."</font>

She was already gone. Jingran watched her go down the drainage steps at a pace that meant she had decided something, and the cat on his shoulder watched too, with both ears forward, which in a cat is a form of quiet approval of the general proceedings.

He sat back down on the flat stone and turned the lamp toward the road.

The last sections of the column were passing through the outer shelf. The light through the thinning rain caught on open hands, on the faces of people who were almost somewhere, on the particular way a person walks when the road has a door at the end of it. Changli stood under the arch. The clerk wrote names in a register that had a heading and no rule and a remarks column that had been sitting there for four hundred years.

Jingran held the lamp up. The cracked pane caught a frequency below the visible and turned it into a colour he has never been able to describe to anyone, including himself — a colour that means *somebody was here, and they carried something, and it mattered.*

Nineteen hundred and seven of them. Going home.

He sat on the ridge with his lamp and his cat and watched every one of them through the gate, and what the lamp held was the inventory nobody had filed and nobody had asked for, and somebody in that city had already written half of it down in a Magistrate's hand, which was the first time in his professional life that the record had got there before him.

He would think about that for a long time. Mengzhou keeps its dead in hollows and spheres, in places you have to crawl to reach, and the business of pulling them out is a solitary one, conducted by lamplight, in silence, by people who go in alone. A city that does it standing in the road with a kitchen sergeant and a chopstick is something else entirely, and the word for it — the word his master would have used, the word that would go in the book between the measurements and the frequencies and the careful inventory of what the war could not digest — was one he had been carrying for seven days without knowing what to do with, and it was *home*.

Below, on the road, Lupa found Baizhi at the sixth hour and told her about the decay curve.

Baizhi took a sample. Held it up. Held it for eleven seconds, which for her is the time it takes to revise a measurement she had been satisfied with.

<font color="#a171d1">"Who."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"A man on the ridge with a lamp and a cat."</font>

<font color="#a171d1">"Mengzhou?"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Mengzhou."</font>

Baizhi wrote *Nethermancer* in the margin of her slide notes, underlined it, and took a second sample.
