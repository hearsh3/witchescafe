# 醒月 — MOON WAKING

*但願人長久，千里共嬋娟。*
*Only let us all live long, and share her light across a thousand li.*
— Su Shi, drunk until dawn on the Mid-Autumn of 1076, writing to a younger brother seven years out of his sight

---

At the foot of the resonance spire in Xuanfang Square stands a board eight lines long, in a hand the Hold insists is the Sentinel's. The seal at the bottom is a paw. The Hold repaints the board every spring and keeps it every autumn, line by line, in the order it was written, and has done so for four hundred years.

> 一　Open the ovens before the sun. Tonight the Hold is one kitchen.
> 二　Hide a fox in every tray.
> 三　Dress the guardians. Turn their faces east.
> 四　Light a lantern for every name you can say.
> 五　Set a place facing the gate.
> 六　If the moon is slow, wake her.
> 七　Chase the fox.
> 八　Sleep late. I will see to the walls.

This year, under the eighth line, somebody had chalked three more characters in a hand that pressed hard: 我們守. *We'll keep them.*

## 一　開爐 — OPEN THE OVENS

Suisui beat Iuno out of bed. It was the first time anyone had managed it since Jinzhou.

The oven bell had rung once, far down the cliff, a single deep note that came up through the storehouse floor and into the soles of everybody's feet. When Iuno opened her eyes, Suisui was dressed, her hair was pinned, and she was standing over the flour sack with the fan in her sash, wearing the face of a woman about to go to war with a kitchen.

<font color="#da44cb">*She's beaten us, darling. On a festival.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*It's her festival.*</font>

The storehouse was dark apart from the stove. Lupa slept upright on the stool by the door with her arms folded, exactly where the eleventh bell had left her. Amy was asleep under the notebook. Cartethyia's foot had found its way into Ciaccona's ear, and Ciaccona had accepted this. At some point in the night Mei had rolled until she lay across the foot of the pallet like a draught-stop, and Iuno's feet were resting on her.

<font color="#89e51f">"Up. The ovens are open."</font>

The room went on sleeping. Suisui crossed to the pallet by the wall and laid one cold hand on the back of her sister's neck.

Yangyang sat up so fast the collar went out all the way round.

<font color="#667cf9">"Jie."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"It's Moon Waking. Sleeves on."</font>

From the floor, Mei said, <font color="#ffdead">"Is it Tuesday?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"It's the fifteenth."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Is that a Tuesday?"</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"Saturday,"</font> said Iuno, with her eyes shut, and took her feet off her.

⁂

Xuanfang Square was blue with the hour before dawn. Jade light ran up the conduits of the resonance spire, and around its foot the stalls were going up: trestles, awnings, a man unrolling a mat of carved wooden moulds, a woman planting candied haws on sticks in a rack like a small red hedge. Somebody was already frying sesame cakes, and the smell went straight across the medallions and into Lupa, who stopped.

<font color="#89e51f">"Later,"</font> said Suisui, eyes front.

<font color="#ff9742">"I didn't say anything."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Your nose did."</font>

Cartethyia reached the board first and bounced on her toes. <font color="#b8ffff">"Oh! It's a list!"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"It's the order of the day."</font> Yangyang read it for them the way she read dispatches, all eight lines, level and exact, and the foreigners stood in a half-circle in the cold and listened as if to a weather report.

<font color="#77f8c0">"'Hide a fox in every tray,'"</font> said Ciaccona. <font color="#77f8c0">"What's the fox?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"You'll see."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Is it the same as Jinzhou's? Chixia said Jinzhou chases the moon."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Same moon. In Jinzhou they race it home."</font> Yangyang looked up at the spire, where the paw was. <font color="#667cf9">"Here they wake it up."</font>

Amy had the notebook open and was copying the chalk. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Who's 'we'? Under eight. That's a different hand."</font>

Chisa put her fingertips on the board over the chalk and held them there. <font color="#ff8a8a">"Someone who pressed hard."</font>

At the hawthorn stall the woman shrieked. A small blue bird went up out of her rack with something red and glossy in its beak and away over the awnings, round-breasted, a little stiff in the wing.

Suisui watched it go. <font color="#89e51f">"That's somebody else's bird."</font>

⁂

The ovens were cut into the cliff under the intake hall: eleven mouths in a row, each as tall as a door, faced in brick that four centuries had turned black. The heat came off them in a wall. A queue of households with trays, bowls and sacks stretched back along the terrace and round the corner, and the oven-keeper worked the row with a long wooden paddle and a slate on a string around her neck.

She was a small woman with forearms like ship's rope. She looked at the household the way the whole Hold had been looking at them all week, at the feathers first, then the wolf, then the one arm, and then she looked into Suisui's bowl.

<font color="#e8b88a">"Millet."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"With jujube, and a great deal of ginger."</font>

<font color="#e8b88a">"Good. It's what everybody's got."</font> She chalked a number on the slate. <font color="#e8b88a">"Last time this Hold baked millet for Moon Waking, it was a siege. The Sentinel cut these ovens the year before Yuan Fortress. When the gorges closed, the whole Hold baked in them for eight months on millet and whatever was left in the jars."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"And you've kept it up?"</font>

<font color="#e8b88a">"One tray of siege cake a year, so the ovens remember."</font> The keeper looked back along the queue, which was millet from end to end. <font color="#e8b88a">"This year they'll remember everything."</font>

Lupa looked into the bowl for a long moment. <font color="#ff9742">"Millet."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"With intent."</font>

The keeper tipped out a basket of moulds onto the tray board. They were dark with oil and old as the ovens, carved by people in a siege whose hands had needed something to do. There was a fox curled nose to tail. There was a round moon with a fox sitting in it. There was a bird with its wings spread, and there was the Core in its rings.

<font color="#e8b88a">"Every cake gets the eye."</font> She drew a short curved line across the top of a pressed round with the point of a knife, a closed lid. <font color="#e8b88a">"In the oven it opens. That's the waking."</font>

They pressed. Chisa worked by feel and turned out cakes with edges like cut glass. Iuno portioned the dough on the keeper's scale with her one hand to the grain, and the High Priestess watched every portion.

<font color="#da44cb">*A siege, darling. Eight months. And they still carved a fox into it.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*Of course they did.*</font>

Cartethyia pressed the fox mould and was delighted by each fox individually. Mei's came out looking faintly like turtles, as everything Mei made did, and she cut the eyes so deep that her cakes came out of the test firing looking astonished. Amy wrote *Ma: turtles (again)* in the notebook and underlined it.

Yangyang took the bird mould. She packed it, pressed, and turned it out onto her palm, and the Xuanling lay there with its wings open, flour on its back.

She looked at it for a while.

<font color="#89e51f">"Don't eat that one,"</font> said Suisui. <font color="#89e51f">"It's you."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"It has better posture."</font>

Above the ovens, a Warden's step came along the top of the wall: slow, heavy, the end of a night round. Yangyang heard it before she looked. Liangyu stood on the parapet in her leathers with the dark still on her, looking down at the queue. She lifted one hand. Yangyang lifted hers.

<font color="#89e51f">"Has she eaten?"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"She's on the wall."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"That's a location. I asked about food."</font>

Suisui pressed one more cake than there were people, in the bird mould, and set it at the side of the tray by itself. Then she looked up. The blue bird was sitting on the brick lip of mouth six, very pleased with itself, with a glaze of sugar on its beak.

<font color="#89e51f">"Off."</font>

It stayed.

## 二　藏狐 — HIDE A FOX IN EVERY TRAY

<font color="#e8b88a">"One fox a tray."</font> The keeper leaned on her paddle. <font color="#e8b88a">"The baker hides it and tells nobody. The fox is whatever you think is funny. Whoever bites it is a fox at moonrise and runs, and whoever catches a fox gets one question, and the fox answers it true."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Why true?"</font>

<font color="#e8b88a">"Because She never told a lie in four hundred years, and it's Her game. She spent one Moon Waking running across the karst with another Sentinel after her, laughing the whole way. That's the chase."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Which Sentinel?"</font>

<font color="#e8b88a">"Depends who's telling it. My mother said the Jinzhou one."</font> The paddle came round. <font color="#e8b88a">"Mouth six is free in a quarter-bell. Hide your fox."</font>

She went down the row to shout at somebody's grandson.

The household stood around the tray and studied the ovens, the queue, the sky and their own sleeves.

Lupa went first, because Lupa distrusted waiting. She took a dried chilli from somewhere inside her coat, pushed it into the heart of one cake with a single claw, pinched the dough shut, and walked away to look at a wall.

Amy waited until Lupa's back was turned. She poked a knot of pickled mustard greens into another cake, wrote something in the notebook, and struck it through so hard the page tore.

<font color="#da44cb">*A fox ought to make a face, darling.*</font>

Iuno had a salted plum. Where she had been keeping it was a matter for the High Priestess. It went into a third cake in one motion, and she smoothed the dough with her thumb and turned to admire the brickwork.

Cartethyia tore a corner off an invoice and folded it into a person the size of her fingernail, arms out, and tucked it into a fourth cake with great tenderness.

<font color="#b8ffff">"So the fox has company,"</font> she whispered to it.

Ciaccona took a spoon, hollowed out a fifth cake through its base, pressed the base shut again over the empty space, and blew away the crumbs.

<font color="#77f8c0">"A rest,"</font> she told the spoon.

Mei put an extra salted yolk into a sixth. She stood back and regarded it with enormous pride.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Ma. That's two yolks."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Now it's a fox."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"That's just a better cake."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Foxes can be generous."</font>

Chisa heard every one of them go in. She stood at the end of the tray with her hands folded and her face perfectly still.

Yangyang and Suisui kept their hands in their sleeves. The board said one per tray, and Yangyang had read the board aloud.

While Suisui was re-pinning a strand of hair, the blue bird dropped from the oven lip onto the corner of the tray with a bead of sugar-red in its beak. It bent its head once over the last cake in the last row, straightened, and hopped back up to the brick. Yangyang saw it. It looked like a bird after crumbs.

<font color="#89e51f">"Off."</font> The fan came out. The bird settled on the lip with its feet tucked under, innocent as a clock.

The keeper came back up the row with the paddle, looked at the tray, and looked at the household.

<font color="#e8b88a">"How many foxes are in this tray?"</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"One,"</font> said Iuno.

<font color="#e8b88a">"Every family tries it their first year."</font> She waited.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Seven,"</font> said Chisa.

Six people looked at one another and counted six guilty faces. Then they all turned to the sisters.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"…Jie? Yangyang?"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"We read the board."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"One per tray. It's written down."</font>

The keeper looked at Chisa, who had said her piece, and then at the bird on the oven lip, which was cleaning its foot.

<font color="#e8b88a">"Then it'll be a good chase,"</font> she said, and slid the tray into mouth six.

## 三　披綵 — DRESS THE GUARDIANS

The guardians stood on the long terrace under the overlook, in the rows where the battle had left them. They were Xuanfang's own, the Sentinel's shield-shapes, and three nights ago they had come up the gorge roads with other people's grief driving their joints. Now their faceplates were tilted east, toward the mountains, where they had turned when the birds went up. Some were scorched. Some had holes through the plating. On the nearest, somebody's lantern still hung from a raised hand, burnt out, and a wreath of dried persimmons had shrivelled around its neck.

At the head of the terrace stair waited the baskets: red ribbon, blue ribbon, paper lanterns on cords, the festival dressing the Hold had put on these machines every autumn for two hundred years.

The Hold came down to the stair and stood there.

Parents kept their hands on their children's shoulders. A Warden stood at the rope with his eyes on the rows. An old man held a ribbon in both fists and looked at it. Near the front a woman said, to the air, <font color="#d8c3a5">"Not this year."</font>

<font color="#c7b8d8">"It's on the board,"</font> said the woman beside her.

<font color="#d8c3a5">"Then the board can do it."</font>

Liangyu came along the terrace from the wall stair with the night still on her. She took a red ribbon from the nearest basket, eyes lowered, and went down the stair.

The first guardian in the first row stood with its right arm lowered and a little out, as if it had stopped halfway through reaching for something. Liangyu stopped in front of it. She stood there for some time. Then she tied the ribbon around its forearm, just below the joint where the plates overlapped, drew the bow tight, and stepped back.

Her hands shook doing it. Yangyang saw that from the top of the stair, and she saw where the ribbon had gone. At Xianling, Liangyu had told her that Qingzhi walked up to the first one because she thought something had caught in its arm.

The ribbon lifted in the wind off the gorge.

Chisa went down next, with Lupa one step behind her. She laid her palm flat on the guardian's shin, below the knee plate, and tilted her head.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"It's quiet."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Meaning?"</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Whatever he put in them went home."</font>

Lupa turned to the stair, filled her lungs, and made it a public announcement in the voice that had once filled an arena.

<font color="#ff9742">"SHE SAYS THEY'RE EMPTY."</font>

The old man with the ribbon came down first. He tied it on the next guardian's wrist with a fisherman's knot and patted the plating twice, as if it were a horse. A girl slipped her mother's hand and ran to the third one with a lantern bigger than her head. Her mother came after her, and then the stair emptied onto the terrace all at once, and the Hold went among its machines with its arms full of colour.

Lupa hung lanterns on the raised hands she could reach, which was most of them. Mei tied ribbons, large and lopsided, and admired each one. Iuno walked the rows and counted.

<font color="#da44cb">*We're counting machines now?*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*Two hundred and twelve. They're on the list.*</font>

Cartethyia looked up at the tallest hands and turned to Suisui with enormous eyes.

<font color="#89e51f">"The quartermaster billed us for the last lantern hook."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"I'll pay for the air!"</font>

The air prickled. Cartethyia went up, and up, gold and white and taller than the guardians, and the terrace made the sound a crowd makes at fireworks. Fleurdelys moved down the row hanging lanterns on the heads of machines three times a man's height, gently, the way you set a hat on a sleeping relative.

At the end of the last row stood the one the Hold's children called Lao Da. He was the eldest, twice the height of the others, with a shoulder broad enough to sit on. People said the Sentinel used to sit up there on festival nights with her paper umbrella and a stick of haws, and so every Moon Waking somebody tied a stick of candied haws to Lao Da's shoulder for Her. By morning it was always gone.

A boy stood at Lao Da's foot holding the haws in both hands, the way he had once held a sword. His aunt stood behind him.

Yangyang knew him. Qingyan looked up the whole height of the guardian, and then at the stick, and then up again.

<font color="#f3d9a4">"Qingzhi always did it,"</font> he said, to the guardian's knee.

Fleurdelys knelt. It took a while; there was a great deal of her. She held out one enormous open palm at the height of his chest and waited.

Qingyan looked back at his aunt. His aunt nodded. He stepped onto the palm, and Fleurdelys lifted him the whole height of Lao Da, slowly, and held him beside the shoulder while he tied the haws on.

He tied them with a short end doubled back under the binding, where you could undo it later. Yangyang saw the knot from the ground. She had seen that knot before, on a sword's tassel, in Xianling.

He was humming while he worked. It was the tune everybody in the Hold knew, and it was one particular verse of it, over and over, the way children practise the part they have been given. At the rail above the terrace, Liangyu went still.

When the knot was done, Qingyan sat on the shoulder, where the Sentinel sat, and looked east for a while. A small blue bird came down out of the air and landed on Lao Da's head above him.

<font color="#f3d9a4">"Hello,"</font> said Qingyan, to the bird.

Then Fleurdelys brought him down, and his aunt took him by the hand, and the haws swung on the shoulder in the wind.

## 四　點燈 — A LANTERN FOR EVERY NAME

By noon the Hold had run out of lantern paper.

It carried on regardless. People folded lanterns from ration slips, from the backs of intake forms, from old kite paper and wrapping and the Guild's receipts. A clerk on the second tier was folding them out of a ledger he said was finished anyway. Every lantern took a name, written or said. The Hold hung them on the lines strung along the freight lanes, where the lift-kites could carry them up at moonrise so the moon, waking, would know who to look for.

In the storehouse yard the household sat on the flagstones in thin sunlight and folded.

Suisui had one real sheet. She had bought it that morning after pricing it three times, and she was painting on it with a borrowed brush: a Xuanling, blue, the sleeves fading to glass at the wrist, white at every tip. Her model sat on an upturned crate in the light, and every so often Suisui took her by the chin and turned her so the collar caught the sun.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"You're painting Yangyang."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"A Xuanling. She happens to be in the light."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Do you do this every year?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Since she was six."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Even when she's away?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"It's the festival. You paint the lantern."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Where do they go?"</font>

Suisui laid a line of white along the tip of a wing. <font color="#89e51f">"The storeroom. Top shelf."</font> The brush went back to the dish. <font color="#89e51f">"Nine."</font>

Yangyang sat very still on the crate.

<font color="#89e51f">"You used to tear them by the second hour. Every year. Ma said I painted the sleeves too thin."</font> Suisui turned her sister's chin a quarter-inch to the left and looked at the collar, and then at the paper, and made a small correction. <font color="#89e51f">"They've kept very well."</font>

The collar went up and stayed up. Two paces away, Chisa's hands stopped folding.

Suisui finished the white tips at her own pace.

⁂

The Hold's characters were beyond Mei, so Mei dictated.

<font color="#ffdead">"Lynae. Rebecca. Zani. Augusta, she'll pretend she hates it. Galbrena. Uncle Cosimo."</font>

Yangyang wrote them, one to a lantern, in a clean small hand.

<font color="#ffdead">"The noodle cook."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"What's her name?"</font>

Mei thought about it. <font color="#ffdead">"Put 'Four Minutes.'"</font>

Yangyang wrote *Four Minutes*.

<font color="#ffdead">"Lilith."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"How does it go?"</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Like it sounds."</font>

Yangyang wrote it by sound, in the characters that came closest, and Mei looked at it for a while and nodded. Then she said, more quietly, <font color="#ffdead">"Phrolova,"</font> and Yangyang wrote that too, and kept her questions.

Iuno had folded one lantern out of a page of her own correspondence and was writing on it in Septimont script. Amy leaned over to read it upside down.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"You're lighting a lantern for a cat."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"He had a name."</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*He had two, darling. The other one isn't fit for a lantern.*</font>

Lupa folded fast and badly and said the names out loud as she went, one to each lantern, the way she always did it, because saying them was the whole mechanism.

<font color="#ff9742">"Cantarella. Augusta. Mornye. Mya."</font> A crease. <font color="#ff9742">"Sumika."</font>

Chisa's hand went to the red string at her wrist.

Lupa folded one more and dropped it in Chisa's lap with her eyes on her own folding, and went on.

Cartethyia was folding hers from the disputed invoices. Suisui watched two go by before she spoke.

<font color="#89e51f">"Those are evidence."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"They're lanterns now."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"She's right,"</font> said Ciaccona. <font color="#77f8c0">"It's the most they'll ever be."</font> She had drawn a whole-bar rest on one of her own, with its value written underneath, and a small uneven star. She left the space for a name blank and hung it with the rest.

Yangyang wrote her own names last. Duzhong. Qingzhi. Qingxiao. Mingyu. Yanlang. Lingfang. Then she took one more lantern, and wrote *Yangyang* on it, and sat looking at it.

Suisui looked up from her painting.

<font color="#89e51f">"That's your name."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"It's hers too."</font>

Suisui put the brush down in the dish. She looked at the lantern for as long as she had looked at the Xuanling's wing.

<font color="#89e51f">"Put 'jie' beside it,"</font> she said. <font color="#89e51f">"So the moon knows which."</font>

Yangyang wrote 師姐 beside her own name, small, and hung it with the others.

⁂

The Hold's one working long-relay stood in a corner of the intake hall, in a booth made of two screens and a stool. The queue for it went out of the door and down the stair. Everyone in it had a name to say to somebody on Moon Waking. There were villagers from Xianling with their children on their hips, workers the household had carried out of the Skyworks, Guild clerks, a grandmother with a list. The Warden on duty kept a sand-glass on the table and turned it for each caller. The sand took four minutes.

The Warden on duty was Liangyu.

She had come straight from the terrace. She wrote each caller's code on a slate, turned the glass, and watched the sand, and when it ran out she said, <font color="#dcf9dc">"Time,"</font> gently, the way you wake somebody, and turned it for the next.

When the sisters reached the stool, Suisui gave the house code. The line clicked, and rang, and hissed, and a voice came through it from very far west, already talking.

<font color="#9fbfdf">"—is it them? Is it both of them? Hold it nearer, I can't—"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Ba."</font>

<font color="#9fbfdf">"Your mother's made osmanthus cakes. Too many. I'm eating the large ones."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Leave some."</font>

<font color="#9fbfdf">"For whom? You're both in the mountains."</font>

Then there was a different breath on the line, and a pause of exactly the kind their family made.

<font color="#38d64b">"…Did the shell come?"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"It's in my sleeve."</font>

<font color="#38d64b">"Good."</font>

The line hissed. Both sisters held their tongues, fluently.

<font color="#9fbfdf">"She's set your places,"</font> said Ba.

<font color="#38d64b">"It's the festival."</font>

Suisui's fan had stopped. <font color="#89e51f">"How many?"</font>

<font color="#38d64b">"…The usual."</font>

Yangyang put her palm flat against the screen of the booth, as if it were a wall she could lean on. The sand ran.

<font color="#667cf9">"Ma. The other one got home."</font>

For a while the line from Mingting carried only the hiss. Suisui turned her face to the wall of the booth.

<font color="#38d64b">"…Good,"</font> said Ma. <font color="#38d64b">"Eat."</font>

Liangyu turned the glass.

Suisui came out of the booth with the fan over her mouth. Yangyang stayed on the stool a moment longer with her hand on the screen, then got up to make room. The next caller was an old man from Xianling who had been rehearsing his first sentence the whole length of the queue.

Amy had been leaning on the wall by the door. She watched the sand for three callers and the slate for four.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"When's yours?"</font>

Liangyu turned the glass. <font color="#dcf9dc">"The long line's down. Mengzhou is past the eastern masts, and the Autopuppets pulled those in the first week."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Who's in Mengzhou?"</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"My grandmother."</font> Liangyu wrote the next code on the slate, and it took her longer than a code should take. <font color="#dcf9dc">"Time,"</font> she said, gently, to the booth.

Amy looked up past her, through the hall's high window, at the lift-kites: sail-wings the size of temple roofs, pinned still on the updraft, with their cables running away east and south through the air in long lanes.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Where do the freight lanes go?"</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Mengzhou."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"On cables."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Yes."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"That's a wire."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"It's a freight lane."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"It's a very long wire."</font>

⁂

The rest of the afternoon went to it.

Iuno spent it in the lane office with the lane-master, a man being asked, for the first time in his life, to sign for a telephone. She walked him through his own form clause by clause and signed it in Septimont script, which he accepted after some thought.

<font color="#da44cb">*We've turned a freight lane into a relay, darling. And signed for it.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*Amy did the turning. Ours is the ink.*</font>

Suisui paid the lane toll and had it itemised. Cartethyia went tall again at the kite terrace, to the delight of a crowd of children who had followed her from the guardians, and hooked the end of Amy's lead onto the anchor cable of the south lane at a height beyond any ladder. Lupa stood at the foot of an idle ladder and held it anyway.

<font color="#ff9742">"If she falls, I'm howling."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"She's fifteen metres tall."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Then I'll howl louder."</font>

Chisa laid her palm on the cable where it came down to the anchor. She stood with her head on one side while the wind worked through the lane.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"It hums."</font> A pause. <font color="#ff8a8a">"A fourth."</font>

Ciaccona's head came up off her lute.

<font color="#77f8c0">"Of course it does."</font>

Amy sat on a crate with the handset in her lap and a coil of lead across her knees, her hands black to the wrist. <font color="#ff8ff4">"It's up. The lanes are full of freight chatter all day. At moonrise they stop the carriers for the lanterns, so it'll be quiet. If it works, it works then."</font>

Liangyu had come up at the change of watch. She wrote her grandmother's code on a slip of paper and gave it to Amy, and her face kept the level look a Warden's face learns.

On the way back through the square Amy stopped at the board. Under the eighth line and the chalked *we'll keep them*, there were names now: a Warden's number, three family names, somebody's thumbprint in chalk dust, a child's fox with enormous ears.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"There's more,"</font> she said.

---

## 五　留席 — SET A PLACE FACING THE GATE

At dusk the Hold carried its tables out and set them end to end down the terraces, from the gate all the way up to the overlook stair, so that for one night the Hold ate at one table. The name lanterns hung on the lane lines overhead, lit and waiting. Every household had its own stretch of board and its own siege cakes, and every stretch had one place set facing the gate.

Suisui laid theirs. Bowls, chopsticks, napkins folded into swans; the tray from mouth six on a board in the middle, every cake with its eye opened; and at the end, by itself, the bird-mould cake. The gate place she laid last: a bowl, chopsticks, a cup turned toward the road.

The table looked at it for a while. Each of them was looking at somebody different.

Mei put her hand flat on her shirt, over the letter. Lupa's ears went back. Iuno's thumb found the rim of her cup and stopped there. Yangyang looked past the gate place, down the terraces, to where the road went out into the dark.

<font color="#89e51f">"Amy. Fetch the Warden. Tell her it's an order."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"From who?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Mine. She'll recognise the type."</font>

Liangyu came up the terrace in her leathers with Amy behind her. Every seat was taken except one. She stopped beside it.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"That one's for the road."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Sit."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"It's for whoever's still out there."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"You've been on it since Xianling."</font> Lupa pushed the bowl an inch toward her with one claw. <font color="#ff9742">"Sit."</font>

Liangyu looked at the seat. At the next stretch of table, the oven-keeper had stopped with a cake halfway to her mouth.

<font color="#e8b88a">"That's what it's for,"</font> she said, to her bowl.

Liangyu sat down facing the gate. Suisui put the bird-mould cake in front of her and a swan beside it.

Iuno served the cakes. She went round the table with her one hand and put a round on every plate, and when she reached her own place she sat, took a bite, chewed, and swallowed.

<font color="#5881fe">"I can't taste anything."</font>

Lupa's ears came up. <font color="#ff9742">"That's my chilli."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"I can't feel it."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Wasted."</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*Our tongue has been dead since the noodles, darling. She never stood a chance.*</font>

Lupa took an injured bite of her own. Her ears flattened, her eyes filled, and she put the cake down very carefully.

<font color="#ff9742">"Who."</font>

Amy's pencil had stopped mid-word. <font color="#ff8ff4">"…Science."</font>

Chisa chewed, paused, and drew out of her mouth a small damp folded paper person with its arms out.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"There's a person in this."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"So the fox isn't lonely!"</font>

Chisa held the paper person on her palm for a moment. <font color="#ff8a8a">"…Thank you."</font>

Ciaccona bit into a salted plum, and her whole face closed like a fist. <font color="#77f8c0">"Madonna."</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*A fox ought to make a face.*</font>

Mei bit into hers and looked inside. <font color="#ffdead">"Mine's empty."</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"That's a rest,"</font> said Ciaccona, still puckered. <font color="#77f8c0">"You're a fox."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"I'm the kind with nothing in it."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"That's so sad, Ma."</font>

Mei ate it anyway, with every sign of enjoyment.

Yangyang broke hers along the eye. There were two yolks inside, side by side, the colour of the moon on water.

<font color="#ffdead">"Oh! That's mine!"</font>

Yangyang looked at the two yolks. On the cliff above Xianling, coming apart, she had once seen two moons on the same water.

Under the table, Suisui's hand came to rest on her knee.

Then Suisui bit into her own cake, stopped, and took something out of her mouth. It was a single candied haw, whole, still glossy with its sugar.

The table went quiet.

At the next stretch, the oven-keeper leaned over. <font color="#e8b88a">"Who put a haw in?"</font>

Six people who had put in a chilli, mustard greens, a plum, a paper person, a hollow and a second yolk looked at one another.

The keeper sat back slowly.

<font color="#e8b88a">"Some years there's one nobody put in."</font> She looked at Suisui with something close to respect. <font color="#e8b88a">"That one's Hers."</font>

Suisui turned the haw over in her fingers. Up on the lantern pole above the table, a small blue bird was sitting with its feet tucked under. In her sleeve, where Suisui kept the Prism wrapped in silk, something had gone warm.

<font color="#89e51f">"She's a sore loser,"</font> said Suisui.

The oven-keeper gave one loud bark of a laugh, and the table next to hers asked what was funny.

Cartethyia had taken a bite of a perfectly ordinary cake. She held it up, beaming. <font color="#b8ffff">"I'm a hunter!"</font>

Amy looked into her own plain cake, turned to a fresh page, and wrote QUESTIONS across the top in capitals.

Liangyu had eaten half of the bird-mould cake. It was plain. She looked at the half that was left, and at the gate, and wrapped the rest in the swan.

Out past the gate the sky had gone green along the eastern rank, and the ninth spire stood up black against it. The Hold looked at the ninth spire. Above it the sky was still bare.

<font color="#e8b88a">"She's slow,"</font> said the oven-keeper, and stood up, and so did the Hold.

## 六　醒月 — IF THE MOON IS SLOW, WAKE HER

The Hold went up to meet her.

It filled the terraces and the stairs and the overlook rail, every tier from the gate to the cliff top, lanterns in hand. Below, the guardians stood in their ribbons with their faces east. Above, the name lanterns hung in their thousands along the freight lanes, and the lift-kites waited at the ends of their cables. Higher still, the Core turned in its rings and caught the last light on its jade.

Amy had the rig on a crate at the overlook rail. The lead ran up from it to the lane anchor, and she had patched the handset into the muster horns, the brass mouths on every terrace that called the Wardens out at dawn. Chisa stood with her palm on the cable. Liangyu stood at the rail with the handset in both hands.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Code's in. Lanes are quiet."</font> Amy bent over the rig. <font color="#ff8ff4">"…It's ringing."</font>

Liangyu put the handset to her ear.

It rang for a long time, far away in the south. Then something clicked.

<font color="#b9d8b9">"Wei?"</font>

Liangyu opened her mouth, and for a moment what came out of it was only breath.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"…Nainai."</font>

<font color="#b9d8b9">"Liangyu?"</font> A pause, full of hiss and distance. <font color="#b9d8b9">"Is it the festival there too?"</font>

Liangyu laughed. It broke in the middle, and she let it.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Nainai. Listen."</font>

She looked at Amy. Amy threw the switch on the horns.

Liangyu sang the first call.

It came out of every brass mouth on every terrace at once: a Warden's voice, trained to carry through fog, going out over the gorges. For one breath the Hold only listened. Then it answered, five thousand people giving the call back exactly as it was given, the way that song is answered, and the sound went out across the dark and came back off the karst.

She sang the next, and the Hold gave it back.

It was a marching song and a farewell, and the Hold had sung it every Moon Waking of its life. Old men sang it with their eyes shut. Children sang it half a beat early. The workers from the Skyworks and the families from Xianling were hearing it for the first time, and they learned the trick of it by the third call, because the trick was simple: you listened, and you said it back.

At the end of the third call Liangyu stopped.

It was the place where Qingzhi had always taken the next verse. The Hold waited, thinking it was a breath. The gap stretched. Liangyu stood with the handset against her collarbone and left it open.

A small voice beside the rail took it.

Qingyan was holding his aunt's sleeve. He sang his sister's verse the way he had hummed it on Lao Da's shoulder, in a boy's voice that went thin at the top and held, and he knew every word of it. Liangyu turned her head and looked at him. The Hold answered him the same as it answered her.

Halfway through the next call, a voice came in late.

It came in on purpose, a beat behind, joining whichever line it could hear best, and it sounded exactly like somebody who was always late. Liangyu heard it, and her face broke open into a laugh with tears running through it. At the rail Ciaccona sang Duzhong's entrance with her lute across her back and her eyes on the moonless spire.

They reached the mist.

For two hundred years the song had stopped here. The Hold stopped too, from habit, all five thousand of it. Mouths closed on every terrace. The old people stood in the held silence they had stood in every Moon Waking of their lives, the rest where the second half ought to be, kept open so long it had become part of the tune.

Liangyu took the next call into the silence alone.

It went out of every horn, over the terraces and the gorges and the dark: the first line of the half that had been lost at Yuan Fortress. It was new to every terrace. The rest held.

Then, out of every brass mouth in the Hold, thin and old and a little late, an old woman in Mengzhou said it back.

The whole Hold heard her. It drew one breath on every tier at once.

On the next call, the Hold answered with her.

After that it ran on. Liangyu gave the calls one by one, the calls she had learned from an Outrider at a rail three nights before, and Mengzhou gave them back down a freight lane, and the Hold gave them back with Mengzhou, and the lost half of the song went out over the karst in five thousand voices and one old one.

At the verse about the road, Suisui answered.

She stood at the rail beside her sister and sang it flat, as she sang everything, with her hand over Yangyang's on the cold stone. When she had reached this line at the overlook three nights ago, she had stopped. Tonight she came in on the beat and sang it straight through.

When the song called for the bird, the people around Yangyang turned to look at her. A child on her father's shoulders pointed at the collar. A small blue bird came down out of the dark and landed on the rail by Liangyu's hand. Yangyang's collar went up all the way round, and she sang her answer level and exact, in the voice she used to confirm that a thing had already been done.

They came to the last line, and sang it together.

On the last line the moon came up.

It cleared the eastern rank huge and gold and slow, the harvest moon. It rose two spires to the left of the ninth.

Five thousand people, all turned toward the ninth spire, shuffled two steps to the left.

An old man on the second tier laughed out loud. <font color="#d9c7a7">"She moved it!"</font>

The laugh ran down the terraces from tier to tier like water finding its level. High above the cloud line the Core turned in its rings and sounded one note, low and round and a little cracked. The lift-kites climbed on their cables, and the name lanterns went up the lanes after them in long gold lines, thousands of names at once, up into the sky where the moon could see them. Lupa went along the rail lighting the children's sparklers with a fingertip, grumbling at each one. Down on the terrace below, the guardians' faces caught the moonlight, row on row, and the haws on Lao Da's shoulder shone.

Chisa took her hand off the cable. <font color="#ff8a8a">"She's up."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"How'd you know?"</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Everybody breathed in."</font>

Amy closed the horns. The handset was still live. Liangyu held it to her ear with both hands and turned her back on the Hold.

<font color="#b9d8b9">"…Liangyu."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Nainai."</font>

<font color="#b9d8b9">"Have you eaten?"</font>

Liangyu looked down the terraces to the long table, to the place facing the gate, where half a cake sat wrapped in a paper swan.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Yes, Nainai."</font>

<font color="#b9d8b9">"What did they give you?"</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Millet."</font>

<font color="#b9d8b9">"Good."</font>

Amy picked up her notebook and walked six paces along the rail and sat down on the step with her back to them, and kept her pencil still.

A little way along the rail, Mei was looking at the moon. Then she looked at Iuno.

<font color="#5881fe">"What?"</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"It came up in the wrong place."</font>

Iuno looked at the moon, gold over the wrong spire, and at the woman beside her, who had once woken on a beach in the place Iuno put her.

<font color="#5881fe">"It seems to have worked out."</font>

<font color="#ffdead">"Yeah,"</font> said Mei, and leaned.

## 七　追狐 — CHASE THE FOX

Somewhere down the terraces a boy screamed <font color="#f3d9a4">"FOXES!"</font> and the Hold came apart.

Every tray in the Hold had carried a fox, and every fox ran. Grandmothers ran. A Warden sergeant ran with his coat over his head. Two teenagers went over the side of the second tier on a rope meant for lanterns. Hunters went after them with lanterns and sparklers and shrieks, and the Moon Waking chase went down the terraces and along the lanes and over the roofs under the harvest moon, the way it had on this night for four hundred years, since a fox ran across the karst laughing with another Sentinel at her heels.

The household's foxes left at their various speeds.

⁂

Cartethyia found Lupa on a roof.

She was sitting on the ridge tiles with her arms round her knees, looking at the wall of the next building. Cartethyia came up the drainpipe with a sparkler in her teeth.

<font color="#b8ffff">"Found you!"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"I'm looking at a wall."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"Question!"</font>

Lupa's ears went flat against her skull.

<font color="#b8ffff">"Did you cry behind the fern?"</font>

A long time passed on the roof. The sparkler burned down to a wire. Four centuries of fox law sat on the ridge between them.

<font color="#ff9742">"…Yes."</font>

Cartethyia put both hands on her cheeks.

<font color="#ff9742">"Go away."</font>

⁂

Iuno and Mei were sitting on the bench at the second pond, in plain sight, with the moonwheel lit on the stone beside them like a second moon. The pond lanterns turned in the water at their feet.

Amy stopped in front of them with the notebook open.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"You're sitting in the light."</font>

<font color="#5881fe">"We're foxes. We're resting. It's permitted."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Question."</font> Amy found the line on the page. <font color="#ff8ff4">"Auntie. Was the lemon adequate?"</font>

Iuno looked at the pond. The rule held her by the collar.

<font color="#5881fe">"…It was dreadful."</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*Traitor.*</font>

Amy wrote it down. Then she turned the page to a line she had written earlier, looked at it, and looked at her mother.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Ma. Does it hurt?"</font>

Mei's hand was on her stitches. She had been holding them all night, through the ovens and the guardians and the stairs and the song.

<font color="#ffdead">"…Yes."</font>

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Okay."</font>

Amy closed the notebook, sat down on the bench between them, and stayed there.

⁂

Ciaccona was hiding in the square, on the steps of the resonance spire, playing. Cartethyia, returning victorious from the roof, followed the lute as a moth follows a lamp.

<font color="#b8ffff">"Found you!"</font>

<font color="#77f8c0">"A bard hides in the melody, cara."</font>

<font color="#b8ffff">"I followed it."</font> Cartethyia sat down on the step below her and put her chin on Ciaccona's knee. <font color="#b8ffff">"Question. Was the seventh knot my best?"</font>

Ciaccona's fingers stopped on the strings.

<font color="#77f8c0">"It nearly hanged her,"</font> she said. <font color="#77f8c0">"It's my favourite knot in the world."</font>

⁂

Chisa sat on Lao Da's foot in full moonlight with the rest of her cake. Three hunters passed her in the time it took the haws on the shoulder above to stop swinging. Each of them assumed somebody else had caught her. Chisa finished the cake and folded the little paper person into her cuff.

⁂

A fox who can hear every hunter a street away can take her time. Yangyang took hers.

She carried the Xuanling lantern Suisui had painted, lit, and she went east, away from the shrieking, down through the quiet of the lower lanes. The festival noise thinned behind her into the sound of the Hold enjoying itself at a distance. Under it, a long way off, the Core held its note.

Under that, very quietly, somebody said her name.

The east postern stood at the end of a lane too narrow for carts. Beyond it the old road went down into the dark toward the gorges and, a long way further, Mengzhou. Suisui stood at the gate with her fan closed in one hand, looking down the road.

<font color="#89e51f">"Yangyang,"</font> she said, in the voice for calling somebody in to eat.

She waited the length of a breath. Then she turned the fan over in her hand and looked down the road again.

<font color="#667cf9">"Here."</font>

Suisui spun round. The fan hit the cobbles.

For a moment the sisters looked at each other in the lantern light: two foxes and one Xuanling, painted.

<font color="#89e51f">"…Foxes can't catch foxes."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"I know."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"Who were you calling?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"A fox asking a fox is just a conversation."</font>

Yangyang waited.

Suisui bent and picked up the fan, and dusted it, and kept her eyes on the road.

<font color="#89e51f">"You. At dinner time."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"How long?"</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Since you left."</font>

The lantern moved in Yangyang's hand.

<font color="#89e51f">"Most nights. On Guild nights I said it on the stairs."</font> Suisui opened the fan and shut it again. <font color="#89e51f">"After the second year, Ma started leaving the door open for me."</font>

The Hold's noise went on up the hill. Down the road the dark ran on to the gorges.

<font color="#667cf9">"Thursday,"</font> said Yangyang.

<font color="#89e51f">"Mm."</font>

Yangyang looked down at the lantern. The painted Xuanling had its wings open, blue, the sleeves fading to glass, white at every tip, and the sleeve on the near wing was very thin.

She put her thumb through it.

<font color="#89e51f">"Yangyang!"</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"You paint the sleeves too thin."</font>

Suisui stared at the torn wing, and the light coming through the hole in it, and then she laughed. It was the loud one, the one she kept out of Guild meetings, and it doubled her over, and she had to hold on to the gate. When she could stand up again she wiped her eyes with the back of the fan hand.

<font color="#89e51f">"Ten,"</font> she said.

At the top of the lane, Liangyu had stopped with her hunter's lantern raised. She took in the gate, the two of them, the torn wing, and her lantern came down. She turned round and called up the hill to the hunters behind her in her Warden's voice.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"East gate's clear!"</font>

On the lintel above the postern a small blue bird fluffed its feathers against the cold, and settled.

## 八　高臥 — SLEEP LATE. I WILL SEE TO THE WALLS.

By the second bell after moonrise the chase had worn itself out, and the Hold was scattered across its own terraces eating the last of the millet in the moonlight.

In the storehouse yard the torn Xuanling hung on the hook by the door with its tear to the front, where it showed.

Lupa peeled a pomelo in one long spiral with a single claw and put the empty peel on Amy's head.

<font color="#ff8ff4">"Gross."</font>

She kept it on. Cartethyia wanted one immediately, and got one, and wore it like a helmet. Lupa lit their sparklers with a fingertip, grumbling, and they wrote names in the air, each one hanging a moment in the dark before it went out: Chixia, Baizhi, Lynae, the noodle cook.

Chisa sat on the step with her shoulder against Lupa's knee.

<font color="#ff8a8a">"What does she look like?"</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"The moon?"</font> Lupa considered it. <font color="#ff9742">"Round. Yellow. Big."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"You should write songs."</font>

<font color="#ff9742">"Like an egg yolk."</font>

<font color="#ff8a8a">"Two, I heard."</font>

Mei had fallen asleep against Iuno on the bench by the stove, mid-sentence, with a pomelo segment still in her hand. Iuno took it out of her fingers and ate it.

<font color="#da44cb">*Three days, darling.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*Two and a half.*</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*You're counting.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*I count what I'm afraid of. You know that.*</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*…And what you love.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*It's the same list.*</font>

Suisui came out of the storehouse with a cake wrapped in a cloth and put it into her sister's hands.

<font color="#89e51f">"She's on the wall. Take her this."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"She had half at dinner."</font>

<font color="#89e51f">"Then she can finish it in front of a witness."</font>

⁂

The wall walk ran along the top of the Hold with the whole of Xuanfang on one side and the gorges on the other. From up there the terraces were a stair of small lights going down into the dark. The name lanterns hung high over the lanes, strung across the sky in lines like beads. The moon had climbed clear of everything and gone small and white and hard, and the karst stood up under it in ranks.

Liangyu was on the north stretch with two night Wardens, walking the round.

Yangyang held out the cloth.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Your sister."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"She says you're to eat it in front of me."</font>

Liangyu unwrapped it on the parapet and ate it in front of her. The Wardens went on along the wall to the next tower and left them there.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"She asked if I'd eaten."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"You told her yes."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"I'd had half."</font> Liangyu looked down at the crumbs on her palm and brushed them off over the gorge. <font color="#dcf9dc">"It's true now."</font>

They stood with their forearms on the parapet. Far down, somebody on a terrace was still singing, badly, one verse of it over and over.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"I wrote the chalk,"</font> Liangyu said. <font color="#dcf9dc">"On the board. The night after the birds went up."</font>

Yangyang waited.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Somebody had to see to the walls."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"There were names under it by the afternoon."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Sixty-one by dinner. I counted on my round."</font>

The wind came along the wall off the gorges and moved the ribbon on Yangyang's hat.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Your sister came in on time,"</font> said Liangyu. <font color="#dcf9dc">"On the road verse."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"…Yes."</font>

Liangyu nodded, as if that were a report she could file. After a while she straightened off the parapet.

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Go to bed, Outrider."</font>

<font color="#667cf9">"They've made me a Watcher."</font>

<font color="#dcf9dc">"Then go and watch the inside of your eyelids."</font>

Yangyang went down the wall stair. At the turn she looked back. Liangyu was walking the north stretch again at the slow pace of the round, alone under the moon, and a small blue bird was keeping pace with her along the parapet in short hops.

⁂

The Hold slept late.

The eighth line was kept. The ovens lay cold in the cliff. The long tables still stood down the terraces with the gate places set on every stretch, and in the storehouse the household slept in a heap of quilts, pomelo peel and paper swans, with a wolf upright on the stool by the door and a torn lantern on the hook.

Iuno woke first, as she always did, and for once let the kettle wait.

She went out into the grey with two cups. The square was empty. Frost had come down on the medallions in the night and on the stalls under their covers, and on the board at the foot of the spire.

Under the eighth line, under *we'll keep them*, the chalk had spread down the board and onto the paving stones. Names. Numbers. Thumbprints. A guardian drawn with a lantern in its hand. Somebody had written *Qingzhi* and somebody else had written *Duzhong* beside it. Further down, in a cook's square hand, was *Four Minutes*, and beside that a single long claw-mark scored into the stone at the height of a wolf's hand. The whole Hold had signed its name under the Sentinel's line, and gone to bed.

Low in the corner of the board, in the frost that had settled over the chalk, there was one small print: four toes and a pad.

<font color="#da44cb">*Countersigned, darling.*</font>

Iuno walked on down to the terrace where the guardians stood in their ribbons with the frost on their shoulders. At the end of the last row, on Lao Da's shoulder, the stick of haws was still tied in its doubled-back knot. The top haw was gone. A small blue bird was asleep on the eldest guardian's head, with its feet tucked under and a glaze of sugar on its beak.

Iuno set the second cup down on Lao Da's foot, with the handle turned outward.

<font color="#da44cb">*She came.*</font>

<font color="#5881fe">*Or a bird did.*</font>

<font color="#da44cb">*It's her festival. Let her have it.*</font>

The steam went up off the cup into the cold and leaned, a little, toward the east.

It was the sixteenth. In Xuanfang they say the moon is roundest then.
