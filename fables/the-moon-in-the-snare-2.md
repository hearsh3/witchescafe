# The Moon in the Snare

The moon in the Skyworks had no sky to rise through.

It hung inside a cage of turning brass, above the dark mouths of the furnaces. Beneath it, nine long arms uncoiled from a single body. Each carried a different instrument: a hook, a blade, a mirror, a spindle wound with something too pale to be silk. Their shadows fell in nine directions. None fell toward the lamp.

Yangyang had cut the arm with the hook three times.

The severed pieces lay about her feet. She could hear the cooling metal contract; she had felt the resistance at each joint. Yet the hook descended again, whole and bright, while above it the little moon continued its untroubled turning.

She gave ground until her heel touched a fallen beam.

Beyond the machine, Muyu's chair stood empty. The door through which he had withdrawn had become a wall, then a door again, then a view of the morning mountains. Once she had seen him standing in it. She had almost followed. A moment later the mountains opened, and a descending press struck the place where she would have put her foot.

He had been gone for some time.

Knowing this did not release her from what he had left.

Along the chamber's upper galleries, people lay beneath narrow bands of light. Some moved when the spindle turned. Some had learned to keep still. From each restraint a fine brightness ran toward the brass cage, gathering at its heart into the counterfeit moon.

Prism of Fate, the inscription called it. Below, in newer characters, Muyu had named the whole apparatus:

*Myriad Snare.*

Yangyang had seen the first captive turn his head when Muyu reached the door. She had thought there might still be time to stop the hand that governed all these others. Her sword had gone straight for him. The room had obligingly supplied a straight road.

Now her blood marked the road's actual length: four paces, ending at a wall.

The hook came down. She stepped inside its curve, laid her edge beneath the wrist, and turned. Metal parted. A narrow gust carried the falling weapon clear of the people above.

The fourth piece struck the first three.

There were spare limbs beneath the floor. She heard them now, passing upward through concealed sleeves. The machine spent iron as freely as Muyu spent people. Its falsehoods required workmanship; its inexhaustibility had a cellar.

She might have found the cellar's entrance if the spindle would have stopped.

A woman cried out on the gallery. Yangyang's hearing opened toward her before she could prevent it. For an instant the restraint closed around her own throat; her right hand forgot the sword and rose toward a wound that belonged to someone else.

The blade-bearing arm struck.

Yangyang caught it too far from the guard. Pain travelled up her wrist. She knelt under the weight, the edge descending by small degrees toward her shoulder, and heard the same woman cry out again.

Exactly the same cry.

Behind it, quieter, the woman was trying to breathe.

Yangyang turned toward that smaller sound.

“I can hear you,” she said. “Please keep breathing. I am still here.”

She could not tell whether the words reached the gallery. The great blade pressed lower. In its polished side she saw a girl kneeling beneath a sword, and behind that girl another, and another, each alone at the bottom of her own bright room.

Then fire crossed the reflection.

Something hit the blade from the side with such force that all the kneeling girls vanished.

“Yangyang, bring your head down. I'm coming over you.”

She knew the voice before the burning spear passed her cheek.

***

At Xuanfang Hold, Suisui heard the gates being struck from within.

She had imagined arriving at a wall and being asked whom she sought. Instead, smoke came through the arrow slits. A Warden knelt beside a fallen guardian with both hands inside its opened shoulder, trying to draw out a companion caught beneath it. Farther along the passage, something enormous dragged its weapon over stone.

Iuno's moonwheel tilted beneath her living hand.

The dressing above her crystal shoulder had darkened during the climb. She had said nothing about it for the last hundred steps. Now she set her feet carefully, looked through the gate, and began judging the distance to the trapped man.

“The people we want aren't here,” she said. “These people are. I can help with the passage if you can keep anyone from walking beneath me.”

Suisui looked toward the western mountains.

The Wind had risen from her scarf. Dust moved about the Prism in her hands, gathering on one facet and leaving the others clean. Its small brightness leaned west as surely as a flower leans toward a window.

Hsin had told her what a call required.

Someone at the other end, alive enough to answer.

Suisui lifted the Prism. The Wind settled beneath it, then faltered. She was still carrying the day's falls, the stair, the fastening at Iuno's wounded shoulder. The crystal rose only the breadth of a finger before Suisui caught its weight again.

“You needn't carry the coat as well,” she said. “I have it. I have both shells.”

The cold turned once around her wrists.

She knew that movement. Her sister had used it as a child when she wished to take something heavy without being told it was heavy.

Suisui kept her palms beneath the Prism until the air held steadily.

“Take this to her. Tell her—”

She stopped. There had been a time when stopping there would have saved her from saying anything difficult.

“She will know you came from me. That will do until I can tell her the rest myself.”

The Wind touched her cheek. Suisui turned into the touch, briefly, and opened her hands.

Iuno watched the brightness climb.

Once, on a shore, she had possessed more knowledge than the woman coming toward her and had used the difference to decide who would arrive. Now she knew only that Mei was somewhere west, injured, and that the little light leaving the gate might find her. Her hand rose before she had chosen whether to call it back.

*We could send one instruction,* the High Priestess murmured. *Something modest. Something beginning with her name and ending with all the things we cannot bear.*

Iuno lowered her hand to the moonwheel.

“When you find them, let them know where we are,” she said. “I intend to be extremely pleased to see them, and I would rather they had fair warning.”

The Wind passed over her brow, cool as the rim of a cup.

Then she went west.

Suisui followed her until the brightness disappeared against the day. Inside the passage, the trapped man cried out. She folded Yangyang's coat beneath the shelter of the gate and went to take his free hand.

***

Lupa's spear struck the descending blade at its root.

Fire entered the joint, ran through the hollow arm, and burst from its shoulder in the shape of a wolf with its jaws wide. The arm flew upward. Mei slid beneath it, one knee grazing the floor, and caught Yangyang under the elbow before the weight left her sword.

For a breath there was no room for greeting. Mei felt how little strength remained in that arm. She put her own shoulder beneath it and rose with her.

“Stay against me until your feet are under you. We've come a long way to get our hands on you, and I'd like to do it properly.”

Yangyang tried to look at her. A second arm unfolded behind Mei's head.

Chisa's shears closed above them.

The blade fell in two pieces, each landing on a different side of Mei's boot. Chisa had followed the weight through the floor. The false strike had sounded earlier and farther away; she had nearly turned toward it, then felt Lupa's passing heel through the stone and found the real interval beneath the noise.

“There are people above us,” Yangyang said. “The light is attached to them. Please don't strike through the galleries.”

“We found the other rooms,” Mei answered. Her voice changed a little on *rooms*. “Carte has opened the lower doors. We're going to open these too.”

The spindle turned.

Three hooks descended together. Mei pulled Yangyang behind her. Gold opened along her sword, and the first two hooks broke against the same rising stroke.

For the third, she had no room.

A white gauntlet came through the ceiling grille and caught it.

Nyx Ravenhollow tore the grille free with her other hand.

They had hauled the machine's damaged cradle off the wrecked road before following the evacuation notices west. One shoulder still wore the transport's torn canvas. Now five metres of white-and-gold armour dropped through the opening, feather blades fanning behind it, and landed between the gallery supports with an exactness that made the whole chamber ring.

“I've checked the floor load,” Amy announced through the speakers. “You may all enjoy the entrance without worrying about the ceiling.”

“I was enjoying the ceiling very much before you came through it,” Ciaccona said from the doorway.

She sat on a low step with Amy's still body supported against her, one arm firm beneath the girl's shoulders. Her lute rested across both their knees. A Sylph bent over its strings; another perched upon the bridge, listening with the grave attention of a musician who intended to be difficult.

Ciaccona gave them the measure.

The first chord laid a pale path across the floor. The second put the same beat beneath six pairs of feet, of which one pair belonged to a machine and one scarcely bore its owner. The third rose through the chamber, green and gold, and the Sylphs went with it like swallows released from a sleeve.

Lupa was already running.

She struck a kneeling construct with the butt of her spear, vaulted from its lifted shoulder, and drove herself through the empty space above the Snare. Fire streamed behind her in a long mane. Below, Mei cut low, gold skimming the paving; above, Lupa's descending point carried a red dawn into the cage.

Between them, Cartethyia grew.

The small knight's upward leap continued in the body of the Blessed Maiden. Crystal thorns shone against the furnace dark. Her great blade came down through the interval Lupa left, and a wind broad enough to bow every hanging chain took the broken constructs sideways, away from the galleries.

“Thou shalt answer for each hand laid upon them,” Fleurdelys said, with enough solemnity to address a kingdom.

Then, in exactly the same deep voice: “Cia, I need the next beat a little sooner.”

Ciaccona gave it to her.

Nyx's feather blades struck the falling debris, changing its course one piece at a time. Chisa opened her shears beneath the Snare's advancing legs. For an instant the floor held a red seam. Mei stepped onto its near edge; Lupa landed on its far one; Chisa drew her hands apart, and the seam carried their two strokes together beneath the machine's breast.

Gold and fire met.

Nyx drove her lance into the meeting point.

The blue line crossed the chamber with the violence of lightning taught to keep an appointment. Brass burst outward. Nine shadows tore from the walls. The upper cage opened from floor to crown, and for a single splendid instant the Myriad Snare stood full of stars.

Yangyang felt the pressure leave her chest.

She drew the first whole breath she had taken since dawn.

Beyond the wreckage, the moon turned.

The great blue lance emerged from a doorway on the opposite wall and struck an empty furnace.

Its doors blew outward. White heat filled the flue. The brass cage they had split fell apart around a hollow space, and another cage appeared three paces to its left, enclosing the same untouched crystal.

Lupa landed hard enough to crack the paving.

“I felt that go through,” she said. “Tell me where it went afterward.”

“Somewhere it didn't mind losing,” Yangyang answered.

She could see it now because they had given her time to look. Each genuine piece of ruin remained where it fell. Beneath those losses, the barrier turned the last reach aside. A corridor received a sword. An empty chamber accepted fire. The crystal hid behind a reflection while the blow completed itself elsewhere.

Mei looked at the ruined furnace, then at the galleries.

“Cia, keep everyone on the same floor. Amy, shorten the lance. We need to know where the far end comes out.”

The machine's mirrors opened.

At once the chamber contained seven Meis.

They moved with the same shoulder, the same small unevenness left by the wound beneath her coat. Lupa's ears flattened. Chisa turned her blade toward one set of approaching footsteps and stopped; the floor was offering her those too.

The real Mei put her hand behind her without looking.

Lupa caught it. Her palm was hot and rough. She drew Mei close enough for their shoulders to touch, then pressed the spear haft into her other hand.

“Keep hold until I tell you. I am getting very tired of this place borrowing you.”

They moved together. Ciaccona's measure marked the ground they actually occupied. Chisa cut only where their passing weight confirmed it. Fleurdelys swept a whole rank of false women apart and found a genuine blade waiting inside the last.

She caught it against her own.

Nyx's lance pierced the joint over her shoulder. Lupa ran up the lowered shaft; Mei came beneath it; the two champions crossed in a red-and-gold wheel that stripped the Snare's left flank to its struts.

For the first time, the spindle missed a turn.

On the gallery, a man's restraint dimmed.

Yangyang called to him. He raised his head. She sent the little air she could gather beneath his fingers, guiding them toward the loosened clasp.

He had nearly reached it when the Snare opened a door at his back.

Through it came the gold of Mei's next stroke.

Mei saw her own light returning toward the captive. She dragged her sword downward so sharply that the old wound pulled beneath her ribs. Gold broke against the lower rail. Fleurdelys caught the falling pieces over the man's head.

The Snare's mirrors shone.

It had found a more useful place for their strength to go.

***

The Wind came through a drain no wider than a child's wrist.

Above the mountain there had been mist, gullies, the long broken line of the road. Here the air passed through hot metal and acquired the taste of old fear. She held the Prism before her with more care than strength, letting its answer turn her at each junction.

Once the passage offered her Chixia's voice.

She stopped.

The voice asked her to come quickly. It came again, without the speaker needing breath. Farther down, beyond two walls, somebody drew air badly through an injured chest.

The Wind went toward the breath.

She entered the chamber beneath a torn grille. Fire opened above her. Through it she saw Mei turn a falling blade away from a woman on the gallery; saw the daughter standing in white armour; saw the small musician holding the daughter's unguarded throat above the stone.

So many hands.

She had asked for them once, on a street of printers, beneath a red kite. Now they were here, and the girl she had spent a year teaching stood among them with her sword lowered and blood running into her glove.

Yangyang turned before the Wind reached her.

Across the chamber, Mei saw the arriving crystal. Her marked hand lifted. Gold answered the light, old recognition waking in the palm which had held so many things the world meant for it.

The Prism passed her.

Mei let her hand fall and turned to catch a striking arm instead.

Yangyang put out her own.

The Wind laid the Prism in it.

It was warm from Suisui's hands.

***

The nine arms stopped between one movement and the next.

A splinter hung beside Yangyang's face. Within it she could see the torn grain, bright where the metal had opened it. The blood at her wrist ceased falling.

The voice came from the small warmth in her palm.

“—Outrider.”

There was no room around it, no throne, no figure upon whom she might fix her eyes. Only moonlight under the closed fingers, and a courtesy sharpened by age.

“This was never meant for you to wield...”

Yangyang listened.

“Thus, I ask of you a vow.

“The moment you choose to defy the ordained course—

“You must be ready... to bear the cost, and—

“Never... never ever forsake your ████.”

The last word reached her as a gap.

She knew the shape of such losses. In Bookbinders' Row, there were trays in which no sort could be made to stand. Her mother's shell carried an inscription whose absences had travelled farther than its tide.

She closed her fingers more securely.

“I cannot hear the last word,” she said. “I will tell you what I mean to do. Then you may decide whether it is a vow you can accept.”

Something moved in the light, slowly, like a tail behind a screen.

“Your sister was equally particular.”

“Suisui gave you a vow?”

“Your sister refused a certainty. I offered your safe return in exchange for the Wind's continued existence. She left the offer where I put it.”

For a moment Yangyang could not feel the Prism.

Beyond the stillness, the Wind rested against her wrist, drawn thin by the journey. Willing, she knew. Willing before the price had finished being spoken. Yangyang could almost see the older shell turning in its cloth.

“And this is what my sister brought away instead?”

“A means to find you. Some shelter. A passage, if you answer. My word to her stands. You may take that passage and leave this chamber.”

Yangyang looked at the gallery.

The man who had reached for his clasp hung motionless with two fingers extended. Beside him, a woman had turned toward Mei's intercepted stroke. The fear in her face belonged to an instant which would resume as soon as Yangyang answered.

“Can the Prism carry a call back to the thing that made it?”

The light narrowed.

“If you can distinguish it from what has been woven through it.”

“And if that thing is another Prism?”

For the first time, the voice took time to answer.

“Then you must keep the two passages distinct until you have turned one into the other. Their powers are of the same origin. Your living frequency would be the point between them. Everything seeking a way through would find you there.”

Yangyang studied the suspended spindle.

“Until we close it.”

“If your companions can close it. You have no protection I can promise once you turn your sister's passage inward. The machine will have a road to you. You know something of what it puts upon its roads.”

She did. The cry that had entered her throat remained there even in the silence.

“Suisui asked you to let both of us live,” she said.

“She asked for the chance. You appear determined to use yours upon a battlefield.”

Yangyang looked at Mei. One arm was raised against an iron blade. The other had gone toward her own side, catching a pain she had not yet had time to acknowledge. Lupa's foot stood beside hers. Farther away, Ciaccona's hand protected Amy's head while the lute continued a note it could not finish here.

Her sister had wanted a door through which someone might still arrive.

Yangyang had chosen the sword at eight. She had wanted to be the person able to go out through that door when the calling began. The desire was still hers. So was the old, foolish conviction that if she could only go far enough, nobody else would have to follow.

In Liangyu's borrowed room, she had written that she was hurt and wanted to come home. She remembered watching her own name dry beneath the request. Yet when Muyu turned toward his machines, she had tried to finish the whole matter with a single stroke, before anyone could reach her.

The ink had been true. So had the wanting. Neither had steadied her hand when she most needed it.

Now people had followed. They stood all around her, hurt from the road.

“I will not promise you my life as though it were already yours to spend,” she said. “I mean to leave with them. I also mean to stop what is happening to the people above us. Tell me if the way I am asking for will make them pay for my attempt.”

“Only if you fail to separate their frequencies before the return closes.”

Yangyang let herself feel the fear of that.

She did not put it elsewhere. It belonged in the hand making the choice.

“Then I must hear them first. And I will need my friends.”

The light stirred again.

“You heard the artisan as well. Does your vow reach so far?”

In the ruins of Yuan Fortress, a young man had knelt amid the day's slaughter and discovered a use for the sound of people dying. Yangyang remembered the terror before the discovery, the relief after it. To have an answer large enough that one need never be helpless again.

She could understand how he had wanted it.

Her hand tightened about the crystal.

“I can pity him without putting anyone else in his hands. If I find him, I will stop him. I will remember that he was a person while I do it.”

The suspended splinter turned by the breadth of a hair.

“And the person holding the sword?”

Yangyang glanced at the Wind.

She thought of a bowl whose steam leaned toward an empty seat. Of the older woman setting down every piece of herself that seemed too heavy for a journey. Of calling her home and meaning that someone should be there to eat tomorrow.

“She is a person too,” she said, more quietly. “I have been slow to learn what follows from that. I will not make her an exception because she is useful.”

The warmth rested against her cut palm.

“You made that exception this morning.”

Yangyang looked at the blood on the floor. Four paces, ending at a wall.

“Yes,” she said. “That is why I am going to tell them what I am asking the Prism to do. I don't trust myself to remember the whole promise while someone is crying out.”

“Then let us see what you remember with company, Outrider.”

Time returned.

The splinter touched her cheek and fell.

***

Yangyang nearly dropped the Prism with it.

The chamber entered her hearing in layers: the brass cage's low pulse; the finer chime within; the spindle's borrowed chorus; beneath all three, a man's teeth striking together as he tried to stop them. Nothing in her had become stronger. She still stood on an injured leg. Her sword still shook. The Prism gave the sounds different roads, and she had to choose which to follow.

The Wind pressed beneath her elbow.

The old correction. Half an inch, inward.

Yangyang lowered the point of her sword.

“Mei, I think I can make it catch itself.”

Mei drove the attacking arm aside and came within reach. The gold at her sword's edge thinned as she held it steady.

“Tell us where to stand.”

“Its threads follow whatever answers them. Muyu has made the people answer, and the Prism keeps finding new ways to reach them. I can send its own call back along those ways. But you must get the people clear before I close the return.”

Another hook rose. Lupa broke it across her knee before its point cleared the floor.

“Then we'll do that,” she said. “Where does this put you?”

“Between the two Prisms. It will know exactly where I am.” Yangyang swallowed. “I would like you close enough to do something about that.”

Lupa's ears lifted.

“That is the first sensible arrangement I've heard in this room.”

The Wind moved up Yangyang's sleeve toward the crystal. For an instant the light opened to receive her, finding in the older woman a likeness of the younger one, a familiar beginning worn thin by another world.

Yangyang turned the facet away.

“Stay on this side, shijie. I need you to help me hold it level.”

The cold hesitated.

“I know you can go farther,” Yangyang whispered. “I am asking you to stay where I can follow.”

The Wind settled around her wrist.

Together they tilted the Prism.

Its first clear beam fell on the captive woman's throat.

She gasped. At once the Snare repeated the gasp through every mirror, filling the room with women who needed help. Yangyang's attention scattered toward them. A hook passed Lupa's spear and tore the cloth above her shoulder before Mei cut it away.

Yangyang shut her eyes.

The repeated gasp had an end. The woman was still trying to breathe after it.

In the mill yard, Yangyang had stopped between the phrases of Liangyu's song because her lungs would hold no more. A dead man had used that pause to finish speaking. She remembered the shame of having so little breath, and the sound that had reached her when she ceased filling the silence herself.

She let the Prism's light rest where it was. She waited through the copied cry.

“Will you lift one finger for me?” Yangyang asked. “I can't make out your hand from here. Any finger you can move.”

Nothing happened in the mirrors.

The real woman moved her thumb.

Yangyang turned the Prism a fraction. The woman's frequency emerged from the borrowed chorus: rough, uneven, living. The light along her restraint became a single visible line.

“Chisa, there. At the rail, below her left hand.”

The shears closed.

The false women vanished. Fleurdelys lifted the real one out of the loosened bands and passed her down into a room Ciaccona's music had laid behind the entrance pillars. Two men freed from the lower workshops waited there. One had torn his own sleeve into dressings; the other held a cup with both hands until he found someone able to drink.

The Snare struck toward Yangyang.

Nyx caught three arms against her lance. Lupa burned the fourth at its hinge. Mei took the fifth, turned with it, and sent its broken length into the gear that fed replacement limbs from below.

For a moment all the hidden machinery screamed together.

Yangyang found the next breath.

There was no grand sweep by which she could free the gallery. Each person required another small distinction: a swallowed word; a shoulder shifting under a band; the effort to turn toward a voice after the recorded cry had ended. Ciaccona kept their answers from being lost under the steel. Chisa severed where Yangyang showed her. Fleurdelys carried the people down, her sword held broadside against the blows that followed them.

The machine learned to strike during Yangyang's questions.

The pack learned the length of her listening.

When she drew breath, Lupa came forward. When her hand steadied, Mei moved beneath the next arm. When she spoke, Nyx's feather blades crossed above her head, a white fan shutting against the rain of knives.

Once the light found a man who gave no answer.

Yangyang waited beyond the time the others had needed. She could hear only the pattern already taken from him, repeating obediently into the cage.

“There isn't a living reply,” she said.

Her voice almost went with it.

Fleurdelys bowed her head. She carried him down with the same care as the others, and Ciaccona let the music grow quiet enough for his neighbour to say his name.

Yangyang kept listening.

By the last restraint, the crystal was slippery in her hand. The Wind had drawn a cool band around her wrist and could scarcely keep it whole. Along the floor, every fallen strip of metal pointed toward the two of them.

The Myriad Snare had stopped pretending to look elsewhere.

Nyx drove her lance through the final spindle support and locked the shaft against a loading frame. Her knees bent. The white armour settled into a brace, holding the torn machinery above the cleared gallery.

Then the light left her eyes.

Against Ciaccona's arm, Amy took a ragged breath.

Twenty-four seconds where she had been. More than seven minutes where her body waited.

She caught the bard's sleeve before trying to sit up.

“Tell me how many are still above us. I couldn't see past the spindle at the end.”

“Carte has the last one. Stay in my lap until your legs remember their occupation.”

Amy looked across the chamber at the two Prisms. One hung inside the machine. The other shook in Yangyang's hand. Between them, strands of light had begun to gather like filings about a needle.

“It still has the stored voices,” she said. “Taking the people out won't empty it.”

“I can hear them,” Yangyang answered. “I need you to close the spare passages when it turns. Otherwise it will send the return through another workshop.”

Amy looked at the service housing beneath the loading frame, then at her own knees.

“Cia, I am about to be inconvenient in a new direction. Will you help me get to those controls?”

Ciaccona rose with an arm around her waist.

“I have been waiting years for you to discover new directions.”

***

At Xuanfang Hold, a great bow of moonlight opened over the gate.

Iuno loosed along the passage. The arrow caught the damaged guardian's falling weapon and pinned it against the arch. Wardens moved beneath the held shaft. Suisui kept the injured man's hand between hers as they drew him clear.

He apologized for the strength of his grip.

She told him he could keep it until they put him down.

The western sky brightened once, far beyond the gate's smoke. Suisui looked up too quickly. A pale thread ran from the shells inside Yangyang's coat toward the mountain, then disappeared.

Her hands began to shake.

She had told Hsin she could live through the uncertainty. Here it was, arriving without anything to distinguish hope from warning.

Iuno came down beside her. Her moonwheel scraped the wall; she no longer had the strength to make the landing elegant.

“I can't tell you what that was,” she said. “I can stay beside you while we wait for something we can understand.”

Suisui nodded without looking away from the mountains.

Then the man on the stretcher asked where they were taking him. She bent toward him and answered.

***

Yangyang held Suisui's light before the moon in the cage.

When the Snare called, she let the second Prism receive it.

When it reached for an answer, she turned the open facet toward its source.

The first returning thread missed. A mirror caught it and laid a false Yangyang across the floor, her wrist open, the crystal broken beside her hand. The Snare's hooks descended upon the image. At the same moment, the pain of that opened wrist ran through Yangyang's own.

She cried out and nearly lost the angle.

The Wind caught the crystal's lower edge.

There had been a shore at which every part of her had wanted to go on reaching. There had been a hand which could not receive her. She remembered the coming apart. Now the light offered another opening, wide enough to enter, and beyond it lay enough of Yangyang's pain to make staying seem unbearable.

She stayed at the wrist.

Yangyang felt the small cold weight. She found the pressure of her own fingers around the Prism, the whole skin beneath the pain, the floor beneath her boots.

“It showed me the wound,” she said, breathing hard. “I still have my hand. Please keep talking to me.”

“Your left foot is on my boot,” Mei said. “You can leave it there if that helps. I've had much worse arrangements today.”

Lupa drove her spear through an approaching knee. Fire rushed up the leg.

“I'm beside your right shoulder. The thing touching your back is my arm. Everything sharp in front of you is going to have to discuss it with me.”

Yangyang turned the Prism again.

This time she listened past the stored cries to the chime that sent them outward. It was faint beneath the chorus, but exact: the same small interval as the crystal in her hand. Two pieces of one moon, each capable of recognizing the other through a hundred painted skies.

The Wind held the angle.

The returning thread entered the cage.

An arm swung inward.

Its hook caught another arm at the joint.

Amy looked up from the controls.

“It found itself. Yangyang, keep that angle!”

The Snare opened nine doors to escape the return.

Within each door shone its own crystal.

There was no new illusion for it to penetrate. The call was genuine; the answer came from its actual source. Yangyang had given the seeking thread a road home. Every turn the Snare made to catch the answer carried another length of itself through its own barrier.

For an instant the whole machine resembled a spider drawing a web about its heart.

Then it began to tear the web apart.

Its strength was terrible. One trapped arm ripped free, leaving half its casing in the hook. A second smashed the mirror holding its reflection. The nine doors multiplied. Along the gallery, empty restraints flared as the Snare tried to send its hunger outward again.

“Now,” Yangyang said. “Please close them now.”

Ciaccona struck the strings.

Her Sylphs rose from the floor in a widening spiral. The song she had kept low for the captives opened to its full voice, filling the cleared chamber from foundation to torn roof. For one measure every real threshold rang in the same key. The false doors shivered against them, outlined by their failure to answer.

Chisa ran her shears through that difference.

She cut from left to right in a long, level stroke. Red seams crossed the chamber. Three false passages folded shut, leaving blank plaster where their depths had been.

At the controls, Amy braced her heel and hauled the first isolating lever down. Muyu had fitted a stop beneath it. She broke the stop with a loose iron pin and hauled again.

The empty restraints went dark.

“I've closed the galleries. Ma, the lower bypass is opening beneath you!”

Mei's sword turned black along one edge.

She cut downward. Havoc entered the service trench, a wing of darkness passing under the floor, and sheared through the diverted channel as it opened. The returning light struck the cut and flew back into the Snare.

Lupa met it there.

Her spear entered the machine's lowest turning ring. Fire ran about the circumference, red becoming white, and the ring sagged around the shaft. Above her, Mei brought Spectro down through the exposed upper gear. Gold and flame crossed a second time, closer now, with the same certainty as two hands closing a clasp.

The Myriad Snare tried to rise.

Fleurdelys descended upon it.

Her sword struck through the central bearing. Wind spread from the blow in a vast pale circle, lifting every hanging chain, flattening the smoke against the roof. For a moment her hair and the broken banners streamed straight outward, and the whole mountain seemed to have drawn breath beneath her feet.

“Remain where thou hast put thyself,” she said.

The bearing split.

The Snare fell into its own returning threads.

Amy dragged the second lever over. Nyx's braced lance held the spindle while the loading frame's great locking teeth engaged beneath it. One tooth broke. The next held; the next caught; the fourth went home with a sound that Yangyang felt in her knees.

Still the moon turned.

The barrier had one passage left.

It opened around Yangyang's hand.

For an instant she saw the chamber from inside the cage. Every thread lay within reach. Every cry had a place. She understood the artisan's desire with a clarity more frightening than hatred: from here, he had believed he could make the whole world answer.

The passage tightened at her wrist.

Yangyang wanted to pull. She knew, with the certainty of the drowning, how much strength remained in her shoulders and how uselessly she could spend it.

Instead she looked toward Chisa.

“It has my hand. The return is holding on its own now. Cut me free.”

Chisa placed one blade beneath the light, where Yangyang's pulse stopped and the passage continued. The Wind withdrew to the sleeve. Mei's arm came around Yangyang's waist.

The shears closed.

The road parted.

Inside the Snare, its Prism sent out another command to seize the thing answering it.

Every thread tightened around the cage.

Brass groaned. The remaining hooks locked against their own breast. Behind the barrier, the little moon vanished beneath layer after layer of its returning light, until the machine held its own heart as helplessly as it had held the people on the gallery.

Fleurdelys kept her blade in the broken bearing while Chisa secured the severed bypasses. Amy cut the power driving the extraction spindles. Ciaccona listened until the stored cries ceased being driven through the machine, then let the great measure descend, softly, into silence.

What remained was a ruined machine, bound in its own enchantment and pinned beneath several tonnes of ordinary iron.

Far away, toward Xuanfang Hold, a gun sounded.

The mountain had other wounds.

***

Yangyang tried to lower the Prism carefully.

Her fingers opened all at once.

It struck the floor beside her boot, still gleaming. She went after it, knees folding beneath her, and Mei caught her before her cheek reached the stone.

The catching was less graceful than the swordwork. Mei went down on one knee, hissed through her teeth, adjusted the arm beneath Yangyang's back, and sat where the floor was least covered in sharp things.

Her other hand settled at Yangyang's waist. For a moment, while they found a position that spared the dressing, Yangyang was held close enough to feel Mei's breath against her temple. Her hat struck Mei's chin. Mei caught the brim, eased it back, and looked down at her.

Yangyang had meant to thank her.

At the cake shop, she had found a folded collar to put straight. Here, Mei's collar was torn, her hair had come loose, and Yangyang could not think of anything to do with her hands except hold on.

“There we are,” Mei said. “I think I've got the important parts. Tell me if anything is bent the wrong way.”

Yangyang's head lay in the bend of her elbow.

For a while the world was made of very near things: a torn collar; a strand of dark hair touching her mouth; the warmth at the side of Mei's throat. Her fingers caught in the fabric above Mei's breast. She loosened them, afraid of pulling something that hurt, and Mei covered the hand with her own.

“You can hold on there. You're clear of the wound.”

Yangyang kept her hand where it was. Beneath it she could feel the hurried beating of Mei's heart.

At tea she had asked for a minute in these arms. She remembered setting down the bag between them, moving her feet, trying again. Now there was nothing to arrange. She had been caught, and her body had accepted the warmth before she could decide how much of her longing to let it show.

The Wind slipped from her sleeve.

She had strength enough to move one loose thread across Yangyang's wrist. Then even that stopped. The cold settled between the two women, sheltered by their bodies, and remained.

“I...” Yangyang began.

Mei bent closer.

Yangyang looked at the empty air beneath her chin.

“It was you. You brought them here.”

The thread moved once.

There had been a kite above a city without wind. A letter lifted from its paper. Chixia carried down a gorge; a little light carried west. So much travelling, to arrive at this small patch of floor where nobody had room to be alone.

Yangyang lifted her eyes to Mei.

Behind that familiar face, a mirror opened and closed. She knew the machine had stopped. Her hearing had not finished with it; somewhere inside her, the copied cry began once more.

“This isn't a dream, is it?”

The question embarrassed her as soon as she heard it. She tried to look away and found that Mei's sleeve occupied most of the available world.

“If it is, it's far too...”

Mei took her hand. She placed it against her own cheek and held it there, warm over the bandage, her thumb resting beside the cut.

“Hey, relax. Feel that? I'm right here.”

Yangyang's fingertips moved against her skin. Mei's eyes closed briefly at the touch. When they opened, Yangyang was still looking at her mouth.

She lifted her gaze too late to pretend she had been looking elsewhere.

“Yes... You're just much earlier than I expected.”

“And yet, much later than I'd hoped.”

Yangyang's thumb had come to rest beside the corner of Mei's mouth. Mei drew a small breath against it. For a moment neither moved; then her fingers shifted gently around Yangyang's wrist, supporting it where the effort of keeping it raised had begun to hurt.

Yangyang remembered the courtyard, the cooling tea, Iuno's name spoken while their hands were joined. She had heard Mei clearly that afternoon. Being held like this did nothing to make her want less.

Mei lowered their hands together to Yangyang's lap. She kept her fingers loosely around hers.

“Is this comfortable?” she asked.

Yangyang nodded, then made herself answer.

“Very. I'm afraid I may be taking some time to recover for reasons that aren't entirely the battle.”

The colour reached Mei's cheeks. Her thumb stilled against Yangyang's hand, and a smile came slowly, as though she was taking care with that too.

“You can have a little time. I'm very glad to be sitting here with you.”

Yangyang looked down at their hands. The heat beneath her hat had become quite unreasonable.

“I had thought,” Yangyang said, then had to begin again, “that when I saw you, I would have time to make myself a little more presentable.”

Mei brushed a fleck of brass from her fringe. Her fingers stopped at the loosened ribbon beside Yangyang's ear.

“You wore this one to tea.”

“I didn't expect you to remember which it was.”

“I was trying to find a way to ask whether I could touch it. I had quite a long time to look.”

Yangyang's eyes rose to hers.

“You may put it straight, if you like.”

Mei eased the twisted ribbon flat. A strand of hair had caught beneath it; she freed that too, slowly, without tugging. Yangyang held still beneath her fingers with a concentration she had not needed for the sword.

When Mei had finished, Yangyang reached for her hand again. Mei settled it into hers.

“There. I liked seeing you in it then. I like seeing you now.”

Yangyang's mouth trembled.

Behind Mei, the dark mouth of Muyu's escape passage stood open at last. No man waited within it. The lower lift had gone; dust had settled across the far landing before the pack entered the chamber.

“I'm sorry. I couldn't stop him.”

Mei's thumb stilled against her hand.

“No, **we** couldn't stop him.”

She let Yangyang look at the empty passage for as long as she needed.

“He got away. We have people to bring out, and we know more than we did when we came in. When we go after him, you'll have company.”

Yangyang looked back at her.

“I would like that.”

“I'd like that too. And I would still like the walk you told me about, past the second bridge. When you're well enough, and when you want company.”

Yangyang's fingers moved within hers, finding the fit they had learned over tea.

“I haven't changed my mind about the company.”

The Wind moved weakly against their joined hands.

Mei glanced down.

“You too. There is no exemption for people who can fit under doors.”

The loose thread drew itself across her knuckle with the faint severity of a correction.

***

Around them, the pack began discovering its injuries.

Lupa found the tear above Yangyang's shoulder, decided it could wait a minute, and went to examine Chisa's hands. Chisa submitted to the inspection while pointing out the burn beneath Lupa's glove. They arrived at a mutual agreement involving water, two strips of cloth, and no convincing attempt by either woman to claim it did not hurt.

Amy checked Nyx's braced lance, secured the controls, and sat down abruptly when her legs reached the end of their patience. Cartethyia, small again, put a hand beneath her elbow before she could object. Together they counted the people by the entrance and sent the strongest of the freed workers for the others waiting in the lower rooms.

Ciaccona came back from the service passage with the Prism wrapped in a clean scrap of lining. She had made certain it lay well away from the Snare before collecting it. Its light showed softly through the cloth.

She stopped at a distance which demonstrated considerable experience of Mei.

Mei looked up.

Ciaccona shook her head.

“Thank goodness Iuno isn't here....” she said under her breath.

Mei heard. The faint colour she had nearly lost returned at once. Yangyang became very interested in the ribbon lying against her shoulder.

Ciaccona uncapped the flask with the air of a woman who had said nothing requiring a reply.

“If you ask me whether you can carry her all the way to the Hold, I shall have Lupa answer. Consider how pleasant this minute is before you spoil it.”

“I was going to ask for water.”

“How fortunate that you have begun there.”

She set the flask within reach and retreated before the golden eyes could acquire a second request. Cartethyia joined her. The bard checked the shallow cut along the knight's jaw, brushing the dust away with a tenderness which made her earlier retreat somewhat less authoritative.

Yangyang watched them, then remembered something with such suddenness that she tried to rise.

“Oh! Did you see Chixia in Jinzhou?”

Mei steadied her beneath the shoulders.

“She's fine. Baizhi is looking after her.”

“Thank goodness.”

The words took the last firmness from her. She settled against Mei's arm, eyes closed, and let the news reach all the places that had continued waiting on the broken landing.

Chixia in a bed. Baizhi beside it. A cup brought close enough to drink.

The Wind cooled the wetness at her temple.

Mei waited until Yangyang opened her eyes again.

“Next time, don't throw yourself into danger like that.”

Yangyang looked at the tear in Mei's coat, at the dressing visible beneath it. She could feel how carefully Mei was breathing around the weight in her arms.

The rest of the pack allowed the sentence a generous amount of time in which to discover itself.

Amy was the first to lose patience.

“...You're one to talk!”

Mei closed her eyes.

“I had hoped for another thirty seconds.”

“You had a piece of our transport through your stomach this morning, Ma. I helped take it out. You are currently sitting on a floor pretending that holding another person over the same place is comfortable.”

Yangyang's head lifted.

Mei opened her eyes again, caught fairly and completely.

“It has closed,” she said. “It still hurts. I should have told you before you had to find that out from my daughter.”

Amy came to sit beside them.

“You can both have the lecture after we get somewhere with chairs. At present I would settle for neither of you making the other one worse.”

Yangyang looked from daughter to mother. A laugh escaped her, small and unsteady. She put a hand against her ribs and laughed again, more carefully, while Mei's face softened at the sound.

“Then perhaps,” Yangyang said, “you could hold me a little higher. It would be better for your dressing.”

Amy looked at her.

Then at Mei.

“I see the difficulty is spreading.”

Lupa's tail swept once behind her.

Mei adjusted her arm. Yangyang's cheek came to rest against her shoulder, which was precisely where the request had been intended to put it.

For a little while, nobody asked either of them to improve.

***

The report from the gate arrived as they were binding the last wound.

The Snare's silence had cleared enough interference for Amy's repaired receiver to catch a Warden's voice. Xuanfang Hold still stood. Liangyu had the civilians behind the inner barricade. A foreign woman with a moonwheel was helping hold the entrance, and another woman wished to know whether anyone had found Outrider Yangyang.

Yangyang reached for the receiver.

Her hand shook. Mei held it steady beneath hers.

“This is Yangyang,” she said. “Please tell Suisui I have been found. The Wind is with us. We are both coming back.”

She paused, feeling the cold tucked beneath her collar.

“And tell her I would like my coat.”

Static passed through the receiver. Then, far away, somebody laughed with a sound perilously close to weeping.

They prepared the road before rising to take it. Amy marked the isolated controls for the Wardens; Chisa checked the bindings about the Snare; Ciaccona gave the freed workers a tune by which to keep together in the passages. Those who could walk gathered beside those who needed carrying. Cartethyia found poles for a litter. Lupa tested each before allowing anyone to lie upon it.

Mei stood first, with Amy's hand at her elbow.

Then she and Lupa helped Yangyang to her feet.

The floor tipped once. Yangyang waited. When it steadied, she sheathed her sword and settled her weight honestly between the two women. The Wind lay quiet within her collar. Ciaccona carried the wrapped Prism; through the cloth, its light was no larger than a lamp seen from the road.

At the threshold, morning entered through the broken roof. It touched the bandage at Yangyang's wrist, the soot on Mei's cheek, the white edge of a feather blade folded for the journey.

Beyond the western crags, someone was keeping a coat warm.

One of the people by the doorway stumbled. Cold stirred at Yangyang's throat; her own hand had already left Mei's sleeve.

Cartethyia reached him first.

Yangyang watched the knight settle his arm across her shoulders. Then she brought her hand back. Beneath her collar, the Wind took a little longer to grow still.

They turned toward Xuanfang Hold. For this part of the road, there were other hands.
