# Neon Green
*A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

---

## I.

The bikes came over the ridge at four in the afternoon with the light going gold and low across the snowfield, and there it was: blue door, stone chimney, wisteria bare against the wall and waiting for a season it had already had twice.

Nine engines cut in a ragged sequence. The quiet came down like a lid.

"That's it?" said Rebecca.

"Blue door's ours."

"That's a *shack*."

"It's a cabin."

"It's four walls and a *hat*." She kicked her stand down and stood in the stirrups of her seat to look at it properly, pink hair flat under the helmet she had refused for two hundred kilometers and then worn for the last forty without comment. "Where's the rest of it?"

"That's the whole building."

"So where do the *other* people live?"

"There are no other people," said Chisa.

Rebecca turned a slow full circle on the ridge. Snowfield, glacier, the long stagger of the frostlands going white and blue and then dark blue, and one chimney, and no light anywhere else in any direction to the curve of the world.

"Okay," she said. "That's messed up. That's actually messed up. You people are *insane*."

Amy was watching the others.

It took about half a second and she caught all four of them.

Iuno's hand stayed on the throttle two beats after the engine died. Ciaccona looked at the door and then very deliberately at the roofline instead. Cartethyia's smile arrived on schedule and arrived from somewhere. Lupa got off her bike and walked eleven steps toward the porch and stopped, and stood there, and then went and started unstrapping cargo that could have waited an hour.

Half a second. All four of them. Then they buried it, cleanly, the way people bury things they have practiced burying, and Ciaccona said something loud about the acoustics and Cartethyia laughed at it and the moment closed over.

Amy took her helmet off and shook her hair out and filed the whole thing somewhere careful.

"Right," she said. "Who's carrying the finch?"

The finch was four feet tall, plush, and had ridden two hundred kilometers strapped to Cartethyia's back like a passenger.

"I'll carry the finch," said Lynae.

"And the cake tin."

"I'll carry the finch and the cake tin, and if the cake tin has a single crack in it I'll be found blameless by any reasonable court."

---

## II.

The cabin took nine people the way a coat takes a ninth button.

Rebecca went through it in four minutes with the tactical thoroughness of somebody who had grown up in a city where a room you had not personally cleared was a room that could kill you. Kitchen. Stairs. Two bedrooms up. The alcove. Bathroom, which produced an eleven-second silence and then a shout of "*It's got a WINDOW*" that nobody entirely understood.

Then the door at the end of the hall.

"What's in here?"

Nobody answered.

She opened it.

The main room: long, low, the good stove, the wide window facing the lake, the big table under it. Cold. Clean. Somebody had swept it at some point in the last year and shut the door on it.

"This is the best room in the building."

Still nobody answered.

Rebecca stuck her head back into the hall and found five people arranged in the specific geometry of a group that had all decided at once to be interested in something else.

"...Okay," she said.

She went back in.

---

Room assignments took forty minutes because Iuno had a plan and Rebecca had a different one and Lynae had opinions about sightlines that turned out to be identical to Lupa's.

"You and me," said Rebecca, pointing at Lynae. "Alcove."

"Alcove's got the quilts."

"Alcove's got *one door*. I'm not sleeping in a room with a window and a door and a stairwell, that's three problems."

"There's nothing out there," said Amy.

"That's *worse*."

Cartethyia set the finch upright in the corner of the alcove, where it stood a head taller than she did, facing outward.

"Sentry," she said.

Rebecca looked at it for a while.

"Yeah," she said. "Okay. That's actually — yeah. Fine. That works."

---

At half past seven Rebecca came into the kitchen holding her deck and said, "Is there a relay out here?"

"There's a repeater on the ridge," said Amy. "One bar. There's a chair."

"So I can hit the city."

"You can hit Lahai-Roi, yeah. Mornye's on nights this week."

"Cool." Rebecca turned the deck over in her hands. "What about — " She stopped. Started again, lighter. "The thing. The rift. Is there a way to, like."

The kitchen kept going: Chisa cutting, Iuno at the stove, Mei doing something structurally unsound to a jar lid.

"It's closed," said Mei.

"Yeah, no, I figured."

"I'm sorry."

"It's fine. It's — yeah." Rebecca shoved the deck in her pocket. "Lucy's got V and V's got Judy and the badlands and whatever. That's — that's the good ending, right? That's the whole thing. So."

"So."

"So what's for dinner, because I've been smelling that for an hour and if it's synth I'm walking back to the city."

Lupa, from the doorway, watching her with an expression that had gone very still somewhere around *is there a way to*, said, "It's a stew. It's got real meat in it."

"*Real*—"

"Sit down."

---

## III.

Mei brought the record and the drawing out of her bag after dinner.

The record went on the player and stayed there, which took eleven seconds and no ceremony at all.

The drawing took longer.

She got it up on the wall with four tacks in the same four holes it had come out of, and stood back, and there it was: a hero, a cape, a galactic empire rendered as a rectangle with angry eyes, a colossal pair of feet.

"The cape's not finished," said Rebecca.

"It was in progress."

"For how long?"

"About twenty years."

"That's a *long* in progress." Rebecca leaned in. "It's good, though. The feet are wrong but the feet are meant to be wrong, right? The feet are a *choice*."

Amy, on the floor, went very red and very pleased.

"They're heroic boats."

"They're *sick*." Rebecca dug in a jacket pocket and came out with a fistful of paint markers, held together with a hair tie, and fanned them out like cards. "Okay so. What color."

"There was a plan," said Iuno.

"Neon green," said Mei.

"*Neon green*," said Amy, at the same moment.

Rebecca pulled one out and popped the cap with her teeth and looked at it, and then at the wall, and then at Amy.

"You do it."

"I was nine when I—"

"You're the artist. It's your wall." She held it out, blunt end first. "I don't put my name on other people's work. That's — that's just, like, a rule."

Amy took the marker.

The cape went neon green in about ninety seconds. It was, by any reasonable standard, hideous. It was the exact acid green of a Night City billboard at three in the morning and it sat on a nine-year-old's crayon drawing in a wooden cabin in the frostlands like a transplanted organ that had taken.

Ciaccona was the first to say anything.

"That is *appalling*."

"That's the best thing in this house," said Lynae.

"It's *luminous*—"

"It's staying," said Amy, capping the pen. "It's staying forever. If this cabin burns down I'm going back in for that wall."

Rebecca, meanwhile, had drifted three feet along and started drawing on a blank patch beside it.

"Rebecca."

"You said it's a gallery."

"Nobody said—"

"There's like nine drawings up here, that's a gallery." She kept going. "I'm putting my crew up. That's Maine, that's Dorio, that's Pilar, that's Kiwi, that's — " She stopped, and did another one, smaller, off to the side. "That's David. He was taller than that but I can't do proportions."

Nobody moved.

Cartethyia, quietly, went and got a chair so Rebecca could reach higher.

"You've got Lucy," said Chisa.

"Lucy's alive."

"Put her up anyway."

Rebecca looked at her.

Then she put Lucy up, in white, right at the front, and drew a moon over her head about the size of a coin.

"Right," she said, capping the marker. "That's done. Somebody said there was a game."

---

## IV.

The lake froze eleven inches deep and Lupa took Rebecca out on it at nine in the morning with a hand auger, two lines, and a bucket.

Chisa had confiscated the C-4 at the packing stage and had not returned it.

"This is the stupidest thing I've ever done."

"You sat in a hole for four hours yesterday."

"That was a *bath*. That was a bath with a window."

"You screamed."

"There was a *bird*."

They drilled two holes eleven feet apart, because Lupa had views about spacing, and sat on upturned crates in the cold with two lines in the water and nothing whatsoever to say to each other.

Forty minutes.

"So this is it," said Rebecca.

"Mm."

"We just sit here."

"Until something happens."

"How long's that?"

"Depends on the fish."

Rebecca stared at her line.

"That's — okay, in Night City, if you sat still for forty minutes, four different people would try to sell you something and one of them would try to take your kidney."

"I know."

"You don't *know*, you've got — " Rebecca gestured at the entire frostlands with one arm. "You've got *this*. You've got nothing. It's so quiet out here I can hear my own pump."

"Yeah."

"How do you sleep?"

"Badly," said Lupa. "First two months I slept in the hall."

Rebecca turned her head.

"...Huh."

They sat.

At the hour mark Rebecca caught something and made a noise that scattered birds two kilometers away, and Lupa had to physically take the line off her before she pulled the fish, the rod, and herself through the ice.

"Line's live."

"THERE'S A *FISH*—"

"Both hands. Under the—" Lupa got a grip on her jacket. "Under the *gills*, you'll drop it—"

"IT'S MOVING—"

"That's the *point*—"

They got it in the bucket. Rebecca sat back down on her crate breathing hard and looked at it with an expression that had nothing whatsoever cynical in it.

"That's a real fish," she said.

"Caught it yourself, too."

"I've never — " She stopped. "They sell fish in the city. In a tube."

Lupa let that lie there for a while.

Then she said, "You threw yourself at Smasher three times."

"So?"

"You did it when Cartethyia was behind you. Then when Lucy was behind you. Then when *I* was behind you, which was a waste, because I heal."

"So does the fish, probably," said Rebecca. "What's your point?"

"You've done it once already. All the way. At that tower." Lupa reeled in and re-baited without looking up. "It took. You're not doing it again with my people behind you."

Rebecca laughed at that, sharp and surprised.

"That's rich," she said. "That is *rich*. You went through a *wall* for the coffee girl."

"That's different."

"That's the *same*, that's exactly the same, I've watched you do it four times and Chisa's got a *log*—"

"Chisa's got a what?"

"There's a log. On a sheet. Of everyone who went down and how long they were down for." Rebecca was grinning now, delighted, vicious. "It's *your handwriting*."

Lupa's ears went flat and stayed flat for a full minute.

"Fine," she said, eventually.

"Meaning?"

"Fine, we're the same. Shut up about it and hold the line."

---

## V.

Iuno set up in the main room at seven because Amy said the big table was better.

Amy said it exactly once, in the kitchen, without emphasis, while doing something else, and then went and got the chairs.

Nobody argued. There was no reason to argue. It was, factually, the best table in the building.

Cartethyia came in with the felt roll, and Wick, and the goats, and stopped in the doorway for a second and a half, and then came in and started setting up.

Ciaccona came in with the lute and put it on the stand by the window, which was where it had lived for four months, once, in a different year, and looked at it there, and sat down and said, "Right, which one of you's got my glossary."

Lupa came in last, walked to the chair with its back to the wall, and sat down in it with the air of a woman completing a maneuver.

Rebecca dropped into the seat opposite with both boots on the table.

"Off," said Iuno.

"It's a table."

"It's a *surface*, and there will be dice on it, and if a die goes off this table into the stove I will hold you responsible in ways you will find creative."

Rebecca took her boots off the table.

---

Character creation took ninety minutes and Rebecca's first question stopped the room.

"Can she die?"

Iuno looked up from the binder.

"Yes."

"For real? Like, gone?"

"There are three failed saves and a body." Iuno set her pen down. "There's a mechanism for bringing people back and I have removed it, because a table that can undo things stops caring about them by session four."

Rebecca thought about that for a while, and everybody let her.

"Good," she said.

"That's a strange thing to be pleased about."

"Otherwise what's the point." She pulled the sheet toward her. "Okay. Name."

"Go."

"Rebecca."

Lupa's head came up so fast her chair barked on the floor.

"*No.*"

"What?"

"You can't—"

"It's a different Rebecca," said Rebecca.

The room detonated.

Amy went off the back of her chair. Ciaccona put her whole head on the table. Lynae, who had been present for none of session one and had heard about all of it, made a sound like a kettle. Chisa said "oh, that's *beautiful*" with more open feeling than she had displayed in four months.

"THAT'S MY THING—"

"What's your thing?"

"That's *my* — Iuno, tell her—"

"It's an established precedent of this table," said Iuno, "and I ruled on it in session one, and I am bound by it."

"SHE STOLE IT—"

"She independently derived it," said Chisa.

"That's WORSE—"

"Spell it," said Iuno, pen ready.

Rebecca spelled it correctly.

Lupa sat back down like a woman whose house had been robbed.

---

Rebecca's build was a gunner, which surprised nobody, and she assembled it with a savagery of focus that surprised everybody. She read the pamphlets. All eleven. She asked about range brackets. She asked what happened if you shot into a crowd, and listened carefully to the answer, and wrote it down.

Then she asked the question that reorganized the evening.

"What do I do if somebody goes down."

"Not Loopa's the Mender," said Amy. "She—"

"What do *I* do."

Iuno turned a page.

"You can stabilize. It's a check. You'd have a poor chance of it."

"So I'd have to get to them."

"You'd have to reach them, yes."

"How far can I move in a round?"

"Thirty feet."

Rebecca wrote *30* on the back of her hand.

---

## VI.

The session started badly and got worse in the best possible way.

Iuno had written a bridge — a real one, with a river under it and a toll and a thing living beneath the third pier — and Rebecca shot the toll collector inside ninety seconds of meeting him.

"You *shot Kettle's cousin*—"

"He wanted eleven shells!"

"That's the *toll*—"

"That's a *scam*, that's a shakedown, that's — okay, where I'm from, if a guy in a booth wants eleven shells to cross a bridge, that guy owns the bridge and he owns the guy under the bridge and he's going to own you—"

"HE HAD A HAT—"

"They all have hats!"

"Roll initiative," said the High Priestess.

The room went up.

"THERE SHE IS—"

"That was *fast*, you didn't even take the—"

"She didn't do the *glasses*—"

"I have dispensed with the glasses," said the High Priestess, laying the d20 in the middle of the table where everybody could see it. "It was theatre and you've all seen it. Rebecca, darling. You have shot a municipal employee in front of nine witnesses and the thing under the third pier has *noticed*. Roll high or don't."

Rebecca stared at her.

"Who's this."

"That's her other one," said Mei, chin in her hand, already grinning.

"Is she always—"

"Only for the fights."

"That's *sick*," said Rebecca, with total sincerity, and rolled a three.

---

The thing under the pier came up at round two and put Ciaccona's duelist in the river.

It put Cartethyia's lamplighter down at round four, which nobody had expected, because in two sessions nothing had touched Wick, and Cartethyia said "oh," in a small voice, and looked at her felt doll.

And Rebecca moved thirty feet.

"I'm going to her."

"That's through the—"

"I know what it's through. I wrote it on my hand." Rebecca was already reaching for dice. "I'm going. Roll whatever you're rolling."

The High Priestess rolled it in the open. Rebecca took eighteen damage and had four left and got to Wick and made the check on a natural nineteen, and Cartethyia screamed and hit the table with both hands and the whole room came off its chairs.

"YES—"

"SHE GOT HER—"

"*Nineteen*—"

And Lupa, on her feet with everybody else, shouting with everybody else, stopped in the middle of it with an expression like a woman who has walked into a doorframe in the dark.

Chisa, beside her, said, "Mm."

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"You said *Mm*."

"That I did," Chisa agreed, and drank her tea.

---

At the end of round six Rebecca had one hit point and was standing over an unconscious lamplighter in the middle of a bridge, and the High Priestess, with visible pleasure, said, "It turns toward you," and Rebecca said, "Yeah, that's the idea," and the room made a noise.

And Lupa said, "Not Loopa's got a shield spell."

"You used it on—"

"I've got the second one. From the level." Lupa was already sliding her sheet forward. "It's on her. It goes on her."

"That's *twenty-five feet*, you'd have to—"

"It goes on her," said Lupa.

The High Priestess looked at Lupa for slightly longer than the moment required.

"Twenty-five feet and a spell," she said. "Granted."

---

They finished at half one with the thing under the pier dead, the toll collector's cousin's cousin owed a formal apology, and Bartolomeo Fen the wealthiest he had been in three sessions, having sold rope to the Aldecaldos-adjacent salvage crew that the High Priestess had invented on the spot and named, under duress, **Bucket**.

"You did it *again*—"

"There was a bucket by the stove."

"You did the SAME THING—"

"It's a family name," said Iuno, back in her own voice, putting the cap on her pen. "It's a regional variant."

---

## VII.

The room emptied by degrees, the way rooms do.

The stove had been going since six and had made the long room warm all the way to the corners for the first time in a very long while. The table was covered in dice and pamphlets and a felt goat in a sentry position and one enormous plush finch that had been carried in at midnight by Lynae for reasons she declined to explain.

Rebecca fell asleep in the chair with her boots back on the table. Nobody moved them.

Ciaccona played four bars of something at the window, badly, twice, and then correctly, and put the lute back on the stand and left it there.

Cartethyia went up with Wick under her arm and a doll in her pocket that she had found in a drawer that afternoon and had said nothing about, and would say nothing about, and had made three years ago for a girl who had been in this house and had not been in this house.

She had put it back in the drawer. She had closed the drawer. And then she had come down and set up her felt on the big table in the good light like a woman going to work.

Amy sat on the stairs with her chin on her knees, watching the last of it through the doorway.

Iuno came out with the binder under her arm and stopped when she saw her.

"You said the table was better," Iuno said.

"It is."

"It's four inches longer than the kitchen table and it has a wobble."

"It's got the good light."

Iuno stood there.

"Amy."

"Mm."

"How long have you had that planned?"

Amy shrugged with one shoulder, which was a gesture she had learned from her mother and had never been told about.

"Since the dorm," she said. "When Ma said 'cabin' and you all did the — " She made a small motion with her hand, a flinch about a quarter of an inch wide. "That."

"You saw that."

"Everybody's got a tell. Chisa taught me to look."

Iuno sat down on the step below her, which put them level, which was deliberate.

"I would have gone in eventually."

"Yeah. In about a year. Alone. At four in the morning, and you'd have sat in there by yourself and had a bad hour and never mentioned it to anybody, and it would've *worked*, kind of." Amy put her chin back on her knees. "This was faster."

"You used the cyborg."

"Point her at a door and she opens it."

"She has no idea."

"None whatsoever," said Amy, "and if you tell her I'll deny it, and Lupa will back me, because Lupa figured it out on the ice and hasn't said a word about it either."

Through the doorway, in the long warm room, Rebecca made a noise in her sleep and shifted, and the felt goat went over, and Chisa reached out without looking up from the dishes and stood it back on its feet.

"Thank you," said Iuno.

"Don't. It's weird when you do that."

"I'll do it once."

"Then that's the one," said Amy.

They sat on the stairs.

"The cape's disgusting," said Iuno.

"The cape is *magnificent*."

"It's the color of a *hangover*."

"It's the color of the place we all went and came back from, and it's on a picture I drew when I was nine, on that wall, in this house." Amy stood up and stretched until something in her back went. "So it's staying. Night, Iuno."

"Goodnight."

Amy went up two steps and stopped.

"Hey."

"Mm."

"Ma says you've been calling yourself her fiancée."

Iuno's spine went straight in a way that could have been used to check a wall for true.

"That is a *provisional* term—"

"Uh huh."

"It's under review."

"For *three weeks*, and I've heard you say it *twice*—"

"Go to bed, Amy."

"Under *review*—"

"*Bed.*"

Amy went up laughing, loudly, all the way, past the wall with the drawings on it — the hero with the boat-feet and the neon green cape, the pack behind her in six people's handwriting, and off to one side, in white paint marker, five figures and a small moon.

— fin.
