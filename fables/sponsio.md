# Sponsio
### *A "Lyre, Speak to Me" side story.*

> 王之臣有託其妻子於其友而之楚遊者，比其反也，則凍餒其妻子，則如之何？
>
> *"Suppose one of Your Majesty's ministers entrusted his wife and children to a friend and went travelling in Chu, and came home to find them cold and hungry. What should be done?"*
> — *Mencius*, 1B.6

> **REGISTRY OF PERSONS · LAHAI-ROI**
> *Notice of Outstanding Items. Reinstatement of a person recorded lost.*
> *Re: AEMEATH, Academy ward no. 734.*
>
> *The Registry is unable to issue papers until the following are regularised.*
>
> *1. Instrument of Wardship. Status: open. Never discharged. The original is required.*
> *2. Guardian of record. Signed on the instrument with a single character. No person of that mark appears in any register of this city.*
> *3. Sponsor of record (Form 7-C). Withheld under Privacy Provision 9(2). A copy of this notice has gone to the sponsor under seal.*
> *4. Casualty entry. Stands until annulled. A statement is required from the person recorded lost.*
>
> *Discharge is by the signatures of ward, guardian and sponsor at a single sitting, each witnessed by the other two.*
>
> *The Registry regrets that it cannot issue papers to a ward.*

---

## I.

Outside Room 304, Amy stopped with her shoulder against the painted plaster until she could breathe through her nose without whistling. Three flights up Engineering South used to be forty seconds and a jump from the fourth-step landing; this spring her calves burned by the second turn, and her ribs still complained if she took corners fast. She waited until the pulse in her ears backed down, hitched the two mess-hall boxes higher onto her hip, and knocked with her boot before opening the door.

<font color=mistyrose>"Brought you lunch,"</font> she said, crossing to the desk at a stroll so casual it nearly hurt. <font color=mistyrose>"Early batch. Before the undergrads get at the braised tofu and leave nothing but the grey kelp."</font>

Mornye sat behind her blotter with the Solistree avenue bright behind her shoulder, two plain steel clips holding her bangs clear of her eyes. Her chair gave its faint, steady hum at rest. Beside her left elbow, Buddy sat cross-legged on the hovering Starstack, plump and round, waving one short arm at the smell of sesame oil.

*"It is ten-forty,"* Mornye said.

<font color=mistyrose>"Which is why there's still tofu. Both are yours."</font>

Amy set the cardboard boxes down, pushed the rolled cuffs of Mei's dark coat back up her forearms where they kept slipping, and slid her own copy of the notice onto the middle of the blotter. Then she hooked a toe under the low window bench, dragged out the scarred pine stool that lived underneath it, and folded herself down onto the seat with her knees up past her elbows. She popped the tabs on the nearer lunch box, pulled apart the wooden chopsticks, and ate a piece of tofu while her free hand dropped into the deep left pocket of the coat.

<font color=mistyrose>"Also,"</font> she said around the bite, <font color=mistyrose>"I need a faculty sleuth. Somebody with senior clearance and zero fear of municipal clerks."</font>

Mornye looked down at the sheet on her blotter. She did not touch it. Just past her knuckles, pinning down the flap of a second buff Registry envelope that lay face down on the oak, sat her tea mug.

<font color=mistyrose>"Item three,"</font> Amy announced, tilting her chin to read upside down off the page in a pinched, dusty Registry drone. <font color=mistyrose>"'Sponsor of record, Form 7-C. Withheld under Privacy Provision nine-two.' Apparently I have a secret patron somewhere in this city, Professor, and until I flush them out of the brush, Lahai-Roi says I'm legally twelve and can't have an ID card."</font>

Inside the coat pocket, her fingers walked the bottom seam from corner to corner, found nothing but wool lint, and stayed there. Since she was seven years old and finding the same scientist at the end of her lunch table three days running, she had had one candidate on her list. Over the top of the cardboard lid, she watched Mornye's hands.

Mornye kept both palms loose on the edge of the desk. *"To unseal an entry under Provision nine-two,"* she said in her flat, even voice, *"the ward files Form 7-F at Window Four. The clerk logs the petition and allows fourteen working days for objection, unless the sponsor attends in person to waive anonymity."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Fourteen days! My library privileges are hostage, Professor. Think faster."</font>

*"Alternatively,"* Mornye said, *"as noted on line three, the Registry messenger will have delivered a duplicate under seal to the sponsor's desk before nine this morning."*

Amy lowered her chopsticks an inch.

Mornye lifted her tea mug, set it on its cork coaster beside the kettle, and drew the folded sheet out of the face-down envelope. She smoothed the crease with two fingers, turned it face up, and slid it across the oak until its edge met Amy's.

Side by side on the blotter, the two notices were identical down to the crooked Registry stamp at the foot, except for line three, where Amy's had a solid black bar and Mornye's read *MORNYE, RM. 304, ENG. SOUTH*.

---

<font color=mistyrose>"Knew it,"</font> Amy said at once, pointing both chopsticks at her. <font color=mistyrose>"Called it first term. Nobody just happens to walk down Stairwell C at lunch twice a week unless they've signed a form. Honestly, you're terrible at undercover work."</font>

*"You asked me to help you look."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Politeness,"</font> Amy said brightly. Her left hand slipped out of the hip pocket, slid under the open lapel of Mei's coat, and felt inside the breast pocket along the stitching. Empty cloth. She pulled her hand no further than her ribs, stopped chewing, and set the half-eaten lunch box down on the floor between her boots.

When she looked up again, she left the grin off. <font color=mistyrose>"Who were the reports for?"</font>

She had asked that twice at fourteen, once in a corridor and once in the mess hall, and got silent datapad typing both times.

Mornye reached down and clicked her wheel brake off. She backed half a pace from the desk, turned to the grey four-drawer card cabinet tucked between the bookcase and the radiator, and pulled the top drawer open on its steel runners. Top drawer only. She rolled six inches back so the space in front of it was clear.

Amy leaned sideways off the child's stool and hooked two fingers into the packed row of stiff white postcards. Every week had one, dated in the upper right, three or four cramped lines of ink across the middle. And in the upper left corner of every card, standing alone without a name or a room number, sat a single four-stroke character.

Amy's thumb stopped on the third card. She knew those four strokes the way she knew the kitchen doorframe at the cabin—from a week of official paper spread across scrubbed pine, pressed hard enough to dent the wood underneath.

Mornye reached into the narrow slot ahead of Week 1, lifted out a small glassine sleeve, and drew a torn triangle of yellowed requisition paper from inside it. She laid it on the corner of the desk. The same four strokes sat in dark ink near the ragged edge.

*"Your mother came down to the sublevel alone and asked me to erase every record of her,"* Mornye said. *"She wrote that mark on the slip before I began. Then she left."*

Amy stared at the torn triangle until the edges blurred, blinked hard, and looked back at the two notices. <font color=mistyrose>"Did Ma ask you to sign Form 7-C?"</font>

*"No."*

Heat came up Amy's neck fast enough to sting her ears. She shoved the stool back two inches with her heels and stood, which reminded her knees about the stairs. <font color=mistyrose>"You knew her."</font>

Mornye tilted her chin slightly to meet her eyes. *"Yes."*

<font color=mistyrose>"I sat on that stool,"</font> Amy said, kicking the pine rung lightly with the side of her boot. <font color=mistyrose>"Before. She used to bring me down out of the snow and park me under that bench with graph paper while you two argued at the board, and then I showed up here at seven with a ward number and you looked straight at me in the mess hall like you'd never seen my face!"</font> Her voice cracked up a step on *face*; she dragged it back down. <font color=mistyrose>"Years, Professor. Same building. You never said her name once. Some weeks in third year I lay in bed thinking I'd made her up!"</font>

Mornye's hands lay flat on her thighs. She did not look away toward the avenue. *"She asked to be gone."*

<font color=mistyrose>"From the computers!"</font>

*"From everywhere,"* Mornye said. Each word came out the exact size of the last, quiet and level. *"I couldn't say it."* She moved two fingers toward the open grey drawer. *"I wrote it on Fridays."*

Amy pressed her lips shut. Her chest hurt worse than it had outside in the hall. Turning back to the open drawer, she walked her fingers deeper down the packed cards—past winter, past thaw, past another winter—until the black character in the upper left corner stopped.

She pulled that card half an inch above its neighbours. A Friday in autumn; two lines about a broken shoe buckle and an arithmetic test; white card stock in the top corner. She checked the next three behind it. All bare at the top, all the way back to the rear plate.

<font color=mistyrose>"I stopped writing 'Dear Ma' at the top of mine too,"</font> Amy said, much quieter, looking down at the clean white corner. <font color=mistyrose>"Way after this. I remember the day."</font>

She lifted the loose handful of cards out of the front of the drawer, tapped their edges flush against the blotter twice, and slid them back in behind the glassine sleeve in their proper order.

---

Both of her hands went straight back into Mei's coat—right pocket first, turning the corner of the lining up toward her knuckles, then the left again, thumbing hard along the hem where a thin fold of paper might slip through a torn stitch.

*"What are you looking for in your mother's coat?"* Mornye asked.

Amy's hands froze inside the wool. For half a second she opened her mouth to invent a lost house key or lip balm; then her shoulders sagged inside the too-wide seams, and she looked at the radiator pipes instead.

<font color=mistyrose>"I kept a numbered list in a biscuit tin at the cabin, folded in eight, things I tried doing so she wouldn't have to go away,"</font> she said, fast and flat. <font color=mistyrose>"The day she walked out, the page wasn't in it. Maybe she read it, and that's why the forms came out eleven days later. Maybe it just got swept behind the stove. Since I came home I've been sneaking into the hall at night while she's asleep and going through her pockets."</font> She let out a short breath through her nose. <font color=mistyrose>"Today I borrowed the coat awake, with permission, so at least I wasn't skulking in the dark. It isn't even the same coat."</font>

Above her left temple, a lock of pink hair skipped white once with a tiny dry crackle, dropping two flecks of pale light onto the dark wool shoulder before going pink again.

Beside Mornye's chair, the Starstack glided six inches forward and dipped toward Amy's hip. Buddy toddled to its rim on his two stubby legs and stepped straight off into the air toward her arm.

Amy yanked both hands out of the pockets just in time to catch him against her rolled cuff. Solid and warm as a hot stone, he settled along her forearm, tucked his two short arms neatly into his round belly, and began his slow amber glow—one, two, rising gently through his skin toward eleven.

*"This one will not go on a card,"* Mornye said.

Amy looked down at Buddy pulsing against her sleeve until eleven finished and dimmed back to one. When she lifted her arm level with the Starstack, he stepped dutifully back aboard and sat down.

Mornye reached across the blotter and pushed the second, unopened lunch box toward Amy until it touched the edge of her copy of the notice. *"Take that with you, or finish it here. You took the stairs."*

<font color=mistyrose>"Snitch building,"</font> Amy muttered, though she tucked both boxes—the half-eaten one stacked neatly atop the full one—into the crook of her elbow and nudged the low pine stool back into its dark slot under the window bench with her boot. She picked up her black-barred copy of the notice, folded it once, and carried the lot to the door.

With the latch open, she looked back over Mei's oversized shoulder seam. <font color=mistyrose>"Ma thinks she strong-armed you into signing Form 7-C that night. All spring she's been apologising to chairs and lampshades every time your name comes up."</font>

Mornye's fingers rested on her wheel rim. *"Did she say that?"*

<font color=mistyrose>"She doesn't have to say it, she wears it like a sandwich board. You tell her by tomorrow afternoon, Professor, or I'm telling her over dinner, and I will do the voices."</font>

Out in the third-floor corridor, Amy pulled the door shut, walked four brisk paces toward the stairwell landing, stopped with her forehead almost touching the fire-exit sign, and turned back to the lift bank, stabbing the down button hard with her thumb.

---

## II.

On Tuesday afternoon Mornye sent a two-line message asking if Mei could spare twenty minutes to reach an archive box off a high rack in Engineering South. Mei made it from the dormitory courtyard to the lift lobby in six minutes flat, absurdly pleased to be recruited for height instead of told to sit on a bench and drink something warm.

When the lift cables engaged for the run down into the sublevels, the car gave a small metal shudder on its guides. Mei's hands came up automatically behind the push-handles of Mornye's chair, hovering an inch off the rubber grips.

*"No,"* Mornye said to the brass doors. Her right hand rested light and steady on her brake lever; the motor kept its low, level purr against the floor tiles.

Mei tucked both thumbs into her belt, grinned at her own reflection in the scratched inspection plate, and stepped sideways so they rode shoulder to shoulder. When the doors parted onto the cold whitewash of Sublevel 2, she matched her stride to the chair's pace down the hall.

Halfway along the pipe-hung corridor, Mornye turned aside to a heavy grey lab door stencilled *B-14* and drew a brass key from her pocket.

Mei stopped beside the jamb. Looking through the wired glass square into the unlit room, she knew nothing about it—not the angle of the pillars, not the smell of machine oil seeping under the sill. It could have been a basement on the other side of the sea. What she knew about this floor, she knew from the medbay.

<font color=moccasin>"Hey,"</font> Mei said before the key turned, rubbing the back of her neck. <font color=moccasin>"About this whole floor. And dumping a seven-year-old on you on my way out the door after making you scrub my name off the walls—I really am sorry, Mornye. That was rotten."</font>

Mornye turned the lock until the bolt clicked back. *"The steps are hooked behind the door,"* she said, and pushed it ajar.

Mei put her shoulder to the grey steel and went in first.

---

Overhead, two banks of strip-lights buzzed once and blinked on. Whatever B-14 had been before, it was an undergraduate teaching lab now: half-assembled actuator rigs clamped to black rubber mats, bundles of test leads hanging from pegboards, a rolling chalkboard covered in someone's messy torque arrows, and in the middle of the scarred central workbench a round plastic food tub mummified in yellow masking tape that read *THURS GROUP B — DO NOT UNPLUG OR EAT*.

Mei did a slow circuit of the room, poking gently at a brass vernier gauge and squinting at the torque diagram before coming back to prop her hip against the front edge of the central bench. High up near the ventilation trunking, atop a seven-foot bay of slotted steel shelving, sat a lone grey fibreboard box.

Measuring the uprights with her eye, Mei hooked her left boot onto the bottom shelf lip, caught the steel post in one hand, and started hauling herself up.

*"Get down from the rack, Mei,"* Mornye said at once. *"Those bays aren't bolted into the masonry, and you're worrying me. Use the steps behind the door."*

Mei dropped back onto both soles. <font color=moccasin>"Testing the bolts,"</font> she offered, went back to the door, unhooked the three-step aluminium ladder, and set it square beneath the shelf with a loud, virtuous click of the spreader bar. Up on the top step, blowing a film of grey fluff off the lid, she noticed how high the bay actually was—chest-high if you stood up here on three steps, out of reach from the floor. Twenty-six-year-old Mornye had climbed these same ribbed treads to shove it under the duct.

Mei carried the box down, the label on its side reading in a younger, narrower hand `CALIBRATION LOGS — SUPERSEDED`, set it on the central bench, and knocked her knuckles lightly against the gouged wood beneath her hip. <font color=moccasin>"Which bench was yours down here?"</font>

Mornye glanced at the dark solder burns under Mei's sleeve. *"That one."*

Mei looked down at the scarred laminate, nudged Thursday Group B's taped lunch three inches left to make space, and pried the tight grey lid off the box.

Inside lay no calibration binders at all, only one thick sheaf of heavy cream Registry stock held together at the corner by a tarnished brass prong.

Mei lifted the sheaf out. Her own handwriting met her across every ruled section: bold, slightly slanted forward, pressed hard enough into the rag paper that her thumb felt the raised tracks on the underside. She flipped straight to the foot of the final page.

In the box marked `Guardian of record` sat four brisk black strokes. Directly below, on the attached Form 7-C strip for `Sponsor of record`, stood *Mornye*, stamped over in red `WITHHELD — PROV. 9(2)`.

Mei let her forehead drop toward the page with a groan. <font color=moccasin>"Gods above, I really made you sign it the same night—"</font>

Mornye rolled forward until her armrest touched Mei's coat and pointed one finger at the bottom of the sheet. *"Look at the ink."*

Mei squinted closer. The four-stroke mark—like every word on the pages above—was dense, dead-black liquid ink from a broad nib that had bitten into the paper grain. *Mornye* on the sponsor's line was fine blue-black roller pen, clean and smooth.

*"That line was blank when Form 7-C reached the sublevel queue,"* Mornye said. *"You asked me for one thing that night, Mei. This was not it."*

Mei stared from the black four-stroke mark to the blue-black name. Mornye kept her finger near the edge of the sheet, her quiet voice staying dead level, every word cut to the exact same height.

*"You asked me to erase every record of you in this city,"* Mornye said. *"I left one standing. You're holding it."*

Mei turned the heavy sheaf slightly under the strip-lights, piecing the order of it together out loud. <font color=moccasin>"So I never asked you to take her. I just walked in here, asked you to wipe me out of existence, and you did all the rest of it because I asked. Same as building the six-point array this spring when I walked back in off the snow and asked."</font> She looked down over the cream edge of the paper into Mornye's calm grey eyes. <font color=moccasin>"Do you ever say no to me?"</font>

*"Once,"* Mornye said, looking at the file in Mei's hands.

---

Mei slid down the cabinet doors beneath the workbench until she sat cross-legged on the cool linoleum with the sheaf spread across her knees. Mornye clicked her brake on a pace away and waited without checking the clock. Beside her on the hovering Starstack, Buddy sat with his two stubby legs dangling over the rim, glowing warm amber in slow, silent beats from one up to eleven, dimming out, and starting again at one.

Mei turned to the welfare schedule pinned inside Page 3, where the guardian was instructed to set down practical particulars for whoever assumed care of the child. Four cramped lines ran out past the printed boxes in her own dark ink:

*Reads: yes. Upside down also.*
*Fears: says none. The lake.*
*Sleep: pretends, at nine. Let her think it works.*
*Food: will try to give you the good end of the herring. Do not accept.*

At the fourth line, a laugh broke straight out of Mei's chest into the empty lab. She covered her mouth with one hand, shoulders shaking once against the bench cupboard, looking down at words written by a woman she couldn't picture who had argued fish portions with a six-year-old and let herself be fooled at bedtime on purpose.

Turning back to Page 1 sobered her fast.

Halfway down the sheet, `Occupation of Guardian` had been buried under black ink—crossed left to right, up and down, pressed so savagely with the broad nib that the rag paper had compressed into a hard, dark mirror that caught the glare of the ceiling tubes when Mei tilted it.

<font color=moccasin>"Was this you?"</font>

*"No,"* Mornye said. *"It came down like that. I don't know what is under it."*

Right below that shining black bar sat `Reason for surrender of care`. From border to border the rectangle was white, except near the left edge, where a single tiny black dot showed that the nib had rested on the paper, waited, and lifted off again.

Mei set her thumb below the empty box. Looking at all that clean paper and one mute dot made the pit of her stomach feel cold and hollow. <font color=moccasin>"What was supposed to go in here?"</font>

*"You didn't tell me,"* Mornye said quietly. *"And I didn't ask."*

Mei looked from the empty `Reason` rectangle to the back sheet where `Sponsor of record` had arrived just as bare at the exact sublevel queue Mornye oversaw. She tipped her head back against the wooden cupboard behind her. <font color=moccasin>"Did I leave the sponsor line empty so you'd find it?"</font>

Mornye folded her hands lightly in her lap. *"I don't know. I have wanted to ask that for a very long time, and the only person who could answer it is sitting on the floor and can't."*

Mei looked steadily up at her. <font color=moccasin>"Ask me anyway."</font>

Mornye turned her chair an inch so they faced each other straight on. *"When you left that second line empty,"* she asked aloud, *"were you trying to spare me, or did you leave it where I worked knowing I would sign my name without you having to ask?"*

Mei rested both wrists on her knees, the old paper rustling between them. <font color=moccasin>"I don't know,"</font> she said plainly. <font color=moccasin>"I haven't got one minute of her in my head. And if she slid an empty box past your bench so you'd catch our kid without her having to look you in the face and ask, I won't defend her—that's an ugly way to treat a friend."</font> She tapped two fingers once against the cream stock. <font color=moccasin>"All I've got is me, sitting here today. If it were me deciding, you're still the person in this world I'd want behind her. I'd just walk up to Room 304 and ask you out loud."</font>

Colour came up faintly along Mornye's cheekbones beneath the plain steel clips. Her gaze dropped at once to the grey fibreboard box on the bench above them. *"Sublevel air is full of solder flux and silicate dust,"* she said briskly. *"Rag stock yellows if you leave it uncovered down here, and the four-o'clock practical group uses this room."*

Mei hauled herself up off the floor, slapped dust from the seat of her trousers, and laid the Instrument open on the bench beside the box, its blank `Reason` line facing up. Instead of apologising again, she rested both palms on the cream pages and asked, <font color=moccasin>"After the door shut behind me that night—what did you do?"</font>

*"Started filing,"* Mornye said.

Mei tilted her head. <font color=moccasin>"Took her under your wing?"</font>

*"She lived in North Dormitory and had friends. I was a woman who turned up at lunch."*

<font color=moccasin>"Stood guard over her, then."</font>

*"I am an engineer, Mei. Have you seen me guard anything?"*

<font color=moccasin>"Kept her alive for me."</font>

*"She ate mess-hall stew and broke into three restricted laboratories before sixth form,"* Mornye said dryly. *"Filing is the word I prefer."*

Mei laughed under her breath, tucked the Instrument back into the grey box, fitted the lid home, folded the aluminium steps back behind the door, and reached to tuck the grey archive box under her own arm; Mornye held out both hands until Mei surrendered it onto her lap instead.

Out in the sublevel corridor, the box was wide enough across Mornye's armrests that she had to steer one-handed around its front corner. After three metres of corridor toward the call plate ahead, Mornye said, *"You may get the lift."*

At the lift doors, Mei hit the up button with proud ceremony, dug into her coat pocket while the cables hummed down the shaft, and came up with two sweets she had raided from Luuk's Tuesday dish that morning. She unwrapped a sour lemon drop onto its crinkled square of wax paper and set it neatly on the middle of the box lid.

---

## III.

At quarter to nine the next morning, Mornye cleared her blotter and laid the four items out from left to right in signing order, sticking a yellow paper flag beside every required line: first the Registry's Form of Discharge in carbon triplicate, then the original Instrument of Wardship, then the half-sheet statement for the casualty entry, and at the right-hand end the printed administrative history of Form 7-C.

She squared that fourth sheet with the edge of the oak and left it face up. While uncapping her steel manifold pen to test the ink flow on scrap paper, she caught her right wrist rolling the barrel twice against her thumb joint—Mei's old bench habit for waking a dry ballpoint before carbon work. Twice already this spring her arm had acted on something Mei taught her long ago before Mornye knew she was doing it; she had logged both dates in the back of her desk planner. She left the pen uncapped beside the first flag.

Down the hall the lift chimed—not the stairwell door—followed by two sets of footsteps, one quick and light, one unhurried.

Amy pushed the door open with Mei half a step behind her carrying a grease-spotted brown paper bag from the dormitory kitchen. Mei had already eaten a third of its contents on the walk over and arrived chewing a warm scallion twist.

<font color=moccasin>"Look at the little yellow flags,"</font> Mei said admiringly, leaning over the desk with the open bag tilted against her ribs. <font color=moccasin>"We're in safe hands, kiddo."</font>

Three flaky crumbs and a sesame seed landed squarely on Page 1 of the original Instrument of Wardship. Mornye slid the Instrument six inches left out of range and brushed the crumbs into her wastepaper basket.

*"Item two first,"* Mornye said, pointing to the Guardian's block on the triplicate sheet. *"Mei."*

<font color=moccasin>"Hang on, I brought a pen, I'm almost sure—"</font> Setting the pastry bag by the kettle, Mei plunged both hands into her coat pockets and began turning everything in them out onto the clear corner of the desk: four twisted sweet wrappers, a stub of carpenter's pencil with a snapped lead, a heavy steel hex bolt, a hank of green garden twine with a dried shred of wisteria bark caught in the knot, and, last, from the inside breast pocket, a white sheet of paper folded into quarters.

Across the corner of the desk, Amy's eyes tracked each item onto the wood: wrappers, stub, bolt, twine. When the folded white square came out of the inner pocket, Amy went completely still beside her mother's sleeve, her gaze fixed hard on the crease—until Mei thumbed one corner open to see if a pen was clipped inside it, showing the printed header of Startorch Infirmary Outpatient Physiotherapy. Amy blinked once and let her shoulders down.

Mornye slid her steel manifold pen across the blotter.

While Mei picked it up, Amy swept the small heap off the oak and dropped each piece back into her mother's coat—wrappers and twine into the left hip pocket, bolt and pencil stub into the right, the folded physio sheet slipped cleanly back into the inside breast pocket.

Mei looked down at Amy's wrists disappearing into her lapels. <font color=moccasin>"How do you know which side the bolt lives on?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"You're predictable,"</font> Amy said.

Mornye tapped the yellow flag beside `Guardian of record`. *"The Registry requires the guardian's mark to match the original instrument."* She pushed yesterday's empty buff envelope within Mei's reach, beside the glassine-sleeved requisition slip. *"Try it first."*

Mei bent over the envelope, tongue caught lightly at the corner of her mouth, and copied the four strokes from the slip. Her hand hooked stiffly on the second turn; it came out tall and lopsided. Frowning, she shifted her grip lower on the steel barrel and drew a second one beside it, much slower. The vertical spine wavered halfway down.

<font color=mistyrose>"Give me that,"</font> Amy said, plucking the pen from her fingers. Standing upright at the corner of the desk, she touched the tip to the buff paper and wrote it once: four quick strokes, no pause, identical down to the snapped tail on the requisition slip. She twirled the pen back toward the triplicate form. <font color=mistyrose>"Let me do the box. Clerk at Window Four won't know from Adam."</font>

*"I would know,"* Mornye said. *"I am the witness."*

Mei took the pen back, leaned over the triplicate sheet, and read the small type directly above the flagged box. Her brows drew together. <font color=moccasin>"Wait a second. 'Relinquishes all tutelary claim'? I'm not relinquishing her, I just got her back!"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Ma, read the top line,"</font> Amy said, pointing. <font color=mistyrose>"Right now the city says I'm an unemancipated school ward who isn't allowed her own adult papers or a bank card. Relinquishing Ward 734 gets you a grown daughter who can sign her own library slip."</font>

Mei muttered under her breath at municipal phrasing, braced her left palm on the blotter, and put the pen into the box. Four strokes, pressing firmly through the carbons, with a clear half-second stop between the second stroke and the third while she checked the angle.

It did not match the slip or the Instrument. Mornye took the pen back from her.

*"The Registry holds nothing else in its files to compare it against,"* Mornye said practically. *"I saw to that myself."* She bent over `First Witness to Guardian's Mark` directly beside Mei's uneven character and signed *Mornye*, attesting that this was the guardian's hand. Amy took the pen and signed the second witness line beneath her.

Across the desk lay three versions of the one character: the vanished hand on the torn slip, Amy's effortless double on the buff envelope, and Mei's paused, crooked four strokes in the box.

---

Mornye moved the signed Instrument aside and brought forward item four: the Casualty Entry extract, clipped at the top corner to the Joint Review Board's closing finding.

Mei leaned over Amy's shoulder to see what they were annulling. Halfway down the attached sheet her eyes caught on the concluding paragraph—*attributable to failure of the supervising officer to engage manual override prior to threshold breach*—and dropped to the nine neat signatures arrayed at the bottom.

The easy slouch left Mei's spine all at once. She straightened up off the desk edge, golden eyes gone flat and bright in the morning light. <font color=moccasin>"Nine of them signed this? Are these nine people still in this building?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Sit down, Ma,"</font> Amy said without looking round.

Mei stood another two seconds staring at the names; then she pulled the wooden visitor's chair six inches closer and sat down hard on the front edge of it.

Amy looked across at Mornye. <font color=mistyrose>"What did you file that day?"</font>

Mornye turned her chair to the grey cabinet, opened the third drawer down, reached behind the final divider at the back, and lifted out the last card before this spring. She handed it across the blotter. One number and one word sat across the top of the unheaded card:

*99.6. Held.*

Amy laid the card face up directly beside the nine signatures on the finding. Then she pulled the half-sheet `Statement of Person Recorded Lost` square in front of her. Below the printed warning—*COMPLETE IN BLOCK CAPITALS. PRESS FIRMLY FOR ALL THREE COPIES*—sat a ruled box three lines deep.

Carbon paper skidded under heavy pressure; Mei leaned forward from the visitor's chair and planted both palms flat on the upper corners of the half-sheet to hold it fast against the wood.

Amy bent low over the desk, took the steel pen, and did not look up once while she printed, saying each word aloud at the pace of her hand and bearing down hard enough that the ballpoint grooved the top sheet:

<font color=mistyrose>"THE. NINE. ARE. WRONG. THE. OUTCOME. WAS. MINE. THE. OFFICER. KEPT. A. PROMISE. I. MADE. HER. MAKE."</font>

She stabbed the final stop in the right-hand corner of the third line, signed *AEMEATH* on the declarant bar below, and turned the half-sheet around toward Mornye without another word.

Mornye looked at the three lines of heavy block capitals. Her right hand rested flat on the blotter beside the witness box. *"A witness attests the hand,"* she said quietly, *"not the content."*

<font color=mistyrose>"I know what a witness does,"</font> Amy said. <font color=mistyrose>"Sign that I said it."</font>

Mornye picked up the pen and signed on the first line that Amy had said it. She passed the pen to Mei, who signed underneath with the same small hitch after the second stroke.

Amy pinched the corner of the top white sheet and lifted it past the yellow middle leaf to check that every capital on the bottom pink copy had come through dark and clear.

---

Only one yellow flag remained on the main Discharge sheet: `Release by Sponsor of Record`. Mornye drew the triplicate form back in front of herself, brought the nib over the box, and stopped with the point in the air above the paper.

Across the desk, Amy's eyes had settled on the single sheet sitting squared and face up at the end of the row—the administrative history of Form 7-C. She had been reading it upside down while Mei signed. Near the bottom of the brief table stood two consecutive lines without dates in the summary column: an application by the sponsor to be released from the arrangement, and the sponsor's withdrawal of that application eleven days later.

<font color=mistyrose>"Eleven days,"</font> Amy said aloud, lifting her eyes straight to Mornye's.

Mei craned her neck sideways over the blotter, squinting upside down at the table with a slight crease between her brows, clearly seeing nothing remarkable in a fortnight's paperwork. Mornye held Amy's gaze across the oak.

<font color=mistyrose>"Why did you take it back?"</font> Amy asked.

*"Form 7-D required a replacement sponsor named before the clerk would accept it,"* Mornye said. She looked down at the uncapped nib hovering above the box. *"There was nobody else I would have let read that drawer."*

She set the pen to the paper, signed her name through the release box in one steady line of ink, and clicked the steel cap shut.

<font color=mistyrose>"Wait, give it back, bottom section,"</font> Amy said, holding out two fingers.

Below the perforated line at the foot of the Discharge sheet sat the intake strip for the Adult Register: `Persons to be Notified (Optional, two lines)`. Amy pulled the cap off again and wrote *Mei* and the dormitory block across Line 1 without looking up.

<font color=moccasin>"Hey,"</font> Mei objected, tapping the table with a knuckle. <font color=moccasin>"You didn't ask me."</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"Mothers don't get asked,"</font> Amy said.

Above Line 2 she stopped, stood up straight beside the visitor's chair, and read the form's printed prompt aloud in a clear, formal voice: <font color=mistyrose>"Dr. Mornye. Does the named person consent to be entered on the register for notification?"</font>

*"What would I be notified of?"* Mornye asked.

<font color=mistyrose>"Everything. Scrapes, honours, address changes, haircuts."</font>

*"I consent,"* Mornye said.

Amy curled her left arm all the way round the top of the sheet like a candidate shielding an exam script, wrote Mornye's name on Line 2, and filled in the narrow box marked `Relationship` inside the barrier of her sleeve. She pressed a strip of blotter over the wet ink, separated her retained pink copy, and folded the top edge of the Registry copy down over the `Relationship` column before sliding the small `Signature of Consenting Party` box under Mornye's hand.

Mornye signed the consent line without having seen what she was called. Amy folded her pink copy neatly in four, pulled her soft-covered diary out of the bag slung on her hip, tucked the sheet behind the back cover, and snapped the band over it.

---

While Amy capped the pen, Mei wandered over to the grey four-drawer cabinet by the bookcase. Resting two fingers lightly on the top pull, she looked back at Mornye first. <font color=moccasin>"May I?"</font>

Mornye nodded once.

Mei slid the top drawer open. Every white card in the front half carried the crisp four-stroke mark she had just failed twice to draw on an envelope. Smiling crookedly over her shoulder, she tapped the steel rim. <font color=moccasin>"Every one of these has my mark at the top. Technically they're addressed to me. Can I take my post home?"</font>

Mornye rolled across the rug and found her own right hand already hooked beneath the lip of the inner card tray, lifting the front of the drawer half an inch off its runners to hand it up to her. She lowered the tray back onto the steel track with a click, took her hand off the metal, and said, *"No."*

<font color=moccasin>"No?"</font>

*"There is one copy, the cabinet is off-system, and archive cards do not travel,"* Mornye said. *"You can read them here."*

Mei was already sliding down the front of the cabinet before the second sentence finished, stretching her boots out across the rug with her spine propped comfortably against the lower drawers. Amy came round the desk, dropped down right beside her, bumped Mei's hip over two inches, and reached straight up into the open top drawer to pull out the first batch of cards herself.

<font color=mistyrose>"Everything goes through the censor first,"</font> Amy announced.

*"Those are in chronological order,"* Mornye said.

<font color=mistyrose>"They'll stay in chronological order."</font> Amy thumbed briskly through the first six, passed five sideways into Mei's lap, stopped dead at the sixth, and clapped it face down onto her own knee under her palm. Mei eyed the card pinned beneath Amy's fingers, laughed quietly through her nose, and left it alone, lifting the top card from her own lap toward the window light instead.

Across the clean white stock ran three lines in Mornye's small, upright hand:

*Week 3. Ate both dinners. Asked for the corridor light left on. Arranged.*

Mei read it through once, her mouth going soft at the corners; then her eyes went back to the left edge and read it a second time. Without getting up, she reached a long arm straight overhead and held the card up for Mornye, who took it from her fingers and slotted it back behind Week 2.

After another dozen cards, Mei rolled up onto her feet, crossed to Mornye's low tea table, lifted the kettle, filled it from the water carafe on the sill without asking, and clicked the switch down. Mornye's hand lifted half an inch off her armrest and settled back down; she let her do it. While the element began to murmur, Mei dropped right back onto the floor beside Amy against the front of the cabinet.

Turning back to her blotter, Mornye drew one fresh, blank card from her wooden holder and uncapped the steel pen. She left the upper left corner empty. Across the middle she wrote:

*Pressed hard. All three copies.*

She wheeled back toward the cabinet to file it and found all three lower drawers blocked by four stretched-out legs and two backs leaned comfortably against the steel. Looking up from her batch, Amy held out her free hand.

Mornye passed the card down to her. *"Fourth drawer. It's empty."*

Amy turned the card right way up and read it. Her mouth opened slightly, stayed open a beat, and shut; for once she had nothing at all to say back.

Beside her on the rug, Mei leaned her chin toward Amy's shoulder to peek. <font color=moccasin>"What does that one say?"</font>

<font color=mistyrose>"It's not addressed to you,"</font> Amy said. She hooked two fingers behind her calf, pulled the empty bottom drawer open a hand's breadth, stood the card upright at the very front against the steel plate, and bumped the drawer shut with her hip.
