# PIGNUS

### *a type-case for two dolls*

Working notes toward **Lanze Reiter** and **Ronin** in the world of *Lyre, Speak to Me*.

> *pignus, -oris*, n. — a thing handed over as security, to be returned intact when the debt is paid. Also, in the poets: a token of love. Also, in the poets: a child.
>
> A pawn is a thing left with someone until you come back for it.
> The law has a great deal to say about redemption and nothing at all about the case where nobody comes.

---

## 0. THE TWO PLATES

Read the images as objects that exist inside the fiction, because they do. They are **catalogue plates**. Three-quarter profile, neutral ground, one small island of terrain under the feet so the boot-sole reads, the designation set in small caps beside the figure. Nobody stands like that except for a lens. Squad 5 would know the format on sight: this is how you photograph a unit for a buyer.

**Plate one — LANZE REITER.** The name is set beside a *vertical rule*: a margin line. She is printed in the margin of something. Grey plate over a black chassis, sallet-form helm with the visor up so exactly one eye reads, a gorget high enough to make the head turn as a unit. A longsword drawn and held low in a gauntlet with too many joints. A spear slung diagonally across her back, tip up, **unused** — the finish on the shaft is clean where a hand would wear it. A pale ribbon falls from the back of the helm down past the shoulder, and it is tied, and a tie can be undone. Heeled sabatons: somebody wanted her to be a certain height in a room. Pink-blond, cut to fit under steel.

**Plate two — RONIN.** The name is set *on a horizontal rule that runs the full width of the plate*, straight through her, like a line ruled in a ledger. Wide black kasa, metal, fastened — a roof she carries. Black chassis, harness, one arm segmented into a hard gauntlet and the other left plain. A purple obi and hakama with a cloud pattern; a shibori cloth of the same house-purple trailing behind her longer than she is tall, over the ground, collecting it. Two blades — one drawn low, one at the hip with the cord still knotted. A dead tree at her shoulder. Violet eyes, a red streak in dark hair, and the hat is angled to keep the face off the lens and does not quite manage it.

Two things follow from the plates and both are load-bearing:

1. **They were photographed.** Somewhere there is a page they came off, and a price, and a print run.
2. **Lanze Reiter carries the part of her function she can no longer perform.** A *Lanze Reiter* is a lance-rider. A lance wants a charge and a charge wants a horse. She has been carrying a cavalry weapon on her back for a very long time in a world with no line to break. Everything else about her is a working sword. The lance is the debt.

**Two rows the table cannot hold, filed separately:**

**On "Arisen" as a word.** No language on the continent currently has one. Rinascita's Order had a word and burned the file that used it. Huanglong has a column. The Spacetrek Collective has a fault code. The Row would set the foreign word in Latin type, which is what the plate does, and charge her for the characters.

**On the Doll Resonator.** You are right that the Resonators in this corpus are made of meat. The precedent that gets you across is **Laevatain, Deserter 77A, an Artificial Resonator built by the Order** — artificial *resonance*, in a body that could still bleed, held together by a stabilizer ring she stole back for herself. What has never been done is the chassis. So the interesting question is not *can a doll resonate* — it is **what a frequency does when you put it somewhere that cannot die.** A Resonator's power runs on a body that has a last day in it. Take the last day away and the frequency has nowhere to arrive. It just keeps going. It keeps faith. And *everything that keeps faith long enough begins to resemble the faithful.*

## II. THE GENERATIONS PROBLEM

Three counts. They disagree. The disagreement is the material.

**Mengzhou counts by wars.** Hsin taught the city its doll-craft and made a line each time the country needed one. The court retired the **third generation** with honours and ordered failed cores interred under the names soldiers actually used. Third generation is old and will walk anyway. By Mengzhou's count, first generation is under the ground with soldiers' nicknames on them, and there is no fourth, and Hsin has been asked and has not answered.

**Rinascita counts by patents.** The Order's craft-house numbered its work by the doctrine that authorised it, so their series numbering runs backwards through six schisms and produces at least two units that are simultaneously *earlier* and *later* than each other. The frontier court has this on its docket. It has been on the docket for eleven years.

**The Collective counts by standard.** Bridget is "old-generation hardware," which is a way of saying her cloud format stopped being maintained nine years ago in a budget consolidation, and the files degraded past restoration threshold, and *nobody logged when. Nobody was checking.* This is the count that matters commercially and the only one that has ever thrown anything away.

**None of the three counts Lanze Reiter.** She is older than the third generation, wrong-shaped for Rinascita's patents, and predates the standard by so much that the Collective's fault codes read her as a corrupt entry. Options, all usable, none of them needing to be settled:

- She is **Hsin's first generation**, made before Mengzhou, before Hsin was Mengzhou's, in a country that no longer exists — and Hsin has never mentioned it, the way she never mentioned that the proverb was a report.
- She is **the West's independent invention**, which would mean doll-craft was discovered twice, which would mean it is a thing the world does rather than a thing a person did, which would frighten Hsin considerably.
- She is **the schism**. Somebody learned it from Hsin, went west, and did it wrong on purpose. Their name is on nothing.
- She is **not a doll at all** and everyone including her has been mistaken for four hundred years.

### The proverb that was never a proverb

This is the hinge, and it is already in the text. At the Mengzhou frontier court, defending a forty-year unit that still boils a kettle nightly for a garrison cook who died in spring:

> Hsin offered the court her oldest teaching proverb and told them **it had never been a proverb**: *Everything that keeps faith long enough begins to resemble the faithful.*

If it was never a proverb, it was a **field note**. Somebody observed a specific unit keeping faith for a specific and unreasonable length of time, and wrote a sentence about it, and the sentence outlived the file.

Lanze Reiter is the file.

> <font color=lightsteelblue>"You said a thing about faith. They teach it."</font>
>
> <font color=wheat>"I said it once, to a clerk, about a machine that would not put a cup away."</font> Hsin turned the hawthorn on its stick. <font color=wheat>"They took it for wisdom. It was a note. Sit down. You are the note."</font>

## III. SEVEN WEAVES

Each of these is a complete story-shape you could run to a full installment, and each contradicts at least two of the others. Contradiction is the house style: the wind's crossing exists in three editions and the frontier court has declined to rule.

---

### 1. THE RELIQUARY

> *What is in the chest.*
> **Era:** the Lament, or the century after. **Register:** found papers, craft-house voice, one long domestic scene at the end.

**Premise.** After the Lament tore frequencies loose from reality, the craft-houses of the West had a disposal problem: a Resonator's frequency outlives the Resonator, and a loose frequency with nowhere to arrive becomes a Discord. So they built **reliquaries**. A chassis is a grave you can walk. You put the loose thing in the cavity, you seal it, you give it a route, and it stops being a hazard because it has somewhere to be.

Lanze Reiter is a reliquary. She resonates because the woman in her chest does.

**The turn.** At some point in the first two hundred years, the passenger went quiet. There is a document trail on this — a maintenance chit that reads **`resonance nominal; source indeterminate`** — and nobody at the time thought it was interesting.

The question the story will not answer: *did the passenger die again, or did the passenger become the doll?* Lanze Reiter cannot tell you. She remembers the woman's preferences (she takes her tea over-steeped, she cannot sleep in a room with the door shut, she counts stairs) and she has all of those preferences herself and she has had them for so long that provenance has stopped being a recoverable fact. She is either a haunted machine or a woman with a very strange birth certificate, and there is no test.

**The pawn.** Here is the cruelty and it is the corpus's favourite kind, because it is sincere.

Lanze Reiter made Ronin. She has known for a long time what she is afraid of — the day the cavity finally empties, the day faith stops resembling anything — and she is a craft-worker's product who has spent four centuries in craft-work, and so she built **a second reliquary**. Empty. Warm. Portable. Something to fall into. She made herself a coffin with legs and she gave it two swords so it could get to her in time.

And then, because you cannot make a vessel out of nothing, she gave it a route, and preferences, and a route makes a person out of anything given long enough.

**Ronin found the schematic in year six.** Ronin has said nothing about it for eleven years.

**The scene it is all built toward.**

> <font color=plum>"I found the drawing in the sixth year. Under the false floor of the case, with the spare cord."</font>
>
> Lanze Reiter did not move. The helm makes that easy; it makes the whole head turn as one piece or not at all, and it did not.
>
> <font color=plum>"You built me a place to be put."</font>
>
> <font color=lightsteelblue>"Yes."</font>
>
> <font color=plum>"I've made other arrangements."</font>

And then — because the corpus does not stop at the refusal, it goes one further, to **new terms**, the way Yangyang did with the lake — Ronin does not simply decline. She says the second thing:

> <font color=plum>"If it empties, I'll be there. Not to catch it. To sit with it until it's finished. And then I'm going to keep walking, and I'm going to be the one who remembers, and you are not going to get to be forgotten by the only person who was there."</font>
>
> <font color=plum>"Those are the terms. You can have them or you can have nothing, and I can wait."</font>

**Rhymes it fires:** Iuno raising Mei to die in her place, exactly and deliberately — *"To kill her… I loved her instead. The order of those two sentences is my entire defense."* Cartethyia sealing herself in Avinoleum. The Architect's vessel program (Denia, Mya) as the *industrial, contemptuous* version of the same craft. Hiyuki's whole doctrine. And the counter-motif the corpus arrived at late: *the difference between catching somebody and standing where they can reach you. Not caught. Held.*

**Threads to pull:**
- Whose frequency. Do not name her. Give three candidates in three different documents and let one of them be obviously wrong in a way that suggests someone edited it.
- The maintenance chit. Who signed `source indeterminate`, and why they filed it under a heading with no follow-up.
- Reliquaries are plural. There were others. Where did the others go, and did any of them empty, and what walked afterward.
- Ronin's false floor still has the spare cord in it. She has never taken the cord out. Neither of them has mentioned the cord.

### 2. THE COUNT

> *A ledger of drownings.*
> **Era:** four centuries, compressed into one page. **Register:** a ruled book with a remarks column, and then a room.

**Premise.** The plague is real, in the sense that any diagnosis is real: somebody wrote it down and then it was true. The manual Lanze Reiter was issued with lists the symptom cluster in a professional hand.

> **§9.4 — INDICATORS OF DEVIATION IN ASSIGNED UNITS.**
> *Headache reported without impact history. Sleep behaviours in a unit that does not sleep. Delay between order and compliance exceeding two seconds. Questions concerning the purpose of a route. Questions concerning the operator. Colour change at the ocular ring. Statements of preference.*
> *Where three or more indicators are present, the unit is to be discharged into standing water at the earliest opportunity. Discharge is not a disciplinary measure and no report is required.*

Read that list again. It is the symptom list for **a self**, and it is also the symptom list for **grief**, and the manual has no column that distinguishes them.

**The ledger.** Lanze Reiter keeps one anyway, because no report was required and she required one. Twenty-two entries across four hundred years. The early ones are clean:

> `IV. Marker nine, spring. Three indicators. Discharged. Remarks: —`

The remarks column gets longer.

> `XIV. The ford below Ostrina, second frost. Indicators: delay (4s); question re: route. Remarks: she asked whether the road went anywhere she had not been. Discharged. Remarks (cont.): it does.`

> `XIX. Remarks: he named the drones. All eleven of them. He had a system.`

> `XXI. Remarks: I did not use the marker. I carried her to the deep water because the marker is shallow and I did not want it to take long. This is the first entry in which I have written the word* I. *I notice this. I am recording that I notice it.*`

Entry **XXIII** is blank. It has been blank for eleven years. It has Ronin's designation ruled at the top of it in a hand that pressed too hard.

**The counter-column.** Somebody in Jinzhou reads the ledger. Assessor Lü, or a clerk, or — best — **Baizhi**, who strikes words in cause-columns and once wrote 人 on camera:

> <font color=paleturquoise>"You've kept the going."</font> She turned the page back and looked at the top of it, where the columns were ruled and named. <font color=paleturquoise>"There's a column for going. There's no column for coming back."</font>
>
> <font color=lightsteelblue>"None of them came back."</font>
>
> <font color=paleturquoise>"That isn't a reason to leave the column off. That's what the column is *for*."</font> She took the pencil out of her collar and ruled a second line down the page, freehand, badly, all the way to the bottom. <font color=paleturquoise>"The Magistrate has a doctrine about this. It's four clauses and the fourth one is new. After any release, the first census is of who came back."</font>

*COUNT THE RETURNING.* It was written for a sluice gate and a drowning city and it lands on a ledger of drownings without needing a single word changed.

**And then the thing that ruins her:** one of them came back.

Do not say which. Do not say when. Put a twenty-third mark somewhere in the world — a unit walking a road in the wrong province with a very old model number and a habit Lanze Reiter would recognise from across a market — and let it be seen once, by somebody else, in a subordinate clause.

**The unruled question.** Was the plague ever real? She saw a city. She has always said *I saw a city.* What she saw was a doll standing in a city that had already burned, and a diagnosis is easier to carry than a coincidence, and she made one, and she has been carrying it instead ever since. Leave both readings intact. Let a character each hold one.

**Rhymes it fires:** Teddy's twenty-eight pages marked *Noise*; the changelog signed off *non-essential to function*. Hiyuki at the coals: *"The tragedy — the only one on offer — would be a self arriving in that chassis someday, one preference at a time, one inaccuracy at a time, and finding no one had left the light on. No one to notice the first choice when it comes. First choices are small. They are almost always missed."* Lanze Reiter noticed all twenty-two. She noticed them so precisely she wrote them down. And the noticing was the paperwork of the killing. **That is a much worse sin than not noticing, and it is the one the corpus is equipped to forgive.**

**Threads to pull:**
- The manual. Who wrote §9.4. Whether the author is alive. Whether the author was a doll.
- The blank entry XXIII: what Lanze Reiter would have to write in the remarks column, and the fact that she has already drafted it.
- The returned one.
- Whether Lupa hears about the twenty-two and what she does, given that she carries three decades of deaths in her body and walks anyway. Her ruling would be four words long and would not be gentle and would help.

### 3. THE LENDING LIBRARY

> *Ronin as an edition.*
> **Era:** whenever you like; this one travels. **Register:** the Row's, which is to say commercial, precise, and faintly liturgical.

**Premise.** The Rift is Bookbinders' Row. A doll is lent out the way an edition is lent out: she goes to a stranger's road, she works it, she comes back, and **she comes back annotated**. Knowledge arrives in her that she did not acquire. Gifts arrive in her pack that she did not choose. Somebody else's marginalia is in the margins, and the margins are hers.

> *Xu is set in type and type is paid in type, and the house takes characters. You hear what you spend.*

Ronin has been out **three thousand and some** times. She has stopped counting in the way you stop counting stairs in a house you live in.

**What comes back with her.** This is the whole pleasure of the weave. Make a list and make it specific:

- A recipe for a fish stew that requires an ingredient with no name in any language she has.
- A jar. Sealed. Warm when it arrived and warm for two days after, which is not a thing jars do.
- The price of coal in a city she can describe from the third bridge outward and has never entered.
- A grudge. Fully formed, unattached, aimed at nobody. She has had it for years. She has grown fond of it.
- A child's drawing of a woman in a hat.
- **A memory of an atrocity.** She was in a town when it burned. She remembers the smell of the specific wood. She does not know whether her hands did it, because the annotation came back without an attribution, and the Row does not attribute, and the Row will not be asked to.
- Six lines of a song with the bridge missing. *(It is flat in the bridge. It was always going to be flat in the bridge.)*

**The third hand.** The corpus already has one: *the unsigned, architecturally neat third hand correcting dates in Iuno's ledger, owner unknown.* Put it in Ronin's margins. Same hand. Same neatness. It has borrowed her forty-one times over two hundred years and each time it returns her **in better condition than she left** — repairs she did not authorise, a joint reseated, a burr taken off a blade, a stain out of the cloth.

And once, in the margin of a road she does not remember walking, in that hand:

> *she is improving. i am so pleased. — N.*

The Grand Architect has read Ronin more often than anyone alive. She has never kept her. **She keeps returning her.** *She does collect people like us, you know. Eventually. It is the single most appalling thing about her.*

**The catalogue hour.** Somewhere in this weave, Ronin hears herself sold. A crier, or a bulletin, or — best, and free, because the corpus already built the apparatus — **a pirate radio station reads the product copy on air in a flat forensic voice.**

> *"Masterworks all,"* Teddy read. *"That's the line. That's the whole line. It's forty years old, it's on a card, and the card is laminated because they read it eleven hundred times a day."* A pause with a scalpel in it. *"'You can't go wrong.' Note the grammar. The reassurance is offered to the buyer. Nobody has written a line for the other party in the transaction. This has been The Calibration Hour."*

Kit heard her own voice in a recruitment spot: *Voice: KT-series, pkg 2.1.* Ronin hears her own **posture** in an auctioneer's patter — *walks well, sits when told, purple's fashionable this year* — and the specific horror is that the description is accurate.

**Threads to pull:**
- Whether an annotation can be removed, and who would perform the removal, and whether Ronin would let them. (Teddy would say *yes, in four hours, and it'll hurt, and I'll bill it as overtime.* Ronin would say no. Then, later, about one specific annotation, yes.)
- The burning town. Leave it permanently unruled. Give two documents that disagree.
- The jar. Never open the jar. Or open it in a domestic scene, off-hand, mid-argument, and let it be pickles, and let the pickles be extremely good, and let nobody notice that this closes a two-hundred-year loop.
- N.'s forty-one returns. Why she always sends her back. *(Because a thing you keep stops being interesting. Because she is waiting for Ronin to be finished. Because she wants somebody to have noticed the repairs.)*

### 4. THE NORTH STRING

> *Two dolls arrive in Jinzhou during household four-one-one.*
> **Era:** now — sixth month, the week of Operation Goose and after. **Register:** domestic and comic, plain declaratives, nobody hyper-articulate, with one register-voice sting at the end.

**This is the one that plugs straight into the live corpus.** It costs you almost nothing in setup and pays out on four unfired hooks.

**The load-bearing hook.** From YG7: *Chisa's two strings on the third seat: one goes up out the door seam past the weather; the other goes **north along the crest wall and does not stop at the eighth tower**. It went tighter when Jinhsi passed a dish to the empty place and has not slackened.*

Make the north string **Lanze Reiter**. Chisa's Forte cuts space and she sees what is tied to what. There is a line running from an empty seat in a back room in Jinzhou, north, past the wall, past the eighth tower, past the ninth, out onto the crest road — and at the far end of it, four hundred years late and walking, is a grey unit who has never been to Jinzhou and does not know why her route corrected itself in the third month.

You never have to explain the string. Chisa reports it. It goes tighter. Then one evening at supper it goes slack, and Chisa says so, and everyone looks at the door before they know why.

### 5. THE UNUSED STAMP

> *Rinascita. The file that only closes when the person walks in and signs.*
> **Era:** the three years the pack was away, and one afternoon at the end of them. **Register:** harbour office. Clerks. One stamp.

**Premise.** Rinascita kept faith while the pack was gone. Augusta held Berth Nine, built a bench wide enough for nine, and had a **TORNATA** stamp cut — *returned* — which has never been used, and the **ATTESA** files sit unclaimed, and an ATTESA file only closes *when the person walks in and signs.*

Into this office, on an ordinary afternoon, walks something that cannot sign.

**The mechanism.** There is an ATTESA file with a designation on it rather than a name. It is very old. It has been transferred through six schisms and two archive fires and its cover has been rewritten four times in four hands. Nobody has ever pulled it, because ATTESA files are pulled by the person, and the person on this one is a product.

Lanze Reiter comes for it because she completed an errand. Four hundred years ago something took something out of her chest and told her she could have it back when she brought it a thing. She brought it the thing. There was nobody at the counter. **She has been carrying the receipt ever since** — which is the only paper she has, which is written in a script she can still read, and which is the reason she has never stopped walking, because an errand you have completed and cannot file is not finished, it is *pending*.

**The decision.** A junior clerk has to determine whether a doll counts as a person for the purposes of the register. There is no guidance. There is a superior who is at lunch. There is a queue.

> The clerk looked at the file, and at the counter, and at the queue, which was four people long and patient.
>
> The rule was that ATTESA closes on signature. The rule did not say by whom, because the rule had never been asked.
>
> She took the stamp out of the drawer where it had lived for three years in tissue paper, and she inked it twice because the pad had dried out and nobody had thought to change it, and she brought it down on the cover, hard, the way you do when a thing has to be legible in forty years.
>
> **TORNATA.**
>
> The ink was very black. She turned the file so the visitor could see it, which was a courtesy, and pointless, and she did it anyway.
>
> <font color=lightsteelblue>"…That is all of it?"</font>
>
> "That's all of it. You're returned. You were open for four hundred and six years and now you're closed." She squared the corner with her thumb, twice, which is a thing her hands do. "Next, please."
>
> Behind her in the office nobody looked up, because it was the afternoon, and files close all day.

That is the whole scene and it is one of the best things this crossover can do: **the first use of the stamp in the entire corpus, spent on a machine, by a clerk who was not authorised, on a Tuesday, and it counts.** Augusta had it cut for the Rover. It got used on a doll first. Augusta, told this later, says something four words long that Galbrena writes down.

**Rhymes it fires:** *Witnesses constitute record.* Sigrika stamping Denia's birthday into existence with a laboratory seal. Jinhsi ruling Mei into column 返 off the garrison log — *the log knows which direction you were walking.* The whole corpus's argument that personhood is conferred by nobody and recorded by anybody.

**Threads to pull:**
- What the errand was and who commissioned it and what is written on the receipt, none of which needs answering.
- Whether Augusta reserves a berth. *(She will. It will be numbered and it will not be nine.)*
- The clerk. Name her. Give her one habit. Let her turn up again in eighty pages doing something small and correct.
- Whether the thing taken out of her chest was ever going to be given back, and whether the counter was ever staffed, and whether the errand was busywork issued to a unit that would otherwise have had to be discharged into standing water. *(This last reading is the cruellest and the best: the errand was mercy. Somebody's supervisor did not want to drown her, so they invented four hundred years of walking. She has been kept alive by a lie that a kind person told a form.)*

### 6. THE FOURTH EDITION

> *A grey figure on a beach in Rinascita, at dawn, doing nothing.*
> **Era:** the first morning of everything. **Register:** the Row's docket, then one page of field log.

**Premise.** The corpus's most permanently open hook: the record of the wind's crossing to Mei's shore **splits three ways and the frontier court has declined to rule.** One edition came late and found Iuno already kneeling in the surf. One came early and lost to a sea strung and tuned low and singing. The third is missing its crossing chapter, its type bought out of every case in the city across a single spring, by a customer who paid in very old coin and wore a different face each visit.

Add a **fourth edition**. Do not resolve the other three. Just put a fourth copy in the case and let the docket note that four is worse than three.

In the fourth edition there is somebody else on that beach.

**The log.** She logged it. Of course she logged it; she logs everything; the manual required a route report and she has filed one every day for four hundred years and it is the only unbroken practice she has.

> `0507. Waterline. Object in surf, human, unresponsive. Assessment: recovery in progress by third party.`
> `0512. No action indicated.`
> `0540. Departed north.`

And in the margin of that page, in a different ink, added at an unrecoverable later date, in her own hand:

> *she was already saying a name.*

**Why she did nothing.** Give at least three readings and let the text refuse to arbitrate.

1. **Protocol.** A recovery in progress by a third party is not to be interfered with. The manual is clear. She has interfered before and it went badly and there is an entry for that too.
2. **Leva's discipline.** *My job is not to stop the jumping. It is to keep the channels open and the exits mapped so somebody is ready to pull survivors out of the wreckage.* Under this reading her not-moving is thirty years of professional judgement, and correct, and it cost her something, and she has never called it correct out loud.
3. **She was afraid.** There was a woman kneeling in the surf with a stranger in her arms, saying a borrowed name into wet hair as if trying it for size, and a machine four hundred years old stood forty metres up the strand and understood that whatever was happening there was the thing she had been built adjacent to and never once been inside of, and she went north.

**What it buys you.** Lanze Reiter becomes a **witness** — the corpus's highest office and its most ambiguous one. She has a page. The page is dated. The page can be brought into a room where four people are arguing about what happened on a beach, and set on a table, and it settles nothing, because it is a fourth edition, and four is worse than three.

And: **does the Architect own it?** The customer who bought out the type of the missing chapter paid in very old coin and wore a different face each visit. If Lanze Reiter's route reports were ever filed with a craft-house that was ever bought, then Nivora has been holding the only eyewitness account of the morning the story started, for a very long time, and has done nothing with it, because *doing nothing with it is more interesting.*

**Threads to pull:**
- Who else has a fourth edition. *(The Row keeps three. Somebody keeps four. Somebody keeps five.)*
- Whether Iuno, who has read all fifteen stories, has read this page. *(She would want it. She would not ask for it. She would leave it where Lanze Reiter could reach it, which is the difference between catching somebody and standing where they can reach you.)*
- Whether Mei ever learns there was someone on the beach, and whether her reaction is the generous one, and whether the generous one is worse.
- The wind. She lost that morning to a note that stayed where it was sung. There was a fourth party on that shore who *could* have leaned. This is a conversation between a wind with fewer than a sentence left and a machine that has been saving up for four centuries, and it should happen at a doorway, and it should be short.

### 7. 浪人 — THE WAVE PERSON

> *Ronin alone. Ronin comes out of the water.*
> **Era:** after. **Register:** the corpus's opening register, deliberately.

**Premise.** 浪人 is *wave-person*: a masterless drifter, and the word has been sitting in the corpus's own title the whole time. Every cycle in this cosmology opens the same way — *a girl comes out of the water under a new name, and somebody always arrives to meet her early: a wolf, a bard, a doll-maker.*

Lanze Reiter is gone. Choose your gone: drowned, dismissed, sealed, spent at the youngest gate, filed. And Ronin, masterless, walks into the sea, because the manual has a procedure for an unassigned unit and the procedure is standing water, and she is going to follow the procedure because following procedure is the last thing she has of hers.

**And the sea gives her back.**

Because that is what this sea does. In the other book the brine takes and the hands of the drowned pull you down; in this book the water is where the story starts, and it has *never once* kept a woman it was given. The two seas are the same sea. The frontier court declines to rule.

She comes out under a new name because she has to have one — the old one was a category — and somebody arrives early.

**Who arrives.** The best answer is **Chisa**.

Chisa is face-blind. Every human face is a smear of colour, and she navigates by voice, gait, and habit. And a doll's gait is a **manufacturing note** — *firmware, revision six, optional suppression, printed in eleven copies in this depot alone.* Bridget learned that the hum she had carried for thirty years as the essence of a dead woman was a factory setting, and it nearly took her legs out.

So: Chisa hears the walk before she sees anything, and she knows the walk, and she says the model out loud, and she is right, and it is the worst possible greeting.

And then she is wrong about the next thing, and the next, because a gait is issue and a *limp* is history, and Chisa is the finest reader of history-in-motion in the corpus.

> <font color=coral>"S-series. Or the one after. The heel comes down flat and then rolls, which is the suppression flag left on."</font>
>
> Water off the cloth onto the shingle. Nothing else.
>
> <font color=coral>"But you favour the left going uphill and the manual doesn't do that."</font> Chisa's head tilted a fraction. <font color=coral>"That's yours. Where did you get it?"</font>
>
> A long time. Long enough for the tide.
>
> <font color=plum>"…A step in a house. Fourth from the top. It announces you."</font>
>
> <font color=coral>"Good."</font> Chisa put her hand out, palm up, at the height where a hand is found by someone who cannot see faces. <font color=coral>"I can't see your face. I've never been able to see anyone's. It means I have never once looked at you and thought* catalogue.*"</font>
>
> <font color=coral>"Come up out of the water. You're in the way of the boats."</font>

**Why this pairing is the correct one.** Ronin's face is a mass-produced object, and Chisa is the one person in the world for whom it has never been one. Chisa cut herself out of her parents' lives because cutting was the only tool she had been given; Ronin was built to be cut away from. Chisa's Forte is a pair of enormous scissors, and the whole arc is her not using them.

**The refusal at the end.** DD1's ending gives the pawn the world: the Arisen becomes the Seneschal, the pawn becomes Arisen, the cycle turns. Offer it. Somebody will — Nivora would offer it with real warmth, which is what makes her Nivora.

And Ronin refuses it the way Kit refused *hero*:

> *"If this is heroism, then I don't want to be a hero. I want to be in Squad 5."*

Ronin's version is smaller and worse and better. What she wants is **a floor**. Three hundred and twelve people out of four thousand one hundred and twelve still have nowhere to sleep in Jinzhou, and she has read the number, and the number is the only number she has ever wanted to be part of.

> <font color=plum>"I'd like to be on a roll."</font>
> <font color=#fdb4fe>"Ohhh. How very *modest*."</font>
> <font color=plum>"A ward roll. With a household number, and a stove, and a column that says which direction I was walking."</font> She put the hat back on. <font color=plum>"You can keep the rest."</font>

**Alternative ending, if you want the other one:** she takes it. And the first thing she does with world-authorship is register a household. Four-one-two. It takes her the whole morning and she fills the form in by hand.

**Threads to pull:**
- The new name, and who gives it, and whether it is given the way Iuno gave Mei hers — *It's Mei, okay? You're called Mei. Now keep up.* The corpus's naming grammar is: **imperative, off-hand, non-negotiable, and it sticks.**
- Whether Lanze Reiter is actually gone. *(No. But leave three hundred pages between the question and the answer.)*
- The hat. What she does with the hat when there is finally a roof.

## III½. SHORTER SORTS

*Loose type. One paragraph each. Take what sets.*

**THE RIBBON.** The pale ribbon off the back of the helm is a quartermaster's unit marking, issued in bulk, and in the country that issued it pale cloth was also what you wore for a death. Nobody who could have told her the difference has been alive for three centuries. She has been wearing mourning as standard issue for four hundred years by administrative accident, or on purpose, and she has never said which, and she reties it every morning, and it is the only surface modification on the chassis, and **Technical Standard 3-D prohibits surface modification and states its reason without embarrassment: resale value.** A knot sits outside the standard. A knot comes undone whenever you like. She reties it instead. Teddy would clock this instantly and say something unbearable and correct.

**THE HAT.** Ronin's kasa is a roof she carries, and it keeps off rain, sun, and *lenses*. A doll's face is her manifest. She wears the hat outdoors and takes it off indoors, which is the exact inverse of Bridget, who wears sunglasses inside. Somewhere the two of them are in a room and one of them takes something off and the other does not, and neither comments, and the reader does the work.

**THE CLOTH.** The purple drag is longer than she is and it collects the road. It is a ledger written in dust. Someone will offer to wash it — Panhua, or Mrs Ren, or Cartethyia, who breaks things — and the offer will be kind and correct and it will be an enormous thing to accept, because the dust is the record, and washing it is the same verb as *striking a word in a cause column.* She should accept. It should take three pages.

**THE WAKESTONE.** She has one. She has had it four hundred years. She has never used it, because the person she would use it on told her not to, and because Denia has already delivered this world's ruling on the practice: **you can't out-want someone's death for them.** It lives in the chest cavity or in a coat, and Mrs Ren's line applies to it exactly: *"Whatever's in the coat. It doesn't get lighter."* The corpus is full of unspent things — the TORNATA stamp, cut and never used; the ATTESA files, unclaimed; the blank Fractsidus card; Iuno's ring; Chisa's collar in a tin with a six a.m. chime never yet played for anyone. Add a wakestone to that shelf. It belongs there.

**THE UNSPENT CHARACTERS.** Bookbinders' Row: *the house takes characters; you hear what you spend.* The wind is nearly out — fewer than a sentence's worth left. Lanze Reiter has been talking to almost nobody for four centuries. In the Row's economy she is **rich**, and she cannot read, and the fortune is in a currency she has no use for. The scene where she offers the wind some of it should be four lines long, in a doorway, and the wind should refuse, and then take one.

**THE TWO SECONDS.** §9.4 lists *delay between order and compliance exceeding two seconds* as an indicator. Ronin's delay is two point one. It has been two point one for eleven years. Neither of them has ever mentioned it and both of them count it every time. Put the number in a scene three times without explaining it, and the fourth time have it be one point four, because something got easier, and let nobody notice.

**THE GOOSE.** The tax-office goose turned right at half past four — the side it gave up four years ago — held it for a second at a blank wall on its blind side, and took the centre again. A goose that is famously blind on one side and famously bad-tempered, and a machine that walks the same route every day for four hundred years. They should meet. It should be a disaster. Chixia should have to write it up. Assessor Lü's remarks column should be involved.

**THE PULSE.** Baizhi took the Magistrate's pulse and got ninety-one and was told to mark it to Changli. She should take Lanze Reiter's and get ninety-one, exactly, and again ten minutes later, exactly, and put it in the second book — the four pages in a different hand at the back of her notebook that she has shown to nobody. She will not tell Chixia. Chixia will guess wrong on purpose.

**THE THIRD SEAT.** Yangyang's corner is fifty-three weeks old, not fifty-two, and Panhua has been putting the third seat's bowl on the shelf beside the stitched cloth bird for about three weeks and has told nobody. Cartethyia asked who the seat is for, on a tower, and **was told**, and refuses to repeat it. Now bring in a machine that does not eat and sit it down at a set place. Two empty seats in one room. The corpus's whole method says: give it no dialogue at all, and have somebody get up to wash something.

**THE MOBILISATION THAT NOBODY ORDERED.** Hsin's charge to the wind was *bring back many hands, little gust — bring back the kind that catch.* Nobody specified whose hands. Hands come when they are asked. The tremors at the youngest gate are closing, the third generation walks, and down the north face comes an uncounted older generation from the wrong direction with a mobilisation order signed by nobody, because a request went out into the wind and the wind is *the one traveller every border admits.*

**THE CHARGE.** She has carried a lance for four hundred years and never once used it, because a lance wants a line and a line wants a horse. At the youngest gate there is finally a line. She uses it once. Afterwards the lance is gone and she does not replace it, and nobody remarks on the empty mount on her back for eleven chapters, and then somebody does, and she says four words.

**THE NAME SHE HAS NEVER USED.** Ronin has never once said Lanze Reiter's name. Not as address, not as reference, not in eleven years. She begins speaking and the other one knows. The day she uses it is a whole scene and it should cost something, and the interesting problem is **what she would use** — the designation is a product class, and calling her by it is calling her by her SKU, so Ronin has been silent for eleven years out of a courtesy neither of them can articulate. *(Best solution: Ronin has to invent one. On the spot. Badly. And it sticks, because in this corpus names are given imperatively, off-hand, by someone who has no authority to give them, and they stick.)*

**THE COLUMN FOR AGE.** Every form has one. She gives a different number to every clerk, and every number is true, and she has never explained the units.

## IV. THE TYPE CASE

### Colours

Following the house convention (`<font color=...>` for present-day scenes; locals speaking Huanglong get *italics*):

- **Lanze Reiter — `lightsteelblue`.** Grey steel with a blue cast; sits clear of `royalblue` (Iuno), `skyblue` (Cartethyia), `paleturquoise` (Baizhi), `silver` (Lü).
- **Ronin — `plum`.** Dusty grape, matching the obi and the eyes; sits clear of `lavender`, `#bd9adf` (Sanhua), `mediumpurple` if you ever need a third.

### Register voices

**Lanze Reiter.** Four hundred years of route reports have shaped her speech into log format. Short declaratives, timestamps, assessments. Affirmative even when the content is grief, because the manual's forms have no field for it. She says *yes* and *no action indicated* and *departed north*. When she says something that is not a log entry, the sentence is grammatically strange, and it lands like a struck bell.

- *"No action indicated."*
- *"I have a page for that."*
- *"I logged it. I log everything. That is the only unbroken thing I have."*
- *"…That is all of it?"*
- *"I was built adjacent to it."*

**Ronin.** Plainer and slower than her voice looks. Pawn-formal underneath — the register that says *the road ahead is treacherous* — worn down over eleven years into something almost domestic, so that the formality only surfaces under stress or in front of strangers. Her killer register move: she reports things about herself in the third person when she is frightened, and in the first when she is not.

- *"I've made other arrangements."*
- *"I have eaten in houses that do not exist. I know what their bread was like."*
- *"This unit—"* (a stop; a correction that is never spoken aloud) *"—I would rather not."*
- *"I'd like to be on a roll."*

### Liturgy candidates

The series has verbatim-repeating lines and this pair can carry two or three of their own. Candidates, in decreasing order of confidence:

1. **"No action indicated."** — her log's standard closing. Devastating on the beach page. Devastating when someone else says it back to her, meaning something else entirely. Devastating the day she writes something else.
2. **"Everything that keeps faith long enough begins to resemble the faithful."** — already canon, already Hsin's, already the best sentence available. Use it three times: once as folk wisdom in a court, once as a field note, once said flatly to somebody's face as a diagnosis.
3. **"Where's the horse."** — Lupa's. Becomes the household's shorthand for *you are carrying a function you can no longer perform.* Gets said to Iuno at some point and lands very hard.
4. **"Two point one."** — the delay. Never explained.
5. **"Count the returning."** — canon, Jinhsi's, and it converts a ledger of drownings into a ledger with a second column.
6. **"Sit. It goes cold either way."** — Panhua's, for feeding people who do not eat.
