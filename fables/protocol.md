# RE-ENTRY PROTOCOL

*drafted before landing, per the kitchen table, four hours*

---

## Why the original works

Look at what your own scene actually does. Six people build Amy's re-entry rules at a kitchen table over four hours, and the design has five properties worth stealing:

1. **It is built cold.** Not during. Before, at a table, with tea, while nobody is in crisis and everybody can think.
2. **Every rule is owned, and named for whose tolerance it is.** Iuno's eleven minutes are *Iuno's* limit, stated as hers. Lupa takes first shift *explicitly for her own reassurance rather than Amy's*. The protocol admits out loud that it is partly for the people holding the rope, which is the only reason anyone can stand to follow it.
3. **There is a forgery check.** Amy states a *different* number on return, so the thing that comes back cannot fake being the thing that went in. You cannot verify yourself from inside.
4. **There is an object.** Cartethyia's felt doll, so the fingers close and nobody has to look at open palms. A prop that handles what words can't.
5. **The rule-setter discloses.** Mei says she made every mistake on that list personally and would like the next generation to skip that step.

Then Amy went in, and came out at nine minutes, per the rules.

## The rules

Draft. Swap anything that doesn't fit — I don't know your family, and rules 4 and 6 are the ones you have to write yourself.

### 1. State a different number.

One paragraph a week, same day, in the journal. It must name **one concrete thing you did for yourself with no justification attached** — and it has to be a new one each week. You may not re-report last week's.

This is the forgery check. The erosion you described is invisible from inside; by the time it's bad you'll have a good reason for every line item. What you can't fake is a blank column. Two blank weeks running is not a mood. It's the gate closing, and it means you go back and read section X.

### 2. Somebody has a hand on you — and say it's for them.

Pick one person in Berlin before you fly. Fixed slot, not "text me sometime." Tell them what you're afraid will happen, in the plain words, so that in October someone who met the August version can tell you whether the October version sounds different.

Say the honest half out loud: this is partly so the door back stays open. Lupa took first shift for her own sake and said so.

### 3. Track the frequency by ear.

Chisa knows before the instruments do. Your instruments are the big events — the career question, the business conversation — and they'll be the last to move. Name the small tells now, while you can still be honest about them, so they can't be rationalised in the moment:

- the portfolio goes back to daily
- you negotiate with yourself before eating
- the vegan thing goes at the first family meal and you feel **relief** rather than regret
- you stop writing entirely — or you start writing at a rate that means you're hiding in it

Any two at once is the signal. Not a verdict, a signal.

### 4. Set a limit, and make it your own tolerance.

**The conversation.** It's coming. Decide now what the true answer is, say it once, properly — and then don't re-open it. Not to protect the answer; to stop it becoming the ambient weather of eight weeks. Iuno's eleven minutes were about what *she* could stand.

*Write your actual sentence here before you land, so you're not composing it at a table with everyone watching.*

**The money.** You already know the gate doesn't open by argument, so remove the argument. Fix a self-directed sum for the two months **now**, before you land, before anything is a specific request. The rule is not that you spend it. The rule is that you don't justify individual items against it. You are allowed to want a thing without a case file.

### 5. Carry the object, and put it where the failure happens.

Not a souvenir — a working prop. It goes in the wallet, against the payment card, because that's the exact millisecond the gate closes. One line, in your handwriting. Candidates:

> *You just never included yourself in the math.*

> *A tree doesn't need a reason to grow.*

> *Just keep leaping.*

Fold it small. You don't have to read it. It has to be in the way.

### 6. Disclose one thing, to one person, out loud.

The habit you're breaking is routing the true sentence through a character. So: **one** true thing, **one** person at home, chosen in advance — because in the moment you will defer, every time. Your sister is the obvious candidate; you'd know better than me.

Not the whole year. Not the trips. One sentence that would normally have gone to Iuno to say.

### 7. Never do it alone.

Keep at least one structure that isn't family: the training, the friends, the writing at a rate you'd defend. Two months of nothing but the house is how the reset gets needed again.
