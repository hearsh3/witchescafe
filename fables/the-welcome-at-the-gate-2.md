# The Welcome at the Gate

Someone had put a pot on at Xuanfang Hold.

It stood upon the gatehouse stove, beside a basket of greens waiting to be washed. Steam passed through the open kitchen window. Beneath the eaves, two shirts dried in the sun; a porter slept against his unloaded cart. A woman was telling a Warden that if he meant to lean over her pot, he could at least stir it.

The Warden accepted the ladle.

Liangyu came from the covered walkway when she saw the first litter. She took its forward pole, called the waiting attendants by name, and began bringing the people from the Skyworks into the courtyard. A man on the second litter recognized the woman at the stove. She put down her spoon before he had finished saying her name.

Yangyang stopped beneath the arch.

For most of the road she had been watching the next place to put her feet. Now the familiar walls rose around her, and someone else was holding the litter, and Liangyu was coming back with both hands free.

“You found them,” Liangyu said.

“Mei and the others found me.” Yangyang looked toward the people being brought inside. “There are more rooms to search, but the machine won't take anyone else while the bindings hold. Amy can show your people where to go.”

“She already has. She was very particular about the white machine.”

“Nobody pulls the lance out,” Amy called from behind them. “I will become considerably more particular if anybody pulls the lance out.”

Liangyu's mouth trembled into a smile. She put a hand against Yangyang's cheek, briefly, then stepped aside for the last litter.

Beyond her, Iuno turned.

She stood beside the gatehouse steps with the moonwheel propped against the wall. Her living hand held a cup. Someone had found a pin for her loosened hair and placed it badly; a long silver-blue strand had escaped over her shoulder.

Mei saw the strand, the cup, the woman holding it.

“Iuiu.”

Iuno had time to put the cup on the step.

Then Mei launched herself across the remaining distance.

Her stride had none of its usual grace by the third pace, but the intention carried. Iuno opened her arm, braced one heel against the lowest step, and received the whole untidy arrival against her living shoulder.

They rocked backward together. Lupa caught Iuno's elbow before either of them could discover what came after the step.

“I've got you both,” she said. “Try to keep the reunion on this side of the wall.”

Mei had buried her face in Iuno's neck.

Iuno's hand moved over the back of her head, found the familiar shape beneath the dust, and tightened there. For a while she could only hold it. Mei's breathing was warm against her skin. It caught once, recovered, and caught again.

*Well,* the High Priestess said, very softly. *There goes everything we were going to say about composure.*

“I tried to reach you,” Mei said. “When the transport fell, I saw your hand, and then I couldn't—”

“I know. I caught the canopy. It was a dreadful substitute.”

Mei made a sound against her throat which might have become a laugh under better circumstances.

“Are you hurt?”

“My shoulder needs a clean dressing. Your buckle is also attempting to become part of me.”

Mei drew back an inch. Iuno's hand followed her, refusing the rest of the distance.

“That much is sufficient. You may leave the woman where she is.”

Mei looked at her.

Whatever she had meant to say did not survive Iuno's expression. She put her forehead against hers instead. Iuno closed her eyes, and Mei kissed her with the care the arrival had lacked.

Lupa looked away without removing her supporting hand.

“You could have started at this speed,” she observed.

Mei laughed into the second kiss. Iuno's thumb moved once behind her ear.

***

Yangyang was smiling when Lupa turned back toward her.

Her fingers rested at the ribbon Mei had put straight. She had been about to move toward the gatehouse bench when Lupa's ears lifted.

“Yangyang, look behind you.”

From somewhere beyond the courtyard well came a voice which had once called her away from every interesting thing in a courtyard.

“YANGYANG—!”

She turned.

“Suisui..?!”

Her sister came at her with Yangyang's spare coat over one arm, a borrowed shoe nearly escaping one heel, and no evident concern for the dignity of anyone present.

Dumpling abandoned her shoulder for a gatepost.

Suisui reached Yangyang before she could finish lifting her hands. The coat opened around them. One arm went behind Yangyang's shoulders, the other about her waist; then Yangyang drew a sharp breath, and Suisui's hands moved at once, higher, gentler, gathering her above the dressing.

“Oh, my poor Yangyang! You've gotten so thin! Is no one in Jinzhou looking after you? Do you not eat properly?”

Yangyang's cheek had been pressed into the familiar fold beneath her sister's chin. She could smell cold road dust, soap, and the faint sweetness of the hair oil Suisui had used for years.

She attempted to answer with the dignity of her posting.

“Jie, please. I'm not a child anymore. And I've been eating perfectly fine.”

Suisui drew back just far enough to inspect her, keeping both hands firmly in place.

“This will not do. This will not do at all. You need to eat more, darling. How else am I supposed to enjoy hugging you?”

“You seem to be managing.”

Her voice wavered on the last word.

Suisui's face changed. The teasing remained about her mouth; her eyes filled so quickly that Yangyang reached toward them before remembering her own bandaged fingers.

“I am managing beautifully,” Suisui said. “I have been preparing for this since Jinzhou. You might allow me to show some of the work.”

Yangyang's hand found the back of her coat.

At once Suisui pulled her close again.

Cold stirred between their collars.

Suisui stopped moving.

“And where do you think you're going?” she asked softly.

The Wind had begun to slip toward the outside of Yangyang's shoulder, making room. Suisui lifted the loose end of her scarf beneath the little current and brought it in with the younger woman's hair.

“I said Yangyang. I am prepared to be quite extravagant about who answers.”

The cold reached her throat.

Suisui bent her head. Her cheek rested against Yangyang's; the Wind gathered in the narrow shelter between them. The coat fell around all three, one sleeve hanging empty toward the paving.

For several breaths, Suisui said nothing at all.

Yangyang felt the trembling in the arms holding her. It was slight, determined, and beyond her sister's control.

“We came back,” she whispered.

“Yes. You did.”

Suisui's cheek moved against hers. A tear found the edge of Yangyang's ear.

“I should like a little while before either of you goes anywhere else.”

Yangyang closed her eyes. Her hand tightened at Suisui's back.

The Wind rested against the older sister's throat. There was no shoulder of her own to put beneath the embracing arm, no face to raise. Yet Suisui kept turning slightly toward her, allowing the cold to find another warm place whenever it lost its hold.

She had remembered how Suisui held people. She had forgotten the small, continuous adjustments.

Here was one. And another.

The scarf rose just enough to touch Suisui beneath the chin.

“There you are,” Suisui murmured. “I was beginning to think I should have to borrow a larger coat.”

***

Mei and Iuno eventually separated far enough for Amy to get between them.

She had approached with a perfectly serviceable remark about the state of the transport. Iuno looked at her, and the remark deserted her.

For a moment she stood with her hands at her sides.

Then Iuno held out her arm.

“Come here, sweetheart. I have spent the morning being unable to ask where you were hurt, and I am finding it very difficult to begin politely.”

Amy went into the embrace with enough force to move Mei backward half a step. Mei came around both of them, one hand at her daughter's back, and Iuno pressed her mouth against Amy's hair.

“Ma was hurt,” Amy said.

“She is going to tell me.”

“I had to help her, and I couldn't find any of you.”

Iuno's hand tightened against the back of her neck.

“I know how long that must have felt.”

Amy swallowed. Mei stroked the dusty place between her shoulders.

For a little while, nobody asked her to finish the account.

When she lifted her head, Iuno brushed the damp hair from her forehead with her thumb.

“There. You can begin insulting me whenever you feel able.”

“Your pin is crooked,” Amy said thickly. “It's doing almost nothing.”

“A promising recovery. You may improve it after someone has found you water.”

Lupa already had the flask open.

She passed it to Amy, then put a broad hand at the back of Iuno's neck and drew her forward. The embrace was brief, close, and careful of the crystal. Iuno's remaining composure nearly went with it.

“I lost the flask you gave me,” she said into Lupa's shoulder.

“You can tell me about that after you've sat down. I am enjoying finding the larger items where I left them.”

Chisa came next. She put her hand against Iuno's living wrist, where the pulse ran, and kept it there while Iuno touched her shoulder.

“I heard the moonwheel against the wall before we came through,” Chisa said. “It was good to hear you making your own noise again.”

“I shall try to make it less conspicuously next time.”

“I didn't mind. The scraping was reassuring.”

Cartethyia waited until Chisa moved aside, then embraced Iuno about the middle with all the sincerity of someone who had been holding it in for several miles.

“We found the driver,” she said. “He's alive. And my plant is still alive, although I need another pot.”

Iuno's hand settled among the crystal thorns with the confidence of long practice.

“We will find you one. Preferably somewhere that also sells replacement flasks.”

Ciaccona approached last. She looked at Iuno's loosened hair, the dust on Mei's shoulder where her forehead had rested, and Amy drinking with both hands around Lupa's flask.

“I have a remarkable amount to tell you,” she said.

Iuno drew her close by the uninjured shoulder.

“I was afraid I should never hear you complain again.”

Ciaccona's answer took a little longer than usual.

“You ought to know better than to entertain such unreasonable hopes.”

She stayed until Iuno let go.

***

By the gatepost, Yangyang had discovered that surviving the Skyworks did not equip her to survive her sister in front of witnesses.

Suisui had wrapped the spare coat about her shoulders. The two shells were secure in its inner pocket. The Wind occupied a fold of the scarf, where she had become very still and was consequently being suspected of listening.

“I have been eating,” Yangyang was saying. “Baizhi checks. Chixia brings things even when I haven't asked, and Lupa has opinions about every bowl I put down.”

“She's accurate about the bowls,” Lupa confirmed.

Suisui glanced toward her.

“Then I shall depend upon you to continue.”

“I was going to anyway.”

“Please don't arrange my meals over my head,” Yangyang said. “I am standing here.”

“Oh? Since when has my little Yangyang been so shy?”

Suisui smoothed a strand of hair behind her ear. Yangyang turned red beneath the hat.

“Sui—sui—”

“Mm. All these years, and you don't hang on to me anymore. I find that rather crushing.”

Yangyang looked down at her own hand, still clutching Suisui's coat.

“That is not how it was! You were the one who dragged me everywhere. I missed so many of my vocal lessons.”

“Oh, really? It all gets quite blurry, doesn't it? But I seem to recall a certain someone begging me to sneak her out to see the festival boats, and pestering me to keep it from Mother.”

“That was one evening. You were always sneaking out at night to watch the opera.”

Ciaccona, some distance away, turned her head.

Suisui noticed and adjusted her expression into something suitable for a respectable household.

“I took an interest in the arts.”

“You took the kitchen stool over the back wall.”

“Because somebody had hidden the ladder.”

“Mother had hidden the ladder.”

“And a very enterprising child showed me where.”

Yangyang drew breath to contest the description. Suisui touched the tip of her nose before she could begin.

“While we are remembering things, I recall a certain little critter who stole my box of osmanthus cakes. Hint: mice don't usually wipe their mouths with napkins after they eat.”

“I left you two.”

The courtyard became attentive.

Yangyang realized what she had said.

Suisui's smile grew very slowly.

“I have waited years for you to tell me that.”

“You knew already!”

“I knew about the napkin. The number remained a subject of considerable household discussion.”

The Wind moved inside the scarf.

It was a small movement. Unfortunately, Suisui was holding the scarf.

“You may stay exactly where you are,” she told the cold. “I have not forgotten that you were present for some of this.”

Yangyang looked toward the lifted fold with sudden interest.

“You remember what she was like.”

The Wind remembered the cakes cooling beneath their paper. She remembered Suisui counting them with one hand on her hip. Beyond that came the lamp-lit water, the festival boats moving so slowly that a child had asked whether someone should help push them.

She had eaten the larger cakes first.

The loose end of Suisui's scarf crept over Yangyang's guilty hand.

Suisui bent over both of them, laughing.

“There. A family resemblance at last.”

Yangyang attempted to remove the scarf. Suisui kissed her forehead before she could, and the attempt lost most of its force.

“I missed you,” Yangyang said, quietly enough that the words stayed inside the coat.

Suisui's laugh caught.

“I missed you too, darling.”

Then she pulled her close again, and the cold at her throat pressed nearer, and for once Yangyang supplied no correction about how old she was.

***

They were persuaded onto benches by the woman with the pot.

She had a practical objection to people embracing in the path of hot broth. Lupa agreed with her so promptly that a small alliance formed before anyone else could speak. Within minutes, cups had been put into hands, boots had been moved clear of the passage, and a healer had begun working through the collection of bandages.

Suisui let go of Yangyang long enough to thank the others.

Mei received both her hands and a look which made her lose the easy sentence she had prepared.

“She asked us to come,” Mei said at last. “I'm very glad we reached her.”

“So am I.” Suisui pressed her hands once. “When I have stopped wanting to cry every time I look at you, I will find something more useful to say.”

“You can sit with us until then.”

Cartethyia accepted her embrace at once. Amy looked briefly surprised, then returned it with the arm that wasn't holding a cup. Chisa put the cup down for her before it could spill. Ciaccona was drawn in while explaining that she had mostly provided accompaniment, an explanation Suisui appeared entirely content to disregard.

Lupa received her own thanks with both ears angled backward.

“She's going cold over there,” she said, pointing at Yangyang. “Take her the bowl before we have to warm it twice.”

Suisui kissed her cheek.

Lupa looked toward the stove as though its management had become urgent.

At the other bench, Iuno knelt in front of Yangyang while the healer fetched fresh cloth. She touched the edge of the sleeve clear of the dressing and looked up.

“May I?”

Yangyang offered the arm. Iuno supported it without pulling.

“I'm glad you found each other,” Yangyang said. Her eyes moved briefly toward Mei, sitting beside Amy.

“I'm glad you were there to be found.” Iuno glanced at the loosened ribbon, then back at Yangyang's face. “The message at the gate improved Suisui's temper toward the entire province. You should have heard the instructions she was giving before it arrived.”

Yangyang's mouth curved.

“She always becomes very polite when she is angry.”

“She asked a running messenger whether he might be persuaded to hurry.”

The laugh surprised Yangyang enough to hurt. Iuno waited through it, keeping the arm steady.

From Suisui's scarf, the Wind crossed to the living wrist beneath Yangyang's hand.

Iuno felt the touch and looked down.

“You found them,” she said softly. “Thank you for telling them where I was waiting.”

The cold stayed for a breath, then returned to the scarf. Iuno let her go. Suisui came back with the bowl, and the healer arrived behind her with enough clean cloth to interrupt everyone.

***

Amy waited until Iuno had a cup in her hand again.

Then she remembered her other report.

“Iuno! While you weren't here, Ma, she, she—”

“Ahem.”

Mei's cough possessed a clarity none of her earlier attempts at speaking had managed.

Iuno looked from one to the other.

“Oh? Take a breath, Amy. Your mother appears to be recovering hers.”

Amy drew herself up. She had been hugged, watered, and assured that several people intended to remain within reach. Her appetite for public service had returned in full.

“Ma caught Yangyang when she fell. That part was very good. I was proud of her.”

“Thank you, sweetheart.”

“Then she began remembering which ribbon Yangyang had worn to tea.”

Mei lowered her cup.

Iuno's eyebrow rose.

“A remarkably detailed examination.”

“She put it straight,” Amy continued. “Then there was hand-holding. Then they discussed a walk along the canal. I think the second bridge is involved.”

Mei turned toward her daughter.

“I had no idea you were paying such close attention to the itinerary.”

“I was looking at your abdomen. You were holding someone over a place that had been full of roof earlier that morning. The itinerary was being discussed right above it.”

Iuno's gaze moved to Mei's coat.

The healer had already examined the closed wound. Mei nevertheless put a hand over it with the instinct of a woman discovering that the day's difficulties had learned to cooperate.

“It was flirting,” Amy added conscientiously. “I wouldn't want that detail to get lost under the medical part.”

Across the narrow space between benches, Yangyang's spoon stopped halfway to her mouth.

Suisui followed her glance.

“Eat while it's warm, darling,” she said, with the exquisite courtesy of someone storing several questions for later.

Yangyang obeyed too quickly and had to wait with her mouth full.

Iuno looked at Mei.

*Our reunion has acquired a second programme,* the High Priestess observed. *The child has brought notes.*

“Well,” Iuno said, “I hope Yangyang's first compliment after that dreadful place was about something as lovely as her ribbon.”

Mei's expression softened before she could help it.

“I was glad to see her. I told her so.”

“I gathered that. You are usually quite legible when pleased.”

Amy leaned against the side of the bench, watching with interest.

Mei rested her cup between her knees.

“There is more I want to tell you about the afternoon we had. And today. When we have somewhere to ourselves.”

Iuno's amusement quieted by a degree.

She put her hand over Mei's, warm around the bruised fingers.

“I would like that.”

Mei turned her hand beneath hers and held on.

Iuno allowed the small silence to remain before looking back at Amy.

“Have you finished your account, or did your mother manage to propose a second excursion while somebody was bleeding?”

“Only the one. She did tell Yangyang not to throw herself into danger.”

Lupa made a sound into her cup.

Iuno closed her eyes briefly.

“Ah. There is the portion which requires immediate correction.”

“I corrected it,” Amy said. “Very promptly.”

“You are a credit to the household.”

Mei leaned her head against Iuno's shoulder, choosing the living side.

“I came all this way to be held.”

“You are being held, darling. You are also being discussed. We have grown quite accomplished at doing both.”

Amy's grin broke through the last of her solemnity.

Mei reached across Iuno's lap to pinch the fabric at her daughter's knee. Amy caught the hand, held it for a moment, and forgot to let go after the joke had finished.

Ciaccona watched from beside Cartethyia.

“I expressed relief that you weren't at the Skyworks,” she told Iuno. “I see now that I misjudged the entertainment.”

“You should have more faith in me.”

“I shall revise the relevant verse.”

“There will be no verse,” Mei said, with very little confidence.

Cartethyia considered this while breaking a piece of steamed bread.

“We could leave out the bridge.”

Yangyang put her face in her hands. Suisui rescued the bowl from her lap before any broth was lost.

***

The courtyard grew quieter by degrees.

The wounded from the Skyworks were taken to the infirmary. Outside the gatehouse, the porter woke and began disputing the number of sacks he had delivered. A little girl came to fetch the dry shirts and was given a bun for carrying back the basket. Liangyu returned from finding beds for the rescued workers to discover that someone had saved her bread beneath an overturned bowl.

The wrapped Prism rested beside Suisui, its light passing faintly through the cloth. There were things to ask Hsin, and a man they had yet to catch. Here, the kitchen woman wanted to know who had finished the pickled greens and left the empty dish for her to discover.

For now, the healer told Yangyang to keep her shoulder still while the bandage settled, and Suisui found this an excellent reason to put an arm around her.

The Wind lay along the warm fold between them.

Suisui had asked for a second, wider bowl. Steam rose from it, wandered beneath the scarf, and returned to the water. Every so often she shifted her hand to leave a little more room.

“What did you do with the napkin?” Yangyang asked at last.

Suisui looked down at her.

“The one with the osmanthus crumbs? Mother washed it.”

Yangyang turned her head so quickly that she forgot the healer's instruction. Suisui steadied the shoulder at once.

“Mother knew?”

“Darling, you folded it into a square and left it beside an empty cake box. Mother was disappointed principally in your assessment of her intelligence.”

The scarf stirred against Yangyang's sleeve.

“You could have told me,” she said to the cold.

Suisui laughed, then pressed her cheek to the top of Yangyang's hat.

Across from them, Amy had acquired the task of fixing Iuno's hair. It required Iuno to sit still, which required Mei to stop making her laugh. Lupa was arguing with Cartethyia over whether a chipped soup bowl constituted adequate accommodation for a camellia. Chisa listened to the bowl when it was tapped and said it would hold soil. Ciaccona began describing the advantages of a plant which already smelled faintly of lunch.

Mei heard Yangyang laugh and looked over.

Their eyes met across the little space. Mei smiled. Yangyang returned it, still warm beneath the hat, then settled more comfortably against her sister.

Iuno's hand rested at the back of Mei's neck. Mei leaned into it while Amy struggled with the crooked pin.

“If you keep moving,” Amy said, “I shall have to tell everyone about the way you looked at her.”

“You have been doing that for ten minutes.”

“I have further details.”

Iuno began laughing again.

Under the arch, the next watch took its place. The pot was refilled; someone fetched another stool. Suisui loosened the coat around Yangyang when the sun reached their bench, then tucked its empty sleeve where the Wind could rest in the warmth.

At last Yangyang began to explain why leaving two osmanthus cakes had seemed generous at the time.

Her sister listened with her arm about her.

Nobody was in a hurry to believe her.
