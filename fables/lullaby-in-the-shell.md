# Lullaby in the Shell

*a reading of Denia, dealt backward*

---

*By and by, I made a friend within frozen light.*

— «Dasvidaniya», trad., attribution contested

---

Winter kept the house's hours now.

Night at the pool: steam standing off the water in slow ropes, the amber veins banked low like coals that had agreed to wait. Under the surface a girl slept with her hair fanned wide, ink deciding against itself, and the veins flickered when she dreamed, which was, by the household's count, most of the time.

The coven sat at the waterline in a loose crescent with tea. Upstairs the snow leaned on the windows. Down here the oldest lamp in the house burned at the pool's edge, four centuries in service, trimmed nightly, because that is what a lamp is for.

"She dreams all day," Elaina said, chin on her knees. "The amber goes like a projector. It's rude to watch without a program."

"Then we'll print one." The deck came out of Black Swan's sleeve, out of silk, out of somewhere. The backs were red — a deep wet red, flowers stamped in darker red, hibiscus by the shape of them, the flower that keeps one day's hours and signs its work at dusk. "A bedtime story. House rules: I deal, the cards talk, everyone else drinks their tea."

"Your cards run backward," said Herta.

"Everything true does."

She dealt onto the water. Seven cards, face-down, and the pool accepted them; they rode there on their own small tides, red on black, and the sleeper's amber rose toward them like interest.

## I. «до»

Snow first. Snow before anything — the fat unhurried kind, each flake taking its full salary of air.

A smell of honey and burnt sugar riding on wool: cake, layered, somebody's whole afternoon. She is small enough to be carried and is being carried. A wall. A door in the wall. Light through frosted glass, and the glass is curved, so the light arrives curved, a lamp bent into a crescent, and she reaches for it because it is the one warm-colored thing in the world.

The wool sets her down on the step. Cold climbs into her boots and she lets it, interested.

A woman's shape against the snow-light, already three steps gone. Turning. A hand up. The word crosses the cold like a bell across water:

"До свидания."

The wave. The snow taking the shape back a flake at a time, in installments. Warmth arriving on her right side, which means the door has opened, which means the story is over.

That is the whole memory. She has carried it her entire life the way other children carry a blanket: by one corner, everywhere, until the color wore through to thread. When the file asked her, years later, for one pleasant memory — the file had a field for it; the file had a field for everything — she entered this one, and the intake officer read it back to her in a flat voice, *abandonment, doorstep, winter*, and she corrected the officer's tone and let the content stand.

Pleasant, she wrote. Comforting.

She is a careful witness and an unreliable narrator and she has always known the difference; the difference is the one luxury she was issued.

At the waterline, Herta set her cup down with the click of a woman opening a lecture. "Look at the cut," she said. Three inks came out; the napkin suffered. They reached into a farewell for a name and their hand came out holding the other end."

## II. LULLABY IN THE SHELL

A room of cribs like a room of parentheses. Hers is third from the wall. The neighbor crib wears a tag gone smudged at the top — *K—*, then weather — and stands made-up and empty the whole time she knows it, sheets changed weekly for somebody the schedule keeps expecting.

On the ward wall, stenciled at adult eye level, the house doctrine:

YOUR FLESH IS A CAGE.

She learns to read on it. It is the longest sentence in the room and the only one with a verb, and for years she thinks *cage* is a kind of promise, the way *crib* is, the way *shell* is — a word for the shape that holds you. Nobody corrects her. The error keeps her warm and she files it under useful.

Days have a grid. Lights, meals, calibration, the gray handheld with the falling blocks — LAHAI-ROI BLOCKS, the cartridge says, a city she has yet to believe in — lines assembling, lines vanishing, the counter feeding her progress and eating it in the same motion. She gets very good. Getting very good changes the speed at which everything vanishes. She notices this at six and puts it away for later like a coin.

The whisper arrives the way frost does: present at the edges first.

*Little tenant,* it says, in the dark between two heartbeats, in the meter of a lullaby played backward. *I know a game with no pieces. Nothing falls there. Nothing clears. Nothing keeps score, nothing loses, nothing is asked of anything. Come play where nothing needs you.*

She asks the day-tech: do you hear —

"Ventilation," says the day-tech.

Next season: "Pipes."

The year after: "Your own pulse, sweetheart. Ears do that in quiet rooms."

The whisper improves. It learns her vocabulary as she does; it does the voices. *Your neighbor had a room with me,* it says, silk over the empty crib. *Ask the sheets.* It sings the golden verses on the bad nights — *my daughters keep a dance for you, nothing aches where nothing is, no door, no test, no morning* — patient as arithmetic, and she lies in the crib she has outgrown with her hands over her ears, which does nothing, because the whisper rents the inside.

High in the ward wall there is a booth with a pane of frosted glass.

Regulation says lights-out is total. The booth breaks regulation gently, nightly: a lamp behind the frost, bent by the curve of the pane into a low warm crescent — the same shape as a door in the snow, though it takes her years to run that ledger. The pane keeps its keeper to itself. She can hear, some nights, a hum.

On the worst night — the whisper deep into its golden verses, the ward's dark gone thick as held breath — the hum changes. It comes down through the glass, takes the whisper's meter, and bends it: same tempo, opposite lean, a melody walking home instead of away. Three chords, moonlight-flavored, then a phrase that rises where the whisper sank.

The whisper hates being rhymed at. It goes out the way tides go out: claiming it meant to.

She lies in the quiet with her heart doing something unregulated and listens to the hum finish its thought. It stops mid-phrase, every time — three chords and the rise and then a held nothing, an ellipsis with a pulse in it — as if the ending were rationed. As if the ending were owed somewhere and the hummer declined to pay it here.

At shift's end, once, very small, through the glass, in the language of the word she was cut from:

"До встречи."

*See you soon.* The casual one. The pocket one.

She turns the two farewells over for years, one in each hand. *До свидания* is marble — formal, cold-veined, final in the mouth of whoever said it to her, and hers now, inherited, load-bearing. *До встречи* is a pebble. Warm from being held. A promise wearing work clothes.

She gives the first one, all her life, to everyone.

She keeps the second in the boot of herself, with the charm.

She learns the null face early and wears it like a uniform. The trick is to answer with the noun and keep the verb for yourself. Rooms stay rooms. Doors stay doors. The pen nods along the flatline and everyone is satisfied, and satisfaction is the currency that buys her the courtyard hour.

In session sixty-one, question six arrives with music under it — three chords, moonlight-flavored, riding low beneath the examiner's voice, and her pulse stands straight up like a struck string.

The pen pauses.

She watches it pause. She watches the hand hold still over the trace for the length of one long breath — a ringed hand, or a scarred one, or neither; the light in that room was bad on purpose — and then the pen comes down and writes, in the calm print of official truth:

*Stable. Within tolerance.*

The machine's line, on the record, stays flat.

She checks the file later. She has ways; she is small and the archive is bored. There it is, session sixty-one, question six: *stable, within tolerance,* and pinned beneath the words, like a flower pressed by someone who could be executed for gardening, her heart's actual leap — visible to anyone who knew where the trace had been trimmed.

So. The record can be kind.

The record can be *made* kind, by a hand willing to hold still and lie in print.

She is nine. She files this under load-bearing and builds on it for the rest of her life: every clean goodbye she will ever manufacture, every smiling exit, every kindness forged in triplicate — the whole architecture goes down onto this one footing, this pen, this pause, this falsified flatline lying over a live heart like a blanket. Somebody hid her pulse to keep it. She learned the trade by being its beneficiary.

---

One more artifact from the ward years, entered into evidence:

MOTOR CALIBRATION — FINE. Materials: paper, wax crayons (8). Prompt: THE SKY.

Above her head the doctrine of the house held that the sky was an error of optimism, a lid, a nothing wearing blue. She was seven and had seen it twice, through transit glass.

She drew it swirling. Stars with wakes, as if stars went somewhere. Wind made visible, rolled up like bolts of cloth. A moon fat as bread and twice as close, and under it, small, a town with every window lit, which was pure invention, which was the point.

The audit, next quarter, found her file short by one sky.

Losses were logged. The log survives. The sky remains at large.

## III. THE LONG TABLE

Dinner is the one ceremony the Architect keeps.

A table built for twenty, laid for two. Candles doing their theater. The child sits where the place card says — the place card says SPECIMEN in a lovely hand, and then one year, with no comment, DENIA, and then one year, with no comment, ДЕНИЯ, as if the name were being slowly translated home — and across the acreage of white cloth sits the woman who owns the building, the grid, the crib, the sky-shaped hole where a drawing used to be.

"Eat your medovik."

"I hate medovik." She takes a second slice.

"Amanojaku." The Architect says it fondly, the way collectors say *mint condition.* "The little spirit that answers everything backward. I had one in a story once; audiences adore them; the trick is they're the easiest instrument in the orchestra. You want the opposite? Score the opposite. Opposition plays itself, dear. I'll show you the fingering someday."

The child eats her contraband cake and studies the fingering from the inside.

"Where did I come from?" she asks, age ten, over soup.

"You wandered in." The Architect's spoon describes a small elegant orbit. "Straight through my door, four years old, one second in the exhibit hall — one second, barely, a camera-flash of the dark — and it nearly unmade you. I kept what was left. Waste offends me."

"Last year you said you grew me."

"Last year that was true."

The candles do their theater. The child files both versions in the drawer where the versions go — there are three in there now; the third involves a doorstep and gets told only when the Architect wants something — and understands, with the sudden flat clarity of ten, that the drawer is the point. A girl with one origin can be stolen by it. A girl with three has been given a lifetime of checking locks.

"Every story needs a before," the Architect says, watching her file it. "I write excellent befores."

---

At dessert the doctrine comes out, as it does, with the good brandy she pours for herself and the winter air she pours for the room.

"Lives come up like winter wheat." The Architect at the window, glass in hand, the city's grid burning below her in its assigned constellations. "Scythed and sown, scythed and sown, and the field forgets each stalk by spring. One name skips the scythe, dear, exactly one." She turns; the candlelight has an understanding with her face. "Agony. A famous end is the one estate that passes to no creditor. I give my children famous ends. You will thank me in the only tense that matters."

She raises the glass an inch, to the window, to the grid, to her own reflection stationed in it.

"Let them say," she offers, "that I walked with the crib of the void."

The child looks at the black window. The child has personally audited the void's promises since the age of frost, nightly, from inside, and has notes.

"Stars die upward," the Architect adds, kindly, sensing resistance and pouring doctrine on it. "The light keeps appointments centuries after the fire is out. That's immortality, dear: be light. Be light, and let the body be the spent thing it was always scheduled to be."

"May I be excused."

"Home by midnight."

"And after midnight?"

"After midnight, nothing fits you." A smile with excellent posture. "Ask the pumpkin."

The child goes up the great stair with her stolen cake in a napkin and her origins in their drawer, and begins, that year, a private census. A bottle cap from the courtyard. A chord, three bars, moonlight-flavored, held in the head, past audit. A pebble of a word in a language she was cut from. Unauthorized components, acquired one at a time, smuggled into the design like contraband cargo into a licensed hull — because she has read her own schematics by then, she has ways, and the schematics list her parts to the last coefficient, and she has decided, with the cold hilarity that will one day be mistaken by an entire campus for laziness, to run a surplus.

## IV. THE AVIARY WINDOW

Startorch Academy processed her under a cover story so complete it included dental records and a grandmother.

Course selection, first morning: the catalog fell open on the counselor's desk like a game board, majors in columns, requirements descending, and she stood over it with her hands in her pockets and played it the only way she knew — rotating the falling pieces, looking for the fit that cleared the most lines with the least motion. Heliodic Theory demanded mornings. Applied Runework demanded evenings. Resonance Studies demanded *enthusiasm*, printed right there in the catalog, and she put it back down as one puts down a live thing.

She chose the major whose lecture hall faced the aviary.

The counselor asked about ambition; the form had a field for it. She looked at the field for a while.

*To be excused,* she wrote, and the counselor read it, and laughed, assuming a joke, and she let the assumption stand, because she had learned by then that a truth wearing a joke passes every checkpoint in the world.

---

Nastasha found her in the second week, at the piano annex, standing outside the practice-room door with the exact stillness of someone pretending to wait for someone.

"You can come in. It's a rental hour, the acoustics are wasted on me alone."

"I don't play."

"Then sit. Sitting's free." And when Denia sat: "Where are you from? You have a from-somewhere face."

"Nowhere in the catalog."

"Everywhere's in some catalog." Nastasha's hands ran a scale like water finding its level. "I collect cities. Want some? I do them by key." And she did them by key, week after week, rental hour after rental hour, a geography lesson scored for one piano: the capital in C major, all avenues and audit; the coast town in A minor, salt in the pedals; and a northern city, once, in D-flat — bells under ice, stalls of layered honey cake going amber in the cold, streetlamps bent by frost into low warm crescents —

Denia's mouth flooded. Sourceless. She sat very still and let the city finish.

"You've been there," Nastasha said, hands settling.

"I've been here."

"Mm." A held look, three chords long. "My mistake."

The letters resume that term. She writes them in her head, mostly; sometimes on scrap, in a cipher of her own laziness; always to the same address.

*Dear Dasvidaniya. The new roommate situation is a girl who recites formulas at two in the morning to keep herself honest. This is noise. I have adjusted my nap schedule around the noise. The adjustment costs me nothing. I sleep in lectures now, which the campus finds hilarious, and the campus's finding it hilarious also costs me nothing. Nothing continues to be my strong suit. — D.*

*Dear Dasvidaniya. There is a dish of candy outside the medical office, freely offered, which makes it worthless as theft, so I have introduced a licensing regime: two pieces, one returned. The doctor pretends not to keep count. I pretend not to notice him pretending. The economy thrives. — D.*

*Dear Dasvidaniya. The formula girl filled a blank exam answer with an unrelated equation and got partial credit and told me about it like a war story. I laughed at her. For a very long time. My ribs hurt, which I am reporting as a malfunction. — D.*

The census, meanwhile, runs its quiet catastrophe. A bottle cap; a chord; a pebble of a word; and now a laugh with her name in it, a seat saved without comment, a bird journal thrust under her nose at six in the morning — *new species, white bird, look* — a whole aviary of unauthorized components, acquired daily, undeclared.

A remote audit finds her that spring. The auditor stays off the page; she sees only the finding, later, with her clearances, one line in a maintenance log flagged for the principal's eyes:

COMPONENT CENSUS EXCEEDS DESIGN. SOURCE OF SURPLUS: UNRESOLVED.

She reads it in the archive twice. Then she goes out and acquires three more components before dinner, on principle, and eats sour candy in the stairwell until her eyes water, so the tears will have a listed cause.

In the water the amber stuttered — three bars of something grave taken at speed, a music with its collar torn — and the sleeper's hair moved as if a current had opinions.

Black Swan looked at the card for the length of the stutter. Then she slid it back into the deck, face-down, and squared the edges.

"We know that one," Mina said, to the water, in the voice harbors use for weather already survived. "Sleep through it. You did the first time."

## V. MILLE-FEUILLE

The ward ceiling has forty-eight tiles, and the girl in the bed is setting a table.

Mille-Feuille by the vent. Pavlova at the corner, structurally unsound, sentimental favorite. Kasutera in the good light. Medovik — she places Medovik dead center, over her own face, where the doctrine used to hang in another white room, and looks up at it every morning with the private satisfaction of a hostess who has finally gotten the seating right. The long table, repossessed. Candles optional. Guests: the machines, the tile called Snow, and the seven-day arithmetic, which she has decided to treat as a dinner companion with dreadful manners, since it insists on attending anyway.

This is the week the world will later read from the outside: the refusals, the bargain, the birthday. From the inside it is mostly logistics and one long letter.

The phone call, for instance.

From outside the booth: a girl performing a mother, a lie with a call clock reading 00:00, sustained for the length of a filial duet. From inside the booth: a kitchen. She builds it while she talks — she has had years of practice building rooms beyond audit — copper pans by seniority, a window with actual weather in it, a samovar with a temper, honey cake going amber on a rack, and at the table, aged forward with great care, a woman in wool, listening. Down the dead line, in the shape of small talk, Denia reads her the collected letters. All of them. Years of them, compressed to a mother's minute: *I'm eating. There's a girl who recites formulas. There's a doctor who counts candy. My ribs malfunction regularly. Don't send anything —*

which, in the original cipher, has always meant: *send everything, send it all, I would take the postage-stamp of your shadow —*

"— next year. I promise. Tell papa."

She hangs up the silence gently, the way you set down someone sleeping.

---

The protocol gets installed on the birthday evening, between the candle and the lullaby, in the eleven relaxed minutes when Sigrika believes the night is ordinary.

"If I ever come back wrong," Denia says, to the ceiling, from the floor, in the tone of a girl proposing a snack, "quiz me."

"You're not going—"

"Quiz me. Candles."

A pause with a whole examination board in it. "That's the entire protocol? Candles?"

"Wrong answers walk." She rolls her head over; the lamplight does what it can. "Promise me the walking part. That's the load-bearing part. Something wearing this face gives you a grand answer, a beautiful answer, an answer that *explains* — you walk."

"And the right answer?"

"Small," says Denia. "The right answer will always be small. It's owed."

Sigrika looks at her for three chords. Files it, visibly, in the drawer where she keeps things too heavy to open standing up. "Quiz you. Candles. Walk on grand."

"Top marks." A yawn, manufactured to specification. "You can really work wonders."

---

Three in the morning, the ward, the last week. On the monitors: a girl asleep. In the bed: a girl with a toy at her ear.

The Dream Maker's reservoir holds one recording. She lies with the device against the pillow and her eyes shut and listens to it the whole way through, once, the way you check contraband before the border — a heartbeat, at rest, unhurried, close, recorded through cloth or years or glass, provenance sealed even from the machines, even from this account —

and whatever she hears in it, her face, on the monitors, reads *stable. Within tolerance.*

She has learned from the best how a record is made kind.

## VI. STILL WATER

The first thing the water gives her is the quiet.

She has shared her skull since before her own memory — a tenant with one adjective and centuries of patience, a voice renting the inside, doing the voices, working the night shift — and now the rooms of her stand open, aired, swept, hers. All that floor space. She drifts through it at first like a subletter afraid of the landlord's return, checking corners, checking the dark between two heartbeats where the whispering used to live.

The dark between two heartbeats holds dark. Then the next heartbeat. That's the whole inventory.

She keeps listening for the tenant the way a tongue keeps finding the gap where the tooth was. The gap holds. The gap goes on holding, night after night — down here it is all night, and all of it soft, lit from the far side by a low warm crescent that keeps its shift off the books — and slowly, the way ice lets go of a shore, she stops bracing for the voice and starts hearing what the voice was over.

Small. Regular. Unarmored. Close. Knocking from the inside.

She has been introduced to everything in the world except this.

Her own heart, with the noise off, meeting her at last. *So that's you,* she thinks, in the first fully private thought of her life. *They kept you off the books.* Sixty sessions of flatline, one falsified spike, a designation entered from available material — and here is the available material, going about its work in the dark, unaudited, unlisted, on time. She listens to it the way she once listened to a lamp: as proof of a keeper.

The tenant's one adjective, all those years, was *endless.* Endless dark, endless game, endless nothing-needs-you.

She lies in the ended dark and gets the joke.

The laugh goes up through the water as bubbles, which is the shape a laugh takes when a bubble-maker does it properly, and somewhere above the surface, at an unreasonable hour, a woman with tea says *did you hear that* and a woman with cards says *she did.*

---

Sentences arrive from above like weather.

*Save the sour ones for Denia. She'll want to make the face.* — a front of warm air, moving through.

*Two candles. The form recurs annually. I filed—* — this one comes from farther, from a fence line on a Sunday, carried down some channel the household keeps discreetly open, and it lands in the water like light through frost, curved.

*She'll take the window room. She'll complain about the window room. Both.* — Herta, three inks, planning in the future tense with citations.

She learns the grammar of it, down there. Every sentence about her that assumes a tomorrow puts a floor under her feet. The tenant built with nothing; the house builds with tense. She lies on the accumulating floor of her own future, listening to it be constructed overhead in ordinary voices, and does the one piece of arithmetic left to her, the surplus census, final audit: a bottle cap, a chord, a pebble of a word, a laugh with her name in it, a registry of one entry, stamped, a saucer of sour candy replenished daily against her waking, a lamp —

Component count exceeds design.

Source of surplus: resolved.

---

Some evenings a keyboard walks out over the water.

Three chords, moonlight-flavored — the old three, the booth three, the question-six three — and then, one evening, past where they have always stopped: a fourth. A fifth. The phrase goes down hand over hand like someone descending to a bedside, unhurried, certain of the depth, and settles into a cadence she has waited for since the ward wall taught her to read.

Finished. Somebody finished it.

The ending was owed somewhere, all those years, and here is where; the ration was for this. The cadence comes to rest the way a person sits down beside sleep, and stays seated, and the water holds the resolved chord in suspension the way amber holds an afternoon.

Under the surface, the sleeper turns toward it, the way plants do it: slowly, and with her whole life.

She dictates one last letter into the dark, to the old address, and the dark takes dictation kindly, having retired.

*Dear Dasvidaniya. Final notice: I'm closing the account. You were good to me, in your way — the one warm thing on file for a long time, and I know what you cost, and I kept you anyway, and I'd keep you again. But I've had the name re-cut. Turns out the meeting-half was inside it the whole time; ask the philologist upstairs, she has diagrams. Forward my mail. — До встречи, D.*

The pocket word. The pebble. Warm from being held for fifteen years.

Above her, past the water, past the frost of the surface, the low warm crescent burns on at the waterline, trimmed nightly, and she rises toward it through her own quiet like a girl coming up a great stair, past midnight, out of uniform, unfit for anything but arrival —

stone lip. Kettle-noise. A saucer scraping wood.

"…Sigrika?"

"At school," says the future tense, pouring. "Eat something first."

## VII. «свидания»

The last card turned itself over — Black Swan's ran backward, and the courteous ones announced themselves — and the coven leaned in over the water.

Snow. A door. A woman's shape, three steps gone, turning, a hand up. A word crossing the cold.

"That's the lab," said Viviane. "Look at the glass in the door. Frosted. Curved."

"That's a dormitory," said Elaina. "Look at the ground. Two sets of bootprints, and the second set arriving."

"Look at the cake," said Herta, ink already uncapped. "Boxed. Cafeteria issue. That places it—"

"There's a cake in the memory too," said Viviane. "Honey. Layered. It's the same picture."

"It can't be the same picture."

"It's a card," said Black Swan. "It can be whatever's true."

They looked at it a while longer, the six of them, the snow on the card refusing to date itself, the woman on the card refusing to age in either direction, the word on the card facing the door.

"Which way is it facing?" Elaina asked, finally. "The goodbye. Is it being said to her or by her?"

Black Swan gathered the deck, and the last card went in among its sisters, red flower down, riding.

"It's facing," she said, "whichever way she is."

In the pool the amber had gone quiet and even, the flicker of a projector at the end of its reel, and the sleeper slept on toward morning with her hair come to rest, and the lamp at the waterline burned with four centuries of practice, and upstairs, on the calendar the engineers keep, the first snow of the cold cycle stood three weeks out, penciled, recurring annually.

## coda

Spring, after the return. The coven house keeps its own weather.

Denia comes down late — she comes down late everywhere now, on principle, having a schedule to run away from at last — and stops in the doorway of the music room.

Above the piano, taped at a child's eye level, yellowed at the corners by more years than the tape was rated for: a crayon sky. Swirling. Stars with wakes, as if stars went somewhere. Wind rolled up in bolts. A moon fat as bread, and under it, small, a town with every window lit.

The audit found the file short by one sky. The log survives. The sky, it turns out, also survives, at a child's eye level, above middle C, in a house at the bottom of the world.

Phrolova is playing. Three chords, and past them, the fourth, the fifth, the whole descent, the cadence coming to rest beside her like a chair drawn up — she plays it through without turning around, the way you keep your eyes on the road when the passenger is finally, finally asleep.

Denia stands in the doorway for the length of the phrase.

"The lamp," she says.

"It's ancient," says Phrolova, hands moving. "It stays lit."

The phrase settles. The house does its morning noises overhead: Herta prosecuting the newspaper, Elaina defending the jam, a kettle with a temper, a harbor drinking tea. Denia crosses the room, and sits on the bench's far end, uninvited, comfortable, the way she sits everywhere, and watches the hands the way she once watched a pane of frozen light: as proof.

"Play the old part," she says. "The three."

"The three are inside the whole now."

"Play it anyway."

Phrolova plays it anyway. Some requests are errands.

---

Later — the same spring, an ordinary Tuesday — Denia writes one letter on actual paper, at the kitchen table, in front of everyone, shielding the page with her arm like a schoolgirl, which fools nobody, which is its own kind of privacy.

She addresses it with a single word instead of an address.

Mina takes it without comment, the way harbors take cargo, and walks it down to the waterline, and sets it on the dark, and the water accepts it; the envelope rides there on its own small tide, white on black, and then the tide has it, and then the tide handles its own routes.

"Will it arrive?" Elaina asks, at the window.

"Everything arrives," says Mina. "Ask the weather."

Denia is already back at the table, licking honey off her thumb, complaining about the window room she asked for, being audited by a saucer of sour candy, three pieces, replenished daily, and taking two, and putting one back.
