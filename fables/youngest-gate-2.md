The south shelf rose above the water tanks in six natural steps. At the top, the highland opened beneath them.

The Norfall plains reached toward Jinzhou in long folds of brown grass and pale earth. Tacet scars crossed them from the northern ridges, violet-black at the center, silver along their raised edges. They branched across the country with the intimate geometry of a Resonator’s mark: a wound grown into a language, each stroke following the land’s muscle.

Life occupied every space the strokes allowed.

Shuanghe gathered around its ancestral hall in a square of grey roofs. Cooking smoke rose from three courtyards. Farther west, another village lay beside a green irrigation cut, its footpaths bright with people returning from fields. A flock of white birds lifted from a flooded terrace, turned above a seam of glassy Tacetite, and settled in the next field.

Old trees held the roads together. Jujubes leaned over boundary walls. Willows followed water that had changed course around the scars. One vast scholar tree grew directly through a split in the plain, its roots saddling purple stone, half its trunk black from an old strike, its crown green enough to catch the entire sunset.

Beyond all of it, Jinzhou rose in terraces.

The outer walls held the late light first. Roofs climbed behind them, bronze and red and rain-dark blue. At the highest terrace, City Hall stood above the city, its long face turned west. The top windows burned gold. The ninth tower cast a thin shadow down the north road.

Yangyang stopped beside a survey post. She opened Captain Bao’s map against the stone and held one corner beneath her palm.

<font color=yellowgreen>"Shuanghe is there, under the three jujubes. The convoy has the ancestral hall with the white ridge tiles. The village beyond the irrigation cut is Lingshu. They send two students into Jinzhou every morning on the post cart."</font>

<font color=moccasin>"From City Hall, this looked like open country."</font>

<font color=yellowgreen>"The upper terrace compresses distance. Up here, every chimney keeps its own line of smoke."</font>

Mei turned her glowing hand toward Jinzhou. The City Hall windows held the last gold. Below them, human hands carried lamp flame down each terrace. Across the plain, village cooking fires came awake one by one, small and steady inside the mark.

<font color=moccasin>"South,"</font> she said. <font color=moccasin>"The city stood beyond them."</font>

Yangyang raised the whistle.

Behind them, the youngest gate prepared its evening watch. Before them, the scarred land kept every village, every road, every old living tree. The sun went down through smoke and amber glass, and Jinzhou shone at the end of the opening.
