# DUCTUS

*ductus, -ūs*, m. — a leading; a command; a stroke of the pen. Also, of water: the course a channel cuts when nobody has told it which way to go.

---

**Colours.** Lanze Reiter = `lightsteelblue`. Ronin = `plum`. Wren (strider, hired) = `darkgoldenrod`. Lennart (Melve) = `peru`.

*Continues from* **STATIO**.

## THE FORK

At the marker below the ridge the road split, south to the ferry and the capital, north into the forest and the hill villages beyond it.

Ronin took the north road.

She had the primer in the outside pocket. She had the pack squared on her shoulders with the boot under the bedroll and the old cord at the bottom, and she took the north road the way she takes everything — she was on it before the question had been asked.

Lanze Reiter stood at the fork.

South was the bounty board and Vernworth and a man across a square who had been raising his hand at belt height for thirteen days. South was a page in the pack with one word she could read — *succession*, in the old hand, two characters — and a letter around it that she could not. Sparrow had read the letter aloud by firelight three weeks ago and folded it back along its own creases, and it was still there.

Ronin was ten paces up the north road.

Reiter followed.

---

## THE PACK

The wolves came down on the third day, seven of them, off a ridge of scrub oak and old stone where the tree line broke.

Ronin drew at the bracken.

She was three paces ahead and she drew clean out of the walk — the turn and the blade the same motion, the kind of thing that costs about six years and another eight of forgetting it cost anything — and she was inside the line before the lead wolf had finished committing. She took the second one at the shoulder and let the third go past on her left, because Reiter was behind her.

Reiter came in about three seconds later.

Forty seconds. Two worth skinning. Five for the field. The lance on Reiter's back with the finish on the shaft still clean.

Ronin wiped the blade and looked north.

<font color=plum>"We should make Melve by the fifth day."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The board is south."</font>

<font color=plum>"The primer came from north."</font>

She shouldered the pack and went. The carcasses steamed in the grass behind her, and Reiter stood in the field for about two seconds, which is almost three, and followed.

---

## THE STROKE

At camp she took the primer out.

She did it after the fire and the log, in the same order she does everything — helm off, strap checked, greaves loosened, book out. She held it flat with her thumbs along the spine, the way she holds all small things she has decided to keep.

A for the awl. She had that. B for the byre. She had that faster.

She was on C.

C for the cart, three strokes: the vertical and two arms. She traced them in the dirt beside the fire with a stick, and the vertical was clean, and the upper arm was clean, and the lower arm kept landing at the wrong height.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The arm drops."</font>

She said this to the fire, in her conditions voice.

<font color=plum>"It's higher in the old script."</font>

The stick stopped.

<font color=plum>"The old orthography has the arm about a third of the way up the stem."</font> Ronin was mending a strap and kept mending it. <font color=plum>"You're writing the right letter four hundred years late."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"How do you know the old script."</font>

<font color=plum>"It came back with me."</font>

She said it flat, owned, unattributed, the way she says everything that arrived from somewhere else. She has years of margins in her, and the tributaries wore off a long time ago.

Reiter lowered the arm. The C came out in the current hand, clean, and she did it seven more times and wiped the dirt with her boot and put the primer back.

It goes in the outside pocket. The folded page with *succession* on it is at the bottom of the same pocket. She puts her hand past it twice a day for the primer.

## THE STONE

Wren was at the stone on the forest road, sitting cross-legged on the flat with a knife across her knees.

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"Thirty-two, thirty-three."</font> She turned the blade and started the other side. <font color=darkgoldenrod>"Morning."</font>

<font color=plum>"Morning."</font>

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"Thirty-three a side. I got it off somebody. I'm keeping it."</font> She sheathed the knife and stood. <font color=darkgoldenrod>"Wren. You need a third?"</font>

She lasted three days. She did the locks, the traps, the things at arm's length, and she counted everything, and the number was always thirty-three.

On the second night she told them that Wilm of Melve sends parcels through the riftstones. Named, addressed, to specific pawns. The whole village thinks he is odd.

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"He heard about you off an archer who talks about you like a weather system."</font> She was working the knife, twenty-nine, thirty. <font color=darkgoldenrod>"Says the grey one gives an hour-long report after every fight and nobody asks for it and it's the best hour on the road, and the other one's called Already and the wood's always cut."</font>

<font color=plum>"What else does the archer say."</font>

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"A third of it is worth hearing."</font> Thirty-three. She sheathed the knife. <font color=darkgoldenrod>"The rest is a bowstring story about Bakbattahl."</font>

---

She left at the stone below the ridge, on the morning before Melve.

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"Somebody's going to ask me about her."</font> She tilted her chin at the road above, where Reiter was standing with the lance across her back. <font color=darkgoldenrod>"What do I say."</font>

<font color=plum>"Say she carries a lance and the shaft is clean."</font>

<font color=darkgoldenrod>"That's the thing?"</font>

<font color=plum>"It's what I've got."</font>

Wren did her half-bow — theatrical, borrowed from a city she had never visited — and the stone took her, the way it does, and the road was two again.

For three days after, Ronin found thirty-three at odd moments — the steps to a ridge, the seconds at a latch, the stones in a ford. Then it faded, and the number went back to being hers.

---

## THE SMOKE

At the village below the ridge she bought two rolls from a baker and ate one on the walk and gave the other, in passing, to an old man at the trough, and neither of them looked at each other, because they had been doing this for eleven days in five villages and the exchange had worn smooth.

She has done this every day for fourteen years. One extra.

Reiter, three paces back, watched the giving, and her hand went to the book inside the plate, and came away.

---

They saw the smoke from the top of the last ridge, in the early afternoon.

Three columns, folding and restarting the way smoke does when what is burning is wet thatch. Three buildings. Under the smoke, if you had four centuries of field work in you, there was a rhythm — rise, fall, intervals — that said people were fighting something they could not finish.

Ronin went down the slope.

She went at a walk, deliberate, reading the footing, the blade coming out on the second step, and she was halfway down the hill before Reiter moved.

The gap was about one second.

---

## THE CHARGE

The dragon had the square.

It was bigger than the calculation, and the calculation had been running since the ridge, and Ronin adjusted the way she adjusts — she put the wrong number away and worked with the animal in front of her, which had a wingspan of about forty feet and was standing on a burning cart.

Villagers held the lanes around the square with axes and hay-forks and the look of people who have been doing this longer than they wanted and have stayed because leaving is a direction and they have run out of directions.

A boy came out of the lane on the left with a spear taller than he was, and the expression of somebody who has committed.

Ronin went into the square.

She went in at the angle she uses for large animals — oblique, shield forward, blade tucked — and the dragon's head came round. The eyes did something with the light that was recognition, which should be impossible, because they had never met.

The fire came.

It came low and it came across the square in a wall that took the remains of the cart and the front of a house and everything between, and Ronin went down behind her shield with the cloth at her shoulder burning through and the ground shaking under her knees.

She was on the ground for about two seconds. Two point one. Reiter counted it from the mouth of the lane, because she counts everything, and the number went into her head and stayed there.

And then Ronin was getting up.

The shield came off the ground. The blade came up. The weight went forward. The line committed — and the committing was the thing Lanze Reiter saw from the lane.

She saw somebody already going. Somebody whose weight was forward and whose line was decided, and a lance is a weapon that works because the horse has already decided, and the horse decides first, and the rider does the aiming.

Her hand went behind her shoulder and the lance came off the mount.

She ran.

The shaft was ash, sixty years old, replaced twice. The head was original. The grip had been unmarked for four hundred years, because a lance is a rider's weapon and the rider aims and the horse decides, and there has been nobody deciding first.

Ronin was deciding first.

The lance came down to the line. The point went past Ronin by about two feet on the left — Ronin heard the boots and knew the cadence and held her ground — and the point went into the shoulder joint above the wing root where the scale changes, and it went in about eighteen inches, and something in the plate, at the chest, behind the steel, behind the steel behind that, did something with no name in the log.

The dragon screamed. The wing folded and it hit the ground with the shoulder and the village shook, and then it was up again, and the lance came out, and the dragon went off the square trailing smoke from the joint and was over the ridge and gone.

---

Lanze Reiter stood in the square with the lance in her hands.

The grip had a mark on it. A dark line worn into the ash where the gauntlet had held for about six seconds. The shaft had been clean there for four hundred years, and now it carried a print.

Ronin came up beside her. Smoke-black on the left side, the cloth at her shoulder burned through, hands steady.

<font color=plum>"It went northeast."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"…"</font>

<font color=plum>"Toward Vernworth."</font>

Reiter put the lance back on the mount. The mark sat between the head and the wrap, visible from three feet in good light, and she put it on her back.

<font color=lightsteelblue>"Noted."</font>

---

The boy was at the edge of the square with the spear still in both hands.

He was about twelve. He looked at Reiter the way you look at something you sent for that arrived.

---

A man came out of a lane. He had a soldier's haircut grown out by a month, and farmer's hands, and the walk of somebody who used to carry weight on his left side and has since put it down.

<font color=peru>"Lennart. I keep the village."</font> He looked at the lance on her back, at the mark, at the smoke still coming off the roofs. <font color=peru>"Captain of the Guard sent word about a month ago. Asked me to watch for a grey one with a lance."</font>

<font color=lightsteelblue>"The Captain sends a great deal of word."</font>

<font color=peru>"That's what he's got to send when there's a dragon on his pasture."</font> He put his hands in his belt. <font color=peru>"I've written to him. Tonight, by name."</font>

He looked at the road north out of the village, the one that bends at the river and comes into the capital from the high side.

<font color=peru>"If you go north, the road comes through Vernworth."</font>

Ronin looked at the road.

Every road in this country bends through the capital. That is what a capital is for.

## THE ENTRY

That evening, at the edge of the village, by a fire that smelled of thatch, in the small square hand, on the ninth page from the back:

```
1402.  Contact. Dragon, adult. Melve, north approach.
       Party of two. No assistance.
       Village defence in progress on arrival.
       Civilian party, variously armed. One boy with a spear.
1404.  Engagement.
       At 1404 this unit deployed the lance.
       First deployment in service. On foot. No mount.
       Contact at shoulder joint above wing root, approx.
       eighteen inches, at a run.
       The going was not mine.
1409.  Dragon departed northeast. Direction of Vernworth.
       Partial. No kill. Three structures damaged.
       No fatalities among the defence.

Weather:  R. on the ground, 2.1 seconds. Recovery unassisted.

Remarks:  The shaft has a mark on it.

Remarks (cont.):
```
