## I. The Sleep Rota
### *twelve hours until the north road opens; drum interval, three minutes thirty-one seconds*

The city accepted Mei's suggestion about sleep by interpreting it as a rota.

Clerk Wen found six campaign cots in the lower records room. Panhua found blankets, two of them still carrying the clean mineral smell of the bathhouse. Lupa found the duty board and wrote names into it before anyone with a seal could turn rest into a matter of character.

Mei received the first ninety minutes.

She looked at the cot, at the map table, and at the north window where the completed moon showed through a sea hanging above the city.

"I can use the first watch to help with the casualty return," she said. "Cartethyia will have half the theatre in pieces by now, and Amy has been awake since yesterday decided to become today."

Lupa drew a line through Mei's name, rewrote it in larger characters, and handed the charcoal to Iuno.

"Lie down while the decision still belongs to you," she said. "In about ten seconds I start exercising field authority over your ankles."

"Does Jinzhou license wolves for medical practice?"

"Jinzhou can file a complaint with the wolf after breakfast."

Jinhsi had come in carrying the eighth-hour shelter return. She read the board, took in the cots, and turned her cup a quarter-turn by the rim.

"City Hall recognizes a rest order issued by a competent person during an emergency," she said. "Your previous record establishes Captain Lupa's competence in ankle matters."

Mei looked toward Changli.

The Counselor sat beyond the paper wall with Wen and three boxes of original records, preparing the account Jinhsi had required. Her shadow moved across the screen as she lifted a page. She had heard. The shape of her shoulders suggested that she would cite precedent if invited.

Mei took off her coat.

Amy claimed the cot beside hers, curled around a relay headset, and announced that she was monitoring the north line. Iuno removed the headset from her hands, set it on the floor between the cots, and lay down on Mei's other side with her crystalline stump against Mei's shoulder blade.

"This room has six beds," Amy said. "You have selected one with two additional people in it."

"Efficient heat distribution," Iuno said.

"You learned that phrase from Lupa."

"A strong treaty survives translation."

Mei laughed into the folded blanket. It took the rest of the room several breaths to realize she had fallen asleep at the end of it.

The duty board continued.

Cartethyia received one hour after the casualty boxes arrived. Ciaccona received forty minutes and spent eight of them arguing that musicians operated on a separate clock. Chisa took seventy minutes in a chair by the door, red string loose around her wrist, and woke at each drum with her fingers resting on the handle of her shears. Lupa slept last, in boots, with the northern freight list under one hand.

Jinhsi stayed at the table through two changes of the lamps. At the third, Panhua came in from the ward kitchens, read the board, and set a bowl over the shelter return.

"Magistrates get a column too," she said.

"The ninth-hour meeting begins in thirty-seven minutes."

"Wen moved it to this room. The citizens who wanted answers are bringing chairs."

Jinhsi looked at Wen.

He kept writing. "The notice was already wet when I received the correction, Excellency. Replacing it would waste paper."

Panhua pointed at the remaining cot.

Jinhsi lay down for forty-three minutes. The first people from Ward Nine arrived carrying chairs and found their Magistrate asleep under a bathhouse blanket with her hand closed around the edge. They put the chairs down quietly. A carpenter fixed the cot's loose crosspiece while she slept.

The ground struck once.

Three minutes and nineteen seconds.

The cups on the table chimed against their saucers. Mei's marked hand warmed under Iuno's palm and settled again. At the north shelf, four guardians lowered their shields. Along Bookbinders' Row, type shifted in its cases and several thousand names made a small metal sound together.

Jinzhou used the interval to breathe.

## II. The Names in Their Working Clothes
### *ten hours, twenty-six minutes; drum interval, three minutes six seconds*

Bookbinders' Row kept every lamp.

The original casualty return occupied three composing stones. The theatre's seven scripts occupied a fourth. Family letters, pension claims, barracks menus, quartermaster complaints, school copies, and one petition concerning a mule occupied every remaining flat surface. The Row had spent the evening borrowing sorts from printers who usually regarded each other as moral failures. By midnight, six rival houses were setting one register.

The first forme held ranks and names.

The second held one detail beside each name, verified by a person or a responsible record.

The third held roads.

Cartethyia moved between them with program paper, scissors, and a pencil worn to half its length. Every completed entry became a figure. She wrote the name across the chest, the detail along one arm, and the road down one leg. The figures stood on Jiyan's field map under brass weights and teacups.

"Tell me what he did after supper," she said to an old woman in a quilted coat. "The army return already gives us his battery."

The woman stared at the line bearing **LUO JIANG, SECOND GUN CREW**.

"He filed splinters off the cheap chopsticks," she said at last. "Every pair in the house. He kept a little knife by the rice jar and worked while people talked."

Cartethyia wrote it down.

At the next stone, the actor who played Geshu Lin set type for his brother.

**SHEN KE — SIGNALMAN, NORTH BATTERY — HATED PEARS — WEST SINKHOLE ROAD.**

The line required two settings because the Row lacked enough pears. Master Liao found the final sort in a menu forme, removed it from a steamed-fruit advertisement, and promised the restaurant a hand-painted replacement by morning.

"A pear has done honest work tonight," he said. "The restaurant will survive the demotion."

Ciaccona sat on the floor beneath the proofing rack with three versions of the march laid across her knees. She gave every name a spoken measure and every detail a different length of silence. The result resisted chorus. A thousand mouths could learn it; each mouth still had to attend.

Amy ran the receiving desk.

"Bookbinders' Row, soldier register," she said into the relay. "Give me the name, your relation, and one thing an official history would waste."

A voice from the eastern village offered a cook named Fan Yuelan, who had salted soup with her left hand and played dice with her right. A schoolmaster supplied Wen Tao, cartographer, who drew accurate roads and fantastical chickens. Two sisters called together about the brothers Peng Rui and Peng Shan, filed under one recovery date, one body recovered with both identity tags; Rui sang harmony, Shan stole blankets, and the family could identify whose tag had come home by the knot in the cord.

Amy separated the entries.

"Describe the knot slowly," she said. "I have a cordmaker beside the west receiver and she can put the answer on paper while you speak."

The cordmaker listened. The sisters argued through three possibilities, cried during the fourth, and agreed on the fifth. Peng Rui received the recovered tag. Peng Shan remained under **PRESENT IN FORMATION, RECOVERY OPEN**.

The register grew.

At two in the morning a proof came off the press with seven names missing from the wet sheet.

The spaces appeared in the middle of complete lines. Ink stood on either side. The paper held the pressure of the type. Chisa put two fingers against the blank for Captain Mi Renshu and listened to the room.

"The forme struck her," she said. "The ink left afterward."

Master Liao swore with professional breadth and reached for the locking key.

The lamps along the rear wall went out in sequence.

One. Two. Three.

Lupa came off the stair before the fourth. Her spear entered the dark at waist height and met a hooked tool with a sound like a plate breaking. Chisa heard the stranger's rear foot turn on the grit and opened her shears across the route to the window. A figure in a printer's apron stopped between the blades.

Their face carried the pleasant vacancy of a person assembled for one errand. Black type sorts hung from cords inside the apron, each one filed smooth where a character had once stood.

"Fractsidus compositor," Ciaccona said. She kept one hand on her lute strings and used the other to pull the family witnesses below the stone tables. "Their reviews must be dreadful."

The figure threw a resonance pin toward the first forme.

Lupa caught the wrist. Chisa cut the pin in flight. It opened in two halves above the stone and spilled a strip of black rain. Ciaccona struck a low chord; the rain held in the sound long enough for Master Liao to slam a galley tray over it.

The fifth lamp stayed lit.

Footsteps crossed the roof. Three sets, spreading toward separate stairs. Lupa heard them and kept both hands on the prisoner.

"The roof crew has four Patrollers and a Ranger pair," she said. "They have the chase. We keep this room."

The decision cost her. Chisa heard it in the pressure of her grip. The nearest set of footsteps passed directly over them, close enough for plaster dust to fall into Lupa's hair, and continued west. She stayed beside the composing stones and the people crouched under them.

The prisoner smiled with somebody else's mouth.

"The company clears during the speech," they said.

Cartethyia picked up Captain Mi Renshu's blank figure and held it where the compositor could see.

"She used to rescue moths from washbasins," said a young man beneath the second stone. His voice shook. "She carried a cup and a postcard for it. My father said she did that on campaign too."

Cartethyia wrote the moth along the figure's arm.

The blank on the proof darkened. Ink returned from the outer strokes inward until the captain's name stood complete.

Across the room, seven families began speaking at once.

The prisoner turned their head toward the sound. Their borrowed smile loosened by a fraction.

Chisa closed one shear blade against the floor beside their boot.

"You came to an occupied room," she said. "Listen."

On the roof, the Patroller teams drove the other three operatives toward the east stair in pairs. The skirmish moved away from the presses. Master Liao lifted the tray over the black rain, inspected the stone, and asked for a brush.

Every proof after that received a hand copy in the same hour. The Row sent duplicates by separate roads. Families took impressions home in their sleeves. Children in the theatre loft wrote names on costume lining. The captured compositor entered the city record under the face they wore, the tools they carried, and the open question of whose name belonged in the first column.

At the next drum, three minutes had become two minutes and fifty-one seconds.

The first register held six hundred and eighty-four names in type.

The second held six hundred and eighty-four lives in their working clothes.

## III. The Shelf Holds in Pairs
### *eight hours, forty minutes; drum interval, two minutes thirty-two seconds*

At the south shelf, every advance left a second set of prints beside it.

Jiyan had written the rule at the top of the field board:

**ONE VOICE CALLS. ONE VOICE ANSWERS. A THIRD KEEPS THE ROAD.**

The old doctrine assigned a vanguard, a support line, and reserves. The new board named who stood beside whom. Sergeant Du had Bao. Bao had Lieutenant Sun. The western gun crew had two shield bearers from Xuanfang and one engineer who could release their harnesses when phantom hands reached through the buckles. Each runner carried the name of the runner behind them. Each officer gave orders from the ground occupied by the people receiving them.

The phantom army tested every link.

At the second beat after moonset, the rain raised Captain Zhou beneath the western gun. He had died in the previous campaign and appeared now with sunlight on his old armor, pointing toward a bridge that had fallen with him.

Bao took one step.

Sergeant Du caught the back of her harness.

"Give me the placement of your living squad," he said.

Bao's breath broke around the captain's title. She put her hand on the field board and named her six people, their cover, their ammunition, and the route home. By the fourth name, the bridge lost its sunlight. By the sixth, Captain Zhou had rain through his chest and the western gun stood on the shelf again.

Jiyan moved Bao to the center for one interval and took her place at the sighting post. Lieutenant Sun moved with him. The board's rule held above rank.

Along the eastern rise, Hsin's four guardians received a remembered charge from crews who had served them fifty years earlier. Old command tones came through the Retroact rain, sweet with recognition. The first platform raised one foot toward the valley.

Hsin stood between its service horns with both hands in her sleeves.

"Present crews, state your names," she said.

Four engineers answered from the shoulder wells. Their voices traveled platform to platform. Shield locks closed in a staggered sequence, each guardian taking weight from the next. The lifted foot came down inside the line.

"Your ancestors built good manners into you," Hsin told the core beneath her. "They also installed current receivers. Attend to the people who climbed the ladder this morning."

The core changed key.

Qiuyuan heard the skirmish arrive before the scouts saw it. Six frequencies moved under the phantom artillery, disciplined and living, each wrapped in a smooth absence where identity should have caught against the air.

"Fractsidus on the north cut," he said. "Two carry resonance pins. One carries a speaking seal in General Geshu's cadence. Three hold the withdrawal path."

Jiyan left his spear against the map table. He signaled with his bare hand.

The forward Ranger pair yielded twelve paces. The Fractsidus followed. Xuanfang's second and third guardians crossed their shields above the cut, making a roof. Du's gun crew fired into the stone behind the infiltrators and closed the route with broken rock. The living attackers found themselves in a room made of four crafts: Jinzhou movement, Xuanfang weight, Qiuyuan's hearing, and Hsin's opinion of guests who entered through the artillery.

One operative struck the speaking seal.

Geshu Lin's voice issued from it.

"Jiyan. The final line belongs to the man who can reach me first."

The words carried the full hall around them. Applause pressed faintly at their edges. Every phantom on the shelf turned its face toward the general.

Jiyan took the speaking relay from Sergeant Du.

"Shen Ke, north-battery signalman, recovered from the west sinkhole road," he said. "His family cooked pears in a separate pot."

The third phantom rank broke formation. One soldier turned west and lifted his hand as though holding flags.

"Mi Renshu, captain, fourth relief company. She rescued moths from washbasins with a cup and a postcard."

A woman near the black banner looked down at her empty palms.

The Fractsidus operative struck the seal again. The heroic cadence rose.

Qiuyuan drew his wrapped sword one handspan from its cloth. The residual theft inside the seal rang against the Qiongqi token in Jiyan's field coffer. He found the seam and turned the blade within its wrapping.

The seal split along a line too fine for the eye.

"The borrowed authority ends here," he said.

The operator carrying it reached for a second pin. Bao and Du entered together, Bao on the wrist, Du at the shoulder. Lieutenant Sun held their return lane. The pin fell whole into the mud.

On the east cut, a seventh Fractsidus frequency had stayed beneath the rain. Qiuyuan heard it rise behind Hsin.

The fox moved first. One tail caught the descending knife. A second swept the nearest engineer into the shoulder well. Her human hand closed over the attacker's collar, and the air around the guardian became a court with very poor prospects for appeal.

"Seven guests," she said. "Your swordsman counted six. This one owes him an apology and me a door fee."

The attacker dissolved their borrowed face before capture. For an instant their features cycled through a dozen dead soldiers, each drawn from memorial prints. The guardian crew saw fathers, teachers, officers, and one son.

They kept the shield locked.

Hsin lowered the captive to the stone.

The skirmish lasted forty-seven seconds. It produced seven prisoners, two pierced shields, Bao's dislocated thumb, and one resonance burn along Qiuyuan's listening hand. Jiyan sent Bao to the medical station with Du beside her and Lieutenant Sun keeping the road. He sent Qiuyuan with the same escort until Baizhi's relay cleared further use of the bamboo decoction.

"The valley offered you a private verdict," Jiyan said as they walked.

Qiuyuan tilted his head toward the phantom ranks reforming beyond the cut. "A private verdict would repeat the first error. I came to give testimony in company."

At the medical shelter, Hsin used the Board of War's recall order to keep rain off the confiscated pins.

"The paper continues to serve," she said. "Mingting should be pleased."

The next beat reached them after two minutes and seventeen seconds.

Beyond the shelf, Geshu Lin resumed his memorial angle. Around him, seven named figures stood a fraction out of place.

The fraction held.

## IV. The Rooms Behind the Number
### *seven hours, twelve minutes; drum interval, two minutes eight seconds*

The official shelter remainder fell overnight from two hundred and fifteen to eighty-three.

It fell by doors.

The pepper merchant's loft cleared after six apprentices moved forty-one sacks onto municipal shelves and received a receipt naming every mark on every tie. The bathhouse added seven places when its proprietor saw that the night-soil cart came on schedule. The theatre manager opened the rehearsal room after the captured compositor had been taken away and required a Patroller at each stair. The tax office admitted eleven people by the east approach; the goose inspected their bedding, selected a folded quilt, and retained it under column twelve.

City Hall opened its west gallery for twenty-six. Jinhsi signed the public-building requisition. The gallery clerks moved their desks against the wall and kept working between bedrolls.

Every offer still produced conditions.

One grandmother needed a ground-floor room because her knees had reached a firm opinion about stairs. A family of five required cooking space apart from sesame oil. Three quarry workers slept by day and needed a room beyond the ward bells. Four shrine exclusions traveled with four households and occupied more floor on the form than the people did. A boy from Lianshui had a night terror at each drum and needed his older cousin within reach when he woke.

Iuno's one-stroke mark for *consent with a stated limit* appeared eighty-nine times.

The limits held.

At Ward Ten, an innkeeper offered her closed dining room, then withdrew the offer when she learned the prefecture could guarantee coal through dawn only. Jinhsi returned the key across the table and paid for the inspection time from emergency cash. The innkeeper watched her write the payment.

"You could compel the room," she said.

"Public power already has the school, the guildhall, the yards, and this building," Jinhsi said. "Your household keeps its own threshold. If the coal return changes, the offer desk stays open."

The innkeeper put the key back in her sleeve. Two hours later she returned with six brazier bricks from her sister's kiln and offered the room through noon.

At the freight yard, twenty-seven ferry laborers remained under canvas because the shelter list had classified them as a work crew. Their census cards classified them as residents. Their foreman placed both papers on Changli's table and asked which one would get them indoors.

Changli read the work order, the cards, and the weather return. Her injured arm rested on fresh cloth. Her authority over the registry channel remained suspended; she asked Wen for the current civic rule and waited while he brought it.

"The census governs the shelter duty," Wen said. "Their shift assignment governs where the ferry office wants them."

"Then the ferry office may want them from the school gymnasium," Changli said. "It has a loading door, dry rack space, and a direct path to the east landing. Enter twenty-seven residents. Bill the ferry office for the rack ropes it neglected to provide."

The foreman took the assignment sheet. "We have two dogs with the night team."

Changli asked the school caretaker.

The caretaker required the dogs to stay in the equipment room, away from the lacquered floor, with one laborer sleeping beside them. The terms entered the sheet. Twenty-seven citizens received a roof.

The remainder fell to fifty-six.

It rose to sixty-one when a north-road cart arrived carrying five people whose receiving village had lost its footbridge in the Retroact rain.

Clerk Wen reopened the line.

Jinhsi watched him add the names.

"I wanted to see zero before I left," she said.

"The register prefers accuracy to morale," Wen said.

"Keep giving accuracy the last word."

The five received places above a salting shed, with a new rail on the stairs and floor-scrubbing entered against the prefecture. The number resumed its descent.

At the ninth-hour ward meeting, people asked about the northern skirmish, the grain rate, the next shuttle, and who would govern the city if the field column departed. Jinhsi answered the first three with current figures. She set the fourth question aside until every ward table had asked it in its own words.

Ward One asked who held the civil seal.

Ward Three asked who could open the medical reserve.

Ward Six asked whether the wedding kitchen would still receive its flour after the army took the north road.

Ward Nine asked who would listen when the next five people arrived.

Eleven versions reached City Hall before dawn.

Jinhsi laid them beside Changli's resignation envelope.

The paper wall between the work room and the account room shone pale with the coming day.

## V. Two Seals and Eleven Tables
### *five hours, fifty-eight minutes; drum interval, one minute fifty-eight seconds*

Jinhsi finished Changli's account at dawn.

The originals lay between them: eleven years of backward returns, seven years of altered dates, copies kept under a private seal, runner maps redirected through the Counselor's hands, Luo Zemin's disclosures, his daughter's protection order, and the resignation Changli had written before the first question.

Clerk Wen's minutes filled thirty-one pages.

The last question concerned the morning when Jué chose a girl of fifteen.

"If you had placed the first return in front of me," Jinhsi said, "what did you believe I would do?"

Changli rested her scarred arm on the folded cloth. The feather-shaped burns ran from wrist to sleeve, pale in the dawn.

"You would have read it until you understood every available implication. Then you would have carried the Council's fear as evidence about yourself. I chose an established record for you and called the choice protection."

"You also chose seven years in which my office investigated the wrong chronology."

"Yes. The false date shaped staffing, budgets, and every inference concerning the silence. I have entered each consequence I can trace. The ones beyond my sight remain my liability as they surface."

Jinhsi looked at the resignation envelope.

"Your answer stays before the Decree Council. My personal judgment requires a longer room than this morning gives us."

"I understand the distinction, and I will keep the appointment."

The drum sounded north of the walls.

One minute and fifty-eight seconds.

Rain climbed the terrace rail and held a row of pale copies of the room: Changli at fifteen beside a bookstore lamp; Jinhsi at fifteen beneath Jué's first descending light; the present table with its records open. The images broke into ordinary drops.

Jinhsi drew the eleven ward questions toward her.

"I will travel with the field column when the north road opens," she said. "Geshu Lin died under Jinzhou's command and has received three years of answers written at a safe distance from him. I will carry the office to the place where he can answer it. The question travels in my mouth; the answer returns to eleven tables."

Changli's gaze moved to the field map. "The city will require an acting Magistrate before the departure order can issue."

"That appointment is why your resignation remains in its envelope."

Wen's brush paused.

Changli held Jinhsi's gaze. Warmth stayed at the surface of her expression. Beneath it, the grandmaster considered every line on the board and found the move that hurt.

"My recent use of authority has placed your trust under strain," she said. "The Decree Council could appoint Senior Clerk Gao by noon."

"That officer knows four wards and the grain schedule. You know eleven wards, seven ministries, the north compact, and the habits of every office currently arguing over our survival. The assignment uses what you have, under terms written in this room."

Jinhsi put a fresh sheet between them and wrote.

Changli would hold the civil seal from departure until Jinhsi resumed the office in person or an open Decree Council proceeding installed a successor. Clerk Wen would hold the key to the prophetic archive and countersign any order touching Jué, the Mingting registry, or Luo Zemin's household. Emergency requisitions of private rooms required the relevant ward table's concurrence. Stores, casualties, shelter, and roads would be read publicly every second hour. Each ward retained its own meeting. The city would continue speaking in eleven rooms.

Jinhsi added a final clause in her own hand:

**THE COMPLETE ACCOUNT BETWEEN MAGISTRATE AND COUNSELOR REMAINS SCHEDULED UPON RETURN.**

She turned the sheet around.

"This appointment carries my confidence in your present work," she said. "The rest keeps its date."

Changli read every line. Her right hand stayed steady as she signed.

"I will keep the tables running," she said. "Wen will have each return before the lamps change."

Jinhsi pressed the civil seal into a square of red cloth and gave it to her.

The transfer made a small sound. Wood against table. Outside, hammers continued on Old Seven's repaired ankle. In the west gallery, a child woke beneath a borrowed blanket and asked where breakfast was. Someone answered from the records desk and pointed downstairs.

Mei had been standing in the doorway with Amy and Iuno. She came in after the seal changed hands.

"You said the question belongs in your mouth," she said to Jinhsi. "Who stands close enough to hear the answer?"

Jinhsi picked up the field seal.

"General Jiyan commands the entry. You and your household hold the center's exits. Qiuyuan listens beneath the performance. Hsin and her guardians cover the shelf. The named Rangers carry the true roads. Yangyang carries the Wind's terms. I carry the city's authority to ask what it owes and the duty to bring the answer home for eleven tables to hear."

Amy leaned one shoulder against the frame. "That sounds like a formation. I want the distances on paper."

"You may design the relay placement with Jiyan."

"Good. Ma drifts when people need catching. Auntie predicts around corners. I want a channel that survives both habits."

*The child reduces our mysteries to equipment faults,* the High Priestess observed inside Iuno's head. *Charming. Accurate. Expensive.*

Iuno touched the ring in her coat pocket.

"Give every member two recall voices," she said. "One from the field, one from the city. If the performance takes a voice, the second remains outside its grammar."

Lupa had entered behind them, still carrying shelf mud on her boots. She put Jiyan's paired-movement board beside the appointment.

"Add a handhold," she said. "Voices help people who can hear. A belt, rope, wrist, shield edge, spear haft, coat seam. Everybody goes in with two ways to be brought back."

Chisa set a coil of red cord on the table. "I can stitch the short tethers. They release when the person holding them chooses."

Jinhsi looked around the room. The formation had assembled while she spoke: law, relay, prediction, muscle, thread, roads, and the city behind them under a seal she had just placed in another woman's hands.

"Put the distances on paper," she said.

Amy smiled and took the largest sheet.

## VI. A Rehearsal With Exits
### *four hours, twenty-one minutes; drum interval, one minute forty-one seconds*

The theatre removed its final beat.

Ciaccona began with the seventh-edition march and took away the audience's cue. Three drum strokes crossed the stage. The held space opened. A company trained for thirty years to rise on the fourth stayed seated.

The silence reached for completion.

From the north relay came Ovathrax's answer, deep enough to shake dust from the fly loft.

One minute and forty-one seconds after the previous blow, the war god supplied the missing beat.

The work lights dimmed. Phantom rain rose through the stage boards. The actor wearing Geshu Lin's armor appeared at center in double exposure: one tired man with a bound knee, one memorial general at the angle engraved in Victory Square.

Cartethyia placed named figures around him along the first-edition blocking.

Ciaccona played again.

This time, the first space held Shen Ke's name and the separate pot for pears. The next held Mi Renshu and the moth postcard. Fan Yuelan entered on the third measure with salt in her left hand and dice in her right. Wen Tao's fantastical chicken occupied an interval the musicians had to count twice.

The heroic march developed uneven feet.

The phantom general turned his head by a degree.

Amy sat at the receiver with Jiyan's field board and called timings north. Sergeant Du repeated them beside the western gun. Each name reached the shelf through a living voice. Qiuyuan reported the separations: two seconds, three, one and a half, four. Hsin reported which guardian cores changed key. Jiyan marked the roads.

"The fourth space still pulls toward the valley center," he said over the relay. Artillery moved behind his voice. "Your details loosen individuals. The black banner keeps the formation's destination."

Ciaccona rested her palm over the strings. "Then the music can help them turn while somebody else gives them a place to turn toward. I can build the opening. I need the answer to remain theirs."

The actor at center removed his helmet. Sweat had reached his jaw.

"When the company clears, I have a speech and an empty house," he said. "With these figures in place, every line lands on somebody. It changes where I breathe."

Jinhsi walked onto the stage and stood in the southern messenger's mark.

"Show me the first edition's final movement."

The actor placed the helmet under one arm. He faced north. The paper company stood behind him.

"The messenger enters here," he said, pointing with two fingers. "She brings the relief order. Geshu reads it. The officers report their sections. He looks back before he answers."

"Why did the later editions remove her?"

"The second playwright called the order redundant. The city already knew relief came too late. By the fifth, the schools needed twelve fewer lines. By the seventh, I received a cleaner ending and the company received darkness."

Jinhsi took the relief order from the prop table. Its paper had softened through years of handling.

"Run the first edition with the names in their true positions."

They did.

Geshu faced north. The messenger approached from the south. Officers reported the west road, the medical tents, the fallen bridge, the reserve that arrived after its own maps. When the general looked back, the actor found a stage full of people engaged in separate tasks. One signalman held flags. A captain carried a moth in a cup. Two brothers argued over a blanket. The cook shook dice in one hand.

The actor's next breath came from his own chest.

"If we advance," he said, reading the first-edition line, "we advance by sections. If we return, we return by name."

At the north shelf, something struck the speaking relay hard enough to fill the theatre with static.

Geshu Lin's phantom voice came through the interference.

"Relief has spent three years arriving. Bring the city that signed it."

Every work light went black.

Cartethyia transformed in the dark. Fleurdelys caught the falling scenery in one hand and held the fly rail with the other while stagehands cleared the children from beneath it. Chisa opened her shears across a seam of black rain and stitched it to the empty orchestra pit. Lupa put herself between the receiver and the north doors. Mei caught the actor as the double exposure pulled his body toward the memorial mark.

Amy kept the relay open.

"South shelf, give me living voices in order," she said. Her performance register filled the dark, warm and carrying. "Start with the person holding the receiver, then travel outward by touch."

Sergeant Du answered. Bao followed, thumb bound to her palm. Lieutenant Sun gave the western gun crew. Four engineers gave the guardian shoulder wells. Qiuyuan named the medical orderly holding his burnt hand. Hsin supplied all nine of her tails as witnesses and received an engineer's laugh in return.

The lights came back one row at a time.

Mei still held the actor by both forearms. Fleurdelys still held the scenery. Chisa's red stitch ran around the black rain. Lupa's spear remained level toward the door. Amy's relay carried the shelf breathing in many tempos.

The actor looked at the paper figures around his boots.

"Keep the messenger," he said. "The schools can have the extra twelve lines."

Ciaccona wrote the change into the score.

She left the fourth beat open.

## VII. What Reached Zero
### *two hours, forty-nine minutes; drum interval, one minute twenty-nine seconds*

Between the appointment of an Acting Magistrate and the second hour before departure, fifty-seven names cleared verified thresholds. Nineteen took the upper rooms of the old posthouse after a roof crew replaced six tiles. Fourteen entered the granary offices with the grain seals moved into Wen's custody. Eleven occupied rooms whose owners had boarded the eastern shuttle and sent their keys back by the same driver. Eight went to the shrine school under four household exclusions copied onto the door. Five received the repaired stove at household seven-two-three and a morning lesson in keeping its temperamental flue open.

The shelter remainder reached four at the second hour before departure.

The four were the Ge family from the north road: grandmother, daughter, a boy of twelve, and a donkey named Minister because he accepted tribute and ignored policy. Three assigned rooms had rejected the donkey. Grandmother Ge accepted each rejection as evidence of weak construction.

Chixia found them beside the south cistern, where the boy had built a dry place under the cart with two doors and a drainage lip.

"The school equipment room has two dogs and a caretaker with firm terms," she said. "The old courier stable has one stall, a sound roof, and a wall shared with the school kitchen. The family room above it has a ground-floor entrance from the slope."

Grandmother Ge inspected the stable before the family room. Minister inspected Chixia's paper bag.

"He bites officials when they smell important," the grandmother said.

"That's a recognized civic tradition here. We have a goose with tenure."

The donkey received the stall. The family received the room. The caretaker required a bucket at the east drain and the boy's help carrying breakfast water. The terms filled half a page.

Clerk Wen read the four names aloud at City Hall.

The official remainder reached zero.

It reached zero through 4,112 individual entries, each with a place, a threshold, a term, or a departure address verified by the receiving end. Forty-seven arrangements still ended within two days. Nine stoves still needed repair. Twenty-three sleepers had quilts folded twice in place of mattresses. The tax-office goose still held one blanket under its body and showed administrative resolve.

Jinhsi signed the return with those conditions attached.

"Read the whole lower section at the ward tables," she said. "Zero can travel with its debts showing."

The citizen register closed for six minutes.

Then the maternity runner arrived from the school infirmary with a new census line.

A daughter had been born to the couple married in the weavers' hall. The mother's family occupied the curtained dais with the sugar cranes under armed supervision from the groom's mother. The infant had a roof, two grandmothers, six ducks' worth of soup, and a name still under discussion.

Wen reopened the register and entered **ONE RESIDENT, NAME PENDING, HOUSED**.

The shelter cohort became 4,113.

The shelter remainder stayed at zero.

At Bookbinders' Row, the soldiers' register resisted the same conclusion.

The original casualty return held 1,907 entries. By the second hour before departure, 1,431 had a name, a personal detail, and a true road. Three hundred and eighty-two had a name and rank while the personal detail traveled. Sixty-one carried variant spellings that required family or unit review. Thirty-three damaged lines preserved a unit, a recovery place, and a human space where the name belonged.

The Row printed all four categories.

The thirty-three spaces received borders deep enough to survive rain. Master Liao locked them into the forme himself.

"The blank is part of the line," he told an apprentice. "Give it the same pressure."

Cartethyia had cut until the skin beneath her thumb opened. Baizhi bandaged it and placed the scissors in Ciaccona's custody for twenty minutes. Cartethyia spent the interval sorting figures with her other hand.

"You have enough paper people to fill the front row," Amy said. She was threading copies into waterproof cases. "Your thumb would enjoy remaining attached for the trip."

"There are four hundred and seventy-six entries waiting for more of themselves."

"The bandage gets twenty minutes. The cases stay open beside you."

Cartethyia looked at the incomplete figures. Each had a name or a road. Some had only the shape of the missing line.

"Then they come with us like this," she said.

"That was always the plan."

Amy closed the first case and gave it to her.

The Wind entered through the Row's high windows. Pages lifted in separate waves. The complete figures stood. The partial figures leaned. The thirty-three bordered blanks remained flat under a long pressure like a hand spread over them.

Yangyang came in below it, coat fastened for the field, a courier case at each hip.

"The east-wall station reports a clear route to the shelf for another forty minutes," she said. "After that, the Retroact front reaches the third bridge."

The Wind turned the map north.

Yangyang placed her palm on its south edge.

"Your present form has carried three valleys of weather since yesterday," she said to the air. "You choose the route. I carry one case, and another person carries the second. The papers arrive through several hands."

The western case strap rose and settled across Amy's shoulder.

"That works for me," Amy said. "I can handle weather that delegates."

The Wind moved through her pink hair, pleased with itself.

Yangyang's ears went faintly red under her hat. "She delegates when the alternative involves luggage."

The pressure over the thirty-three blanks deepened once, then released.

Every figure went into the cases, complete and incomplete together.

## VIII. The Order With Room in It
### *one hour, thirteen minutes; drum interval, one minute twelve seconds*

Grand Marshal Li Zhaoying reached the gorge road with Falling Rain unsheathed across her saddle.

Her column brought fresh medical wagons, shield couplings from two gates, three companies of infantry, a mobile kitchen, and the capital's considered response to Jinhsi's challenge. The response occupied one sheet.

She read it herself over the field relay.

"The Norfall restriction is amended at the twelfth hour. General Jiyan retains field command. The composite entry column will carry Jinzhou civil authority, Xuanfang sovereign undertaking, ISA material testimony, the restored casualty register, and the first-edition field roads. The outer line passes to my headquarters upon entry."

At City Hall, Jinhsi stood with one hand on the receiver.

"State the objective for the public return," she said.

Li Zhaoying's voice had the working grain of a sword handle worn eleven years.

"Separate the dead from the performance. Keep a named road south available to every living member and resource each route through relief. Preserve each dead soldier's capacity to answer apart from the chorus. Identify the Fractsidus control structure. Deny Ovathrax the completed cue."

"Give the limit by which your staff expects to spend people."

The relay carried wind over the gorge road, horse tack, and the quiet of officers hearing an old field on a new morning.

"Casualty planning will use named recovery obligations and route-specific extraction capacity," the Grand Marshal said. "Every section enters with its own return line. My headquarters will hold those lines until Jiyan's column comes south."

Jinhsi kept her hand on the receiver. "Your headquarters inherits the shelf and every person standing on it. Put their names under the same recovery standard."

The Grand Marshal answered at the pace of a roll already prepared.

"Third Relief enters eighty-six names under Captain Lan Shuo. Two medical teams answer to Doctor Fei An at the east cut. Fifth Engineers enters thirty-one under Master Gong Mi and extracts by the cradle road. Captain He Yuan's gorge reserve enters seventy-two and rotates every second interval. Adjutant Chen is transmitting full rolls to City Hall and Bookbinders' Row. My own line sits at the bottom."

"Who carries your way back?"

Harness rings moved near the relay. A man beside Li Zhaoying made a sound of long-suffering recognition.

"Zhou Lian, my driver," she said. "He has carried Falling Rain for eleven years and dislikes every road I choose."

"Grand Marshals improve slowly," Zhou Lian said.

"The state has invested heavily in his candor."

The relay carried one brief current of laughter through both commands. Then Li Zhaoying's voice returned to the order.

"Disposition of any surviving Threnodian remainder remains open. This authority covers defense, observation, and separation. Further disposition requires field evidence and Jinzhou's civic process."

Jinhsi looked at Mei.

The golden mark had warmed at the phrase *comes south.*

"Jinzhou accepts the amended order," Jinhsi said.

Li Zhaoying remained on the channel.

"Magistrate. I signed the assessment three years ago. The word used was acceptable. Bring me evidence fit to revise it."

"The packet carries names, personal details, and thirty-three spaces with their sources intact."

"My headquarters will read every line before the next order moves."

The line closed.

Baizhi took the receiver immediately and reopened it to the north medical shelter.

"Qiuyuan's amplified listening is limited to two measured doses before physician review," she said. "Sergeant Du holds the flask. General Jiyan holds the second dose authorization. A third dose requires my voice on the channel, and the burn receives a clean dressing before movement."

Qiuyuan answered through artillery and the small clink of porcelain. "The terms are clear. Sergeant Du has the flask, and Orderly Han is preparing the dressing now."

"Keep her beside you when the valley changes the sound."

"She has already assigned herself to my return line and informed me that senior operatives require close supervision."

Somewhere near Qiuyuan, Orderly Han confirmed this assessment. Baizhi wrote her name beside his on the medical return.

Hsin entered the channel from the shoulder of the first guardian.

"My four old ladies will open the shelf and hold the sides," she said. "The Board's opinion remains beneath my lunch. Old Seven stays in Jinzhou with the boy, the bridge, and forty-one cups of tea. A sovereign should leave one good argument behind."

In the recovery yard, Old Seven turned its service lens toward the north. The boy with the model bridge had attached a paper sign to the truss:

**FOR WHEN YOU COME BACK.**

The automaton lifted its repaired foot, set it down, and opened its warm service shutter to the Wind.

The Wind crossed the gap and carried the smell of machine oil to the gate.

## IX. Before It Starts
### *forty-two minutes; drum interval, fifty-seven seconds*

Panhua fed the departure column at eleven tables.

The ward kitchens sent millet congee, duck broth from the wedding, steamed buns marked with the cooks' thumbprints, salted greens, and one tray of pear pastries that Master Liao regarded as professional provocation. Soldiers ate beside printers. Rangers sat beside the families whose details they would carry. The field cases stayed under chairs with straps looped around ankles.

Mei moved through the room with a bowl in one hand and her marked palm landing on shoulders as she passed. She checked Cartethyia's bandage, stole one of Amy's greens, put it back after Amy stared at her, and leaned against Iuno long enough for both of them to finish half a cup of Earl Grey.

"Four minutes," Iuno said, watching the tea while the drum clock ran. "I finally have a crisis that respects steeping time."

"The drum gives you fifty-seven seconds."

"Then it can watch me finish."

Mei drank. The tea was hot.

Amy sat across from them marking relay cards.

"Your field call is Fool," she told Mei. "Your return calls are Ma on my channel and Mei on Iuno's. If either of us says your full title, you've wandered into something theatrical and we are insulting you back to the road."

"You prepared this in advance."

"Of course. I brought extra paper."

Iuno glanced at the stack. "Laureate will bring her back through spite."

"Champion of Septimont will do it faster."

Mei put her head in one hand. "My family has weaponized my résumé."

Lupa sat at the end of the table checking knots. Each person received a short red tether with a release toggle at both ends. She paired Chisa with Cartethyia, Ciaccona with Amy, Mei with Iuno, then ran a second set across the field roles so every pair belonged to a larger triangle.

"The rope says where your body is," she said. "The voices say where your people are. The moment either story disagrees, speak."

Chisa tested the toggle. "The stitch will open under a deliberate pull. Panic tightens it."

"Good. Panic will have to work against the stitching."

"I learned from a door."

Cartethyia tucked the camellia terrarium deeper into her pack. A new pale leaf pressed against the glass.

Ciaccona saw it. "You are taking a plant into a war valley."

"She has spent the month hearing all our plans. Leaving her behind would be rude."

"Cantarella will poison me with etiquette when she hears."

"She'll have to get in line behind the camellia."

Ciaccona tightened the plant's upper strap herself.

At the Magistrate's table, the eleven ward representatives brought Jinhsi their last questions.

The cooper asked who would authorize timber while she traveled. Changli gave the schedule and the countersignature route. The bathhouse owner asked whether the coal rate held through noon. Wen read the stores figure. The bride's mother asked for the return of six carving knives loaned to the garrison kitchen and offered a seventh if someone brought it back clean.

An old man from the north terraces placed a wooden chopstick on the table.

"My son died under Geshu Lin," he said. "His name is Gao Shun. He clicked these against the bowl when he wanted another serving and thought his mother missed it. Tell him she heard every time."

Jinhsi accepted the chopstick across both palms.

"Gao Shun enters with the fourth relief section. I will carry this detail."

The old man bowed and returned to his ward table.

The city kept its speech at eleven tables. Each one produced its own sentence.

Ward One said the eastern shuttle would run until the third bridge closed.

Ward Two said the mill owed forty sacks at noon.

Ward Three said the hot pool remained available to medical crews.

Ward Four said household four-one-one had paid its stove feed through the week.

Ward Five sent a list of families awaiting soldier details.

Ward Six said the mother and daughter were resting and the infant had begun life loudly.

Ward Seven said the north cistern held.

Ward Eight sent indigo compensation claims and six boiler workers.

Ward Nine said every new arrival should report at the dye-house table.

Ward Ten said the inn dining room had coal through noon.

Ward Eleven sent a repair crew to the third bridge and requested that departing soldiers step around the fresh mortar.

Each ward sheet carried one final field: **WHO RETURNS.**

The clerks left it open. Around it, the tables wrote what their thresholds could carry: beds, hot water, medical isolation, translation, witnesses, storage for weapons freely surrendered, and the household terms attached to each offer. The field column would bring names, needs, and chosen destinations. The eleven rooms would answer from there.

Changli stood beside the north gate in the Acting Magistrate's plain outer robe. The civil seal rested inside her sleeve. Wen stood at her right with the prophetic key. Chixia stood at her left with the city patrol return and a paper bag carrying the column's road breakfast.

Jinhsi approached in field clothes, the Loong Scale at her waist and the city's chopstick detail secured inside her map case.

"The tables will hear the fifty-seven-second interval in the next return," Changli said. "The third bridge report arrives in twelve minutes. The registry key stays with Wen."

"Keep the numbers attached to their rooms."

"Each clerk has a street and each street has a table."

The unfinished account stood between them with thirty-one pages and another appointment.

Changli adjusted the map-case strap where it crossed Jinhsi's shoulder. The gesture came from years of departures and carried the care that had survived its own methods.

"Do as you wish," she said. "I will support the road home."

Jinhsi placed one hand over Changli's scarred wrist for the length of a breath.

"Have the next room ready when I return."

"Wen already reserved it."

The Clerk looked toward the gate with the expression of a man whose administrative foresight had become emotionally inconvenient.

## X. Departure
### *the north gate; the next interval already running*

The column formed by names.

Jinhsi and the city field clerks stood with the Midnight Rangers assigned to the true roads. Mei's household assembled around the two waterproof cases. Yangyang took the left strap of the first; Amy took the right. The Wind moved between them, turning the upper pages in order. Lupa checked every tether. Chisa listened down the line for breathing she had heard before. Cartethyia counted figures. Ciaccona carried the unfinished measure under her hand. Iuno poured the last of the tea into a field flask and gave it to Mei.

"It's still hot," Mei said after tasting it.

"Treat that as a standing order."

At the south shelf, Jiyan received the departure signal. He returned Qiuyuan's wrapped sword and Qiongqi token under field custody. Sergeant Du kept the bamboo flask. Orderly Han kept Qiuyuan's medical line. Hsin's four guardians rose behind them, shields overlapping, their fifth note answering from Old Seven in the city.

The Grand Marshal's green-and-gold command took the outer shelf. Captain Lan Shuo's Third Relief opened the eastern road. Master Gong Mi's Fifth Engineers opened the west for the medical cradles. Captain He Yuan's reserve took the middle and set its rotation clock beside Jiyan's. Two copies of their rolls traveled south by separate relays. Every forward element had a protecting section. Every protecting section had a relief. The battle that wanted one hero received a diagram crowded with hands.

Jiyan looked north.

Geshu Lin stood at the valley mouth beneath the black banner. The rain gave him ceremonial armor and a wound arranged for the gallery. Behind him, named soldiers held themselves a fraction away from the chorus. An older wind moved along the torn edge of the banner, carrying the third night of the battle in its folds. When the Welcoming Wind reached the shelf, the older pressure turned toward it.

Qiuyuan heard both currents meet.

"The record recognizes the impression," he said.

Hsin's tails lifted in the charged air. "Then perhaps they have postponed an introduction for three years."

Jiyan gave the column its route by voice and whistle. The battle drums stayed under covers. Sergeant Du spoke each section forward. Lieutenant Sun answered from the rear. Bao carried the western road with her bound thumb against the map.

In Jinzhou, the north gate opened.

The departure column walked through at the pace of people carrying paper.

Citizens stood along the wall stair with copies of the register. As each living name passed, somebody marked the departure time and the names of the people beside it. The towers kept the returning beat in reserve.

Mei reached the first turn beyond the gate and felt the golden mark draw toward Norfall. The pull offered a straight line: one woman, one sword, one center.

Her tether tightened gently at the wrist.

Iuno walked on her right. Amy and Ciaccona carried the case behind. Lupa held the outer edge with Chisa. Cartethyia's camellia tapped against its glass. Yangyang walked inside the Wind's patient pressure. Jinhsi came level with the field seal and Gao Shun's chopstick in her map case. Around them, Rangers carried 1,907 entries in several hands.

Mei chose to match their pace.

At the bend past the eighth tower, she looked back.

Changli stood beneath the gate with Wen, Chixia, eleven ward runners, and a city behind them. Its 4,113 displaced citizens had verified roofs whose conditions remained written down. Along the wall, every lamp burned. In the recovery yard, the tea unit had begun setting out cups for the next shift and placed an extra row along the north side. Panhua's kitchen raised steam from a stockpot broad enough to serve people who had yet to arrive.

The Wind lifted one page from the complete register and carried it ahead of the column. It bore a soldier's name, a true road, and room beside both for the answer.

North of them, fifty-seven seconds of silence approached its end.

Geshu Lin waited with his weapon raised.

Jinzhou kept the water hot.
